Indication:  Characterization and localization of recurrent focal aware seizures in a patient with h/o structural focal epilepsy. Seizure onset at age 42, with current frequency approximately 3/week. MRI demonstrates encephalomalacia.

AED Changes During Admission:  Home lacosamide was continued without change. No ASM taper was required. Outpatient rescue medication is lorazepam sublingual PRN.

Technique:  Continuous scalp video EEG was obtained using the international 10-20 electrode placement system with additional anterior temporal electrodes. EEG and synchronized digital video were reviewed continuously. Standard bedside activation procedures were performed as clinically appropriate.

Background:  
The waking background was well organized with appropriate anterior-to-posterior gradients and a symmetric, reactive 9-10 Hz PDR. Expected lower-voltage beta activity was present anteriorly. Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Slow-wave sleep and REM sleep were recorded. No persistent focal slowing or diffuse background abnormality was identified. Overall, the background was normal.

Interictal Findings:  
1. Frequent sharp waves and sharp-and-slow-wave complexes over the right parietal region, maximal at P4 and P8 with a field extending to CP4 and O2. These discharges were enhanced during drowsiness and sleep.  
2. No independent left hemispheric IEDs.  
3. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or electrographic status epilepticus.

Ictal Events:  
Total events captured:  3 typical clinical events.

Event 1 (Day 1):  
Behavioral: The patient reported a stereotyped emotional sensation followed by tingling of the hand. This progressed to unilateral clonic jerking of the hand and distal arm. Awareness and ability to respond to staff were preserved throughout.  
EEG: Initial low-voltage fast activity over the right parietal region, maximal at P4/CP4, followed by evolving rhythmic 6-7 Hz theta activity. The discharge increased in amplitude, slowed to 3-4 Hz, and spread within the right centroparietal region before abrupt termination.  
Post-ictal: Brief right parietal delta slowing without confusion or language disturbance.

Events 2 and 3 (Days 2-3):  
Stereotyped focal aware events with an emotional nonmotor onset, hand tingling, and subsequent clonic jerking. Awareness remained preserved. Both demonstrated a concordant right parietal ictal onset with evolution in frequency, morphology, and spatial distribution. No bilateral tonic-clonic activity occurred.

Impression:  
1. Abnormal long-term video EEG due to frequent right parietal interictal epileptiform discharges and three electroclinical seizures arising from the right parietal region.  
2. The recorded events are focal aware seizures, characterized by emotional nonmotor onset, hand paresthesia, and clonic motor activity with preserved awareness.  
3. Findings support a diagnosis of right parietal structural focal epilepsy, concordant with the known MRI finding of encephalomalacia. The interictal background is otherwise normal.  
4. Epilepsy is currently drug responsive on lacosamide. Continue sublingual lorazepam PRN as the prescribed rescue medication. Should he have a seizure cluster, use nasal midazolam rescue and call 911.
