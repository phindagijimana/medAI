Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy beginning at age 66. The habitual episodes are characterized by an emotional/nonmotor onset with déjà vu, followed by versive head turning and preserved awareness, consistent with focal aware seizures. Current frequency is approximately 2 per month.

The reported hippocampal sclerosis supports a structural etiology. The available prolonged EEG was abnormal because of mild diffuse slowing but did not show IEDs or a definite ictal onset. A normal or nonepileptiform prolonged EEG would not r/o focal epilepsy, particularly when events are infrequent and no habitual spell is recorded.

The patient has shown a therapeutic response to valproate and brivaracetam and is therefore considered drug responsive at this time, though not seizure-free. Lacosamide was previously stopped because of mood adverse effects. Based on the medication history supplied, the available information does not establish drug-resistant epilepsy.

Atypical absence is a possible consideration and cannot be excluded with absolute certainty. However, the focal aura, versive head turning, preserved awareness, and structural MRI finding are more suggestive of focal-onset epilepsy than an absence epilepsy syndrome.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Depakote (valproate) and Briviact (brivaracetam) without change pending confirmation of the current doses and dosing schedules. The patient will provide an updated medication list or have the prescribing pharmacy/referring office forward the exact mg strength and frequency.

2. Maintain a seizure diary documenting frequency and the sequence of symptoms, particularly the déjà vu or emotional onset, direction of head turning, and whether awareness remains fully preserved.

4. Please forward the prolonged EEG tracing if available. If diagnostic uncertainty persists, prolonged VEEG or EMU monitoring may be considered to capture a habitual event and clarify focal seizure versus the less likely possibility of atypical absence.

5. We reviewed standard seizure precautions, including avoiding driving unless permitted under applicable state requirements, avoiding unprotected heights, and using appropriate supervision around water or other potentially hazardous situations.

6. At present, the hx is most consistent with drug-responsive focal epilepsy. A presurgical evaluation or neuromodulation workup is not indicated based solely on the available medication history. This can be reconsidered if adequate ASM trials fail and seizures remain uncontrolled.

7. Follow-up will be arranged after the medication doses and original MRI/EEG data are available for review. The patient should contact the treating neurologist sooner for increased seizure frequency, change in semiology, or medication intolerance.
