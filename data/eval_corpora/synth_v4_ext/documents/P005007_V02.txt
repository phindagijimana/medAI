Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx

Indication: H/o drug-responsive focal epilepsy of unknown etiology, onset at age 28, with impaired awareness, versive head turning, hyperkinetic movements, and focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures approximately once yearly; currently on perampanel. Routine EEG demonstrated right temporal slowing and interictal epileptiform discharges. Evaluate for epileptogenic lesion; r/o mesial temporal sclerosis.

Comparison: None.

Technique: Multiplanar, multisequence MRI of the brain performed on a 3T scanner. Sequences included sagittal T1, axial and coronal T2, axial and coronal FLAIR with thin coronal imaging through the temporal lobes, DIR, axial DWI/ADC, axial SWI, and 3D T1 MPRAGE. No IV contrast administered.

Findings:

Ventricles and Extra-axial Spaces: Ventricles and sulci within normal limits for age. No hydrocephalus, extra-axial collection, mass effect, or midline shift.

Brain Parenchyma: Subtle focal cortical thickening and blurring of the gray-white junction involving the right anterior inferior temporal lobe, with faint associated T2/FLAIR hyperintensity extending into the immediately subjacent white matter. No restricted diffusion, hemorrhage, or abnormal susceptibility. No acute infarct or intracranial mass.

Mesial Temporal Structures: Bilateral hippocampi are symmetric in size and signal, with preserved internal architecture. No hippocampal T2/FLAIR hyperintensity or volume loss. No mesial temporal sclerosis.

Cortex: Right anterior temporal cortical abnormality as described above, possibly reflecting a subtle malformation of cortical development. Otherwise normal gyral and sulcal pattern. No additional cortical thickening, gray-white blurring, or heterotopic gray matter identified.

White Matter: No additional focal white matter signal abnormality.

Basal Ganglia and Thalami: Normal signal and morphology.

Brainstem and Cerebellum: Normal. No focal signal abnormality or cerebellar volume loss.

Vasculature: Preserved major intracranial arterial flow voids on conventional sequences.

Skull Base and Calvarium: No focal marrow-replacing lesion.

Paranasal Sinuses and Mastoid Air Cells: Mild scattered ethmoid sinus mucosal thickening. Mastoid air cells are clear.

Orbits: Unremarkable.

Impression:

1. Subtle focal cortical thickening, gray-white junction blurring, and mild T2/FLAIR hyperintensity in the right anterior inferior temporal lobe, suggestive of focal cortical dysplasia or another subtle malformation of cortical development. This focal MRI lesion is likely epileptogenic and concordant with the reported right temporal EEG abnormalities.
2. Normal hippocampal volume, signal, and internal architecture. No mesial temporal sclerosis.
3. No acute intracranial abnormality.
