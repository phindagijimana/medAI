**Referral Question:**  
Diagnostic/presurgical neuropsychological evaluation to characterize current cognitive functioning following left laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT) and to inform PRC discussion regarding potential future epilepsy treatment. Particular attention was given to language, verbal memory, executive functioning, and risk associated with additional left hemispheric intervention.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o drug-resistant focal epilepsy with onset at age 13. Typical events are focal aware seizures beginning with an epigastric rising sensation and emotional nonmotor symptoms, followed by hyperkinetic movements with preserved awareness. Current frequency is zero seizures per month; the patient is presently seizure-free by report.

Etiology is structural. MRI demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia. The patient is s/p left LITT in 2023. A routine EEG showed focal slowing, without interictal epileptiform discharges. No electrographic seizures or localizable ictal onset were captured on that study.

Current maintenance ASMs are oxcarbazepine, brivaracetam, and topiramate. Doses were not available for review. Rescue medication is diazepam by rectal route PRN. Previously trialed medications include levetiracetam, discontinued due to rash; zonisamide, discontinued due to cognitive side effects; lacosamide and perampanel, each discontinued due to mood side effects; and lamotrigine, discontinued due to sedation. This treatment hx is consistent with drug-resistant epilepsy despite the current period of seizure freedom.

Available records did not provide sufficient detail regarding psychiatric treatment, developmental history, educational attainment, or occupational functioning. These factors should be integrated with the present findings when additional collateral information becomes available.

**Behavioral Observations:**

**Appearance and Cooperation:**  
Arrived appropriately dressed and groomed. In NAD. Alert, oriented x3. Cooperative throughout the interview and testing.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent and grammatical, with normal rate and prosody. No overt paraphasic errors. Comprehension adequate for multistep test instructions.

**Mood and Affect:**  
Mood described as generally stable. Affect appropriately modulated and congruent with conversational content. No behavioral evidence of acute psychiatric distress.

**Effort and Engagement:**  
Sustained task engagement across the evaluation. Embedded validity indicators were within expectation. TOMM Trial 2 = 50/50, supporting adequate effort. Results are considered a valid estimate of current cognitive functioning.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; Trail Making Test Parts A and B; Boston Naming Test; COWAT-FAS and Animal Fluency; CVLT-3; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure Test; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test; Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; BDI-II; and BAI.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was Average (FSIQ = 96, 39th percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 98, 45th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 102, 55th percentile). Working Memory was Low Average (WMI = 89, 23rd percentile). The profile did not indicate generalized intellectual decline.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Basic auditory attention was Average (Digit Span scaled score = 9). Processing Speed was Low Average (PSI = 86, 18th percentile). Trail Making A performance was Low Average (T = 41), without errors. Mild slowing may reflect combined effects of epilepsy, polytherapy, and topiramate exposure rather than a focal deficit.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Low Average to Borderline (BNT T = 38, 12th percentile), with benefit from phonemic cues. Phonemic verbal fluency was Low Average (FAS T = 40), and semantic fluency was Borderline (Animals T = 35). Receptive language and conversational speech remained intact. The relative weakness in confrontation naming and semantic retrieval raises concern for dominant temporal language-network inefficiency, although language dominance has not been established.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, total learning across five trials was Borderline (T = 36). Initial acquisition was limited, with modest benefit from repetition. Short-delay free recall was Borderline (scaled score = 5), and long-delay free recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7). Recognition discrimination was Low Average, with several false-positive responses. WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7), while delayed recall was Borderline (scaled score = 5). This pattern reflects reduced verbal learning and retrieval, with less robust retention of contextual verbal information.

**Visual Memory:**  
Visual memory was comparatively stronger. WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 10), and delayed recall was Average (scaled score = 9). ROCF delayed recall was Low Average to Average (T = 44), with preserved recognition. There was no clear evidence of a disproportionate nonverbal memory deficit.

**Executive Function:**  
Trail Making B was Low Average (T = 40), with one sequencing error. Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T = 42). WCST performance was broadly Average, including category completion and perseverative responding. Findings suggest mild inefficiency in speeded cognitive flexibility and inhibition without a primary executive syndrome.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Block Design was Average (scaled score = 11). ROCF copy was intact, with an organized approach and accurate representation of major structural details. No visuospatial constructional impairment was identified.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average bilaterally, without a clinically meaningful lateralized discrepancy. No gross motor abnormality was observed during testing.

**Mood/Personality:**  
BDI-II and BAI responses fell within the minimal range. There was no indication that depression or anxiety substantially suppressed cognitive performance. Continued monitoring is reasonable given prior mood-related adverse effects from lacosamide and perampanel.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
The evaluation documents Average overall intellectual functioning and intact visuospatial construction, visual memory, and untimed executive problem-solving. Relative weaknesses were observed in processing speed, semantic retrieval, confrontation naming, and verbal learning/memory. The pattern is suggestive of dominant temporal-system inefficiency, but it is not fully localizing. Routine EEG showed focal slowing without epileptiform discharges and therefore provides limited lateralizing information.

Given the h/o left LITT in 2023 and current weaknesses in naming and verbal memory, an additional left temporal intervention may carry moderate risk for further decline in verbal learning, delayed verbal recall, and word retrieval, particularly if language is left dominant and functional tissue remains near the proposed treatment margin. Current seizure freedom is an important favorable clinical factor and should be weighed against the cognitive risks of further intervention.

**Recommendations:**

1. Discuss findings at multidisciplinary PRC, integrating MRI, operative history, seizure course, and electrophysiology.
2. Obtain task-based fMRI for language lateralization and mapping if additional left hemispheric surgery is considered. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant.
3. If recurrent seizures prompt neuromodulation consideration, cognitive risk may be lower with a network-based option such as the NeuroPace RNS System than with additional dominant temporal resection, depending on localization.
4. Per the documented contingency plan: “If she develops another breakthrough seizure, escalate to LTM.” The chart also states: “Should he have a cluster, use nasal midazolam rescue and call 911.” These instructions should be reconciled with the currently listed rectal diazepam rescue prescription.
5. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6–12 months after any additional intervention, or sooner if meaningful cognitive decline is reported.
6. Use written schedules, medication organizers, repetition, and external memory aids. Cognitive rehabilitation may be appropriate if verbal memory or word-finding difficulties interfere with daily functioning.
