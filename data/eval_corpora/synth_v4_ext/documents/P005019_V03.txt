Indication: Hx of structural focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures, onset at age 66, occurring approximately twice per month. Prolonged EEG requested to characterize events, assess for interictal epileptiform activity, and r/o an alternative generalized seizure type. Atypical absence is considered possible and cannot be excluded clinically.

Relevant Clinical History: MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis. Epilepsy has been drug responsive. Current maintenance ASMs are valproate and brivaracetam. Previously trialed lacosamide, discontinued due to mood side effects. No h/o developmental delay.

ASM Changes During Monitoring: Valproate and brivaracetam were continued without documented adjustment during this recording.

Technique: Prolonged digital video EEG was recorded using the international 10-20 system with additional anterior temporal electrodes. EEG and synchronized video were reviewed continuously. Automated seizure and spike detection programs were utilized as adjuncts to visual analysis.

Background:  
The waking background was continuous and symmetric, with expected anterior-to-posterior organization. A poorly sustained posterior dominant rhythm of approximately 7-8 Hz was present and reactive to eye opening. There was a mild diffuse excess of theta activity, at times admixed with low-amplitude delta, consistent with mild diffuse slowing. Faster frequencies were symmetric without persistent focal attenuation.

Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Sleep was characterized by bilaterally synchronous vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was preserved. No sustained focal slowing was identified.

Interictal Findings:  
1. No focal or generalized IEDs were seen. Specifically, there were no temporal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs.  
2. Mild diffuse background slowing was present throughout the study.  
3. No electrographic finding provided localization or lateralization of the patient’s epilepsy.

Ictal Events:  
Total clinical events captured: 0.

No habitual episodes of déjà vu, emotional sensation, or versive head turning were recorded. No electrographic seizures or subclinical ictal patterns occurred. Accordingly, no ictal onset region or laterality could be determined. The absence of captured events limits assessment of the reported focal aware spells and does not exclude epilepsy. Likewise, because no representative staring or behavioral-arrest episode was recorded, atypical absence cannot be definitively excluded, although no generalized spike-and-wave pattern was seen.

Impression:  
1. Abnormal prolonged video EEG due to mild diffuse background slowing, a nonspecific finding indicative of mild diffuse cerebral dysfunction.  
2. No interictal epileptiform discharges and no electrographic seizures were recorded.  
3. No habitual clinical events were captured. This study therefore does not electrographically localize the patient’s focal epilepsy. Clinical semiology remains consistent with focal aware seizures of nonmotor emotional onset, with déjà vu aura and subsequent versive head turning while awareness remains preserved. Correlation with the known structural lesion of hippocampal sclerosis and the overall clinical course is recommended.
