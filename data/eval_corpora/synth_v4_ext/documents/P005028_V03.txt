Indication: Hx of drug-resistant epilepsy with impaired-awareness events consisting of brief staring and eyelid fluttering; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o electrographic seizures.

Clinical History: Adult with seizure onset at age 34. Typical events consist of brief staring spells with eyelid fluttering and impaired awareness, without aura, clinically described as typical absence seizures. Current frequency is approximately 2 seizures/year. Epilepsy has been classified as generalized and drug-resistant. There is also a prior working diagnosis of probable/likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy based in part on structural etiology, with MRI demonstrating focal cortical dysplasia; thus, the electroclinical and imaging data are potentially discordant.

Current maintenance ASMs are valproate and lamotrigine. Rescue medication is diazepam per rectum PRN. Previously trialed topiramate and brivaracetam were discontinued due to inadequate seizure control; oxcarbazepine was discontinued due to cognitive side effects. S/p vagus nerve stimulator implantation in 2023. Family hx notable for a brother with stroke.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system, with additional electrocardiographic monitoring. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and became drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and well organized. A 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and appropriately reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. No persistent focal slowing or diffuse background slowing was identified.

Awake State: Normal organization without sustained asymmetry. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs were present.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. No definite sleep spindles or K-complexes were seen.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing. There were brief generalized spike-and-wave discharges during and following hyperventilation, without a definite behavioral correlate.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings: Occasional generalized, bilaterally synchronous spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were seen, with bifrontal predominance at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. Discharges occurred singly and in brief 3-4 Hz runs lasting up to approximately 2 seconds. No consistent focal lead-in or lateralizing maximum was identified. These findings represent generalized interictal epileptiform discharges.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or habitual clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, bilaterally synchronous spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges on an otherwise normal awake and drowsy background.

2. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized seizures and are compatible with a generalized epilepsy syndrome, including the reported typical absence seizure phenotype.

3. No focal electrographic abnormality or ictal onset was demonstrated during this study. The generalized EEG findings should be correlated with the MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia and the prior probable/likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy formulation. A normal background does not exclude epilepsy.
