Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was well organized and symmetric, with a sustained 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that showed appropriate reactivity to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was noted anteriorly. There was no persistent focal or generalized slowing.

Awake State: Normal organization without asymmetry. No focal attenuation or breach rhythm was identified.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of symmetric, diffuse theta activity. No definite sleep spindles or K complexes were seen.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal abnormality or epileptiform activation.

Photic Stimulation: PS elicited a symmetric photic driving response at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was present.

Interictal Findings: No focal, multifocal, or generalized IEDs were identified. Specifically, there were no temporal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No electrographic abnormality was seen to localize an epileptogenic region.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. Accordingly, no ictal onset or lateralizing information was obtained.

ECG: Single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness and drowsiness.  
2. No focal slowing or interictal epileptiform discharges were seen. No electrographic seizures were recorded.  
3. This study does not provide electrographic localization or classification of the patient’s epilepsy. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy, particularly in a patient with a known structural lesion and established clinical hx. If further characterization is required following a breakthrough event, prolonged video-EEG monitoring may be considered.
