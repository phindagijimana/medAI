History of Present Illness:  
The patient is a 67-year-old right-handed man with seizure onset at age 66. Habitual events occur without a recognized aura. Witnesses describe cognitive/behavioral impairment at onset, followed by impaired awareness, versive head turning, and tonic posturing. These are clinically concerning for focal impaired awareness seizures with both nonmotor cognitive and focal motor features. The precise direction of head version and distribution of tonic posturing are not reliably available from the referral history. No separate spell type is clearly described. He denies a h/o developmental delay or psychiatric comorbidity.

His epilepsy is considered structural in the setting of encephalomalacia on MRI and prior left-sided lesionectomy in 2018. The indication for the lesionectomy and the pathology report are not available in the current referral material. Despite surgery and treatment with multiple ASMs, he continues to have breakthrough events, currently averaging approximately one seizure per year. Although the event frequency is low, the spells remain clinically significant because of impaired awareness and motor posturing. No consistent provoking factor has been identified in the available history.

Prior routine EEG demonstrated mild diffuse background slowing and left frontal IEDs. No electrographic seizure or definite ictal onset was recorded on that study. Thus, the available data support focal epilepsy but do not establish whether the habitual events arise from the left frontal region, another region adjacent to prior surgical change, or represent a nonepileptic mimic. He is admitted for diagnostic event characterization and r/o nonepileptic events, with attention to localization if a typical seizure is captured.

His current maintenance regimen consists of Vimpat (lacosamide), Fycompa (perampanel), Keppra (levetiracetam), and Lamictal (lamotrigine). Exact outpatient doses were not included in the referral documentation and will be confirmed with the patient, family, pharmacy, and medication bottles before administration or dose reduction. He previously trialed Trileptal (oxcarbazepine), discontinued because of cognitive side effects, and Depakote (valproate), discontinued because of sedation. He has Nayzilam (midazolam) intranasal available as an outpatient rescue medication. Continued seizures despite multiple appropriately selected ASMs and prior epilepsy surgery are consistent with drug-resistant epilepsy.
