Assessment / Impression

This is an adult with seizure onset at age 34 and recurrent brief staring spells with eyelid fluttering and impaired awareness, currently occurring approximately twice yearly. The referral diagnosis is generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures, supported by bilateral generalized IEDs on routine EEG. The normal EEG background and generalized interictal pattern are consistent with that classification, although no habitual event or electrographic seizure was captured.

There are several atypical or potentially discordant features. Specifically, adult onset at age 34 and the reported focal cortical dysplasia on MRI raise the possibility of a focal epileptogenic substrate. The overall presentation is therefore suggestive of generalized epilepsy but may represent likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy with rapid bilateral synchronization. A focal and generalized network disorder cannot be excluded on the data currently available. The MRI lesion should not be assumed to be causative without electroclinical concordance.

The patient meets the clinical profile of drug-resistant epilepsy, given continued seizures despite current valproate and lamotrigine, failed prior trials of topiramate and brivaracetam for inadequate efficacy, intolerance of oxcarbazepine, and persistent events despite VNS implantation. Additional characterization is important before further medication changes or any lesion-directed surgical recommendation.

Recommendations / Plan

1. We recommend inpatient EMU admission for prolonged VEEG/LTM to capture the habitual staring and eyelid-fluttering events. The principal goal is to determine whether these are generalized-onset typical absence seizures or focal impaired-awareness seizures with rapid bilateral spread.

2. We will request the original MRI images for direct review rather than relying only on the report. If the prior study is not adequate for presurgical assessment, an epilepsy-protocol brain MRI may be appropriate to better characterize the reported focal cortical dysplasia.

3. Continue Depakote (valproate) and Lamictal (lamotrigine) at the established outpatient doses pending medication reconciliation and review of the complete treatment history. I would avoid empiric changes today because the current doses and frequencies were not included in the referral records.
