History of Present Illness:  
The patient is a 55-year-old right-handed woman with seizure onset at age 36. Her habitual events are generalized tonic-clonic seizures without warning. She specifically denies an aura. Witnessed semiology consists of abrupt impairment of awareness with tonic stiffening followed by clonic jerking. Available history does not establish a focal onset, consistent lateralizing signs, or a preceding focal impaired-awareness phase. Her epilepsy has been attributed to an infectious cause, although details of the antecedent infection are not available in the records reviewed for this admission.

She continues to have approximately one generalized tonic-clonic seizure per month despite treatment with four maintenance ASMs and prior neuromodulation, meeting criteria for drug-resistant epilepsy. Current therapy consists of Depakote (valproate), Zonegran (zonisamide), Briviact (brivaracetam), and Vimpat (lacosamide). Exact outpatient doses and administration times are pending formal medication reconciliation. Previously trialed Trileptal (oxcarbazepine), which was discontinued because of mood side effects. She also failed an adequate trial of Lamictal (lamotrigine) because of inadequate seizure control. No additional prior ASM trials are documented in the available source material.

Prior ambulatory EEG showed a normal background with bilateral generalized interictal epileptiform discharges. No electrographic seizure or ictal onset localization was recorded. Thus, the available EEG data support a generalized epilepsy pattern but have not captured a habitual clinical event. She underwent vagus nerve stimulator implantation in 2018 and remains s/p VNS therapy; device model, current settings, battery status, and degree of prior benefit will be confirmed during this admission.

Given persistent monthly convulsions despite polytherapy and VNS, she is admitted for presurgical evaluation. Although the current syndrome is classified as generalized rather than focal epilepsy, inpatient VEEG is needed to r/o an unrecognized focal onset with rapid bilateral synchronization and to determine whether resective/ablative therapy is plausible. If generalized onset is confirmed, findings may inform VNS optimization or consideration of network neuromodulation. No interval events have been documented since her most recent community neurology evaluation.
