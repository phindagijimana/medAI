Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with seizure onset at age 28 and generalized epilepsy characterized by typical absence seizures. The habitual semiology is brief staring with eyelid fluttering and impaired awareness, without aura or urinary incontinence. The routine EEG supports the generalized classification, showing bilateral generalized IEDs, with mild diffuse background slowing. The identified etiology is immune-mediated and related to a causative NMDA receptor antibody.

The patient has a prior drug-resistant course, including discontinued trials of lamotrigine due to cognitive adverse effects, topiramate due to rash, and levetiracetam due to mood adverse effects. The patient is also s/p left LITT in 2017. Nevertheless, seizure control is currently excellent, with zero seizures per month on valproate and oxcarbazepine.

The combination of a generalized electroclinical phenotype and prior left-sided ablative treatment warrants careful review of the original presurgical data. The LITT history alone does not establish focal-onset epilepsy, particularly when the currently available semiology and routine EEG are generalized. At present, there is no reported seizure activity requiring an immediate change in maintenance therapy.
