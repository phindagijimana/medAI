Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  03/12/20XX  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. [Deidentified]  
Indication:  H/o structural focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 31, reported frequency approximately five/month. Typical events include a fear and sensory aura followed by impaired awareness, hyperkinetic movements, and versive head turning. MRI reportedly demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia. EEG obtained to r/o interictal epileptiform activity and further characterize seizure risk. Current maintenance ASMs are zonisamide and brivaracetam; epilepsy has been medication responsive.

Technique:  A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and stepwise PS. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. No sedation was administered.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was distributed symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. No persistent focal slowing or generalized background slowing was identified.

Awake state:  Normal organization and reactivity. No asymmetry of the PDR. The patient remained clinically in NAD during the recording.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was otherwise normal.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric theta slowing without focal abnormality or activation of additional epileptiform discharges.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal findings:  Occasional medium-amplitude sharp waves were present independently over the right anterior-to-mid temporal region, maximal at F8/T8 with a field extending to FT10. These discharges were more apparent during drowsiness and sleep and demonstrated an after-going slow wave. No left temporal or generalized epileptiform discharges were identified. No TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs were present.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. There was therefore no ictal onset identified on this routine study.

ECG:  Regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional right temporal interictal epileptiform discharges, maximal over the right anterior-to-mid temporal region. These findings indicate increased epileptogenic potential in the right temporal region and support the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy.

2. The background organization and sleep architecture are normal. No focal slowing is present.

3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. The patient’s described focal impaired-awareness seizures—with fear/sensory onset, hyperkinetic motor behavior, versive head turning, and impaired awareness—are not captured by this study. A normal ictal record during a brief routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended, particularly with the known focal cortical dysplasia.
