Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and focal cerebral dysfunction.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system. Additional ECG and eye-movement channels were recorded. The study was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation (HV) for 3 minutes and photic stimulation (PS). The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Clinical History: The patient has focal epilepsy of unknown etiology, with seizure onset at age 28. Typical events begin without a reported aura and consist of impaired awareness, versive head turning, and hyperkinetic movements, with occasional progression to a bilateral tonic-clonic seizure. Current frequency is approximately one seizure per year, consistent with drug-responsive epilepsy. Current maintenance ASM is perampanel. Prior MRI reportedly demonstrates a focal lesion that is likely epileptogenic; correlation with the lesion location and other clinical data is recommended.

Description:

Background: In the awake state, there was a well-organized, symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm (PDR), reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent generalized slowing.

Awake state: Intermittent polymorphic 2–5 Hz delta-theta slowing was present over the right temporal region, maximal at F8/T8 and at times extending to P8. This slowing was present independently of drowsiness and was occasionally sharply contoured.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Right temporal slowing remained evident during sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of additional abnormalities.

Photic stimulation: PS from 1–20 Hz produced a symmetric photic driving response at several frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal findings: Occasional right temporal sharp waves were identified, maximal at F8/T8, with a field extending to P8. These discharges were more prominent during drowsiness and sleep. No independent left temporal or generalized epileptiform discharges were seen. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or electrographic status epilepticus was present.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. Accordingly, an ictal onset zone cannot be determined from this routine study.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional right temporal sharp-wave discharges, indicating increased epileptogenic potential in the right temporal region and supporting the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy.
2. Intermittent right temporal polymorphic delta-theta slowing, consistent with focal cerebral dysfunction in this region.
3. No seizures were recorded. The reported focal MRI lesion is likely epileptogenic, although electroclinical and imaging correlation is required; a routine EEG cannot independently establish that relationship.
