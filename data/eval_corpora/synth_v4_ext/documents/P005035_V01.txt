Assessment / Impression

This patient has generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures, beginning at 6 years of age. The habitual semiology is brief staring with eyelid fluttering and impaired awareness, without an aura. The routine EEG showed a normal background with bilateral generalized IEDs, which is concordant with the reported generalized epilepsy phenotype.

The epilepsy has a reported genetic etiology related to STXBP1 c.902A>G. The brain MRI showed only nonspecific white matter changes in the available report. There is no reported hx of CNS infection.

The supplied information labels the epilepsy as drug responsive, and the patient remains on perampanel. However, residual events are reported approximately 3 times per month. Before changing therapy, I would like to confirm the current perampanel dose, frequency, adherence, tolerability, and the exact nature of the ongoing spells. There is not enough information today to determine whether the current regimen has been fully optimized. There is also no documented list of prior ASMs, so the patient does not meet a documented drug-resistant epilepsy profile based on the information available to me.

The clinical and EEG data are most consistent with generalized rather than focal epilepsy. At present, there is no indication from the supplied records for a focal epilepsy surgery evaluation or intracranial neuromodulation assessment.
