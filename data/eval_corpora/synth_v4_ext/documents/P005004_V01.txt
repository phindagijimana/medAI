Assessment / Impression

The patient has focal epilepsy of genetic etiology, with onset at age 16 and ongoing focal impaired-awareness seizures occurring approximately three times per year. The habitual semiology consists of a fear aura and autonomic nonmotor onset, followed by impaired awareness, tonic posturing, and versive head turning. The patient denies complete loss of consciousness. The routine EEG is abnormal, demonstrating focal slowing with left occipital IEDs, although no electrographic seizure or ictal onset was captured.

The patient continues to have seizures while taking brivaracetam and carbamazepine and has failed prior trials of topiramate, oxcarbazepine, and perampanel because of adverse effects. This is drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Because routine interictal EEG findings alone do not establish the seizure-onset zone, prolonged VEEG would be needed before considering any focal surgical or device-based therapy.

We discussed that drug resistance warrants consideration of a comprehensive presurgical evaluation. Depending on localization and the results of additional testing, potential treatment pathways could include resective or ablative therapy, the NeuroPace RNS System, Medtronic DBS Percept, or LivaNova VNS SenTiva. No specific procedure can be recommended from the currently available data.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Briviact (brivaracetam) and Tegretol (carbamazepine) without change pending confirmation of the exact doses, frequencies, adherence, and tolerability. The patient should provide an updated medication list for reconciliation.

2. Continue Nayzilam (midazolam) intranasal PRN as the prescribed rescue medication. Rescue-use instructions should be reviewed once the dispensed formulation and directions are confirmed.

3. Arrange prolonged inpatient VEEG/LTM in the EMU to characterize habitual events and attempt ictal localization. Current routine EEG data show left occipital IEDs and focal slowing but no recorded ictal onset.

4. Obtain and review prior neuroimaging if available. If no epilepsy-protocol study has been completed, an epilepsy-protocol brain MRI should be considered as part of the DRE evaluation.

5. Following VEEG and imaging review, discuss the case in a multidisciplinary epilepsy conference to determine whether further presurgical testing is appropriate and to r/o a surgically remediable focal onset.

6. Continue standard seizure precautions and follow applicable NY driving restrictions for patients with ongoing seizures involving impaired awareness.

7. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after EMU testing, or sooner for increased seizure frequency, medication intolerance, or other clinical concerns.
