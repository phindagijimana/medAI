Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures and progression to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. The habitual semiology consists of an autonomic aura described as an epigastric rising sensation, followed by impaired awareness, epileptic spasms, and tonic posturing. The events are reported to occur approximately three times per week.

The available data support a structural etiology. MRI reportedly shows hippocampal sclerosis, while prolonged EEG demonstrates a normal background with left temporal IEDs. The clinical aura and impaired awareness are compatible with temporal network involvement. However, MRI laterality was not supplied, and the prolonged EEG did not provide an ictal onset. Therefore, full electroclinical concordance cannot yet be established from the available records.

The patient is currently taking topiramate and has been classified as drug-responsive. At the same time, the reported frequency of approximately three events per week indicates an ongoing seizure burden that warrants clarification. Medication dose, adherence, treatment duration, baseline seizure frequency, and the exact response to topiramate need to be reconciled before making a major change. There is no documented evidence in the supplied information of failed adequate trials of multiple appropriately selected ASMs; accordingly, the available record does not establish drug-resistant epilepsy.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Topamax (topiramate) at the currently prescribed regimen for now. The patient should provide the exact tablet strength, total daily dose, and dosing schedule so that medication reconciliation can be completed before adjustment.

2. Continue Klonopin (clonazepam) orally disintegrating formulation as the prescribed PRN rescue medication. We will verify the dose, maximum frequency, and instructions for use. Rescue medication is separate from the daily maintenance ASM.

3. Request the complete MRI brain report and actual images for direct review, with particular attention to the side and extent of hippocampal sclerosis and concordance with the left temporal EEG abnormalities.

4. Obtain the complete prolonged EEG report and, when available, representative EEG data. The prior study showed left temporal IEDs but did not document an ictal onset.

5. Maintain a seizure diary documenting the aura, awareness, motor features, progression to bilateral tonic-clonic activity, rescue medication use, and event frequency. This will help quantify response to topiramate and distinguish each habitual event type.

6. We reviewed standard seizure precautions, including avoidance of unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Driving should follow applicable NY seizure-related restrictions and physician guidance.

7. Once the medication regimen and prior testing are verified, we will reassess whether additional VEEG/LTM is needed to capture and characterize the habitual events. At present, the supplied data support focal epilepsy, but no ictal localization was established by the prior prolonged EEG.

8. Follow-up will be arranged after receipt of the outside MRI and EEG materials. Earlier neurologic reassessment is appropriate for increasing seizure frequency, prolonged convulsive activity, recurrent seizures without recovery, injury, or other acute concern.
