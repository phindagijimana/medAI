Study Type: Ambulatory EEG  
Indication: H/o drug-resistant generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures, approximately one per month; r/o subclinical seizures and characterize interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: The patient developed seizures at age 36 in the setting of an infectious etiology. Typical events occur without aura and consist of impaired awareness with tonic stiffening followed by clonic jerking, consistent with GTC seizures. Current frequency is approximately one seizure per month despite polytherapy. Current maintenance ASMs are valproate, zonisamide, brivaracetam, and lacosamide. Previously trialed oxcarbazepine, discontinued due to mood side effects, and lamotrigine, discontinued because of inadequate seizure control. The patient is s/p vagus nerve stimulator implantation in 2018.

Technique: A prolonged ambulatory digital EEG was recorded using electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed, with computerized spike and seizure detection used as an adjunct. The recording included awake and drowsy states. Electrode and movement artifact intermittently affected portions of the ambulatory record but did not preclude interpretation. Hyperventilation and photic stimulation were not performed as part of this ambulatory study.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-formed posterior dominant rhythm in the alpha range that was reactive to eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. There was no persistent focal slowing or diffuse background attenuation.

Awake State: Normal admixture of alpha and beta activity. No sustained asymmetry was identified.

Drowsiness: Attenuation of the posterior dominant rhythm with emergence of diffuse theta activity. State transitions were physiologic.

Interictal Findings: Generalized, bilaterally synchronous interictal epileptiform discharges were present. These discharges showed no consistent hemispheric lead-in, focal field, or reproducible focal maximum. No independent focal epileptiform discharges were identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures were recorded. No definite focal or generalized ictal onset was identified. No electroclinical event corresponding to the patient’s habitual tonic-clonic seizure was captured during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal ambulatory EEG due to generalized, bilaterally synchronous interictal epileptiform discharges. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support an increased propensity for generalized seizures and are consistent with the patient’s diagnosis of generalized epilepsy.

2. The background is normal, without focal slowing or persistent asymmetry.

3. No electrographic seizures or habitual clinical events were captured. Accordingly, this study does not provide an ictal onset localization.

Clinical correlation is recommended in this patient with drug-resistant generalized tonic-clonic seizures despite valproate, zonisamide, brivaracetam, lacosamide, and prior VNS implantation.
