Indication:  H/o genetic generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Clinical History:  Seizure onset at age 30. Typical events consist of brief, shock-like jerks of both arms on waking, without aura and with preserved awareness. Current frequency is approximately four events per week. No associated urinary incontinence. The patient denies a family hx of epilepsy. Epilepsy has been clinically drug responsive. Current maintenance ASMs are perampanel and topiramate. Rescue medication: diazepam rectally PRN for a prolonged or clustered seizure; this is not a maintenance ASM.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and photic stimulation at sequential flash frequencies. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

- **Background:** The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and well organized, with a 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

- **Awake state:** Normal organization and reactivity. No alteration in the background accompanied the reported state transitions.

- **Drowsy/sleep:** Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of symmetric theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes.

- **Hyperventilation:** Mild symmetric buildup of theta activity occurred without a sustained pathologic response. No clinical event was elicited.

- **Photic stimulation:** Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was seen.

- **Interictal findings:** Occasional bilaterally synchronous, generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, with frontal predominance at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. These occurred singly and in brief bursts lasting less than 2 seconds, predominantly during drowsiness and sleep. There was no consistent focal lead-in or lateralizing predominance. No LPDs, focal sharp waves, TIRDA, or focal slowing were identified.

- **Ictal events:** None captured. No electrographic seizures occurred. No typical arm jerks were marked or observed during this recording.

- **ECG:** Regular rhythm throughout the sampled tracing.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional bilateral, synchronous generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. These findings indicate an increased risk for generalized seizures and are consistent with the patient’s hx of genetic generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures.
2. Normal awake and sleep background without focal slowing or lateralizing abnormality.
3. No clinical or electrographic seizure was recorded. A normal ictal sample is not required for diagnosis; clinical correlation with the described morning myoclonus is recommended.
