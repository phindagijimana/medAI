Indication: Hx of structural focal epilepsy with focal aware motor seizures beginning at age 41, currently occurring approximately 4 times/month. Typical events consist of epileptic spasms with hyperkinetic movements and preserved awareness; no aura. EEG obtained to r/o interictal epileptiform activity and further characterize seizure risk.

Relevant Medications: Lamotrigine and topiramate. Intranasal midazolam PRN as rescue medication. Previously trialed oxcarbazepine, discontinued due to GI intolerance. Epilepsy has been clinically drug responsive.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The recording included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study, and stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude frontocentral beta activity was seen. There was no persistent focal slowing or diffuse background slowing.

Awake State: Normal organization and reactivity. No significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of generalized theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K-complexes.

Hyperventilation: Mild symmetric buildup of theta activity without activation of epileptiform discharges. No clinical event occurred.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: Occasional medium-amplitude sharp waves were present over the right temporal region, maximal at F8/T8 with a field extending to FT10. These discharges were more evident during drowsiness and sleep. No independent left temporal or generalized epileptiform discharges were identified. No TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs were seen.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or habitual clinical events were captured. Accordingly, ictal onset and electroclinical correlation could not be assessed on this routine study.

Clinical Correlation: The patient’s reported spells are focal aware motor seizures characterized by epileptic spasms and hyperkinetic movements with preserved awareness. MRI reportedly demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis. This focal structural lesion is likely epileptogenic, although electroclinical localization cannot be established from the present recording alone.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional right temporal interictal epileptiform discharges, maximal at F8/T8. These findings indicate increased epileptogenic potential in the right temporal region and support the diagnosis of focal epilepsy.
2. Normal waking and sleep background without focal or diffuse slowing.
3. No electrographic seizure or typical clinical event was recorded. A normal ictal sample is not available; if further spell characterization or localization is required, prolonged video EEG monitoring may be considered.
