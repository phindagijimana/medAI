Data Reviewed

The available routine EEG demonstrated a normal background and left temporal interictal epileptiform discharges. No ictal onset was identified. The left temporal IEDs support a focal epilepsy diagnosis and may suggest left temporal epileptogenic potential, but an interictal routine EEG alone does not definitively establish the site of seizure onset.

Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy of unknown etiology, with onset at age 54. The habitual events are focal impaired-awareness seizures. The described onset is nonmotor and emotional, consisting of déjà vu, followed by impaired awareness and epileptic spasms. Current reported frequency is approximately one seizure per week.

The routine EEG had a normal background with left temporal IEDs. This finding supports focal epilepsy and is concordant with a possible temporal network given the déjà vu aura. However, no habitual seizure was captured, and there is no documented ictal EEG onset. The precise epileptogenic zone therefore remains unconfirmed.

The patient is currently taking levetiracetam and oxcarbazepine. Treatment response is categorized as drug-responsive in the referral information. Although lacosamide was previously stopped, it was discontinued because of GI intolerance and should not be considered a definite efficacy failure. Based on the information currently available, I would not classify this patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy or initiate a presurgical evaluation. The apparent discrepancy between “drug-responsive” status and the reported frequency of one event per week should first be addressed by confirming whether all reported episodes represent epileptic seizures, documenting adherence, and verifying the current ASM doses.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Keppra and Trileptal at the currently prescribed doses pending complete medication reconciliation. The patient was asked to provide the prescription bottles or an updated medication list confirming dose and whether the schedule is BID or otherwise. I would avoid empiric dose changes until the exact regimen is verified.

2. Maintain a seizure calendar documenting the date, approximate duration, aura, degree of impaired awareness, motor manifestations, and recovery for each event. Witness descriptions or video, when safely obtained, may help determine whether the weekly episodes are stereotyped epileptic events.

3. Obtain an MRI brain with epilepsy protocol if one has not already been completed and made available for review. This is appropriate to r/o a structural etiology given focal epilepsy beginning at age 54 and left temporal IEDs.

4. Request the original routine EEG tracing for direct review when available. The report of a normal background with left temporal IEDs is supportive, but the morphology and distribution of the discharges should be confirmed.

5. Continue clinical follow-up to assess seizure burden and medication tolerability. Prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient EMU/LTM may be considered as part of further diagnostic clarification, particularly because the routine EEG did not establish ictal onset. At present, there is insufficient information to recommend epilepsy surgery or neuromodulation.

6. We reviewed general seizure precautions. The patient should avoid unsupervised swimming or bathing, climbing at heights, operating dangerous machinery, and situations in which sudden impaired awareness could result in injury. Driving should follow applicable state law and treating-provider guidance.

7. Lacosamide should remain listed as a prior ASM discontinued for GI intolerance rather than as a current medication. Any future medication adjustment should account for this prior adverse effect.
