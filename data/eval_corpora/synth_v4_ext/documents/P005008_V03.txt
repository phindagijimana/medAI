Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 37. Events occur approximately 7/week and consist of tonic stiffening followed by clonic jerking with impaired awareness and no preceding aura. Epilepsy is considered structurally mediated; MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia. Currently treated with carbamazepine, with reported medication responsiveness. Routine EEG requested to evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures. No h/o febrile seizures in childhood.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The recording included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with an anterior-to-posterior gradient of frequencies and amplitudes. A well-defined, symmetric 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude frontocentral beta and diffuse alpha/theta activity was seen. No persistent focal slowing or diffuse background slowing was present.

Awake state: Normal organization and reactivity. No significant interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Drowsiness / sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, predominantly central theta activity with slow roving eye movements. Definitive sleep architecture was not recorded.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild symmetric buildup of generalized theta activity without a focal abnormality or electrographic seizure.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. There was no definite photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional bursts of bilaterally synchronous, generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, maximal over the bifrontal regions at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with a broad bilateral field. Discharges occurred as isolated complexes and brief runs lasting less than 2 seconds, without clinical correlate or sustained evolution. There were no independent focal epileptiform discharges, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs.

Ictal events: No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No habitual clinical events were reported during the recording.

ECG: Regular rhythm throughout the sampled tracing.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, bilaterally synchronous spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges on an otherwise normal awake and drowsy background. These findings indicate an increased generalized epileptic potential and are supportive of the reported generalized epilepsy phenotype.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. There is no focal or diffuse background slowing on this study. A normal background does not exclude the known structural MRI abnormality of focal cortical dysplasia.

Clinical correlation is recommended. The patient remains on carbamazepine with drug-responsive epilepsy. If interval MRI demonstrates progression of the structural lesion, PET and/or SPECT should be obtained to assess concordance with the clinical and electrographic data.
