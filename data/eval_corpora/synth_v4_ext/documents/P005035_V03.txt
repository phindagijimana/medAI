Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of genetic generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures beginning at age 6 years. Events consist of brief staring with eyelid fluttering and impaired awareness, without aura, currently occurring approximately 3 times/month. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Clinical History: The patient has drug-responsive generalized epilepsy attributed to a causative STXBP1 variant, c.902A>G. Current maintenance ASM is perampanel. Brain MRI reportedly demonstrated nonspecific white matter changes. No h/o CNS infection. Typical clinical events are characterized by abrupt behavioral arrest/staring, eyelid fluttering, and impaired awareness, without a preceding aura.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal or diffuse slowing.

Awake state: Normal organization and reactivity. No asymmetry or focal attenuation was identified.

Drowsiness: Attenuation of the PDR with emergence of symmetric diffuse theta activity and slow roving eye movements. No definite sleep architecture was recorded.

Hyperventilation: Mild symmetric generalized theta slowing developed and resolved promptly after cessation. Generalized epileptiform discharges were seen but no electroclinical absence seizure was elicited.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response occurred.

Interictal findings: Occasional bursts of generalized, bilaterally synchronous 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, maximal over the bifrontal regions but without a consistent lateralized predominance. Bursts lasted approximately 0.5-2 seconds and occurred sporadically during wakefulness and drowsiness. There was no associated behavioral arrest or other definite clinical correlate. No focal epileptiform discharges, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or focal slowing were identified.

Ictal events: None captured. No electrographic seizures were recorded. The patient’s habitual staring spells with eyelid fluttering were not observed during this study.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, bilaterally synchronous spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges on an otherwise normal awake and drowsy background.
2. These findings indicate an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are supportive of the patient’s known genetic generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures.
3. No clinical or electrographic seizure was captured. Absence of a recorded habitual event does not exclude ongoing seizures; correlate with the reported frequency of approximately 3 events/month and clinical response to perampanel.
