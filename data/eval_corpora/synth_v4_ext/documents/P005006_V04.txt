**Referral Question:**  
Diagnostic/presurgical neuropsychological evaluation to characterize current cognitive functioning, assess memory and language lateralization, and establish a baseline for PRC consideration of additional epilepsy treatment.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o structural, drug-resistant epilepsy with seizure onset at age 7. Epilepsy type remains unknown, and available records classify the habitual events as unknown-onset nonmotor seizures. Current reported seizure frequency is 0/month; the patient is presently seizure-free. The patient denies syncope.

Brain MRI demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis; laterality was not specified in the available information. A routine EEG showed normal background activity without interictal epileptiform discharges. No electrographic seizures or ictal onset were identified on that routine study.

The patient is s/p responsive neurostimulation implantation in 2019. Current maintenance ASMs are brivaracetam, lamotrigine, zonisamide, and lacosamide. Intranasal midazolam is available PRN as seizure rescue medication. Previously trialed medications include perampanel, discontinued due to sedation; carbamazepine, discontinued due to rash; valproate, discontinued because of inadequate seizure control; levetiracetam, discontinued due to sedation; and oxcarbazepine, discontinued because of mood side effects.

Detailed psychiatric, developmental, educational, and occupational records were not available in the referral materials. These factors were considered when interpreting the present findings.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent and grammatically intact. Normal rate, volume, and prosody. Auditory comprehension adequate for interview and test instructions. No frank aphasia observed.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was Average (FSIQ = 90, 25th percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 93, 32nd percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 98, 45th percentile). Working Memory fell in the Low Average range (WMI = 89, 23rd percentile), and Processing Speed was also Low Average (PSI = 84, 14th percentile). The profile suggests generally preserved reasoning abilities with relative inefficiency on speeded tasks.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Simple auditory attention was Low Average (Digit Span scaled score = 8). Performance on Trail Making Test Part A was Average (T = 43). WAIS-IV Coding and Symbol Search were Low Average (scaled scores = 7 and 7). Overall, basic attention was intact, although processing speed was mildly reduced, potentially reflecting cumulative epilepsy and ASM effects.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Low Average on the Boston Naming Test (T = 39). Phonemic verbal fluency was Average (FAS T = 42), while semantic fluency was Low Average (Animals T = 38). Receptive language and conversational speech were intact. The mild naming and semantic retrieval weakness may be consistent with temporal-system inefficiency, although the findings are not sufficiently focal to determine hemispheric language dominance.

**Verbal Memory:**  
CVLT-3 performance showed inefficient acquisition across repeated learning trials (Total Trials 1–5 T = 35, Borderline). Initial learning was Low Average (Trial 1 scaled score = 7), and final-trial recall was Borderline (Trial 5 scaled score = 6). Long-delay free recall was Borderline (scaled score = 5), with limited benefit from recognition cueing (Recognition Discriminability scaled score = 6). WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7), and delayed recall was Borderline (scaled score = 5). The pattern reflects reduced verbal learning and retention rather than solely a retrieval deficit.

**Visual Memory:**  
WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 8), with Low Average delayed recall (scaled score = 6). Rey Complex Figure delayed recall was Borderline (T = 36). Recognition did not fully normalize performance. Visual memory was therefore also reduced, though somewhat better than verbal memory. This difference is modest and should not be viewed as definitive evidence of lateralization.

**Executive Functioning:**  
Trail Making Test Part B was Low Average (T = 40), without set-loss errors. Stroop interference performance was Average (T = 44). WCST performance was broadly Average, with adequate concept formation and cognitive flexibility. There was no evidence of a major executive syndrome.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10). Rey Complex Figure copy was organized and within normal limits. No clinically significant visuoconstructional deficit was identified.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average bilaterally (right hand T = 40; left hand T = 42), without a meaningful lateralized discrepancy.

**Mood/Personality:**  
BDI-II and BAI scores fell within the minimal range (BDI-II = 8; BAI = 6). Self-report did not indicate clinically significant current depression or anxiety.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
The evaluation documents Average general intellectual ability with relative weaknesses in processing speed, semantic retrieval, and episodic memory. The most clinically significant finding is reduced acquisition and delayed retention of verbal information, accompanied by milder visual memory weakness. Attention, visuospatial construction, and core executive abilities were largely preserved. Performance validity was adequate.

The memory profile is broadly compatible with temporal-system dysfunction in the context of MRI-confirmed hippocampal sclerosis and longstanding structural epilepsy. However, because MRI laterality was not specified, routine EEG was normal without epileptiform discharges, and memory weakness was present across verbal and visual modalities, the current cognitive data are not independently lateralizing. Current polytherapy may also contribute to reduced processing speed and learning efficiency.

If additional temporal intervention is considered, reduced baseline memory represents limited cognitive reserve and may increase risk for postoperative decline. Risk will depend on the proposed side, functional integrity of the contralateral hippocampus, and language dominance. PRC review should integrate neuropsychological findings with MRI, prolonged video-EEG, functional imaging, and NeuroPace RNS System data.

**Recommendations:**

1. Discuss findings at PRC in conjunction with imaging, electrophysiology, and NeuroPace RNS System recordings.
2. Obtain fMRI language and memory mapping before any additional temporal surgical procedure.
3. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant with other lateralizing data.
4. Continue close epilepsy follow-up and current ASM management through Epileptology.
5. Use external memory supports, including written schedules, alarms, repetition, and structured organization.
6. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6–12 months after any surgical or neuromodulation change, or sooner if meaningful cognitive decline occurs.
