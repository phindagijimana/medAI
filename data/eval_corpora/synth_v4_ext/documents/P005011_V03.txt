Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, possibly focal onset; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and focal cerebral dysfunction.

Clinical History: Seizure onset at age 6 years. Current events occur approximately 7 times/week and begin with an emotional nonmotor manifestation, followed by impaired awareness and tonic posturing. No aura is reported. Etiology remains unknown. The epilepsy has been drug responsive. MRI brain showed nonspecific white matter changes. The patient denies photosensitivity. Current maintenance ASMs are carbamazepine and perampanel. Rescue medication is clonazepam orally disintegrating tablet PRN.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system, with additional electrocardiographic monitoring. The study was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of 9 Hz alpha activity, better organized over the left posterior head region, reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly.

Awake State: There was intermittent polymorphic 2–5 Hz delta-theta slowing over the right posterior quadrant, maximal at P4-O2, at times extending to T6/P8. This slowing was not consistently rhythmic and did not evolve.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and an increase in diffuse theta activity. No well-formed sleep spindles or K-complexes were recorded.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric background slowing without activation of epileptiform activity.

Photic Stimulation: Photic stimulation produced a symmetric driving response at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was present.

Interictal Findings: Occasional sharp waves were present over the right occipital region, maximal at O2 with a field to P4 and P8/T6. These discharges were more apparent during drowsiness and had an after-going slow wave. No independent left-sided or generalized IEDs were identified. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs were seen.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. Accordingly, no ictal onset or electroclinical localization can be determined from this routine study.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional right occipital epileptiform discharges, maximal at O2, indicating increased epileptic potential in the right occipital region.
2. Intermittent right posterior quadrant delta-theta slowing, consistent with focal cerebral dysfunction in that region.
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. The findings support a focal epilepsy in the appropriate clinical context; correlation with the described possibly focal onset seizures and MRI findings is recommended.
