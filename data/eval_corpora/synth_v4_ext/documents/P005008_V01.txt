Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with generalized epilepsy characterized by generalized tonic-clonic seizures with impaired awareness, tonic stiffening, and subsequent clonic jerking. Seizure onset occurred at age 37, and the available record documents approximately seven events per week. The patient denies aura. There is also no hx of febrile seizures in childhood.

The routine EEG demonstrated a normal background with bilateral generalized IEDs, supporting the generalized epilepsy classification. No seizure was captured on the routine study, so there is no documented ictal onset. MRI reportedly demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia, and the epilepsy is therefore considered structural in etiology.

There is some electroclinical-imaging complexity because focal cortical dysplasia is a focal structural lesion, whereas the routine EEG showed generalized bilateral discharges and the documented seizure type is generalized tonic-clonic. At present, however, the available information supports the supplied diagnosis of generalized epilepsy rather than a definite focal-onset epilepsy. Direct review of the MRI and EEG would be helpful to ensure that the generalized discharges are not being overinterpreted and to r/o an imaging change requiring additional evaluation.

The epilepsy is characterized as drug responsive to carbamazepine. Although the reported seizure burden is substantial, the available plan does not indicate drug-resistant epilepsy, prior epilepsy surgery, or a current presurgical pathway. Accordingly, invasive evaluation or neuromodulation is not indicated based on the information currently available.
