Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and focal cerebral dysfunction.

Clinical History: Patient with h/o focal epilepsy beginning at age 16, with approximately 3 seizures/year despite ASM therapy. Typical events have focal nonmotor autonomic onset with an aura of fear, followed by impaired awareness, tonic posturing, and versive head turning. Patient denies complete loss of consciousness. Epilepsy etiology is considered genetic. Current maintenance ASMs are brivaracetam and carbamazepine. Previously trialed topiramate (discontinued due to rash), oxcarbazepine (discontinued due to GI intolerance), and perampanel (discontinued due to rash). Intranasal midazolam is prescribed PRN as rescue medication.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous and organized, with an 8.5-9 Hz PDR that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was intermittent polymorphic 2-5 Hz delta-theta slowing over the left posterior quadrant, maximal in the left occipital region at O1, with lesser involvement of P7 and P3. The right posterior background was comparatively preserved.

Awake state: No sustained generalized slowing was present. No significant asymmetry was identified apart from the intermittent left posterior slowing described above.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Definite sleep architecture was not recorded.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild symmetric background slowing without activation of additional epileptiform abnormalities.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response occurred.

Interictal findings: Rare medium-amplitude sharp waves were present over the left occipital region, maximal at O1 with a field extending to P7/P3. These discharges were distinct from the background and had an after-going slow wave. No independent right-sided IEDs, generalized spike-wave discharges, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA were seen.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. Therefore, ictal onset and electroclinical correlation could not be determined from this routine study.

ECG: Single-lead tracing demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to rare left occipital epileptiform discharges, indicating increased epileptogenic potential in the left occipital region.
2. Intermittent left posterior quadrant slowing, maximal occipitally, consistent with focal cerebral dysfunction in that region.
3. No seizures were recorded. The absence of an ictal event on this routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. Findings should be correlated with the patient’s focal impaired-awareness semiology and neuroimaging; prolonged video EEG monitoring may be considered if needed to further characterize seizure onset.
