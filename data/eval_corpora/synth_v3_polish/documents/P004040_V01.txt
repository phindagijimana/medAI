Assessment / Impression

This patient has generalized epilepsy manifested by generalized tonic-clonic seizures, with onset at age 19. Current seizure frequency is reported as approximately one per week. The identified etiology is structural, based on MRI evidence of encephalomalacia.

There are important gaps in the available documentation. Specifically, the carbamazepine dose and schedule, primary MRI images, and prior EEG findings were not provided. These data are needed to confirm the current treatment regimen and electroclinical classification. Although the epilepsy is characterized in the referral as drug responsive, weekly generalized tonic-clonic seizures represent a clinically meaningful ongoing burden and require reconciliation with that designation. Based on the information supplied, there is not enough medication-trial history to characterize the condition as drug-resistant epilepsy.

A possible autoimmune contribution has been suggested, but this remains only a consideration. The current records do not establish an autoimmune etiology. Further review of the original evaluation is needed before assigning significance to that possibility.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Tegretol (carbamazepine) at the currently prescribed regimen for now. I did not recommend an empiric dose change because the actual dose and frequency were not available today.

2. Complete medication reconciliation through the patient’s pharmacy and referring office, including tablet strength, total daily dose, dosing schedule, and the timing of the most recent dose.

3. Obtain the complete prior MRI report and the original MRI images for direct review. The goal is to define the location and extent of the encephalomalacia and determine how well the structural finding supports the reported epilepsy classification.

4. Obtain all prior EEG records, including any routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, or EMU/LTM studies. In the absence of an adequate prior study, I recommend an EEG through xxx Medical Center to evaluate for generalized or focal IEDs and to better establish the electroclinical diagnosis.

5. Maintain a seizure log documenting generalized tonic-clonic event frequency. Accurate prospective tracking will help confirm whether the current frequency remains approximately one event per week and will assist with assessment of response to carbamazepine.

6. Review the records that raised concern for a possible autoimmune contribution. At present, this possibility is not confirmed, and I would avoid labeling the epilepsy autoimmune without supporting documentation.

7. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after the medication details, MRI images, and EEG records have been obtained. Further treatment recommendations will be based on the verified regimen and updated electroclinical review.
