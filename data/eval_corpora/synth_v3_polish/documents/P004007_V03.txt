Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Hx of structural focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 11 and MRI evidence of hippocampal sclerosis. Currently seizure-free on levetiracetam and perampanel. EEG obtained to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Clinical History:  The patient has a h/o focal impaired-awareness seizures in the setting of hippocampal sclerosis. Epilepsy has been drug responsive, with no seizures reported during the current month. Typical historical events involved impaired awareness; the patient denies tongue biting. There has also been concern that a component of the reported events may possibly be nonepileptic, suggestive of a PNES component. Current ASMs are levetiracetam and perampanel. Brivaracetam was previously trialed and discontinued because of cognitive side effects.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Additional anterior temporal electrodes were included. The recording was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  In the awake state, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz PDR over the bilateral posterior head regions, reactive to eye opening and closure. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. No persistent interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  The waking background showed the expected anterior-to-posterior gradient. There was no focal or generalized background slowing.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, K-complexes, and sleep spindles. No epileptiform activation occurred during sleep.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges.

Photic Stimulation:  Intermittent PS at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced a symmetric posterior driving response. No photoparoxysmal response was present.

Interictal Findings:  No focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or other definite IEDs were identified. No focal slowing was seen.

Ictal Events:  No electrographic seizures were recorded. No habitual clinical events or push-button events occurred. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal awake and sleep routine EEG. No focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities and no electrographic seizures were identified.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. The clinical h/o of focal impaired-awareness seizures with hippocampal sclerosis remains consistent with structural focal epilepsy, currently drug responsive and seizure-free on levetiracetam and perampanel.
3. No habitual event was captured; therefore, the possible PNES component cannot be assessed by this recording. If diagnostic uncertainty recurs with breakthrough events, prolonged video-EEG monitoring may be considered for electroclinical characterization.
