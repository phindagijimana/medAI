Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: H/o epilepsy beginning at age 2 years, characterized clinically by generalized tonic-clonic (GTC) seizures, currently occurring approximately 2/month. Etiology remains unknown. The epilepsy has been categorized clinically as drug-responsive, with current ASM therapy consisting of perampanel. Referral documentation also raises the discrepant possibility of likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy and describes a focal MRI lesion as likely epileptogenic, although the lesion has otherwise been characterized as incidental. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o focal versus generalized epilepsy.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation (HV) for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation (PS). Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 9 Hz alpha rhythm, reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was distributed symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. No persistent focal attenuation or focal delta slowing was identified.

Awake state: The awake background was continuous and symmetric without significant interhemispheric asymmetry. Occasional generalized bursts of sharply contoured 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity were present, maximal bifrontally at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with a broad bilateral field. Bursts lasted 0.5-2 seconds and did not have a consistent focal lead-in or associated clinical change.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was marked by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges became more frequent in sleep, at times occurring in brief runs lasting up to 3 seconds. No electrographic or subtle clinical seizure accompanied these runs.

Hyperventilation: HV produced expected symmetric generalized theta slowing. There was a modest increase in generalized spike-and-wave burden, without a sustained electrographic seizure or clinical correlate.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings: Occasional to frequent generalized, bifrontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness, sleep, and HV. No independent focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing were seen.

Ictal events: None captured. No push-button events occurred.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional to frequent generalized, bifrontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated during drowsiness and sleep. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support a generalized epilepsy and are concordant with the reported GTC seizure type.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. There was no definite focal epileptiform abnormality or focal slowing to support the proposed diagnosis of likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy on this study.

3. Although the reported focal MRI lesion has been considered possibly or likely epileptogenic, this EEG provides no localizing electrographic correlate. The lesion may remain incidental; correlation with MRI characteristics and the longitudinal clinical semiology is recommended.
