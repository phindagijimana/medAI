Assessment / Impression

This patient has drug-resistant generalized epilepsy manifested by frequent generalized tonic-clonic seizures, currently reported at approximately seven per week. Seizures began at age 15. The proposed etiology is immune-mediated epilepsy associated with GAD65 antibody.

The epilepsy meets criteria for drug resistance based on ongoing seizures despite the current combination of lamotrigine, carbamazepine, and lacosamide, as well as a prior unsuccessful trial of brivaracetam that was stopped for inadequate seizure control.

There is important diagnostic uncertainty regarding seizure onset. The events are classified in the referral materials as generalized tonic-clonic seizures in the setting of generalized epilepsy; however, they may be possibly focal onset seizures with rapid bilateral spread. I cannot resolve that distinction without review of prior testing and, ideally, capture of habitual events on VEEG/LTM. This distinction is particularly relevant because carbamazepine and lacosamide are focal-seizure medications, while treatment strategy may differ if a generalized epilepsy is confirmed.

Recommendations / Plan

1. I recommend admission to the EMU for inpatient VEEG/LTM. The goal will be to capture representative habitual seizures, characterize ictal onset, evaluate interictal findings, and clarify whether this is generalized epilepsy or possibly focal onset epilepsy with rapid bilateral spread.

2. We will request prior EEG reports and tracings, brain MRI images and reports, and the original GAD65 antibody results. If prior imaging was not performed with an epilepsy protocol or is not available for adequate review, an epilepsy-protocol brain MRI should be obtained through xxx Medical Center.
