Indication: Hx of focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: Adult with seizure onset at age 24 years. Typical events are classified as focal impaired-awareness seizures, historically occurring approximately 2 times/week. Etiology remains unknown. Epilepsy is considered drug-responsive. Current ASMs are brivaracetam and perampanel. Previously trialed lamotrigine, discontinued due to cognitive side effects, and carbamazepine, discontinued due to sedation.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. The study included awake, drowsy, and sleep states. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded throughout the study.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, there was a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm with amplitudes of 30-60 µV, reactive to eye opening and closure. An appropriate anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent focal asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: The awake background was continuous and composed predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with admixed low-amplitude theta activity. There was no focal slowing. No LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs were present.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse theta activity, and slow rolling eye movements. Stage N2 sleep was achieved, with symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform abnormalities were activated by sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform features. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of HV.

Photic Stimulation: PS at frequencies from 1-30 Hz produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal Findings: No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, or electrographic patterns suggestive of an increased focal seizure propensity during this recording.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures were recorded. No clinical events or patient-reported typical spells occurred during the study.

ECG: Single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.  
3. No typical focal impaired-awareness event was captured.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy, as interictal epileptiform abnormalities may be intermittent and absent during a brief recording. Clinical correlation is recommended. If diagnostic uncertainty persists or typical events continue despite the current ASM regimen, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may provide greater sampling.
