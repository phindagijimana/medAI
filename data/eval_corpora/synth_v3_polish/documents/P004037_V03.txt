Indication: H/o genetic focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures beginning at age 10; r/o interictal epileptiform activity. Currently seizure-free on perampanel.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, with a brief period of stage N2 sleep achieved. The patient was In NAD throughout the recording.

Relevant Clinical History: The patient has focal epilepsy attributed to a pathogenic/causative DEPDC5 variant, c.4396C>T. Typical events have been classified as focal aware seizures. Current seizure frequency is 0/month. The epilepsy is presently drug-responsive on perampanel. Previously trialed levetiracetam, discontinued due to sedation; patient is s/p transition to the current ASM regimen. Prior documentation characterized the epilepsy as likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy, although the current sustained seizure freedom is more consistent with a drug-responsive course. MRI reportedly demonstrates nonspecific white matter changes. Family hx is notable for a paternal cousin with generalized epilepsy of childhood.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized, with a symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen frontocentrally. No persistent background asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: Occasional brief runs of irregular 4-6 Hz theta slowing were present over the left temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, without sustained rhythmic evolution.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep contained symmetric vertex waves and sleep spindles. Epileptiform discharges were mildly activated during sleep.

Hyperventilation: Mild, symmetric diffuse slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform activation.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: Rare, moderate-amplitude sharp waves were observed independently over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7 with a field to T7 and FP1. These discharges were most evident during drowsiness and light sleep. No independent right temporal IEDs, generalized spike-and-wave discharges, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA were seen.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No clinical correlate accompanied the interictal discharges.

ECG: Regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to rare left anterior temporal sharp waves and occasional left temporal theta slowing. These findings support increased epileptogenic potential and mild focal cerebral dysfunction in the left temporal region, compatible with the patient’s hx of focal epilepsy.

2. No seizures were recorded. A normal or seizure-free brief sample does not exclude active epilepsy.

3. The left temporal localization could be consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis in the appropriate electroclinical context; however, EEG cannot establish that structural diagnosis, and the available MRI description reports only nonspecific white matter changes. Correlation with direct review of epilepsy-protocol MRI is recommended.

4. Clinical correlation should recognize the underlying genetic etiology associated with DEPDC5 c.4396C>T, current focal aware seizure classification, and present drug-responsive course on perampanel despite prior concern for likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy.
