Neurology Progress Note

Chief Complaint: Follow-up focal epilepsy

History of Present Illness:  
This is a 37-year-old man with h/o focal epilepsy beginning at age 21, with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Epilepsy has been attributed to an infectious etiology and has remained drug responsive on combination therapy with Depakote (valproate) and Topamax (topiramate). Prior documentation also describes events for which atypical absence cannot be excluded, although these remain only possible and are not the established primary seizure type.

Since the last visit, his seizure calendar shows no interval seizures, with a current frequency of 0/month. Specifically, there have been no recorded breakthrough focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. He remains on valproate and topiramate. Current medication doses and frequencies were not included in the available medication record and will be confirmed through medication reconciliation. No additional interval medication changes, adverse effects, or seizure-related ED visits are documented in the available history.

Overall seizure control remains satisfactory. We reviewed the importance of continued adherence and maintaining an updated seizure diary. If brief staring or behavioral-arrest episodes are observed, these should also be documented carefully, as atypical absence remains possible but unconfirmed. Further characterization with EEG could be pursued if such events recur or become clinically concerning.
