Assessment / Impression

This patient has epilepsy beginning at age 2 years, with generalized tonic-clonic seizures currently occurring approximately 2 times per month. The documented epilepsy classification is generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology. Current treatment is Fycompa (perampanel), and the source information categorizes the condition as drug responsive.

There are important ambiguities in the available records. A second formulation suggests likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy, and the focal MRI lesion is considered likely epileptogenic. Both conclusions remain probable rather than established. At present, there is insufficient electroclinical information to determine whether the generalized tonic-clonic seizures arise within a generalized epilepsy syndrome or represent focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. There is also insufficient information regarding prior ASM exposure to confirm drug resistance.

The MRI finding may be incidental, as originally reported, but correlation with seizure semiology and EEG is required. Before considering a presurgical pathway or a device such as the NeuroPace RNS System, the epilepsy classification and relationship of the lesion to the seizures must be clarified.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Fycompa (perampanel) at the current prescribed regimen pending medication reconciliation. The patient will confirm the tablet strength, total daily dose, frequency, adherence, and prescribing pharmacy. I would avoid making a dose adjustment without those details.

2. Obtain the complete prior neurology records, including all routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, or EMU reports. Original EEG tracings should be requested when available to evaluate for generalized versus focal IEDs and to r/o focal onset with rapid bilateral spread.

3. Obtain the original brain MRI report and images for direct review. The lesion’s location and imaging characteristics need to be correlated with any electroclinical localization before it can be regarded as epileptogenic rather than incidental.

4. Clarify the lifetime ASM history, including medication names, highest tolerated doses, duration of treatment, response, and reasons for discontinuation. This is necessary to determine whether the patient meets criteria for drug-resistant epilepsy despite the current designation of drug-responsive disease.

5. Maintain a seizure log documenting the date and type of each event. A detailed witness description or video, when safely obtainable, would be useful because the available semiology is limited to generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

6. Once the outside data are assembled, we will reassess the working diagnosis. If generalized epilepsy is confirmed, therapy should be selected accordingly. If the data instead support focal epilepsy associated with the MRI lesion, further localization and treatment planning may be appropriate. At this time, no recommendation can be made for resection, ablation, NeuroPace RNS System, Medtronic DBS Percept, or LivaNova VNS SenTiva.

7. Follow up with xxx Medicine Neurology after records and imaging have been received, or sooner for a meaningful change in seizure pattern or medication tolerance.
