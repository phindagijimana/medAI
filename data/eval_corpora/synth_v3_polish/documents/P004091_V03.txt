Indication:  Hx of generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures beginning at age 6, historically occurring approximately 2/week. Reported structural etiology with prior MRI formally read as normal, although retrospective concern was probably consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis. Drug-responsive s/p initiation of levetiracetam. Routine EEG to characterize interictal activity and r/o focal epileptiform features. Patient denies tongue biting with habitual events.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Standard bipolar and referential montages were reviewed. Activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation (HV) and photic stimulation (PS) at stepwise frequencies. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. Current ASM: levetiracetam.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was present. Low-amplitude frontocentral beta activity was seen bilaterally. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state: During wakefulness, occasional generalized 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges occurred in brief bursts lasting 0.5-2 seconds. These were bifrontally predominant, maximal at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, but had a broad, bilaterally synchronous field. There was no consistent focal lead-in or clinical correlate.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves and synchronous sleep spindles. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges were mildly activated by drowsiness and sleep. No temporal sharp waves, TIRDA, LPDs, or independent focal IEDs were present.

Hyperventilation: HV produced expected symmetric diffuse theta slowing. A single 1.5-second burst of generalized 4 Hz spike-and-wave activity occurred without behavioral change.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, bifrontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, more apparent during drowsiness and sleep. No electrographic evidence of a focal temporal epileptogenic abnormality.

Ictal events: None captured. No electrographic seizures occurred.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support an increased seizure propensity and are most consistent with generalized epilepsy.

2. No focal slowing or focal interictal epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there was no left or right temporal electrographic abnormality to support a mesial temporal focus during this recording.

3. The clinical designation of structural etiology, with imaging reportedly normal but retrospectively considered probably consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis, is discordant with the generalized EEG pattern. Correlation with the original MRI images, epilepsy-protocol imaging if clinically warranted, and seizure semiology is recommended. A normal or nonfocal routine EEG component does not independently exclude a structural lesion.
