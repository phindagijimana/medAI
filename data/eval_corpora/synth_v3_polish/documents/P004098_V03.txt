Indication: H/o generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures beginning at age 8, currently occurring approximately 3/month. EEG obtained to characterize interictal activity and r/o focal epileptiform abnormalities given MRI evidence of focal cortical dysplasia.

Clinical History: The patient has a structural epilepsy etiology based on MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia, although the reported seizure phenotype is generalized typical absence. Epilepsy has been drug responsive. Current ASM is brivaracetam. Family hx notable for a brother with stroke.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation (HV) and photic stimulation (PS) at sequential flash frequencies. Video was recorded throughout. The patient was cooperative and In NAD during electrode application.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated with eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent focal slowing or background asymmetry was identified.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes.

Hyperventilation: HV produced expected diffuse theta slowing. In addition, several brief bursts of frontally predominant, generalized 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave discharges (SWD) occurred, lasting 2-4 seconds. No definite behavioral arrest was evident on video, and no formal testing was performed during the shortest bursts.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal Findings: Occasional generalized, bifrontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, both spontaneously and during HV. Discharges were synchronous and symmetric without a consistent focal lead-in. No independent focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA were seen.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or habitual clinical absence events were captured during this routine recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, bifrontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by hyperventilation. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized seizures and are consistent with the reported typical absence seizure phenotype.
2. No electrographic seizure was recorded. No definite focal epileptiform abnormality or focal slowing was identified.
3. The generalized electrographic pattern should be correlated with the known MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia and the assigned structural etiology, as the lesion had no clear focal EEG correlate in this limited study.

Clinical correlation is advised. Continue brivaracetam per the treating neurologist. Should he have a seizure cluster, use prescribed nasal midazolam rescue PRN and call 911.
