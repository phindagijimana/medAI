Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Hx of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 19, currently occurring approximately once weekly. MRI demonstrates encephalomalacia, supporting a structural etiology. Routine EEG to characterize background and r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History:  The patient has generalized-onset epilepsy manifested by GTC seizures. Seizures have been drug responsive to carbamazepine, although events continue at a reported frequency of approximately one per week. Current ASM: carbamazepine. The patient denies any h/o CNS infection. MRI has shown encephalomalacia, consistent with s/p structural cerebral injury. A possible autoimmune contribution has also been raised clinically, though this remains uncertain. Family hx is notable for epilepsy in the patient’s father.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including bipolar and referential montages. A single-channel ECG was recorded. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N1 and brief stage N2 sleep were achieved.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-sustained 9 Hz alpha rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude frontocentral beta activity was seen. No persistent focal background asymmetry was identified.

Awake state:  The awake background was organized and reactive. There was no continuous focal delta slowing. No LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs were present.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric diffuse theta slowing. Occasional generalized spike-and-wave discharges were activated during and shortly after hyperventilation. No clinical change occurred.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. There was no definite photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were observed, typically lasting 0.5-2 seconds. These were maximal over the bilateral frontal regions at F3/F4 and Fp1/Fp2, without a consistent focal lead-in or sustained hemispheric predominance. The discharges were more frequent during drowsiness and following HV. No electroclinical absence seizure was identified.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or habitual clinical events were captured. Specifically, no GTC seizure occurred during this recording.

ECG:  Regular rhythm throughout the study.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are consistent with the patient’s diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures.
2. No electrographic seizure was recorded.
3. The background was otherwise organized and symmetric, without definite focal slowing. The known MRI finding of encephalomalacia is not specifically localized by this study. Clinical and imaging correlation is advised regarding the structural etiology and the possibly contributory autoimmune process.
