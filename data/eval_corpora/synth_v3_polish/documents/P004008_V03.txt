Indication: H/o genetic generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures, onset at age 55 years; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures. Reported seizure frequency is approximately one per week. Genetic testing identified a pathogenic STXBP1 c.902A>G variant. Epilepsy is clinically classified as drug-responsive. Current ASMs are levetiracetam and topiramate.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded continuously.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity, reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state: During wakefulness, there were occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3-4 Hz. These were maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with broad bilateral synchrony and no consistent focal lead-in. Discharges occurred singly and in brief bursts lasting 1-2 seconds without an apparent clinical correlate.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was associated with attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves and sleep spindles. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges became more frequent during drowsiness and sleep, at times occurring in bursts up to 3 seconds. No clinical change was evident on video. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs were seen.

Hyperventilation: HV produced expected diffuse theta slowing. There was a mild increase in generalized epileptiform discharge frequency, without electrographic seizure or clinical event.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Ictal events: No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No patient-reported events occurred during the recording.

ECG: Regular rhythm throughout the sampled tracing.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness and sleep.

2. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are consistent with the patient’s clinical diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures. There is no evidence of a focal epileptogenic abnormality on this recording.

3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal or seizure-free brief recording does not exclude ongoing epilepsy; clinical correlation is advised.

Continue levetiracetam and topiramate as directed by the treating neurologist. Should seizure recurrence occur after sustained control, revisit presurgical workup, with LTM/VEEG as clinically appropriate for event characterization.
