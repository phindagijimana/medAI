**Referral Question:**  
Diagnostic/presurgical neuropsychological baseline for PRC review. Characterize cognition, including memory and language functioning, in the context of combined generalized and focal epilepsy with hippocampal sclerosis.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o combined generalized and focal epilepsy, with seizure onset at age 10. Seizures include generalized tonic-clonic events and currently occur approximately twice per month. Etiology is considered structural. Brain MRI demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis; laterality was not specified in the records available for this evaluation. Detailed scalp EEG localization data were likewise unavailable.

The epilepsy has been characterized as drug responsive. Current antiseizure therapy consists of topiramate; dose and schedule were not provided. Previously trialed levetiracetam was discontinued due to mood side effects. Topiramate may contribute to slowed processing, reduced verbal fluency, and word-finding inefficiency and should therefore be considered when interpreting the present profile.

Available records did not provide sufficient information regarding psychiatric treatment hx, family history, allergies, or prior neurosurgical procedures. Educational and occupational details were also limited. Accordingly, demographic corrections were applied where possible, but interpretation emphasizes the overall pattern rather than isolated scores.

**Behavioral Observations:**

**Appearance and Cooperation:**  
Arrived on time. Appropriately dressed and groomed. In NAD. Alert and oriented. Cooperative throughout the evaluation.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent and grammatical. Normal volume and prosody. Mild intermittent word-finding pauses. Comprehension adequate for interview and multistep test instructions.

**Mood and Affect:**  
Mood neutral. Affect appropriate and stable. No marked behavioral dysregulation observed.

**Effort and Engagement:**  
Sustained task engagement over the evaluation. Embedded validity indices within expected limits. TOMM Trial 2 = 50/50, indicating adequate performance validity. Results considered a valid estimate of current cognitive functioning.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; Trail Making Test Parts A and B; Boston Naming Test; COWAT-FAS and Animal Fluency; CVLT-3; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test; Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; BDI-II; BAI; Test of Memory Malingering.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV General Ability Index fell in the Average range (GAI = 98, 45th percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 96, 39th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 102, 55th percentile). Working Memory was Low Average (WMI = 86, 18th percentile), while Processing Speed was Borderline (PSI = 79, 8th percentile). The discrepancy between reasoning abilities and cognitive efficiency suggests relative slowing rather than a generalized intellectual deficit.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Auditory attention span was Low Average (Digit Span scaled score = 8, 25th percentile). Trail Making A was Borderline (T = 36, 8th percentile), without sequencing errors. WAIS-IV Coding was Borderline (scaled score = 6, 9th percentile), and Symbol Search was Low Average (scaled score = 7, 16th percentile). Findings indicate reduced processing speed and mild attentional inefficiency, potentially exacerbated by antiseizure medication effects.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Low Average on the Boston Naming Test (T = 40, 16th percentile), with benefit from phonemic cueing. Phonemic verbal fluency was Borderline (FAS T = 37, 10th percentile), whereas semantic fluency was Low Average (Animals T = 41, 18th percentile). Receptive language and verbal abstraction were intact. The mild retrieval weakness is nonspecific and could reflect temporal-system dysfunction, slowed access associated with topiramate, or both. Given unspecified MRI laterality, these findings cannot independently establish dominant temporal involvement.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, initial acquisition was Low Average, and total learning across five trials was Borderline (Trials 1–5 T = 36, 8th percentile). Short-delay free recall was Borderline (scaled score = 6, 9th percentile), and long-delay free recall was Borderline (scaled score = 5, 5th percentile). Recognition discriminability was Low Average, with several false-positive responses. WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7, 16th percentile), with Borderline delayed recall (scaled score = 6, 9th percentile). The pattern reflects inefficient acquisition with reduced retention and retrieval of verbal information.

**Visual Memory:**  
WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 9, 37th percentile), while delayed recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7, 16th percentile). ROCF delayed recall was Borderline (T = 35, 7th percentile), although recognition was Low Average. Visual memory was variable and partly affected by inefficient initial organization; this does not represent a clearly isolated material-specific deficit.

**Executive Function:**  
Trail Making B was Borderline (T = 35, 7th percentile), with one set-loss error. Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T = 40, 16th percentile). On the WCST, the patient completed four categories; perseverative responses were Low Average (T = 41). Overall, mild executive inefficiency was evident, especially under speeded conditions, without severe perseveration or loss of conceptual reasoning.

**Visuospatial:**  
WAIS-IV Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10, 50th percentile). ROCF copy was Low Average due to a fragmented organizational approach rather than frank visuoperceptual distortion. Basic spatial perception was intact.

**Motor:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average bilaterally, without a clinically meaningful lateralized discrepancy. No motor findings clearly localizing to one hemisphere.

**Mood/Personality:**  
BDI-II and BAI scores were within the minimal range. No evidence of a current severe depressive or anxiety syndrome emerged from self-report measures. Prior mood side effects associated with levetiracetam are noted.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
This evaluation documents Average general reasoning abilities with relative weaknesses in processing speed, working memory, verbal fluency, executive efficiency, and learning/memory. Verbal memory was Borderline, with inefficient acquisition and reduced delayed recall. Visual memory was variable from Borderline to Average and was influenced by weak organization. Confrontation naming was Low Average rather than frankly impaired. Performance validity was adequate.

The profile suggests temporal-system memory dysfunction with additional frontally mediated inefficiency; however, it is not strongly lateralizing. MRI-confirmed hippocampal sclerosis provides a structural correlate, but MRI laterality and EEG localization were not available. Topiramate likely contributes to slowed processing and lexical retrieval difficulty. Because the patient carries combined generalized and focal epilepsy, concordance among semiology, prolonged video-EEG, imaging, and functional mapping will be particularly important before attributing generalized tonic-clonic seizures to a single resectable focus.

If dominant temporal surgery is considered, the current Borderline verbal memory performance indicates meaningful risk for additional postoperative decline, especially if the affected hippocampus retains functional capacity. Nondominant temporal intervention may carry greater risk to visual memory, although the current visual findings are not cleanly lateralizing.

**Recommendations:**

1. Discuss findings at multidisciplinary PRC, integrating MRI laterality, prolonged video-EEG, seizure semiology, and neuropsychological data.
2. Obtain fMRI language mapping before any temporal resection or ablation. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant.
3. Clarify focal seizure onset and exclude an independently generalized network before proceeding with focal surgery.
4. If resection/ablation is not appropriate and disabling focal seizures persist, consider neuromodulation options, including the NeuroPace RNS System, based on PRC consensus.
5. Review topiramate-related cognitive adverse effects with Epileptology. Any medication change should be weighed against current seizure control.
6. Use external memory supports, including written schedules, alarms, repetition, and structured organization of new information.
7. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6–12 months s/p any surgical or neuromodulation intervention, or sooner if substantial cognitive change occurs.
