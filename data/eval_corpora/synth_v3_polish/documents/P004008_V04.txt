**Referral Question:**  
Diagnostic neuropsychological baseline in the context of genetically mediated generalized epilepsy. Characterize current cognitive functioning and provide data for PRC review should recurrence prompt reconsideration of a presurgical workup.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o generalized epilepsy with onset at age 55. Seizures are described as generalized tonic-clonic events, with a reported frequency of approximately one per week. Etiology is genetic, associated with an STXBP1 c.902A>G variant. The epilepsy is currently classified as drug-responsive.

Current antiseizure medications include levetiracetam and topiramate; dose and dosing schedule were not available for review. Topiramate can contribute to cognitive slowing, word-retrieval difficulty, and reduced learning efficiency, although medication effects cannot be isolated from epilepsy-related and individual factors in the present evaluation.

Prior neuroimaging and EEG results were not provided. Accordingly, no independent conclusions regarding electrographic or structural lateralization can be offered. Educational, occupational, psychiatric, developmental, and prior neurosurgical histories were also unavailable in the referral materials.

**Behavioral Observations:**

**Appearance and Cooperation:**  
Arrived appropriately dressed and groomed. In NAD. Alert and oriented. Cooperative throughout the evaluation.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent and grammatical. Normal rate and volume. No overt paraphasic errors. Occasional word-finding pauses during demanding tasks.

**Mood and Affect:**  
Mood appeared neutral. Affect appropriately reactive and congruent with the interview context.

**Effort and Engagement:**  
Task persistence adequate. Performance on the Test of Memory Malingering was within expectations (TOMM Trial 2 = 50/50). Embedded validity indices, including Reliable Digit Span, were also acceptable. Results are considered a valid estimate of current cognitive functioning.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; Trail Making Test Parts A and B; Boston Naming Test; COWAT-FAS and Animal Fluency; CVLT-3; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test; Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; BDI-II; BAI; and performance-validity measures.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was in the Low Average range (FSIQ = 89, 23rd percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 92, 30th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 94, 34th percentile). Working Memory was Low Average (WMI = 86, 18th percentile), and Processing Speed represented a relative weakness (PSI = 81, 10th percentile). The profile suggests broadly preserved reasoning abilities with reduced cognitive efficiency.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Auditory attention span was Low Average to Average (Digit Span scaled score = 8). Mental manipulation was mildly inefficient. Trail Making Test Part A was Low Average (T = 39), without errors. Timed visual scanning and graphomotor coding were generally below expectation. This pattern reflects slowed processing speed rather than a primary attentional disorder.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Average (BNT T = 47), with benefit from phonemic cues on missed items. Letter fluency was Low Average (FAS T = 41), while semantic fluency was Average (Animals T = 46). Verbal abstraction was intact. There was no clear evidence of an aphasic syndrome or a focal dominant-temporal language deficit.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, initial acquisition was Low Average, with a shallow but positive learning curve across trials (Trials 1-5 total scaled score = 7). Short-delay and long-delay free recall were Low Average (scaled scores = 7 and 8, respectively). Recognition discrimination was Average, with relatively few false-positive errors. WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate and delayed recall were Low Average (scaled scores = 8 and 8), with adequate retention of initially learned information. Overall, verbal learning was inefficient, but retention and recognition were comparatively preserved. The pattern is more consistent with reduced acquisition/organizational efficiency than rapid forgetting.

**Visual Memory:**  
Visual Reproduction immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 10), and delayed recall was Low Average to Average (scaled score = 9). ROCF delayed recall was Average (T = 46). Recognition was preserved. There was no convincing evidence of disproportionate nonverbal memory impairment.

**Executive Function:**  
Trail Making Test Part B was Low Average (T = 38), with one sequencing error. Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T = 40). WCST performance showed adequate concept formation, with mild inefficiency in shifting strategy after feedback. No clinically significant perseverative tendency was observed. Findings indicate mild executive inefficiency under speeded or complex conditions.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10). ROCF copy was intact, although the approach was somewhat piecemeal. Basic visual perception, constructional ability, and spatial reasoning were preserved.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was mildly slowed bilaterally, without a meaningful dominant versus nondominant asymmetry. No lateralizing motor pattern emerged.

**Mood/Personality:**  
Self-report measures indicated minimal depressive symptoms (BDI-II = 6) and minimal anxiety symptoms (BAI = 5). There was no psychometric evidence that emotional distress substantially interfered with cognitive performance.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
This valid evaluation demonstrated Low Average overall intellectual functioning, with relative strengths in verbal reasoning, visuospatial construction, confrontation naming, recognition memory, and visual memory. Weaknesses were most apparent in processing speed, working memory, rapid verbal generation, and efficient acquisition of new verbal information. Retention of learned verbal and visual material was broadly preserved.

The profile is nonspecific and does not show a compelling lateralized temporal-lobe pattern. In particular, confrontation naming was intact, verbal memory did not show rapid forgetting, and visual memory was not disproportionately impaired. Mild cognitive inefficiency may be multifactorial in the setting of generalized epilepsy and antiseizure treatment; topiramate-related slowing or word-retrieval effects cannot be excluded.

Given the generalized epilepsy phenotype and absence of supplied focal imaging or EEG data, these findings do not independently support a focal resective target. The reported frequency of approximately one generalized tonic-clonic seizure per week should be reconciled with the chart designation of drug-responsive epilepsy at multidisciplinary review.

**Recommendations:**

1. Review these findings at PRC in conjunction with seizure chronology, EEG, imaging, genetic data, and medication response.
2. Continue epilepsy management with levetiracetam and topiramate under Epileptology. Medication adjustment should remain a clinical decision; monitor for cognitive slowing or word-finding difficulty.
3. Use compensatory strategies, including written schedules, repetition, structured learning, and reduced multitasking.
4. Should recurrence occur, revisit the presurgical workup. This may include updated video-EEG monitoring and imaging to r/o an unsuspected focal onset.
5. If future data identify a focal drug-resistant network, PRC may consider appropriate surgical or neuromodulation options, including the NeuroPace RNS System; current cognitive findings alone do not establish candidacy.
6. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation after a meaningful clinical change or before any invasive intervention to quantify interval change and refine cognitive risk.
