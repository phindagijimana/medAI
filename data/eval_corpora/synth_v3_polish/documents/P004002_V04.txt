Referral Question:  Diagnostic/presurgical neuropsychological baseline to characterize cognitive functioning in the context of generalized epilepsy and a focal cortical dysplasia, and to assist with multidisciplinary decision-making at PRC.

Relevant History:  
The patient has a hx of epilepsy beginning at age 7. Available records characterize the epilepsy as generalized, with generalized tonic-clonic seizures occurring approximately five times per week. Neurology documentation classifies the condition as drug responsive. Current ASM is carbamazepine; dose and schedule were not available for review. No prior ASM trials were documented in the materials provided.

Etiology is considered structural. Brain MRI demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia. The focal MRI lesion is probably epileptogenic, although concordance with seizure onset requires integration with EEG/semiology and other presurgical data. EEG findings and lesion laterality were not available for this evaluation. The patient denies photosensitivity and reports no family h/o epilepsy.

Psychiatric, developmental, educational, and occupational histories were not fully documented. No collateral information was available regarding baseline academic achievement, special education services, or interval cognitive change. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the present findings.

Behavioral Observations:

Appearance and Cooperation:  Arrived appropriately dressed and groomed. In NAD. Alert and adequately cooperative throughout the evaluation. No overt seizure activity observed during testing.

Speech and Language:  Speech fluent and appropriately articulated. Comprehension adequate for multistep instructions. No evident paraphasic errors or gross word-finding disturbance in conversation.

Mood and Affect:  Mood appeared neutral. Affect congruent and appropriately reactive. No behavioral evidence of severe depression, anxiety, psychosis, or acute distress.

Effort and Engagement:  Task persistence adequate. Performance on the Test of Memory Malingering was within expectations (TOMM Trial 2 = 50/50), supporting valid engagement. Embedded indices, including Reliable Digit Span, were also acceptable. Results are considered a valid estimate of current cognitive functioning.

Tests Administered:  
WAIS-IV; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT); Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure (ROCF); Boston Naming Test (BNT); COWAT phonemic and animal fluency; Trail Making Test Parts A and B; Stroop Color-Word Test; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (WCST); Grooved Pegboard; Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II); Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI); and TOMM.

Results:

Intellectual Functioning:  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was 94 (34th percentile; Average). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 98, 45th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 96, 39th percentile). Working Memory fell in the Low Average range (WMI = 89, 23rd percentile), while Processing Speed was also Low Average (PSI = 84, 14th percentile). The profile indicates broadly preserved general reasoning with relative inefficiency on speeded and attentionally demanding tasks.

Attention/Processing Speed:  
Auditory attention span was Low Average to Average (Digit Span scaled score = 8). Trail Making A was Low Average (T = 42), without errors. Speeded visual scanning and graphomotor output were mildly reduced. The findings suggest slowed processing efficiency rather than a primary deficit in basic attention.

Language:  
Confrontation naming was Average (BNT T = 48), with benefit from phonemic cueing on missed items. Phonemic verbal fluency was Low Average (T = 42), while semantic fluency was Average (T = 47). Vocabulary and verbal abstraction were Average. There was no clear evidence of clinically significant aphasia or dominant temporal dysfunction on the available language measures.

Verbal Memory:  
RAVLT acquisition across five learning trials was Average overall (total learning z = -0.3). Delayed free recall remained Average (z = -0.4), and recognition discrimination was intact. WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate and delayed recall were Average (scaled scores = 9 and 10, respectively). Retention was appropriate, without evidence of rapid forgetting. This pattern does not suggest a prominent verbal encoding or storage deficit.

Visual Memory:  
ROCF copy was intact, although organization was mildly inefficient. Delayed recall was Low Average (T = 40), with preserved recognition of major figure elements. WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate and delayed recall were Low Average to Average (scaled scores = 8 and 9). Visual memory was somewhat weaker than verbal memory but was not frankly impaired. In the absence of lesion laterality and handedness/language-dominance data, this mild discrepancy should not be overinterpreted as a lateralizing sign.

Executive Function:  
Trail Making B was Low Average (T = 41), with one sequencing error that was self-corrected. Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T = 42). WCST performance showed Average concept formation with mild perseverative tendency. Overall, executive functioning was broadly intact, with relative weakness in cognitive flexibility and speed under increased task demands.

Visuospatial Functioning:  
WAIS-IV Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10). ROCF copy was within normal limits, with minor organizational inefficiency. No clinically significant visuoconstructional deficit was identified.

Motor Functioning:  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average bilaterally, without a meaningful asymmetry. Results do not provide convincing evidence of focal lateralized fine-motor dysfunction.

Mood/Personality:  
BDI-II and BAI scores fell in the minimal range. Self-report did not indicate clinically significant current depressive or anxiety symptoms. Continued monitoring is appropriate given the psychosocial burden associated with frequent seizures.

Summary and Impressions:  
The evaluation yielded valid findings. General intellectual functioning was Average, with intact verbal reasoning, visuospatial construction, confrontation naming, and verbal learning and retention. Relative weaknesses were observed in processing speed, working memory, cognitive flexibility, and aspects of visual recall. These weaknesses were generally Low Average rather than frankly impaired and may reflect the combined effects of chronic epilepsy, frequent seizures, and ASM treatment.

The current profile is not strongly lateralizing. Verbal memory and naming were preserved, while visual memory was mildly weaker but not impaired. MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia, and this lesion is probably epileptogenic; however, the documented generalized epilepsy phenotype and generalized tonic-clonic seizure type require careful electroclinical correlation. Neuropsychological findings alone neither confirm nor exclude focal seizure onset. Lesion location, scalp/video EEG, and functional language data are needed before estimating domain-specific surgical risk.

Recommendations:  
1. Discuss findings at PRC with integration of MRI, prolonged video-EEG, seizure semiology, and medication response. Clarify whether the reported frequency of five seizures per week reflects the current carbamazepine regimen.  
2. Obtain fMRI language mapping if lesion-directed surgery is under consideration. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is technically limited or discordant.  
3. If generalized onset remains confirmed, carefully r/o a coincidental/noncausal MRI lesion before focal resection. If a focal epileptogenic network is established, lesion-directed surgery or neuromodulation, including the NeuroPace RNS System, may be discussed according to PRC consensus.  
4. Continue compensatory strategies for slowed processing: written instructions, reduced distraction, pacing, and additional time for complex tasks.  
5. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6–12 months s/p surgical or neuromodulation intervention, or earlier if there is a substantial cognitive change.
