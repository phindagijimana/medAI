Indication:  Hx of drug-resistant epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 8; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o focal versus generalized onset.

Clinical History:  Patient with structural epilepsy and historical GTC seizures, currently seizure-free with 0 seizures per month on perampanel, levetiracetam, and oxcarbazepine. MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis. Although the epilepsy has been classified clinically as generalized, available history raises the possibility of focal-onset seizures with progression to bilateral tonic-clonic activity. Some reported spell features are also possibly suggestive of a PNES component.

Previously trialed ASMs include topiramate, discontinued for inadequate seizure control; lacosamide, discontinued due to sedation; carbamazepine, discontinued due to cognitive side effects; and lamotrigine, discontinued due to rash.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. In NAD throughout the study. No medication reduction was performed.

Description:

- **Background:** The awake background was continuous and symmetric, composed predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with an appropriately organized anterior-to-posterior gradient. A well-formed 10 Hz PDR was present bilaterally and reactive to eye opening. No persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry was identified.

- **Awake state:** Low-amplitude frontocentral beta activity and a symmetric posterior alpha rhythm were present. No focal slowing, LPDs, GPDs, or rhythmic delta activity was seen.

- **Drowsy/sleep:** Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No sleep-activated epileptiform discharges were identified.

- **Hyperventilation:** HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without epileptiform activation.

- **Photic stimulation:** PS elicited a symmetric photic driving response at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was present.

- **Interictal findings:** No focal, multifocal, or generalized IEDs were recorded. Specifically, there were no temporal sharp waves, TIRDA, generalized spike-and-wave discharges, or polyspike-and-wave complexes.

- **Ictal events:** None captured. No clinical or electrographic seizures occurred. No habitual nonepileptic events were recorded.

- **ECG:** Regular rhythm throughout the sampled tracing.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states. No focal slowing or epileptiform abnormalities were identified.
2. No electrographic evidence of either focal or generalized epilepsy was demonstrated during this recording. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude epilepsy, including clinically generalized epilepsy or possibly focal-onset seizures with bilateral tonic-clonic evolution.
3. The reported possible PNES component cannot be assessed because no habitual clinical events were captured. Video EEG monitoring would be required for definitive electroclinical characterization.
4. Clinical and imaging correlation is advised given the MRI finding of hippocampal sclerosis and the historical designation of drug-resistant structural epilepsy. Should seizure recurrence occur, revisit prolonged video EEG monitoring and presurgical workup.
