**Referral Question:**  
Presurgical neuropsychological baseline to characterize cognitive strengths and weaknesses, evaluate memory and language functioning, and assist PRC decision-making regarding potential epilepsy intervention.

**Relevant History:**  
P004019 has a hx of combined generalized and focal epilepsy with seizure onset at age 10. The documented seizure type is generalized tonic-clonic, occurring at an average frequency of approximately two per month. Etiology is considered structural. Brain MRI demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis; laterality was not specified in the records available for this evaluation. EEG localization/lateralization data were not available for review.

The epilepsy has been classified by the treating team as drug-responsive despite ongoing breakthrough events. Current antiseizure medication is topiramate. The patient previously trialed levetiracetam, which was discontinued due to mood side effects. Medication dose and schedule were not provided. Topiramate may contribute to reduced processing speed, word retrieval, and learning efficiency and should be considered when interpreting the present findings.

Detailed psychiatric, developmental, educational, occupational, and family histories were not available in the referral materials. Accordingly, estimates of premorbid functioning and conclusions regarding interval cognitive change should be interpreted cautiously.

**Behavioral Observations:**

**Appearance and Cooperation:**  
Arrived appropriately dressed and groomed. In NAD. Alert and oriented. Cooperative throughout interview and testing. No overt seizure activity observed.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent and grammatically intact. Normal prosody and articulation. Mild word-finding pauses during extended responses. Comprehension adequate for interview questions and multistep test instructions.

**Mood and Affect:**  
Mood described as generally stable during the evaluation. Affect appropriate and congruent. No marked behavioral dysregulation observed.

**Effort and Engagement:**  
Task persistence and frustration tolerance adequate. TOMM Trial 2 = 50/50, indicating valid performance and adequate engagement. Embedded validity indicators were also within acceptable limits. Results are considered a reliable estimate of current cognitive functioning.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; Trail Making Test, Parts A and B; Boston Naming Test; COWAT letter and semantic fluency; CVLT-3; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test; Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; Beck Depression Inventory-II; Beck Anxiety Inventory.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was 92 (30th percentile; Average). Verbal Comprehension was 95 (37th percentile; Average), Perceptual Reasoning was 100 (50th percentile; Average), Working Memory was 89 (23rd percentile; Low Average), and Processing Speed was 81 (10th percentile; Low Average). The profile showed relatively stronger nonverbal reasoning than speeded output and auditory working memory.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Basic auditory attention was Low Average (Digit Span scaled score = 8). Mental manipulation was variable, with greater difficulty as working-memory demands increased. Trail Making Test Part A was Low Average (T = 40). WAIS-IV Coding was Low Average (scaled score = 7), while Symbol Search fell in the Borderline range (scaled score = 6). Results indicate slowed visual scanning and graphomotor processing rather than a primary attentional disorder.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming on the Boston Naming Test was Low Average (T = 42), with benefit from phonemic cues. Letter fluency was Borderline (T = 35), and semantic fluency was Low Average (T = 39). Verbal abstraction was Average. The mild weakness in rapid lexical retrieval could reflect temporal-system inefficiency, medication effects, or reduced processing speed. In the absence of MRI laterality, handedness information, and language mapping, these findings do not independently establish hemispheric language dominance.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, initial acquisition was Borderline, with total learning across five trials in the Low Average range (Trials 1–5 standard score = 82). The learning curve was shallow but positive. Short-delay free recall was Borderline (scaled score = 6), and long-delay free recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7). Recognition discrimination improved to the Low Average range, suggesting retrieval inefficiency with at least partial retention of learned material.

WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7), and delayed recall was Borderline (scaled score = 6). Recognition was Low Average. Overall, verbal memory was weaker than expected relative to general reasoning ability, with inefficient encoding and retrieval but no evidence of complete rapid forgetting.

**Visual Memory:**  
ROCF copy was intact, with an organized overall approach. Delayed recall was Low Average (T = 41), and recognition was Average. WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 9), with Low Average delayed recall (scaled score = 7). Visual memory was mildly inefficient but somewhat stronger than verbal memory. The difference was modest and should not be viewed as definitive evidence of lateralization.

**Executive Function:**  
Trail Making Test Part B was Low Average (T = 38), with no set-loss errors. Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T = 40). On the WCST, the patient completed five categories; perseverative responses were Average (T = 47). Executive functioning was broadly intact, although speeded cognitive flexibility and inhibition were mildly reduced.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10). ROCF copy was within normal limits. No visuoconstructional impairment or spatial neglect was evident.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average bilaterally, without a clinically meaningful asymmetry. Interpretation is limited by undocumented handedness.

**Mood/Personality:**  
BDI-II score was 8, and BAI score was 6, reflecting minimal current depressive and anxiety symptoms. These self-report findings do not suggest that significant emotional distress accounted for the cognitive profile observed.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
The evaluation produced valid results. Overall intellectual functioning was Average, with preserved visuospatial reasoning, verbal abstraction, constructional ability, recognition memory, and untimed problem solving. Relative weaknesses were observed in processing speed, rapid verbal fluency, auditory working memory, and new learning. Verbal memory was somewhat weaker than visual memory, particularly for unstructured list learning and delayed narrative recall.

This pattern is consistent with mild temporal-memory system inefficiency in the context of structural epilepsy and MRI-confirmed hippocampal sclerosis. However, the findings are only weakly lateralizing. The modest verbal-versus-visual memory difference may suggest relatively greater dominant temporal-system vulnerability, but this cannot be established without the laterality of the MRI lesion, EEG findings, handedness, and formal language-dominance data. Current topiramate treatment may also contribute to slowed processing and lexical retrieval.

If a dominant temporal resection is contemplated, there may be a moderate risk of further decline in verbal learning, delayed recall, and naming. Risk may be lower for nondominant temporal intervention, although visual-memory findings do not provide strong evidence of preserved contralateral reserve. These results should therefore be integrated with seizure semiology, prolonged EEG data, imaging laterality, and functional language mapping.

**Recommendations:**

1. Discuss findings at PRC with integration of MRI laterality, scalp or intracranial EEG, seizure semiology, and medication response.
2. Obtain fMRI language mapping before any temporal resection or ablation. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant with other lateralizing data.
3. Given combined generalized and focal epilepsy, confirm that the proposed surgical target represents an independent focal seizure onset zone rather than an incidental structural lesion.
4. If resection or ablation is not appropriate and a focal onset zone is confirmed, neuromodulation options such as the NeuroPace RNS System may be reviewed by the multidisciplinary epilepsy team.
5. Minimize additional medications with adverse cognitive effects when clinically feasible. Any ASM adjustment should be directed by epileptology.
6. Use external memory supports, including written schedules, alarms, medication organizers, and repetition of important information.
7. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6–12 months s/p surgical or neuromodulation intervention, or sooner if meaningful cognitive change is reported.
