Indication:  H/o genetic focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History:  Adult with seizure onset at age 35 years. Typical events are focal impaired-awareness seizures, currently occurring approximately once per month. Epilepsy is attributed to a causative SCN1A variant, c.5734C>T. Current ASMs are lamotrigine and brivaracetam. Previously trialed levetiracetam, discontinued due to mood side effects. The epilepsy is documented as drug responsive on the current regimen, although the referral history raises concern for probable/likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy given continued monthly events. A possible autoimmune contribution has also been considered. No history of epilepsy in the family.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS. Video was recorded throughout. The patient was awake and drowsy; stage N2 sleep was not achieved. In NAD during the recording. Study completed s/p routine activation procedures without complication.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined 10 Hz PDR that was reactive to eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. No persistent focal asymmetry or attenuation was identified.

Awake state:  No focal delta slowing, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or rhythmic evolving activity was present. There were occasional sharply contoured temporal transients during movement and eye closure, without a consistent field or after-going slow wave; these were not considered epileptiform.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric theta activity. Positive occipital sharp transients of sleep were present. No definite sleep spindles or K complexes were observed.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings:  No definite focal or generalized IEDs were identified.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No patient- or technologist-initiated event markers occurred.

ECG:  Regular rhythm throughout the sampled tracing.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake and drowsy states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy. The absence of a typical clinical event limits assessment of the reported focal impaired-awareness seizures.
3. If further electroclinical characterization is required because of continued monthly events or concern for likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient LTM may be considered. Clinical correlation is advised regarding the known SCN1A-related genetic etiology and the possible autoimmune contribution.
