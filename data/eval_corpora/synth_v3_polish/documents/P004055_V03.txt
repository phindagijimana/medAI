Indication:  H/o structural focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History:  Patient with focal epilepsy beginning at age 6 years. Typical events are focal impaired-awareness seizures, currently occurring approximately 4 times per month. Epilepsy is classified as drug-responsive and is associated with a structural etiology; prior brain MRI demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis. Current ASM is zonisamide. Previously trialed brivaracetam, discontinued due to mood side effects.

Family History:  Sister with epilepsy.

Technique:  A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background:  The awake background was continuous and organized, with an appropriate anterior-to-posterior gradient. A symmetric 10 Hz PDR was present over the bilateral posterior head regions and attenuated normally with eye opening. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  During maximal wakefulness, the background consisted predominantly of alpha activity posteriorly with admixed frontocentral beta and low-amplitude theta frequencies. Reactivity was preserved. No focal slowing was seen.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse theta activity, and slow roving eye movements. Vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes were not observed; therefore, definitive stage N2 sleep was not captured.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of HV.

Photic Stimulation:  Intermittent PS from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings:  No epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal spikes or sharp waves, generalized spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No focal or generalized pathologic slowing was present.

Ictal Events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No push-button events occurred.

ECG:  Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness and drowsiness. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were recorded.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. Clinical correlation is advised in this patient with hx of focal impaired-awareness seizures and MRI-demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis.
