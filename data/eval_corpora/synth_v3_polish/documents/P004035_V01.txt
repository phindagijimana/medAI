Assessment / Impression

This is an XX-year-old patient with epilepsy beginning at age 15, currently characterized by generalized tonic-clonic seizures occurring approximately 7 times per week. The epilepsy has been classified as generalized and is reported to have an immune etiology related to GAD65 antibodies.

The patient continues to have frequent seizures while taking lamotrigine, carbamazepine, and lacosamide and previously discontinued brivaracetam because of inadequate seizure control. The current course meets clinical criteria for drug-resistant epilepsy.

The exact epilepsy type requires clarification. Generalized epilepsy is the working classification; however, these may be possibly focal onset seizures evolving to bilateral tonic-clonic activity. We cannot distinguish these possibilities from the referral description alone. This distinction is clinically important because it will influence medication selection and whether a resective, ablative, or neuromodulation strategy could be appropriate.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Lamictal (lamotrigine), Tegretol (carbamazepine), and Vimpat (lacosamide) without abrupt interruption pending formal medication reconciliation. The patient will provide the exact tablet strengths and whether each medication is taken BID, TID, or on another schedule. I would not make dose recommendations until those details are verified.

2. Arrange inpatient EMU admission for LTM with video EEG. The goal is to capture representative events, confirm the generalized tonic-clonic semiology, assess for generalized versus focal IEDs, and r/o a focal onset with rapid bilateral spread.

3. Obtain the prior EEG/VEEG reports and, when feasible, the original EEG tracings for review at xxx Medicine Neurology.

4. Obtain or review a brain MRI performed with an epilepsy protocol. If no adequate study exists, an updated epilepsy-protocol MRI should be completed to evaluate for a potentially relevant structural abnormality.

No device recommendation is being made at this stage.
