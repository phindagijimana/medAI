Assessment and Plan:

1. Generalized Epilepsy With Generalized Tonic-Clonic Seizures, Structural Etiology, Drug Responsive  
   Epilepsy began at age 28. Prior MRI demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis. Although hippocampal sclerosis is often associated with focal epilepsy, her established electroclinical diagnosis in the prior record is generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures. She is currently clinically stable and seizure-free, with a reported seizure frequency of 0/month on dual therapy.
   - Continue Fycompa (perampanel) 6 mg PO nightly.
   - Continue Depakote ER (valproate) 1,000 mg PO nightly.
   - Check CBC, CMP/LFTs, and valproate trough level for routine medication surveillance.
   - Continue seizure calendar and bring it to future visits.
   - Reinforced strict adherence; avoid abrupt discontinuation of either ASM.
   - Reviewed seizure precautions, including caution around heights, open water, fire, and operating heavy machinery. Driving should remain consistent with applicable state regulations.
   - Discussed general reproductive safety considerations associated with valproate, including substantial fetal risk. She should contact neurology before any planned pregnancy or immediately if pregnancy is suspected so that risks, benefits, and treatment alternatives can be reviewed. Medication changes should not be made without neurologic supervision.
   - If a breakthrough seizure occurs, she should document the event, assess for potential provoking factors, and contact the clinic. Urgent evaluation is warranted for a seizure lasting longer than 5 minutes, recurrent seizures without return to baseline, significant injury, or respiratory compromise.
