Neurology Progress Note

Chief Complaint: Follow-up drug-resistant epilepsy

History of Present Illness:

The patient is a 22-year-old man with h/o epilepsy beginning at age 8, historically classified as generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Structural etiology has been considered in view of MRI evidence of hippocampal sclerosis, although the overall electroclinical picture may represent possibly focal onset seizures with rapid bilateral spread. He is s/p multiple ASM trials and remains on Fycompa (perampanel), Keppra (levetiracetam), and Trileptal (oxcarbazepine). His epilepsy has historically met criteria for drug resistance.

He was last seen on 10/22/2025 for a new-patient epilepsy consultation. Since that visit, he reports no breakthrough seizures, with current frequency 0/month. Seizure calendar reviewed and contains no interval generalized tonic-clonic events. No medication changes or other interval events are documented. He continues all three current ASMs.

There has also been concern that some prior events may have a possible nonepileptic component, with aspects suggestive of PNES. This remains a hedged consideration rather than a confirmed diagnosis. Likewise, given the hippocampal sclerosis, focal onset cannot be excluded despite the prior generalized epilepsy classification. At present, seizure control is stable, and there are no new concerns reported today.

Assessment and Plan:

1. **Epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures, historically drug-resistant, currently seizure-free**

   Epilepsy began at age 8 and has previously been classified as generalized. However, MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis, raising the possibility of focal onset seizures with rapid bilateral spread. He has failed or not tolerated adequate trials of topiramate, lacosamide, carbamazepine, and lamotrigine. Despite the historical drug-resistant designation, he currently reports no interval seizures and a frequency of 0/month on triple therapy.

   - Continue Fycompa (perampanel), Keppra (levetiracetam), and Trileptal (oxcarbazepine) at the current MAR doses without change.
   - Continue maintaining a seizure calendar and bring it to future visits.
   - Reinforced adherence and avoidance of missed doses.
   - Standard seizure precautions reviewed.
   - If seizures recur, revisit the presurgical workup, including clarification of focal versus generalized onset.

2. **Possible focal epilepsy associated with hippocampal sclerosis**

   MRI finding is structurally significant but does not, by itself, establish that all habitual events are focal. Further electroclinical correlation would be needed if seizures recur. At that point, prolonged video-EEG monitoring may be appropriate to r/o alternative event types and better define seizure onset before considering an intervention.

3. **Possible nonepileptic event component**

   Prior event descriptions have reportedly been suggestive of a possible PNES component, but this has not been confirmed. No such events were reported during the current interval. If atypical spells recur, video capture and inpatient or ambulatory EEG characterization should be considered before escalating ASMs.

Follow-up: 6 months, sooner PRN for breakthrough seizures or medication concerns.
