Medical Decision-Making (MDM):  
Known focal epilepsy presenting s/p one typical breakthrough focal-aware seizure with return to baseline. No evidence of status epilepticus. Differential included epileptic seizure, syncope, metabolic derangement, toxic ingestion, CNS infection, TIA, and psychogenic non-epileptic event. Typical semiology, known epilepsy, and preserved awareness favor focal-aware seizure. Afebrile, well appearing, normal glucose/electrolytes, and nonfocal exam lower concern for acute infectious, metabolic, or structural cause.

Given ongoing seizures approximately 4/week despite adequate trials of multiple ASMs, clinical picture is likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy; this remains a probable diagnosis, with etiology unknown. No Ativan (lorazepam) or IV antiseizure load required because the event terminated spontaneously and no recurrence occurred during ED observation. Tolerated PO and remained at neurologic baseline.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough focal-aware seizure.  
2. Probable drug-resistant focal epilepsy of unknown etiology.

Disposition:  
Discharge home with parent after ED observation and neurology consultation. Continue Topamax (topiramate) and Vimpat (lacosamide) as prescribed; no ED medication changes. Expedited pediatric epilepsy follow-up advised. Seizure precautions reviewed, including constant supervision around water, avoidance of heights, medication adherence, and use of prescribed rescue medication per home seizure plan. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, recurrent seizures without return to baseline, breathing difficulty, injury, fever with altered mental status, new weakness, or other concerning change.
