Ordering Provider: Dr. [REDACTED]  
Indication: H/o immune-mediated focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 63. Seizures remain drug resistant, occurring approximately once per year. EEG obtained to r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Current ASMs: Brivaracetam, valproate, topiramate, and oxcarbazepine. No medication changes were made for this study.

Prior ASMs: Previously trialed perampanel, discontinued due to cognitive side effects; carbamazepine, discontinued due to sedation; and lacosamide, discontinued due to mood side effects.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS. Video was recorded throughout the study. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and well organized. A 9 Hz PDR was present over the bilateral posterior head regions, reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry.

Awake state: Normal admixture of alpha and beta frequencies. No focal slowing, generalized slowing, or breach rhythm was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Vertex waves and well-formed sleep spindles were not seen; therefore, sustained stage N2 sleep was not recorded.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric buildup of theta activity without focal abnormality or epileptiform discharges.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response occurred.

Interictal findings: No epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No electrographic seizures were present.

Ictal events: None captured. No push-button events or clinical episodes of impaired awareness were recorded.

ECG: Regular rhythm throughout the study.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake and drowsy states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. No habitual focal impaired-awareness seizure was captured.
3. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy, including this patient’s drug-resistant focal epilepsy of immune etiology. If further electroclinical characterization is required, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may provide greater diagnostic yield.
