Indication: H/o generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures beginning at age 9, currently occurring approximately once per month. Etiology categorized as structural in the setting of MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia. Study obtained to r/o interictal epileptiform activity and assess for electrographic seizures.

Clinical History: The patient has drug-responsive generalized epilepsy characterized by myoclonic seizures. Current ASM is valproate. Patient is s/p trials of perampanel, discontinued due to rash, and zonisamide, discontinued due to cognitive side effects. No h/o febrile seizures in childhood. The patient denies urinary incontinence with events. Family hx is notable for a brother with stroke.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including temporal leads, with simultaneous single-lead ECG and video recording. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, with brief stage N2 sleep achieved. Patient cooperative and in NAD. The study was technically adequate for interpretation.

Description:

- **Background:** The awake background was continuous and organized, with an anterior-to-posterior gradient. A symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and reactive to eye opening. Anteriorly, there was expected low-amplitude beta activity with admixed alpha and theta frequencies. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry was identified.

- **Awake state:** During maximal wakefulness, there were occasional generalized bursts of 3.5-4.5 Hz polyspike-and-wave and spike-and-wave activity. These discharges were bifrontocentrally predominant, maximal at F3/F4, Fz, and Cz, and lasted approximately 0.5-2 seconds. No definite clinical correlate was evident on video.

- **Drowsy/sleep:** Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Brief stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized epileptiform discharges were mildly activated by drowsiness and sleep.

- **Hyperventilation:** Produced expected symmetric generalized theta/delta slowing without a sustained epileptiform or ictal pattern.

- **Photic stimulation:** Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

- **Interictal findings:** Occasional generalized polyspike-and-wave and spike-and-wave discharges with bifrontocentral predominance, as detailed above. No consistent focal phase reversal, focal slowing, TIRDA, LPDs, or independent focal IEDs were seen.

- **Ictal events:** No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, bifrontocentrally predominant polyspike-and-wave and spike-and-wave discharges, mildly activated during drowsiness and sleep. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support an increased seizure propensity and are consistent with the reported generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. There was no persistent focal slowing or independent focal epileptiform activity to provide electrographic localization corresponding to the known MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia. Clinical and imaging correlation remains advised.
