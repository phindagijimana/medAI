Indication:  H/o generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures, onset at age 30. Epilepsy of unknown etiology, clinically classified as drug-resistant. Currently seizure-free with 0 seizures/month on brivaracetam and perampanel. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o ongoing subclinical seizures.

Relevant History/Medications:  Current ASMs are brivaracetam and perampanel. Previously trialed levetiracetam, discontinued due to cognitive side effects. Family hx notable for a sister with febrile seizures.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded throughout the study. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient of lower-voltage beta and mixed alpha/theta activity was present. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  During wakefulness, occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present. These were frontally predominant, maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, bilaterally synchronous, and symmetric. Most occurred as isolated complexes or brief 1- to 2-second bursts without a definite clinical correlate.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike-and-wave discharges became mildly more frequent during drowsiness and sleep, occasionally occurring in brief runs lasting up to 3 seconds. No associated behavioral arrest or other clear clinical change was evident on video.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced the expected diffuse buildup of high-amplitude theta/delta activity. There was activation of generalized 3 Hz spike-and-wave activity, including one approximately 4-second burst. No definite impairment of responsiveness was documented; therefore, this is considered an interictal discharge rather than an electroclinical absence seizure.

Photic Stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings:  Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness, sleep, and hyperventilation. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA were seen.

Ictal Events:  No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No typical absence event was recorded.

ECG:  Regular rhythm throughout the sampled tracing.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with activation during drowsiness, sleep, and HV.  
2. These findings are consistent with an increased epileptogenic potential of generalized onset and support the patient’s established diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures.  
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. The absence of a captured clinical event does not exclude ongoing epilepsy; clinical correlation is advised.
