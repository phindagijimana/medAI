Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy characterized by focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 25. The current reported seizure frequency is approximately 5 per week. The epilepsy is considered structural in etiology based on the reported vascular malformation.

The MRI report also raises a probable finding consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis. At this time, I cannot determine whether the vascular malformation, probable MTS, or another focal network best explains the seizure onset zone without reviewing the MRI images and available electrophysiologic data.

The patient’s epilepsy has been described as drug-responsive and is currently managed with levetiracetam and oxcarbazepine. Nevertheless, the ongoing event frequency is clinically significant and warrants confirmation of the exact ASM doses, adherence to the prescribed regimen, and diagnostic characterization. Perampanel was previously stopped because of sedation and should remain clearly separated from the active medication list.

On the information provided, I would not yet classify the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy. There is also insufficient information at this first consultation to pursue a presurgical evaluation or neuromodulation strategy. Dr. xxx and I discussed the case, including the need to correlate the structural MRI findings with the patient’s focal seizure type.
