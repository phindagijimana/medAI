**Referral Question:**  
Diagnostic/presurgical neuropsychological evaluation to characterize cognitive functioning in the context of epilepsy and establish a baseline for multidisciplinary review at PRC. Particular attention was given to language, memory, processing speed, and executive functioning.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures beginning at age 8. Current seizure frequency is approximately three per month. The epilepsy has been classified as drug-responsive and of structural etiology. Current ASM is brivaracetam; dose and schedule were not available for review. No prior ASM trials were documented in the available records.

Brain MRI demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia; the specific location and laterality were not provided. EEG findings were likewise unavailable for this evaluation. Thus, concordance among seizure semiology, electrophysiology, and the structural lesion could not be independently assessed. There was no reported h/o epilepsy surgery or neuromodulation.

Family h/o is notable for a brother with stroke. Educational, occupational, developmental, psychiatric, and substance-use histories were not sufficiently detailed in the available documentation. NKDA status was not documented.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; Trail Making Test Parts A and B; Boston Naming Test; COWAT phonemic and semantic fluency; CVLT-3; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test; Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; BDI-II; BAI; PHQ-9; and Test of Memory Malingering.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was Average (FSIQ = 96, 39th percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 100, 50th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 98, 45th percentile). Working Memory fell in the Average range (WMI = 92, 30th percentile). Processing Speed was Low Average (PSI = 84, 14th percentile) and represented a relative weakness compared with verbal reasoning.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Basic auditory attention and mental manipulation were intact (Digit Span scaled score = 9; Arithmetic scaled score = 8). Visual scanning and sequencing were Low Average on Trail Making A (T = 40, 16th percentile). Processing speed was similarly reduced on WAIS-IV Coding (scaled score = 7) and Symbol Search (scaled score = 7). The pattern suggests mild generalized cognitive slowing rather than a primary attentional disorder.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Average on the Boston Naming Test (T = 48, 42nd percentile), without a pattern of prominent semantic errors. Phonemic verbal fluency was Average (FAS T = 45, 31st percentile), as was semantic fluency for animals (T = 47, 38th percentile). Verbal abstraction was intact. There was no clear evidence of dominant temporal language dysfunction on the present measures.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, initial acquisition was mildly inefficient, followed by an appropriate learning curve across repeated trials (Trials 1–5 total T = 47, 38th percentile). Short-delay free recall was Average (z = -0.3), and long-delay free recall was Low Average to Average (z = -0.5). Recognition discriminability was intact (z = -0.2), with no excessive false-positive responding. WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 9), while delayed recall was Low Average (scaled score = 8). Overall, verbal retention was preserved, with mild inefficiency during initial encoding but no evidence of rapid forgetting.

**Visual Memory:**  
WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 10), and delayed recall was Average (scaled score = 9). ROCF delayed recall fell in the Average range (T = 44, 27th percentile). Recognition was intact. These results do not indicate a clinically significant visual memory deficit or a clearly lateralizing right temporal pattern.

**Executive Function:**  
Mental flexibility on Trail Making B was Low Average (T = 37, 10th percentile). WCST performance showed five categories completed, with perseverative responses in the Low Average range (T = 42). Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T = 41). Mild inefficiency was observed on timed set-shifting and inhibition tasks, although conceptual reasoning and response to feedback remained intact.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10). ROCF copy was broadly intact (32/36), with adequate organization and no evidence of spatial neglect or constructional apraxia.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average with the dominant hand (T = 40) and Average with the nondominant hand (T = 43). No clinically meaningful lateralized motor slowing was identified.

**Mood/Personality:**  
Self-report measures indicated minimal depressive symptoms (BDI-II = 8; PHQ-9 = 4) and minimal anxiety symptoms (BAI = 6). Current emotional distress is unlikely to account for the observed cognitive profile.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
The evaluation documents broadly Average intellectual functioning, intact confrontation naming, preserved verbal and visual retention, adequate visuospatial abilities, and intact conceptual reasoning. Relative weaknesses were observed in processing speed and selected timed executive tasks. Verbal learning was mildly inefficient, but retention and recognition were preserved. There was no convincing material-specific memory asymmetry or language deficit that would independently lateralize cerebral dysfunction.

The patient has generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures, onset at age 8, currently occurring approximately three times per month. Epilepsy is classified as drug-responsive and structural, with MRI evidence of focal cortical dysplasia. Because the lesion location and EEG data were unavailable, the relationship between the dysplasia and generalized electroclinical phenotype remains uncertain. The present cognitive findings do not provide strong independent lateralizing information.

If lesion-directed intervention is considered, cognitive risk will depend substantially on lesion location, language dominance, and the extent of any proposed resection. Consideration of resection or a NeuroPace RNS System should require clear electroclinical and imaging concordance. The current profile does not indicate an established dominant temporal syndrome, though mild decline in processing efficiency could occur s/p intervention involving eloquent cortex or distributed networks.

**Recommendations:**

1. Review findings at PRC with MRI, EEG, seizure semiology, and medication response.
2. Obtain fMRI language mapping if a dominant-hemisphere procedure is contemplated. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is equivocal or discordant with other localization data.
3. Continue epilepsy management with brivaracetam under Epileptology.
4. Should he have a cluster, use nasal midazolam rescue PRN and call 911.
5. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6–12 months s/p any surgical or neuromodulation procedure, or earlier if meaningful cognitive change occurs.
6. Use written schedules, external reminders, and additional time for complex tasks when processing-speed demands are high.
