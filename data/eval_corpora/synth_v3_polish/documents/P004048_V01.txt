Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy manifested by focal impaired awareness seizures, with onset at age 7 and a current reported frequency of approximately 5 per month. The epilepsy etiology is unknown. Based on the limited clinical description, the habitual events are possibly focal onset seizures; further characterization is required before assigning a more specific localization.

The course is classified as drug-responsive, and the current regimen consists of perampanel and lamotrigine. Nevertheless, the ongoing frequency indicates incomplete seizure control at present. Before changing therapy, we need to confirm the exact doses, administration schedule, adherence, prior response to titration, and whether the reported events all represent the same habitual seizure type.

There is insufficient information at this initial review to determine whether the patient requires inpatient VEEG/LTM or another advanced epilepsy evaluation. There also is not enough information to define a structural etiology. Updated EEG and epilepsy-protocol MRI data would help r/o an unrecognized electrographic or structural substrate.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Fycompa (perampanel) and Lamictal (lamotrigine) at the currently prescribed doses for now. I did not make a dose change because the strengths and dosing frequencies were not available.

2. Complete formal medication reconciliation, including tablet strengths, QD versus BID schedule, adherence, tolerability, and prescribing pharmacy information. The patient should not independently stop either medication, as abrupt withdrawal may increase seizure risk.

3. Request all prior neurology notes, EEG/VEEG reports, MRI brain reports and images, and any available medication-monitoring laboratory results from the referring practice and prior facilities.

4. Obtain a detailed seizure history at follow-up, including behavioral arrest, automatisms, responsiveness, duration, recovery, and any progression to bilateral convulsive activity. This will help determine whether all reported episodes are consistent with focal impaired awareness seizures.

6. Consider an epilepsy-protocol MRI brain if no adequate prior study is available. This would be directed toward identifying or r/o a structural cause for the focal epilepsy.

7. Maintain a seizure diary documenting event frequency and observable features. Given the current frequency of approximately 5 per month, accurate tracking will be important when assessing medication response.

8. Standard seizure precautions were reviewed. The patient should avoid driving unless permitted under applicable state law and cleared by the treating clinician. Additional precautions include avoiding unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery.
