**Referral Question:**  
Diagnostic/presurgical neuropsychological baseline in the context of focal epilepsy. Assess cognitive functioning, including language and memory lateralization, and inform discussion at PRC regarding potential treatment options.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o focal epilepsy, with onset at age 35. Typical events are described as focal impaired-awareness seizures occurring approximately once per month. Etiology is considered genetic, associated with an **SCN1A c.5734C>T** variant. The patient denies a family history of epilepsy.

Current ASMs include lamotrigine and brivaracetam; doses were not available for review. Levetiracetam was previously trialed and discontinued due to mood side effects. Although the epilepsy has been designated drug-responsive in portions of the available record, referral information also characterizes the condition as probably/likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy. This discrepancy should be clarified through review of seizure control, adherence, and adequacy of prior ASM trials. A possible autoimmune contribution has also been raised, although this remains unconfirmed.

Prior EEG and neuroimaging findings were not provided for this evaluation; therefore, cognitive results cannot be definitively compared with electrophysiologic or structural lateralization. No clear h/o epilepsy surgery was documented. Educational, occupational, developmental, and psychiatric histories were incompletely available. No PRN rescue medication information was provided.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent and normally articulated. No overt paraphasic errors. Auditory comprehension adequate for interview and task instructions.

**Mood and Affect:**  
Mood broadly euthymic. Affect appropriate and reactive. No behavioral evidence of acute psychiatric distress.

**Effort and Engagement:**

**Tests Administered:**

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was Average (FSIQ = 98, 45th percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 100), as were Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 102) and Working Memory (WMI = 95). Processing Speed was Low Average (PSI = 89). The profile showed generally intact intellectual functioning with a relative weakness in speeded output.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Basic auditory attention and working memory ranged from Low Average to Average (Digit Span scaled score = 9; Arithmetic scaled score = 9). Trail Making Test Part A was Low Average (T = 43). Performance on speeded visual scanning and graphomotor coding was also Low Average. There was no evidence of a primary attentional disorder, although mild slowing was evident.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Low Average on the Boston Naming Test (T = 42), with benefit from phonemic cueing. Letter fluency was Average (FAS T = 46), while semantic fluency was Low Average (Animals T = 44). The mild naming inefficiency could reflect subtle dominant temporal-system weakness; however, it was not sufficiently pronounced or specific to establish language lateralization. Functional language remained intact during the evaluation.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, initial acquisition across five learning trials was Low Average (Trials 1–5 z = -0.8). Short-delay free recall was Low Average (z = -1.0), and long-delay free recall was Borderline to Low Average (z = -1.2). Recognition discrimination was comparatively stronger (z = -0.5), indicating that retrieval inefficiency contributed to the reduced free-recall scores. WMS-IV Logical Memory was Low Average for immediate recall (scaled score = 8) and Borderline for delayed recall (scaled score = 7), with adequate recognition. Overall, verbal learning and delayed retrieval represented relative weaknesses.

**Visual Memory:**  
Visual memory was comparatively preserved. WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 10), and delayed recall was Average (scaled score = 9). ROCF delayed recall was Average (T = 48). This verbal–visual discrepancy is suggestive of relatively greater inefficiency in dominant-hemisphere memory systems, but the pattern is modest and should not be interpreted as definitive lateralization without imaging, EEG, and language-mapping data.

**Executive Function:**  
Trail Making Test Part B was Low Average (T = 41), with no set-loss errors. WCST performance was Average, including five categories completed and total errors in the Average range (T = 47). Stroop inhibition was Average (T = 45). Executive functioning was broadly intact, with mild slowing on tasks requiring rapid mental flexibility.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Block Design was Average (scaled score = 11). ROCF copy was intact and well organized. No clinically significant visuoconstructional or spatial-perceptual deficit was identified.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average with the preferred hand (T = 44) and Average with the nonpreferred hand (T = 46). No meaningful lateralized fine-motor deficit was demonstrated.

**Mood/Personality:**  
Self-report measures indicated minimal current depressive and anxiety symptoms (BDI-II = 8; BAI = 6; PHQ-9 = 4). These findings do not suggest that emotional distress substantially affected test performance. Continued monitoring is appropriate given prior mood side effects on levetiracetam.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
This evaluation produced valid findings. Overall intellectual functioning was Average. Relative strengths included visuospatial construction, visual memory, recognition memory, and core executive abilities. Weaknesses involved processing speed, confrontation naming, semantic retrieval, and verbal learning/delayed free recall. The profile is mildly suggestive of greater dominant temporal-system inefficiency, but it is not strongly lateralizing or localizing. Definitive interpretation requires integration with EEG, MRI, PET/SPECT if obtained, and seizure semiology.

The record contains potentially conflicting descriptions of drug responsiveness: the epilepsy is listed as drug-responsive, while also characterized as probably/likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Given seizures occurring approximately monthly despite lamotrigine and brivaracetam, review at PRC should establish whether accepted criteria for drug resistance have been met. A possible autoimmune contribution cannot be excluded but is not established by the data reviewed. From a cognitive standpoint, dominant temporal resection or ablation could carry risk for further decline in verbal memory and naming, particularly if the epileptogenic zone is left-sided and language is left-dominant.

**Recommendations:**  
1. Discuss findings at PRC with direct integration of EEG, imaging, seizure semiology, and adequacy of prior ASM trials.  
2. Obtain task-based fMRI for language dominance and memory-network assessment before any dominant temporal intervention.  
3. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant with other lateralizing data.  
4. If resection or ablation is not appropriate, neuromodulation options, including the **NeuroPace RNS System**, may be considered based on confirmed seizure localization and drug-resistance status.  
5. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation 6–12 months s/p any surgical or neuromodulation procedure, or sooner with substantial cognitive change.  
6. Use compensatory memory strategies, including external reminders, structured routines, and reduced multitasking during important verbal learning.
