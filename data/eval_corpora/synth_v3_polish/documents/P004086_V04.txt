**Referral Question:**  
Presurgical neuropsychological baseline to characterize cognitive functioning and assess for a lateralizing pattern in the context of drug-resistant generalized epilepsy, frequent myoclonic seizures, and MRI evidence of focal cortical dysplasia. Findings will inform discussion at PRC.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a hx of epilepsy beginning at age 12. The documented epilepsy type is generalized, with myoclonic seizures occurring approximately six times per week. Available history also indicates episodes that possibly represent atypical absence seizures; atypical absence cannot be excluded based on the information available.

The epilepsy is considered drug-resistant and attributed to a structural etiology. Brain MRI reportedly demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia, although the specific location and laterality were not provided for this evaluation. Detailed scalp EEG or long-term monitoring findings were also unavailable. The relationship between the focal MRI abnormality and generalized electroclinical presentation therefore requires multidisciplinary review.

Current ASMs are lacosamide, carbamazepine, lamotrigine, and perampanel; dosing information was not available. Previously trialed ASMs include brivaracetam, discontinued due to cognitive side effects; oxcarbazepine, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control; and topiramate, discontinued due to sedation. Educational, occupational, developmental, and psychiatric histories were not sufficiently documented to permit detailed interpretation. No additional neurologic or surgical h/o was provided.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was Average (FSIQ=94, 34th percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI=100, 50th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI=98, 45th percentile). Working Memory was Low Average (WMI=89, 23rd percentile), and Processing Speed was Low Average (PSI=84, 14th percentile). The profile suggests preserved general reasoning abilities with relative inefficiency on speeded and attentionally demanding tasks.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Auditory attention span was Low Average to Average (Digit Span scaled score=8). Mental arithmetic was Low Average (scaled score=8). Trail Making Test Part A was Low Average (T=41), without errors. WAIS-IV Coding was Low Average (scaled score=7), while Symbol Search was Low Average (scaled score=7). Findings indicate mild generalized slowing rather than a focal deficit.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Average on the Boston Naming Test (T=48). Phonemic verbal fluency was Low Average (FAS T=42), and semantic fluency was Average (Animals T=46). Verbal abstraction was Average (Similarities scaled score=10). There was no compelling evidence of aphasia or disproportionate dominant temporal dysfunction. Naming performance does not independently suggest elevated dominant temporal risk, although language laterality cannot be established by neuropsychological testing alone.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, total learning across five trials was Low Average (Trials 1-5 T=43). Learning improved with repetition, though acquisition was somewhat inefficient. Short-delay free recall was Low Average (z=-0.5), and long-delay free recall was Average (z=-0.3). Recognition discriminability was Average (z=0.0), with limited intrusion errors. WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate and delayed recall were Average (scaled scores=9 and 9, respectively). Overall, verbal memory was broadly preserved, with mild acquisition inefficiency but adequate retention and recognition. The pattern is more consistent with reduced attention or retrieval efficiency than rapid forgetting.

**Visual Memory:**  
WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate and delayed recall were Average (scaled scores=9 and 10). ROCF delayed recall was Low Average (T=44), with retention proportional to initial learning. There was no clear evidence of a selective visual memory deficit or strongly lateralizing right temporal pattern.

**Executive Function:**  
Trail Making Test Part B was Low Average (T=40), with no set-loss errors. Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T=41). On the WCST, the patient completed five categories; perseverative responses were Low Average (T=43). Findings indicate mild inefficiency in cognitive flexibility and inhibitory control, without frank executive impairment.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Block Design was Average (scaled score=10). ROCF copy was within normal limits (31/36), although organization was mildly piecemeal. No visuospatial neglect, constructional apraxia, or clear lateralized deficit was observed.

**Motor Function:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average with the dominant/right hand (T=43) and Average with the nondominant/left hand (T=46). No clinically meaningful lateralized fine-motor discrepancy was identified.

**Mood/Personality:**  
Self-report measures indicated minimal depressive symptoms (BDI-II=8; PHQ-9=4) and minimal anxiety symptoms (BAI=6). Mood symptoms are unlikely to account for the cognitive profile.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
The evaluation was valid and demonstrated broadly Average intellectual, language, visuospatial, and memory abilities. Relative weaknesses were observed in processing speed, working memory, verbal fluency, and aspects of executive functioning. Memory retention was intact across verbal and visual modalities, and confrontation naming was preserved. The profile is mildly inefficient but not amnestic and does not provide a compelling lateralizing pattern.

Mild cognitive slowing may be multifactorial, including frequent seizures and cumulative ASM burden. This is particularly relevant given four current ASMs and prior discontinuation of brivaracetam for cognitive side effects and topiramate for sedation. The generalized epilepsy diagnosis and myoclonic semiology are not straightforwardly concordant with the reported focal cortical dysplasia. Atypical absence remains possible. Localization and causality of the MRI lesion should therefore be clarified before resective planning.

From a cognitive standpoint, site-specific surgical risk cannot be estimated without the location of the dysplasia, EEG localization, and language-dominance data. Resection involving dominant perisylvian or mesial temporal regions could pose risk to naming and verbal memory despite currently preserved performance. Nondominant posterior or temporal intervention could affect visuospatial or visual memory abilities. Current findings provide a useful baseline.

**Recommendations:**

1. Discuss the clinical, imaging, EEG, and neuropsychological findings at PRC, with particular attention to the apparent generalized-versus-focal discordance.
2. Obtain task-based fMRI for language lateralization and mapping if the proposed intervention approaches language-relevant cortex.
3. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant with other localization data.
4. If resection is not appropriate because of generalized or nonlocalizing seizure onset, PRC may consider neuromodulation options. The NeuroPace RNS System would require identification of an appropriate treatment target.
5. Review the ASM regimen for cumulative cognitive effects while maintaining seizure safety.
6. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6 and 12 months s/p surgical or neuromodulation intervention, or sooner if there is a significant cognitive change.
