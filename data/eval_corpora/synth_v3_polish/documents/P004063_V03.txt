Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  H/o structural focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, onset at age 7. Prior to effective therapy, seizure frequency was approximately 6/week. MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis. Currently drug responsive on lamotrigine. Routine EEG to evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Technique:  A  thirty-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Relevant Medications:  Lamotrigine, current ASM. Previously trialed carbamazepine, discontinued due to rash.

Description:

Background:  The awake background was continuous and demonstrated appropriate anterior-posterior organization. A symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No generalized background slowing was identified.

Awake State:  During wakefulness, there were occasional brief runs of polymorphic 2-4 Hz delta and 4-6 Hz theta slowing over the left temporal region, maximal at F7-T7, without evolution. At times this slowing was sharply contoured but remained nonrhythmic.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and increased diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Epileptiform discharges were more frequent during drowsiness and sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Mild symmetric buildup occurred without activation of epileptiform activity. No electrographic seizure was induced.

Photic Stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings:  Occasional medium-amplitude sharp waves were present over the left anterior-to-mid temporal region, maximal at F7 and T7, with a field extending to FP1 and P7. These discharges occurred singly, predominantly during drowsiness and N2 sleep. Intermittent focal slowing was also present in the same region. No independent right temporal IEDs, generalized spike-wave discharges, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA were identified.

Ictal Events:  No clinical events or electrographic seizures were captured. No focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizure occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional left anterior-to-mid temporal sharp waves, indicating increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region.

2. Intermittent left temporal polymorphic delta/theta slowing, consistent with focal cerebral dysfunction in that region. In the setting of MRI-demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis and a hx of focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, the findings support a structural focal epilepsy, likely temporal in origin.

3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal or seizure-free sampling interval would not exclude epilepsy; clinical and imaging correlation is recommended. The patient’s epilepsy is currently described as drug responsive on lamotrigine, despite a prior baseline frequency of approximately 6 seizures/week.
