Indication: H/o structural focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 10; s/p MRI showing hippocampal sclerosis. EEG obtained to r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: The patient has focal epilepsy of structural etiology, with reported seizure frequency of approximately 2/year. Seizures may progress to bilateral tonic-clonic activity. The epilepsy has been drug responsive on current brivaracetam therapy. The patient denies tongue biting and has no h/o CNS infection. Family hx is notable for stroke in the patient’s mother. The focal MRI lesion, consisting of hippocampal sclerosis, is likely epileptogenic.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The record was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

- **Background:** The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with an 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening. An expected anterior-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was maximal over the frontocentral regions.

- **Awake state:** No persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing was identified. There was no generalized background slowing.

- **Drowsy/sleep:** Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform activation occurred during sleep.

- **Hyperventilation:** HV produced mild, symmetric buildup of theta activity without focal abnormality or epileptiform discharges.

- **Photic stimulation:** PS at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced a symmetric photic driving response, best seen over O1/O2. No photoparoxysmal response was present.

- **Interictal findings:** No definite focal or generalized IEDs were seen. Specifically, there were no temporal sharp waves, spikes, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No persistent focal slowing was identified.

- **Ictal events:** No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG showed a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal awake and sleep routine EEG. There are no focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities and no electrographic seizures.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy. The clinical semiology and hippocampal sclerosis remain consistent with a structural focal epilepsy syndrome, and the MRI lesion is probably epileptogenic. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended.
3. Continue current brivaracetam management through the treating neurologist. If intolerable adverse effects arise, cross-titration to lacosamide may be considered. No medication change should be based solely on this recording.
