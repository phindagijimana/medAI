Indication:  Characterization of recurrent convulsive seizures and r/o focal onset. Seizures began at age 34 and have been described as generalized tonic-clonic (GTC), previously occurring approximately 3/month. Outside evaluation raised concern for likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy given a structural MRI abnormality, although the clinical course has been drug responsive on valproate. MRI reportedly demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis, with appearance consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis. Family hx notable for a maternal aunt with febrile seizures.

AED Changes During Admission:  Home valproate was held after the morning dose on hospital day 1 to facilitate event capture and resumed BID following the second recorded seizure. No other ASM was administered chronically during monitoring.

Technique:  Continuous digital video EEG was recorded using the international 10-20 system with additional anterior temporal electrodes. EEG and synchronized video were reviewed daily. The patient was awake, drowsy, and asleep during the recording. In NAD between events.

Background:  Awake background was continuous and symmetric, composed of predominantly alpha and beta frequencies with a well-formed 9-Hz PDR reactive to eye opening. Drowsiness produced attenuation of the PDR and diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Stage N3 and REM sleep were recorded. No persistent focal slowing was identified.

Interictal Findings:

1. Occasional generalized 3.5- to 4.5-Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, bifrontally predominant at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, lasting 0.5-2 seconds without clinical correlate. Discharges became frequent during drowsiness and sleep.

2. Rare fragments of generalized epileptiform activity showed shifting hemispheric emphasis but no consistent focal maximum.

3. No definite independent temporal IEDs, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. Specifically, no reproducible epileptiform abnormality was seen over F7/T7 or F8/T8 despite the reported hippocampal sclerosis.

Ictal Events:

Total events captured:  2 electroclinical generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

Event 1 (Day 2, 03:14):  
Behavioral: Abrupt arousal from sleep, bilateral arm elevation and tonic extension, followed by symmetric generalized clonic activity. No consistent versive head movement or unilateral dystonic posture. Duration 78 seconds.  
EEG: Sudden generalized low-voltage fast activity followed by diffuse rhythmic polyspike activity, maximal bifrontally, evolving to generalized 3- to 4-Hz spike-and-wave and then 1- to 2-Hz rhythmic delta before abrupt cessation. No focal lead-in.  
Post-ictal: Generalized suppression for approximately 18 seconds, followed by diffuse delta slowing and confusion.

Event 2 (Day 3, 10:26):  
Behavioral: Behavioral arrest followed by symmetric tonic stiffening and generalized clonic jerking. Duration 64 seconds.  
EEG: Generalized bifrontally predominant polyspike onset with evolution similar to Event 1. No lateralizing electrographic feature.  
Post-ictal: Diffuse attenuation and generalized slowing for approximately 7 minutes.

Impression:

1. Abnormal long-term video EEG due to generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges and two recorded generalized tonic-clonic seizures with generalized electrographic onset. Findings support generalized epilepsy.

2. No electrographic evidence of a mesial temporal seizure onset was identified. The reported hippocampal sclerosis, likely consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis structurally, may be incidental; clinical and imaging correlation is advised.

3. Although the referral concern was for likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy, this recording instead supports a valproate-responsive generalized epilepsy phenotype.
