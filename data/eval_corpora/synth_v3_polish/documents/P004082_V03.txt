Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Patient with h/o drug-resistant combined generalized and focal epilepsy, including GTC seizures beginning at age 8 and currently occurring approximately 7/week. Genetic etiology associated with a pathogenic SCN1A c.5734C>T variant. EEG obtained to assess interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Current ASMs: Carbamazepine, valproate, lamotrigine, and lacosamide. Previously trialed topiramate, discontinued due to mood side effects, and oxcarbazepine, discontinued due to GI intolerance. No family history of epilepsy.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable technical limits. The recording included wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz PDR over the bilateral posterior head regions, reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state: Occasional generalized bursts of high-amplitude 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity were present, maximal bifrontally at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, lasting 0.5-2 seconds. There was no definite clinical correlate on video. In addition, independent sharp waves occurred occasionally over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, and rarely over the right anterior temporal region, maximal at F8/T8.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. The generalized epileptiform discharges increased modestly during sleep. Independent left greater than right anterior temporal sharp waves were also more apparent during sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced physiologic generalized theta slowing. There was a brief increase in generalized spike-and-wave discharge frequency, without an electroclinical seizure.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings:
1. Occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges with bifrontal predominance.
2. Independent left greater than right anterior temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/T7 and F8/T8, respectively.
3. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing.

Ictal events: No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No habitual GTC event occurred during this recording.

Impression:
1. Abnormal routine EEG due to generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges, indicating an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures.
2. Independent left greater than right anterior temporal epileptiform discharges indicate additional focal epileptogenic potential.
3. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support the established diagnosis of combined generalized and focal epilepsy. The patient’s epilepsy is drug resistant despite current treatment with four ASMs and is associated with a genetic etiology due to the SCN1A c.5734C>T variant.
4. No seizures were recorded during this study.
