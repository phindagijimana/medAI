Assessment / Impression

This is a patient with drug-resistant epilepsy characterized by frequent myoclonic seizures, with onset at age 12 and a current frequency of approximately 6 events per week. The epilepsy has been classified as generalized. Atypical absence seizures remain possible and cannot be excluded based on the information currently available.

The patient continues to have seizures despite a four-drug regimen consisting of lacosamide, carbamazepine, lamotrigine, and perampanel, with prior unsuccessful trials of brivaracetam, oxcarbazepine, and topiramate. The prior agents were discontinued for cognitive side effects, inadequate seizure control, and sedation, respectively. This treatment hx is consistent with DRE.

MRI reportedly demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia, making the etiology structural. The relationship between this lesion and the generalized/myoclonic epilepsy phenotype is not yet established. Further evaluation in the EMU is appropriate to confirm seizure type, r/o atypical absence, and determine whether the focal cortical dysplasia has electroclinical relevance.

If the dysplasia proves concordant with seizure onset, a formal epilepsy surgery evaluation may be appropriate. If a resective approach is not supported by the complete presurgical data, neuromodulation could be considered, including the LivaNova VNS SenTiva system. Selection of any surgical or device-based therapy should follow electroclinical characterization and multidisciplinary epilepsy conference review.
