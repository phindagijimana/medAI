Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: H/o generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 8 years, currently occurring approximately 2/month. Referral materials characterize the epilepsy as drug-responsive. Current ASMs are zonisamide and valproate. EEG requested to r/o ongoing interictal epileptiform activity and further characterize the epilepsy syndrome.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single electrocardiographic channel. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable technical limits. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness and drowsiness, with brief stage N2 sleep achieved. Video was recorded concurrently.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized. A well-formed 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude frontocentral beta activity was present. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, there were occasional generalized bursts of high-amplitude 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity lasting 1-3 seconds. These discharges were frontally predominant, maximal over Fp1/Fp2, F3/F4, and Fz, with synchronous bilateral expression and no consistent focal lead-in. No clinical change was apparent on video during these brief bursts.

Drowsy / sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. Generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges became more frequent during drowsiness and light sleep, at times occurring in brief runs lasting up to 4 seconds. No definite behavioral or motor correlate accompanied these runs.

Hyperventilation: HV produced expected diffuse background slowing and increased the frequency of generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave bursts. No electrographic seizure was provoked.

Photic stimulation: Photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No definite photoparoxysmal response was identified.

Interictal findings: Occasional to frequent generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness, sleep, and hyperventilation. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs were seen.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness, sleep, and HV.
2. These findings are supportive of a generalized epilepsy and are concordant with the reported hx of generalized tonic-clonic seizures. No focal epileptiform abnormality was identified.
3. No electrographic or electroclinical seizure occurred during this recording. A normal background does not otherwise exclude breakthrough seizures; clinical correlation with the reported frequency of approximately two GTC seizures/month and the current zonisamide/valproate regimen is recommended.
