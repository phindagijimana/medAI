Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and focal cerebral dysfunction.

Clinical History: Patient with h/o focal epilepsy beginning at age 11 years. Typical events progress to bilateral tonic-clonic activity without a preceding aura and currently occur approximately once monthly. Etiology remains unknown. Epilepsy has been considered drug-responsive on the current regimen of lacosamide and perampanel; home doses were not provided. Previously trialed valproate, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control. No h/o febrile seizures in childhood. Patient denies a family hx of epilepsy, although a brother reportedly had febrile seizures without known epilepsy.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The recording was obtained during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The study was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm of 30-60 µV that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen frontocentrally. No persistent focal asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: The waking background was continuous and composed predominantly of alpha activity with admixed low-amplitude beta and theta frequencies. Reactivity was preserved. No focal delta slowing, rhythmic temporal activity, or attenuation was present.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, slow roving eye movements, and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was achieved, with symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform abnormalities were activated by sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation.

Photic Stimulation: PS at sequential flash frequencies produced a symmetric photic driving response, maximal over O1/O2. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings: No focal or generalized spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs were identified. No focal slowing was present.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No push-button events occurred.

ECG: Single-lead tracing demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. If diagnostic uncertainty persists or habitual events remain incompletely characterized, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may be considered to r/o an electroclinical correlate.
