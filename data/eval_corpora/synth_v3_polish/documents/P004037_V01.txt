Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures beginning at age 10. The reported etiology is genetic, associated with a causative DEPDC5 c.4396C>T variant. There is also a family hx of generalized epilepsy of childhood in a paternal cousin.

The patient is currently seizure-free on perampanel, with a reported frequency of 0 seizures per month. Based on the available treatment hx, including discontinuation of levetiracetam for sedation rather than lack of efficacy, the epilepsy is best classified at present as drug responsive. Prior records apparently raised the probable impression of likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy, but the available information does not establish ongoing DRE while the patient remains seizure-free on the current ASM.

The MRI information is not fully concordant. The formal summary describes nonspecific white matter changes, while another hedged interpretation was “consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis.” MTS is therefore not confirmed. Direct review of the MRI, and correlation with any prior EEG data, would be needed before assigning a structural temporal focus.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Fycompa (perampanel) as the current ASM, given the reported seizure freedom and drug-responsive course. The patient or referring office should confirm the prescribed dose and frequency so that the medication list can be reconciled accurately.

2. Keppra (levetiracetam) should remain listed as a prior medication, discontinued because of sedation, and not as an active ASM.

3. Please forward the original brain MRI images to xxx Medical Center for independent review. The specific question is whether there is convincing evidence of MTS versus only nonspecific white matter changes.

4. Please send any prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, EMU, or LTM reports. No EEG localization or IED data were available today.

5. No presurgical epilepsy evaluation is indicated on the currently available information because the patient is seizure-free and appears drug responsive. The prior phrase “likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy” should be interpreted cautiously unless additional treatment failures or recurrent seizures are documented.

6. Continue longitudinal follow-up for focal epilepsy and the DEPDC5-associated genetic etiology. Medication changes are not recommended solely on the basis of the nonspecific MRI findings while seizure control remains stable.
