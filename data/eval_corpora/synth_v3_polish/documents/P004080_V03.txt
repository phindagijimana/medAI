Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: 33-year-old woman with onset of generalized epilepsy at age 33, characterized clinically by typical absence seizures occurring approximately 3/month. Etiology categorized as metabolic. Study obtained to assess interictal burden and r/o subclinical seizures. Epilepsy has been drug responsive; current ASM is lacosamide.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. The record was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy; brief stage N1 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: In the awake state, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity, reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. No persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing was identified.

Awake state: During wakefulness, occasional generalized bursts of high-amplitude 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were seen. These were bifrontally predominant, symmetric, and synchronous, lasting 1-3 seconds. No consistent behavioral interruption was apparent on video during these brief bursts.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of generalized theta activity. Brief stage N1 sleep showed vertex transients. Generalized spike-and-wave discharges remained present and were mildly accentuated by drowsiness. Deeper sleep was not recorded.

Hyperventilation: HV produced physiologic generalized theta slowing and activated several 2-4 second bursts of generalized 3 Hz spike-and-wave activity. No definite clinical absence seizure was identified.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, increased during HV and drowsiness. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or lateralized slowing.

Ictal events: No electroclinical seizures captured. No patient-reported events occurred during the recording.

Clinical Context: H/o typical absence seizures with a reported frequency of approximately three per month. Current therapy is lacosamide; the epilepsy is considered drug responsive. Prior brain MRI reportedly demonstrated nonspecific white matter changes without a definite focal epileptogenic lesion. Patient remained In NAD throughout the study.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with activation by hyperventilation and drowsiness. These findings support an increased generalized seizure propensity and are consistent with the patient’s hx of generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures.
2. No focal slowing or focal epileptiform abnormality. No electroclinical seizure was recorded.
3. Clinical correlation is advised in the setting of a reported metabolic etiology and nonspecific white matter changes on MRI. If she develops another breakthrough seizure, escalate to long-term video EEG monitoring for event characterization and quantification.
