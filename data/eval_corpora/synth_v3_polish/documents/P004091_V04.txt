**Referral Question:**  
Diagnostic/presurgical neuropsychological baseline for PRC review. Evaluate cognitive functioning, including memory and language abilities, and assess for evidence of lateralized cerebral dysfunction relevant to potential epilepsy intervention.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 6. Current seizure frequency was reported as approximately two events per week. The patient denies tongue biting. Epilepsy has been classified as structural in etiology and drug-responsive by the treating team. Current antiseizure medication is levetiracetam; dosage was not available for review.

Available MRI was formally interpreted as normal. However, separate clinical review reportedly raised a probable finding consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis; laterality was not specified in the available referral information. Thus, the relationship between the generalized seizure phenotype and the possible mesial temporal abnormality remains uncertain. Available records did not provide definitive lateralizing EEG findings. Further electroclinical correlation is needed to r/o a focal onset with rapid bilateral propagation.

Psychiatric, developmental, educational, and occupational histories were not sufficiently detailed in the referral materials. No prior neuropsychological evaluation was available for comparison.

**Behavioral Observations:**

**Appearance and Cooperation:**  
Arrived appropriately dressed and groomed. In NAD. Alert and oriented. Cooperative throughout the interview and testing.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent, grammatical, and normally articulated. No evident paraphasic errors. Auditory comprehension adequate for multistep instructions.

**Mood and Affect:**  
Mood described as generally stable. Affect appropriate and congruent. No behavioral evidence of severe depression, anxiety, psychosis, or acute emotional distress.

**Effort and Engagement:**  
Task persistence and frustration tolerance adequate. Test of Memory Malingering Trial 2 = 50/50. Reliable Digit Span = 9. Performance validity findings were within acceptable limits, indicating adequate engagement. Results are considered a valid estimate of current cognitive functioning.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; Trail Making Test Parts A and B; Boston Naming Test; COWAT phonemic and semantic fluency; CVLT-3; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test; Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; Beck Depression Inventory-II; Beck Anxiety Inventory; Test of Memory Malingering.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was in the Average range (FSIQ = 96, 39th percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 98, 45th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 100, 50th percentile). Working Memory fell in the Low Average range (WMI = 89, 23rd percentile), while Processing Speed was Average (PSI = 97, 42nd percentile). The profile did not show a clinically meaningful verbal–nonverbal discrepancy.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Basic auditory attention was Average (Digit Span scaled score = 9). Mental manipulation was mildly less efficient but remained within the Low Average range. Trail Making A was Average (T = 48) without errors. WAIS-IV Coding and Symbol Search were Average (scaled scores = 9 and 10, respectively). Overall, attention and processing speed were broadly intact, with mild variability under increased working-memory demand.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming on the Boston Naming Test was Low Average (T = 42), with benefit from phonemic cues. Phonemic verbal fluency was Average (T = 47), and animal fluency was Low Average (T = 43). Vocabulary and verbal abstract reasoning were Average. These findings suggest mild lexical retrieval inefficiency but do not establish dominant temporal dysfunction. No clear aphasic pattern was identified.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, acquisition across five learning trials was Low Average (Trials 1–5 total, T = 42). Learning improved with repetition, although organization was somewhat inefficient. Short-delay free recall was Low Average (scaled score = 8), and long-delay free recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7). Recognition discriminability was Average (scaled score = 9), indicating that reduced spontaneous retrieval exceeded any loss of stored information.

WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 9), with Low Average delayed recall (scaled score = 8) and intact recognition. The verbal memory profile reflects mild retrieval and learning inefficiency rather than rapid forgetting or a clear amnestic syndrome.

**Visual Memory:**  
ROCF copy was intact and well organized (32/36). Immediate recall was Average (T = 47), and delayed recall remained Average (T = 46). WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate and delayed recall were Average (scaled scores = 10 and 9, respectively), with intact recognition. There was no compelling evidence of disproportionate nonverbal memory impairment.

**Executive Function:**  
Trail Making B was Low Average (T = 42), with one self-corrected sequencing error. Stroop inhibition was Average (T = 46). On the WCST, the patient completed five categories with broadly Average conceptual reasoning; perseverative responses were Low Average (T = 43). Findings indicate mild variability in cognitive flexibility but no major executive dysfunction.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10). ROCF copy and spatial organization were intact. No evidence of visuospatial neglect, constructional apraxia, or clinically significant perceptual impairment was observed.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average bilaterally, without a meaningful lateralized difference. Fine-motor speed did not indicate focal motor dysfunction.

**Mood/Personality:**  
BDI-II and BAI scores were within the minimal range. Self-report did not suggest clinically significant current depression or anxiety. Continued monitoring is appropriate given the psychosocial burden associated with recurrent seizures and possible surgical evaluation.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
This valid evaluation demonstrates broadly Average general intellectual functioning. Relative strengths include visuospatial construction, visual learning and memory, recognition memory, processing speed, and basic reasoning. Mild weaknesses were observed in working memory, confrontation naming, verbal fluency, verbal list-learning efficiency, delayed free recall, and cognitive flexibility. Verbal recognition was preserved, supporting a retrieval-based inefficiency rather than a primary storage deficit.

The profile is not strongly lateralizing. Mild verbal memory and naming weaknesses could be compatible with subtle dominant temporal network inefficiency, but they are nonspecific and do not independently confirm mesial temporal dysfunction. Visual memory was intact. The cognitive findings should be interpreted alongside the generalized tonic-clonic phenotype, normal formal MRI interpretation, and the separately reported probable finding consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis. Additional clarification of seizure onset and MRI laterality is essential before attributing the pattern to a focal temporal process.

Should a dominant temporal resection be considered, there may be mild-to-moderate risk for decline in verbal learning and naming, as these abilities are currently somewhat vulnerable but not frankly impaired. The present data do not support surgical lateralization in isolation.

**Recommendations:**

1. Discuss findings at PRC with integrated review of semiology, prolonged video-EEG, and epilepsy-protocol MRI.
2. Obtain fMRI language mapping if temporal surgery is under consideration. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is equivocal or discordant.
3. Clarify whether the probable finding consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis represents a reproducible structural lesion and determine laterality.
4. Given the generalized epilepsy classification, confirm focal seizure onset before considering resection or a focal neuromodulation strategy such as the NeuroPace RNS System.
5. Use external memory supports, including written schedules, alarms, repetition, and structured organization of new information.
6. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation 6–12 months s/p any surgical or neuromodulation intervention, or earlier if meaningful cognitive decline is reported.
