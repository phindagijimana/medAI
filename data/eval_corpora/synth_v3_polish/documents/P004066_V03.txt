Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: [DATE]

Indication: Hx of drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: Patient with focal epilepsy of clinically classified structural etiology, with seizure onset at age 29. Typical events involve impaired awareness and currently occur approximately 2 times/year despite treatment with multiple ASMs. Current ASMs are topiramate, lacosamide, and levetiracetam. Previously trialed lamotrigine, discontinued due to cognitive side effects; oxcarbazepine, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control; and valproate, discontinued due to mood side effects. Prior brain MRI was reported as normal.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. The study included wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS across a range of flash frequencies. Video was recorded concurrently.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz alpha activity of moderate amplitude, reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude frontocentral beta activity was seen bilaterally. No persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing was identified.

Awake State: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. Occasional bilateral temporal wicket waves were present during relaxed wakefulness and drowsiness, representing a benign variant. No definite sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA were seen.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was achieved, with symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep did not activate epileptiform discharges.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of HV.

Photic Stimulation: PS elicited symmetric photic driving, maximal over O1/O2, at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal Findings: No interictal epileptiform discharges were identified. There was no electrographic evidence of a focal cerebral disturbance during this recording.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the study.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform abnormality, or electrographic seizure was recorded.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy, particularly given the patient’s established h/o focal impaired-awareness seizures and ongoing events despite multiple appropriately selected ASMs. Clinical correlation is advised; prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient LTM may provide greater diagnostic yield if further electroclinical characterization or presurgical evaluation is required.
