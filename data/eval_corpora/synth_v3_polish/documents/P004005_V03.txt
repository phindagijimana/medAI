Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  H/o focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Clinical History:  Patient with seizure onset at age 61. Typical events are classified as focal impaired-awareness seizures, currently occurring approximately once per year. Etiology remains unknown. Epilepsy has been drug responsive. Current ASMs are perampanel and topiramate. Previously trialed lamotrigine, discontinued due to rash, and lacosamide, discontinued due to mood side effects. No history of epilepsy in the family. The referring medication contingency is that, if intolerable side effects arise, the patient may be cross-titrated to lacosamide; given the prior mood adverse effects, tolerability would need to be reassessed clinically.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Recording quality was adequate for interpretation, with intermittent myogenic and movement artifact not obscuring the underlying cerebral activity.

Description:

Background:  In the awake state, there was a well-formed, symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm of medium amplitude, reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. No persistent focal asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No focal delta slowing, rhythmic temporal delta activity, LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns were present.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No sleep-activated epileptiform discharges were seen.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline after completion.

Photic Stimulation:  PS at sequential flash frequencies produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings:  No spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, or focal slowing were identified. In particular, there was no definite epileptiform activity over the anterior temporal electrodes F7/F8, temporal electrodes T7/T8, or other sampled regions.

Ictal Events:  No clinical events, electrographic seizures, or subclinical ictal patterns were captured. The single-channel ECG showed a regular rhythm during sampled portions of the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states.  
2. No focal slowing or interictal epileptiform discharges were identified, and no seizures were recorded.  
3. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy. Clinical correlation remains appropriate given the established h/o drug-responsive focal impaired-awareness seizures.
