Indication: H/o genetic generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures beginning at age 55, reported frequency approximately one/week. Study obtained to assess interictal epileptiform activity and r/o an unrecognized focal onset. The epilepsy is attributed to a causative STXBP1 variant, c.902A>G. Current ASMs are levetiracetam and topiramate.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable technical limits. The recording included wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation (HV) and intermittent photic stimulation (PS). Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was in NAD throughout the study.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, consisting predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with an 9-Hz posterior dominant rhythm (PDR). The PDR was symmetric and reactive to eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was present. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, there were occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. These were bilaterally synchronous, maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, and occurred in brief bursts lasting less than 1 second without clinical change. No consistent focal lead-in was evident.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was achieved, with symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized epileptiform discharges were mildly activated by sleep and remained frontally predominant. No focal IEDs, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs were present.

Hyperventilation: HV produced expected diffuse theta slowing. A single brief burst of generalized spike-and-wave activity occurred without clinical correlate. No seizure was provoked.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response occurred.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, approximately 3–4 Hz within the longer fragments, with bifrontal predominance. No independent focal epileptiform abnormality was identified.

Ictal events: No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No habitual GTC event occurred. ECG channel showed a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, mildly activated by drowsiness and sleep. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support an increased seizure propensity and are consistent with generalized epilepsy.

2. No focal slowing, independent focal IEDs, or seizures were recorded. A normal ictal sample cannot exclude epilepsy, as no habitual event was captured.

Clinical correlation: The patient has generalized GTC seizures with onset at age 55 and a genetic etiology attributed to the causative STXBP1 c.902A>G variant. Clinical records describe seizures occurring approximately once weekly, although the epilepsy is considered drug responsive to the current levetiracetam and topiramate regimen. Continue ASM management through the treating neurologist. Should recurrence occur, revisit presurgical workup, including prolonged LTM/VEEG to characterize habitual events before consideration of an invasive or device-based therapy.
