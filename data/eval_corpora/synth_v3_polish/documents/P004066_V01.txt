Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy manifested by focal impaired-awareness seizures, with onset at age 29 and a current frequency of approximately 2 seizures per year. The epilepsy is documented as structural in etiology, although the available MRI report is normal and the specific structural basis is not apparent from the records sent to us.

The patient has persistent seizures despite ongoing treatment with topiramate, lacosamide, and levetiracetam, as well as prior trials of lamotrigine, oxcarbazepine, and valproate. This pattern is consistent with drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Given the failure of multiple ASMs, continued medication substitution alone has a diminishing likelihood of producing durable seizure freedom.

A comprehensive presurgical evaluation is reasonable even with a normal MRI. The immediate goal is to confirm the electroclinical diagnosis and characterize seizure onset with VEEG/LTM. Depending on those results, additional localization studies may be appropriate. Potential treatment pathways could include resective or ablative therapy if a single safe epileptogenic focus is identified. If resection is not feasible, neuromodulation options may include the NeuroPace RNS System, Medtronic DBS Percept, or LivaNova VNS SenTiva. Device selection would require completion of the presurgical workup and multidisciplinary epilepsy conference review.
