Assessment / Impression

The patient has focal epilepsy manifested by focal impaired awareness seizures, with onset at age 34 and unknown etiology. The epilepsy is drug-resistant based on the documented treatment course. The patient is presently seizure-free, reporting 0 seizures per month, while taking lamotrigine, brivaracetam, and lacosamide.

There is a possible PNES component. This is only suggestive based on the information provided and is not a confirmed diagnosis. Definitive distinction between epileptic seizures and PNES would require capture of a typical event with video-EEG when clinically feasible.

Although the patient carries a diagnosis of drug-resistant epilepsy, the currently reported seizure freedom does not by itself support immediate invasive evaluation. If disabling seizures recur despite confirmed adherence and an optimized ASM regimen, a comprehensive presurgical workup could be revisited. Depending on localization and the results of that evaluation, options could include resection or neuromodulation with the NeuroPace RNS System, Medtronic DBS Percept, or LivaNova VNS SenTiva. No specific procedure or device is recommended at this time because localization, imaging findings, and surgical eligibility have not yet been established.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current ASMs—lamotrigine, brivaracetam, and lacosamide—without a documented change today. The exact dose and frequency of each medication should be reconciled with the patient, pharmacy, and referring neurology records. The patient should not stop any ASM abruptly.

2. Obtain outside neurology notes, prior EEG or VEEG reports and tracings, brain MRI reports and images, and the complete ASM treatment hx. These records are needed to better define prior response, localization, and etiology.

3. If no adequate prior imaging is available, consider an epilepsy-protocol brain MRI to evaluate for a structural cause of focal epilepsy. Etiology is currently unknown.

4. If the patient has another typical breakthrough event, I would favor admission to the EMU for LTM/VEEG when feasible. The goal would be to capture the habitual event, clarify focal onset, and r/o a PNES component.

5. If recurrent disabling focal impaired awareness seizures are confirmed despite an optimized regimen, referral for a formal multidisciplinary epilepsy surgery evaluation would be appropriate. 6. Continue standard seizure precautions in accordance with NY requirements and applicable local regulations. Avoid unsupervised situations in which loss of awareness could result in serious injury.
