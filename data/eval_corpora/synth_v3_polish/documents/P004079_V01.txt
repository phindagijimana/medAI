Neurology Progress Note

Chief Complaint: Follow-up focal epilepsy

History of Present Illness:  
This is an 80-year-old man with h/o focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, onset at age 80. Epilepsy is considered structural in etiology based on MRI evidence of hippocampal sclerosis. A possible autoimmune contribution has also been raised, although this remains uncertain. He is currently treated with Vimpat (lacosamide) as monotherapy and has been drug responsive. He denies any hx of developmental delay.

His reported seizure frequency is approximately one seizure per year per seizure calendar. Specific interval seizure dates were not available for review today. No change to his maintenance lacosamide regimen is documented. He previously trialed Keppra (levetiracetam), which was discontinued because of rash and is not a current medication. Available information does not identify additional antiseizure medication trials.

He presents for routine follow-up and ongoing medication management. We reviewed the importance of maintaining a seizure diary and recording the date, duration, and recovery associated with any recurrent focal impaired-awareness event. Rescue precautions were reviewed, including the emergency plan for a seizure cluster. No additional quality-of-life concern was identified in the available interval history.

Assessment and Plan:

1. Focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, lateralization not specified, drug responsive  
   - Onset at age 80, with reported seizure burden of approximately one event per year.  
   - Continue Vimpat (lacosamide) at the established maintenance dose. No dose change was made based on the information available today.

   - Should he have a seizure cluster, use Nayzilam (midazolam) nasal rescue medication and call 911. Emergency evaluation is also appropriate for a prolonged seizure, repeated events without return to baseline, respiratory difficulty, or significant injury.

2. Structural epilepsy associated with hippocampal sclerosis  
   - MRI finding of hippocampal sclerosis supports a structural epilepsy etiology.  
   - Continue clinical surveillance. No presurgical evaluation is indicated at present given the drug-responsive phenotype and low reported seizure frequency.

3. Possible autoimmune contribution  
   - An autoimmune contribution remains possible but is not established. No additional autoimmune testing or treatment change is specified today. Reconsider further evaluation if there is a meaningful increase in seizures, new cognitive/behavioral symptoms, or other concerning neurologic change.

Follow-up: 6 months, sooner PRN for breakthrough seizures or medication concerns.
