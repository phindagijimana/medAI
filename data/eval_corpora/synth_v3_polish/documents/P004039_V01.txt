Assessment / Impression

This is an XX-year-old patient with epilepsy beginning at age 10. The documented seizure type is generalized tonic-clonic, with a current reported frequency of approximately 2 seizures per month. The referral diagnosis is combined generalized and focal epilepsy. The available MRI report demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis, supporting a structural etiology.

The patient is currently taking Topamax (topiramate), although the exact dose and frequency require confirmation. The epilepsy is described in the source documentation as drug-responsive. There is no basis in the currently available information to relabel the condition as drug-resistant epilepsy. In particular, only one prior ASM, levetiracetam, is documented; it was discontinued because of mood side effects rather than clearly documented inefficacy.

The combination of generalized tonic-clonic events, a combined generalized and focal classification, and structural hippocampal sclerosis warrants careful review of the original MRI and EEG data. Hippocampal sclerosis is a focal structural finding, but the supplied clinical history does not include sufficient semiologic or electrographic detail to determine how that finding relates to the generalized tonic-clonic seizures. We should therefore confirm the existing classification before making major changes to therapy or considering an EMU or surgical pathway.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Topamax (topiramate) at the patient’s established regimen for now. The patient should confirm the exact dose and frequency with the prescribing neurologist or pharmacy before any refill or dose adjustment is made.

2. Obtain prior neurology notes and the complete ASM treatment history, including prior topiramate doses, response over time, and any additional medications not captured in the current referral packet.

3. Request the prior EEG report and, when possible, the original EEG tracings for review. A routine EEG may be obtained if prior data cannot be located, with the goal of corroborating the combined generalized and focal classification and r/o an electrographic pattern that would alter medication selection.
