Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with genetic focal epilepsy associated with DEPDC5 c.4396C>T. Seizure onset occurred at age 37. The habitual events are classified as focal aware seizures and currently occur approximately five times per month. The patient denies post-ictal confusion.

The epilepsy is drug resistant, with ongoing seizures despite current treatment with levetiracetam, topiramate, perampanel, and carbamazepine, as well as prior trials of valproate and lacosamide. Valproate caused sedation, while lacosamide was stopped because of rash.

The leading diagnosis remains drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures. However, atypical absence is still possible and cannot be excluded on the information currently available. Capturing typical events during VEEG would help establish whether the electroclinical onset is focal or generalized and would guide further treatment.

Given the ongoing seizure burden despite multiple ASMs, the patient is appropriate for comprehensive drug-resistant epilepsy evaluation. This should include clarification of seizure onset and consideration of surgical or device-based therapies if a resective strategy is not appropriate.

1. Continue the current ASM regimen for now: levetiracetam, topiramate, perampanel, and carbamazepine. No dose changes can be recommended safely until the exact doses, frequencies, adherence, and prior titration history are reconciled.
