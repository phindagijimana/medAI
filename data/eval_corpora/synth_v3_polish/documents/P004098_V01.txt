Assessment / Impression

The patient has generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures, with onset at age 8 and a current frequency of approximately 3 seizures per month. The epilepsy is considered drug-responsive on Briviact (brivaracetam). A prior MRI reportedly demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia, supporting a structural etiology.

There is an important electroclinical question regarding the relationship between the focal cortical dysplasia and the generalized epilepsy phenotype. Focal cortical dysplasia is a focal structural abnormality, whereas typical absence seizures are generally associated with generalized epileptic networks. Review of the original MRI and EEG data is therefore important to confirm whether these represent concordant findings or whether the focal lesion is incidental to the generalized epilepsy classification. EEG correlation would also help r/o a focal epilepsy with rapid bilateral synchrony that could resemble a generalized pattern.

At present, the supplied clinical information identifies the epilepsy as drug-responsive rather than drug-resistant. There is insufficient information to support a presurgical evaluation, particularly without prior EEG data, medication dosing, or documentation of failed adequate ASM trials. The immediate priority is diagnostic confirmation and optimization of the current medical regimen.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Briviact (brivaracetam) without an empiric change today. The current dose and frequency should be confirmed with the patient, pharmacy, or referring office before any adjustment is made.

2. Obtain and review the prior MRI images demonstrating focal cortical dysplasia, if available. If the original images cannot be obtained or are not adequate for epilepsy-focused review, an epilepsy-protocol brain MRI may be considered through xxx Medical Center.

3. Obtain the prior EEG report and tracing, if available. If no adequate prior study exists, I recommend an EEG to assess for generalized IEDs and to r/o focal onset with rapid bilateral synchrony. Depending on the results and ongoing event frequency, prolonged ambulatory EEG, VEEG, or EMU/LTM evaluation may be considered.

4. Maintain a seizure log documenting the monthly frequency of typical absence seizures. This will help determine whether the present rate of approximately 3 per month remains stable and whether additional treatment adjustment is needed.

5. Review adherence and the exact brivaracetam regimen at follow-up. Because the epilepsy is described as drug-responsive, medication optimization should precede consideration of more invasive diagnostic or therapeutic options.
