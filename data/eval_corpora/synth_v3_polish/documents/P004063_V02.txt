1. **Structural focal epilepsy with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, lateralization not established in the available documentation; drug responsive but not seizure-free.**  
   Epilepsy began at age 7. MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis, supporting a structural etiology. She currently reports approximately 6 seizures per week despite treatment with lamotrigine. Although characterized as drug responsive, this remains a clinically significant seizure burden and requires close reassessment of medication dosing, adherence, and treatment response.
   - Continue lamotrigine for now; verify the exact dose and frequency from the home bottle, pharmacy fill history, and reconciled MAR before changing therapy.

   - Continue a detailed seizure calendar, including date, duration, possible provoking factors, recovery time, and missed doses.

   - Obtain/update prior MRI images and epilepsy records for direct review, with attention to the reported hippocampal sclerosis and any available lateralizing data.

Avoid driving and other activities in which sudden loss of awareness or convulsion could cause harm, including unsupervised swimming, bathing alone, heights, open flames, and operating heavy machinery. Use showers rather than baths when possible.
   - Call 911 for a convulsive seizure lasting 5 minutes or longer, repeated seizures without return to baseline, significant injury, breathing difficulty, or other acute concern. Contact clinic PRN for increased seizure frequency, medication intolerance, rash, or new neurologic symptoms.
