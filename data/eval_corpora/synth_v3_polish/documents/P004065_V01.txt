Assessment / Impression

This is an XX-year-old patient with focal epilepsy and focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 11. The reported seizure frequency remains high at approximately five per week. The patient specifically denies loss of consciousness.

The reported etiology is immune, with CASPR2 identified as the causative antibody. I would like to review the original antibody results and the prior immune evaluation to clarify the basis for this attribution and determine whether any additional records are relevant to current seizure management.

The referral describes the epilepsy as drug responsive on levetiracetam. At the same time, the continued weekly seizure burden and prior exposure to carbamazepine, brivaracetam, perampanel, lacosamide, and zonisamide are suggestive of likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy. I consider drug resistance probable, but confirmation requires review of the doses, duration, tolerability, adherence, and response associated with each prior ASM. Several prior therapies were stopped for adverse effects rather than clearly documented lack of efficacy.

Further characterization is also needed to confirm that the current events are epileptic and to define electrographic onset. LTM in the EMU would be useful to capture typical events and r/o an alternative event type, particularly before making major treatment changes or considering a formal presurgical pathway.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Keppra (levetiracetam) at the existing prescribed regimen for now. The patient should not change or discontinue the medication abruptly. The current dose and frequency need to be confirmed with the patient’s pharmacy or referring neurology records.

2. Obtain complete prior ASM records, including maximum doses, duration of treatment, adherence, efficacy, and reasons for discontinuation.
