Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of structural focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, onset at age 11 years. Reported seizure frequency approximately 4/month. Study requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Clinical History: The patient has a h/o focal epilepsy characterized by focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Urinary incontinence is denied with the habitual events. Epilepsy has been characterized as drug-responsive. Current ASMs are oxcarbazepine and perampanel. MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis, probably consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. In NAD during the recording. No medication changes were made for this study.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous and organized, with an 8.5-9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was symmetric and reactive to eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was preserved. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent diffuse background slowing.

Awake State: Intermittent polymorphic 3-6 Hz delta-theta slowing was present over the left temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, at times extending to P7. This activity was more apparent during drowsiness.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of generalized theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was identified by symmetric vertex waves and sleep spindles. Left temporal epileptiform discharges increased modestly during sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of additional abnormalities.

Photic Stimulation: PS elicited a symmetric photic driving response at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response occurred.

Interictal Findings: Rare to occasional sharp waves were seen independently over the left anterior-to-mid temporal region, maximal at F7 and T7, with a field to FT9 and P7. These discharges were more frequent in drowsiness and sleep. No definite right temporal IEDs, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs were identified.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No push-button events occurred. The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to rare to occasional left anterior-to-mid temporal epileptiform discharges, maximal at F7/T7. These findings indicate increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region and support the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy.

2. Intermittent left temporal polymorphic slowing, suggesting focal cerebral dysfunction in that region. In the setting of MRI-demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis, this is probably consistent with mesial temporal structural pathology.

3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal ictal sample was not obtained; therefore, this study does not further characterize the reported focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended.
