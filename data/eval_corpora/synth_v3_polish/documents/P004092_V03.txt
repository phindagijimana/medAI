Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures; r/o interictal epileptiform activity. Seizure onset at age 11 years, with reported frequency of approximately 4/month. Presumed structural etiology despite a reportedly normal brain MRI. Current ASM: carbamazepine. Epilepsy has been clinically drug responsive. No history of CNS infection.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background:  In the awake state, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state:  Patient awake, cooperative, and in NAD. There were occasional brief runs of polymorphic 4-6 Hz theta slowing over the left temporal region, maximal at F7-T7, without definite clinical correlate.

Drowsy/sleep:  With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity with slow lateral eye movements. Normal vertex transients were present. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of epileptiform discharges.

Photic stimulation:  PS at frequencies from 1-30 Hz elicited symmetric photic driving, best developed posteriorly. No photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Rare, medium-amplitude sharp waves were present independently over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7 with a field to T7 and FP1. These discharges were more apparent during drowsiness. No independent right-sided IEDs were identified. No TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or generalized spike-wave discharges were seen.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizure occurred during this recording.

ECG:  Regular rhythm throughout the sampled tracing.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and drowsy EEG due to rare left anterior temporal sharp waves and occasional left temporal theta slowing. The sharp waves indicate increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region; the slowing is consistent with mild focal cerebral dysfunction in the same region.

2. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support a focal epilepsy syndrome and are concordant with the reported hx of focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Absence of a captured seizure does not exclude epilepsy.

3. MRI is reportedly normal. The clinical h/o remains categorized as focal epilepsy of presumed structural etiology, recognizing that a conventional MRI may be nonlesional. If MRI shows progression, obtain PET/SPECT for concordance with the electroclinical localization.
