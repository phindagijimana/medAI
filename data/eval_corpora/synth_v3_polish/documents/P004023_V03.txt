Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: H/o structurally mediated focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, onset at age 75 and occurring approximately 4/week; r/o interictal epileptiform activity. MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved. Video was recorded throughout the study.

Relevant Clinical History/Medications: The patient has focal epilepsy attributed to a structural etiology, with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 75. Reported seizure frequency is approximately four per week. The epilepsy has been clinically drug responsive. Current ASMs are carbamazepine and valproate. Previously trialed perampanel was discontinued due to rash. The patient denies a family history of epilepsy.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized, with a symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was distributed symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: During maximal wakefulness, the background remained symmetric and reactive. No focal attenuation or sustained focal slowing was seen.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse theta activity, and slow rolling eye movements. Brief vertex transients were present. Definite stage N2 sleep architecture was not recorded.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal abnormality or epileptiform activation.

Photic Stimulation: PS at sequential frequencies produced a symmetric posterior driving response, best developed between 8 and 18 Hz. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings: No definite focal or generalized IEDs were identified. Specifically, there were no temporal sharp waves, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or persistent focal slowing. Occasional sharply contoured temporal transients lacked a consistent field or after-going slow wave and were not considered epileptiform.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG showed a regular rhythm during sampled portions of the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal awake and drowsy routine EEG. No definite focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities were identified.
2. No seizures were recorded.
3. A normal routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy, particularly in a patient with focal impaired-awareness seizures and a structural lesion such as hippocampal sclerosis. Clinical and imaging correlation is advised.
4. If follow-up MRI shows progression, consider PET/SPECT to assess concordance with the suspected epileptogenic region. Should seizure recurrence occur, revisit presurgical workup, including prolonged video-EEG monitoring as clinically appropriate.
