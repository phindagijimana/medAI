Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: H/o focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Clinical History: Seizure onset at age 24 years. Typical events are classified as focal impaired-awareness seizures and currently occur approximately 2 times per week. Epilepsy etiology remains unknown. The epilepsy has been characterized clinically as drug-responsive. Current ASMs are brivaracetam and perampanel; doses were not provided. Previously trialed lamotrigine, discontinued due to cognitive side effects, and carbamazepine, discontinued due to sedation.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including a single-channel ECG. Data were acquired with referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

- **Background:** The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradients. A well-defined 10 Hz PDR was present bilaterally and was reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. No persistent focal asymmetry was identified.

- **Awake state:** No focal slowing, diffuse slowing, or attenuation was seen. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs.

- **Drowsy/sleep:** Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was preserved.

- **Hyperventilation:** HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation. The response resolved promptly following cessation of the maneuver.

- **Photic stimulation:** PS produced symmetric photic driving at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

- **Interictal findings:** No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, or periodic discharges.

- **Ictal events:** None captured. No electrographic seizures or clinical events of concern were recorded.

- **ECG:** Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm during the sampled portions of the study.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy, as interictal epileptiform abnormalities may be intermittent and absent during a brief recording. Clinical correlation is recommended. If further electroclinical characterization of the patient’s recurrent focal impaired-awareness events is needed, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may be considered.
