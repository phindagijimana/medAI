Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  H/o structural focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 11; r/o interictal epileptiform activity. MRI reportedly demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Clinical History:  The patient has a hx of focal impaired-awareness seizures in the setting of a structural etiology, specifically hippocampal sclerosis. Seizures have been drug responsive, with no seizures reported during the past month (0/month). Current ASMs are levetiracetam and perampanel. Brivaracetam was previously trialed and discontinued because of cognitive side effects. The patient denies tongue biting. Some historical events are possibly suggestive of a PNES component; however, no habitual event was recorded during this study, and this possibility cannot be evaluated electrographically here. Patient cooperative and in NAD during electrode application.

Description:

Background:  The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-sustained 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing was identified.

Awake state:  Normal admixture of alpha and beta frequencies. Movement, eye blink, and myogenic artifact were intermittently present but did not materially limit interpretation.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, slow rolling eye movements, and diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Brief vertex transients were present. Definite sleep spindles and K complexes were not observed; sustained stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present over the posterior regions at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings:  No focal or generalized IEDs were identified. There were no temporal sharp waves, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic/rhythmic patterns.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No event marked as habitual occurred during the recording.

ECG:  Single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake and drowsy states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy. The clinical hx remains consistent with drug-responsive focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures and a structural etiology related to hippocampal sclerosis.
3. No habitual event was captured; therefore, the possible PNES component cannot be confirmed or excluded by this recording. If diagnostic uncertainty persists or events recur, prolonged video-EEG monitoring may be considered.
