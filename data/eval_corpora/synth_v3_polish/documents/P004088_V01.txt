History of Present Illness:  
The patient is a 34-year-old right-handed man with h/o seizures since age 10. Available history is most consistent with focal epilepsy. Habitual events have been described as focal impaired awareness seizures, with impaired responsiveness and automatisms, at times progressing to focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. When progression occurs, the event consists of bilateral tonic-clonic activity with possible lateral tongue biting followed by prolonged post-ictal confusion and dysphasia. Detailed lateralizing signs and duration are not consistently documented, which is one reason for the present diagnostic admission.

He continues to have approximately 3 seizures per month despite treatment with three concurrent ASMs. The current pre-wean regimen consists of Depakote (valproate), Briviact (brivaracetam), and Zonegran (zonisamide); exact home doses are being verified with the outpatient pharmacy and medication bottles. He has previously trialed Lamictal (lamotrigine), discontinued because of rash, and Trileptal (oxcarbazepine), discontinued because of GI intolerance. Given persistent seizures after adequate trials of multiple appropriate medications, his epilepsy meets criteria for drug-resistant epilepsy.

The underlying etiology remains unknown. Prior imaging has reportedly shown a mesial temporal abnormality that is likely/probably consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis, although the available referral information does not establish reliable lateralization or concordance with EEG. A possible autoimmune contribution has also been suspected, but this remains unconfirmed and cannot currently be considered the established cause of his epilepsy. Available records do not provide sufficient details regarding prior serum/CSF testing, prior ictal EEG localization, or PET/SPECT.

The outpatient contingency had been to escalate to LTM if another breakthrough seizure occurred. In the setting of continued events at approximately 3/month, he is now admitted for diagnostic capture. Goals include distinguishing epileptic seizures from other possible spell types, evaluating for interictal epileptiform discharges and ictal onset, and determining whether a temporal hypothesis is supported. This is not currently designated as a definitive presurgical admission, though localization data may inform future PRC review if focal drug-resistant epilepsy is confirmed.
