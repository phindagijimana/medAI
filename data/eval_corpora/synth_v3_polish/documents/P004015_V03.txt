Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Patient with hx of drug-resistant epilepsy beginning at age 15, characterized primarily by generalized tonic-clonic (GTC) seizures occurring approximately 7/week. Suspected immune-mediated etiology associated with GAD65 antibody. Routine EEG obtained to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o focal features. Clinical events are possibly focal onset seizures despite the generalized convulsive semiology.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Current ASMs include lamotrigine, carbamazepine, and lacosamide. Brivaracetam was previously trialed and discontinued due to inadequate seizure control.

Description:

Background:  The awake background was continuous and symmetric, with appropriate anterior-to-posterior organization. A 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present over O1/O2 and attenuated normally with eye opening. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  Occasional brief bursts of frontally predominant, generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were seen, typically at 3–4 Hz and lasting 0.5–2 seconds. These were maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, without a reproducible unilateral lead-in. At times, fragmented discharges demonstrated shifting left or right frontal predominance; however, no consistently localized field was identified. There was no associated definite clinical change.

Drowsy/sleep:  With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized epileptiform discharges became more frequent in sleep, including occasional isolated generalized polyspikes. No focal temporal sharp waves, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs were identified.

Hyperventilation:  Mild symmetric theta slowing was elicited. There was a modest increase in generalized spike-and-wave activity, without electrographic seizure or clinical correlate.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was produced.

Interictal findings:  Occasional sleep-activated, frontally predominant generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. Some discharges had shifting asymmetry, but there was no consistent focal onset.

Ictal events:  No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured. No habitual GTC event occurred during this routine recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, accentuated by sleep and hyperventilation. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support an increased epileptic potential with a generalized electrographic pattern.

2. The shifting frontal asymmetry of fragmented discharges is not definitively localizing. Given the reported semiology, possibly focal onset seizures with rapid bilateral synchrony cannot be excluded. Correlation with prolonged video EEG monitoring would be helpful if clarification of seizure onset is needed.

3. No electrographic seizure was recorded. A normal background and absence of seizures during this study do not exclude the clinical diagnosis of drug-resistant epilepsy. The reported immune etiology related to GAD65 antibody is a clinical determination and cannot be established by EEG alone.
