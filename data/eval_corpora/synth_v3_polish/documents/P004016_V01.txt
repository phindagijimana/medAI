Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy manifested by focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, with onset at age 11. Events currently occur approximately once per month. There is no reported aura. Etiology is unknown based on the data available today.

The patient is currently taking lacosamide and perampanel. Prior valproate was stopped because of inadequate seizure control. The supplied phenotype classifies the epilepsy as drug-responsive. At the same time, the reported monthly seizure frequency indicates ongoing active seizures and warrants careful confirmation of medication adherence, current doses, treatment response, and the adequacy of prior ASM trials. The available information is not sufficient to reconstruct the complete prior medication course or determine the degree of response to each current agent.

There is no personal hx of childhood febrile seizures and no family hx of epilepsy. The brother’s hx of febrile seizures is noted but does not establish familial epilepsy.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Vimpat (lacosamide) and Fycompa (perampanel) at the presently prescribed regimens pending confirmation of the exact doses and frequencies. The patient should not stop either ASM abruptly.

2. Complete medication reconciliation, including tablet strengths, BID or other dosing schedule, duration of therapy, adherence, tolerability, and any missed doses. No dose changes can be recommended safely from the referral information alone.

3. Request prior neurology notes and any available EEG, VEEG/LTM, MRI brain, and relevant laboratory results for direct review. The current etiology remains unknown, and these records will help determine whether further diagnostic testing is needed.

4. Maintain a seizure diary documenting the date and type of each event. Because there is no aura, the patient may not receive a reliable warning before progression to a bilateral tonic-clonic seizure.

5. Continue standard seizure precautions. The patient should avoid driving unless permitted under applicable NY requirements and cleared by the treating clinician, and should avoid unsupervised swimming, bathing in a tub, climbing at heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery.

6. Reinforce consistent ASM administration. Medication doses should not be missed or changed without discussion with the prescribing neurologist.

7. Follow up after outside records and the complete medication list have been obtained. At that visit, we will reassess monthly seizure frequency, response to lacosamide and perampanel, and whether additional diagnostic clarification or treatment adjustment is appropriate.
