Neurology Progress Note

Chief Complaint: Follow-up focal epilepsy

History of Present Illness:  
This is a 42-year-old man with focal epilepsy beginning at age 25, characterized by focal impaired-awareness seizures. Etiology is presumed structural in the setting of a vascular malformation on MRI. Prior imaging has also been described as probably consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis. He remains on dual therapy with Keppra (levetiracetam) and Trileptal (oxcarbazepine). His epilepsy has shown medication responsiveness, although he has not achieved complete seizure freedom.

Since the last visit, his seizure diary continues to show approximately five focal impaired-awareness seizures per week. Events remain similar to his established seizure type, with impaired awareness and incomplete recall. No new seizure semiology is reported. There have been no documented interval ASM changes, ED visits, or hospitalizations. He reports taking levetiracetam and oxcarbazepine as prescribed. Previously trialed perampanel, which was discontinued due to sedation.

His primary concern remains the ongoing seizure frequency and its effect on day-to-day safety and independence. We reviewed the importance of maintaining an accurate seizure calendar and documenting missed medication doses, illness, sleep disruption, or other possible associations with breakthrough events. No additional interval concerns were raised today.

1. **Focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, structural etiology, without established lateralization; drug responsive but not seizure-free.**

MRI demonstrates a vascular malformation, supporting a structural etiology; there is also a probable finding consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis. The relationship between these imaging abnormalities and seizure onset remains to be clarified. Despite persistent events, his course has been medication responsive and is not currently classified as drug-resistant.

   - Continue Trileptal (oxcarbazepine) 600 mg BID.

3. **Probable mesial temporal sclerosis on prior imaging.**  
   - Imaging description is probably consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis, though this remains a hedged finding rather than a definitive localization.  
   - Correlate with MRI review, EEG data, and clinical semiology before assigning lateralization.
