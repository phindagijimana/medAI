**Referral Question:**  
Presurgical neuropsychological evaluation to characterize current cognitive functioning, assess for evidence of cerebral lateralization/localization, and establish a baseline for discussion at the Patient Review Conference (PRC).

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o genetically mediated, drug-resistant focal epilepsy, with seizure onset at age 37. Current events are described as focal aware seizures occurring approximately five times per month. The patient denies post-ictal confusion. Although the reported semiology is most consistent with focal aware seizures, atypical absence cannot be excluded and remains a possible alternative characterization for at least some events.

Genetic evaluation identified a causative **DEPDC5 c.4396C>T variant**. Current antiseizure medications include levetiracetam, topiramate, perampanel, and carbamazepine; dosing information was not available for review. Previously trialed medications include valproate, discontinued due to sedation, and lacosamide, discontinued due to rash. The clinical course is consistent with drug-resistant epilepsy.

Prior neuroimaging and EEG findings were not provided with the referral materials; accordingly, the present cognitive findings cannot be compared directly with an independently established electroclinical focus. Family hx is notable for a sister with stroke. Information concerning psychiatric treatment, developmental history, educational attainment, occupational functioning, and allergies was not available in the supplied records.

**Behavioral Observations:**  

**Appearance and Cooperation:** Neatly groomed. Arrived as scheduled. In NAD. Alert and oriented. Cooperative throughout the interview and testing.

**Speech and Language:** Speech fluent and grammatical, with normal prosody. No gross receptive-language difficulty. Occasional word-finding pauses during more demanding tasks.

**Mood and Affect:** Mood described as generally stable. Affect appropriate and adequately modulated. No behavioral evidence of psychosis or marked emotional distress.

**Effort and Engagement:** Sustained task engagement. Frustration tolerance adequate. TOMM Trial 2 = 50/50, indicating valid performance. Embedded indices were also within acceptable limits. Results are considered a valid estimate of current cognitive functioning.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; California Verbal Learning Test–Third Edition (CVLT-3); Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure Test (ROCF); Boston Naming Test (BNT); COWAT phonemic and animal fluency; Trail Making Test Parts A and B; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (WCST); Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; Beck Depression Inventory–II (BDI-II); Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI); Test of Memory Malingering (TOMM).

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was 94 (34th percentile; Average). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI=98), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI=100). Working Memory fell in the Average range (WMI=92). Processing Speed was Low Average (PSI=84), representing a relative weakness compared with reasoning abilities.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Simple auditory attention and working memory were broadly Average (Digit Span scaled score=9). Trail Making Test Part A was Low Average (T=41). Timed visual scanning and graphomotor coding ranged from Low Average to Borderline. The pattern indicates reduced cognitive efficiency rather than a primary attentional disorder. Antiseizure polytherapy, particularly topiramate exposure, may contribute to slowed processing, though medication effects cannot be isolated from seizure burden.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Low Average (BNT=47/60; T=39), with benefit from phonemic cueing. Phonemic verbal fluency was Low Average (T=42), while semantic fluency was at the Borderline/Low Average boundary (T=38). Verbal abstraction remained Average. These findings suggest mild lexical retrieval inefficiency. Confrontation naming weakness can be associated with dominant temporal dysfunction, although the degree of impairment here is modest and is not independently localizing.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, initial acquisition was Low Average, with a shallow but positive learning curve across trials (Trials 1–5 composite z=-1.0). Short-delay free recall was Low Average (z=-1.0), and long-delay free recall was Borderline (z=-1.3). Recognition discrimination improved to the Average range, indicating that retrieval inefficiency contributed to reduced spontaneous recall. WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate and delayed recall were Low Average (scaled scores=8 and 7, respectively), with adequate recognition. Overall, verbal learning and free recall were mildly reduced, without evidence of rapid forgetting or a dense amnestic syndrome.

**Visual Memory:**  
WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate and delayed recall were Average (scaled scores=10 and 9). ROCF delayed recall was Average (T=50), with intact retention of the initially encoded figure. Visual recognition was also within normal limits. Visual memory was stronger than verbal memory.

**Executive Functioning:**  
Trail Making Test Part B was Low Average (T=41), without set-loss errors. WCST performance was Average, with five categories completed and no clinically significant perseverative tendency. Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T=43). Findings reflect mild slowing under executive demand but preserved concept formation, cognitive flexibility, and inhibitory control.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
Block Design was Average (scaled score=10). ROCF copy was intact, with organized construction and no evidence of spatial neglect or significant visuoconstructional impairment.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average bilaterally, without a meaningful lateralized discrepancy. No focal motor deficit was apparent during testing.

**Mood/Personality:**  
BDI-II and BAI scores fell in the minimal range. Self-report did not indicate a current major depressive or anxiety syndrome. Continued monitoring is warranted given the psychosocial burden associated with drug-resistant epilepsy and possible surgical treatment.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
This is a valid neuropsychological evaluation of an adult with DEPDC5-related, drug-resistant focal epilepsy beginning at age 37. The profile is characterized by Average general intellectual ability, intact visuospatial skills, preserved visual memory, and generally intact higher-order executive functioning. Relative weaknesses were observed in processing speed, lexical retrieval, semantic fluency, and verbal learning/free recall. Recognition performance was stronger than spontaneous recall, arguing against a severe storage deficit.

The relative weakness in naming and verbal memory, compared with intact visual memory, is suggestive of greater dominant-hemisphere temporal-system inefficiency. However, the findings are mild and are not sufficiently specific to establish seizure localization or language dominance. Interpretation should be integrated with video-EEG, MRI, PET/SPECT, and other available presurgical data. There is no strong neuropsychological evidence for broad bilateral cerebral dysfunction.

If a dominant temporal resection or ablation is contemplated, there may be a moderate risk of further decline in naming and verbal memory, particularly if the targeted tissue remains functionally intact. Conversely, baseline verbal-memory weakness may limit—but does not eliminate—the magnitude of postoperative decline. For poorly localized, multifocal, or eloquent-cortex onset, neuromodulation options such as the **NeuroPace RNS System** may be considered by the multidisciplinary epilepsy team.

**Recommendations:**  
1. Review these findings at PRC alongside EEG, imaging, genetic, and seizure-semiology data.  
2. Obtain task-based fMRI for language lateralization and memory-risk assessment if dominant temporal intervention is under consideration.  
3. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant with electroclinical findings.  
4. Continue medication review, given possible cognitive slowing and word-retrieval effects associated with the current multidrug regimen.  
5. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6–12 months s/p surgery, ablation, or neuromodulation to document cognitive outcome.  
6. Use external memory supports, including written schedules, alarms, medication organizers, and structured repetition of newly learned information.  
7. Continue diagnostic clarification of event type; r/o atypical absence through correlation of typical events with prolonged video-EEG monitoring.
