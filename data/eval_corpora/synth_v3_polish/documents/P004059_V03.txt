Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o electrographic seizures.

Clinical History: Patient with hx of focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 56, currently occurring approximately 5 times/month. Epilepsy etiology remains unknown and has been clinically drug responsive. Current ASM is lacosamide. Previously trialed perampanel, discontinued due to GI intolerance, and lamotrigine, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control. MRI brain demonstrated nonspecific white matter changes without a reported definite epileptogenic lesion. No h/o head trauma or febrile seizures in childhood.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined anterior-to-posterior gradient. A 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and was reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of lower-voltage beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal asymmetry.

Awake State: During maximal wakefulness, the background consisted predominantly of posterior alpha activity with intermixed low-amplitude theta and beta frequencies. No focal delta slowing was identified. No breach rhythm was present.

Drowsiness/Sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric 4-7 Hz theta activity. Slow roving eye movements were observed. Definite vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes were not recorded; therefore, stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation. No epileptiform discharges or electrographic seizures were provoked.

Photic Stimulation: Intermittent PS at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: No epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No focal or generalized pathologic slowing was present.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures were captured. No clinical events were reported or marked during the recording.

ECG: Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the study.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness and drowsiness.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.  
3. This study does not provide electrographic localization of the patient’s focal epilepsy. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. If events remain diagnostically uncertain or continue despite treatment, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may be considered to capture a habitual episode.
