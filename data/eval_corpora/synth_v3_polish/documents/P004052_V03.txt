Indication: Drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures beginning at age 37, currently occurring approximately 5/month. Genetic etiology associated with a pathogenic DEPDC5 c.4396C>T variant. Separate staring episodes are possibly atypical absence seizures; r/o generalized epileptiform correlate.

Clinical History: Patient with h/o focal aware seizures without post-ictal confusion. Current ASMs are levetiracetam, topiramate, perampanel, and carbamazepine. Previously trialed valproate, discontinued due to sedation, and lacosamide, discontinued due to rash. Family hx notable for a sister with stroke.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. No ASM changes were made for this study.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous and organized, consisting predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with an 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm. The PDR was symmetric and reactive to eye opening. An anterior-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was maintained. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry was present.

Awake state: Occasional brief runs of irregular 5-7 Hz theta slowing were present over the left temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, without definite clinical correlate. Muscle and eye-movement artifact intermittently obscured the frontotemporal leads.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes.

Hyperventilation: Mild diffuse theta buildup occurred without epileptiform activation. No electroclinical event was elicited.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response occurred.

Interictal findings: Rare sharp waves were seen independently over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7 with a field to T7 and FP1. These discharges were more apparent during drowsiness and sleep. In addition, occasional short runs of rhythmic 2.5-3 Hz left temporal delta activity were present, consistent with TIRDA. No LPDs, GPDs, or generalized spike-and-wave discharges were identified.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No subclinical ictal patterns were present. The patient denied typical symptoms during the recording.

ECG: Regular rhythm throughout the study.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to rare left anterior temporal sharp waves and occasional left temporal intermittent rhythmic delta activity (TIRDA), with mild intermittent left temporal slowing. These findings are supportive of focal cerebral dysfunction and an increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region, consistent with the patient’s known focal epilepsy.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. There was no generalized spike-and-wave activity or other EEG evidence of an absence seizure during this limited sample. The reported staring episodes are possibly nonepileptic or focal in origin; however, atypical absence cannot be excluded on the basis of this routine EEG.

3. Clinical correlation is recommended. Given ongoing seizures approximately 5/month despite four current ASMs, prolonged video-EEG monitoring may be considered for event characterization and localization.
