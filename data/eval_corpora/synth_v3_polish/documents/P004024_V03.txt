Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 29 in the setting of LGI1 antibody-associated immune disease. Currently seizure-free on perampanel and valproate. Routine EEG to evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: Drug-responsive focal epilepsy attributed to LGI1 autoimmunity. The patient reports no seizures over the current monthly reporting interval. Denies loss of consciousness and has no h/o episodes involving complete LOC. Current ASMs are perampanel and valproate. Prior MRI demonstrated an incidental lesion; further lesion characterization was not available for review with this study.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG channel. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable technical limits. The recording included wakefulness and drowsiness. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation (HV) and intermittent photic stimulation (PS). Video was recorded concurrently.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm (PDR) of 30-50 µV that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was preserved. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly and was symmetric. No persistent focal asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: The awake background was continuous and composed predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with admixed low-amplitude theta. Reactivity and variability were intact. No focal slowing, rhythmic delta activity, or breach rhythm was seen.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse theta activity, and slow roving eye movements. Stage N2 sleep contained symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform activation occurred during sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of HV.

Photic Stimulation: PS from 1 to 30 Hz elicited a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: No focal or generalized IEDs were identified. Specifically, there were no temporal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No focal cerebral slowing was present.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures were recorded. No clinical events or push-button activations occurred. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm during sampled portions of the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.  
3. A normal routine EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. The known hx of drug-responsive focal impaired-awareness seizures associated with LGI1 autoimmunity should be correlated clinically. If recurrent or atypical events arise and there is a need to r/o subclinical seizures, prolonged ambulatory EEG or LTM/VEEG may provide greater diagnostic yield.
