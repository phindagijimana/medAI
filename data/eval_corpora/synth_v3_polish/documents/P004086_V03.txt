Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of drug-resistant generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures beginning at age 12, currently occurring approximately 6 times/week. MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia, supporting a structural etiology. EEG requested to evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o electrographic correlate of possible atypical absence events.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single electrocardiographic channel. Electrode impedances were maintained at acceptable levels. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded throughout. The patient was awake and drowsy; stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Relevant Medications: Current ASMs include lacosamide, carbamazepine, lamotrigine, and perampanel. Previously trialed brivaracetam, discontinued due to cognitive side effects; oxcarbazepine, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control; and topiramate, discontinued due to sedation.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous and organized, with an 8.5-9 Hz symmetric posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with shifting hemispheric emphasis. Discharges occurred singly and in brief bursts lasting 1-3 seconds. No definite clinical change was apparent on video during the shorter bursts.

Drowsy/Sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges increased in frequency during drowsiness and sleep. No focal sleep-activated IEDs were identified.

Hyperventilation: HV produced expected diffuse theta slowing. A brief generalized 3 Hz spike-and-wave burst occurred without a clear behavioral correlate. Given its short duration and absence of formal responsiveness testing, a possible atypical absence discharge cannot be excluded.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings: Occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, frontally predominant and activated by drowsiness/sleep. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or definite clinical myoclonic seizures were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, increased during drowsiness and sleep. These findings support an increased risk for generalized-onset seizures and are consistent with the patient’s h/o generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures.

2. A brief generalized discharge during HV had no definite clinical correlate; atypical absence cannot be excluded, although no electroclinical absence seizure was established during this recording.

3. No focal epileptiform abnormality corresponding to the known MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia was demonstrated. A normal focal EEG sample does not exclude seizures arising from a structural lesion. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended in this drug-resistant epilepsy.
