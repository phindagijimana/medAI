Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy characterized by focal aware seizures, with onset at age 36 and a current frequency of approximately 2 seizures per month. MRI reportedly demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia, supporting a structural etiology.

The epilepsy is drug-resistant. Seizures have persisted despite the current combination of perampanel, carbamazepine, and valproate, as well as prior trials of lacosamide, zonisamide, and topiramate. The prior medications were limited by mood side effects with lacosamide and topiramate and GI intolerance with zonisamide.

The reported focal cortical dysplasia raises the possibility of a surgically remediable epileptogenic lesion. However, the available information is not sufficient to determine localization, concordance, or candidacy for resection. Presurgical evaluation should include confirmation of the habitual events with scalp VEEG and correlation with the structural imaging. The goal is to determine whether the seizures arise from the region of focal cortical dysplasia and whether treatment can be undertaken safely.

We also discussed that treatment pathways for drug-resistant focal epilepsy may include resective or ablative surgery when a sufficiently localized epileptogenic zone can be identified. Neuromodulation options may also be reviewed during a comprehensive surgical evaluation, including the NeuroPace RNS System, LivaNova VNS SenTiva, or Medtronic DBS Percept. Discussion of these devices does not establish candidacy; selection depends on the results of the presurgical evaluation.
