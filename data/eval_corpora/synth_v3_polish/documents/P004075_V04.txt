Medical Decision-Making (MDM):  
Reported event is most consistent with breakthrough seizure given established focal epilepsy and known progression to bilateral tonic-clonic activity. Potential precipitants include missed oxcarbazepine, metabolic disturbance—particularly hyponatremia—or intercurrent illness. Differential also includes convulsive syncope, TIA, and psychogenic non-epileptic event, though these are less favored based on the known epilepsy history. No focal deficit to suggest acute stroke. No ongoing seizure or persistent encephalopathy to suggest status epilepticus.

Patient remained stable s/p ED observation without recurrent event. Ativan (lorazepam) and IV second-line ASM were not indicated because seizure activity had terminated before arrival and mental status improved. Neurology does not recommend escalation beyond current oxcarbazepine at this time. Medication reconciliation and close outpatient neurology follow-up emphasized.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizure  
2. Drug-responsive focal epilepsy due to LGI1 autoimmune disease

Disposition:  
Discharge home in stable condition after return to neurologic baseline and neurology review. Continue oxcarbazepine as prescribed; no missed or extra doses. Neurology follow-up within 1–2 weeks. Seizure precautions reviewed: no driving per applicable state law, no swimming or bathing alone, no climbing or operating heavy machinery. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, new weakness, persistent confusion, fever, head injury, or inability to take medications.
