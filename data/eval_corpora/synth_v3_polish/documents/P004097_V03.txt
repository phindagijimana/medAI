Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, there is a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm (PDR) at 30-60 µV, reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient is present. Low-amplitude beta activity is seen frontocentrally. No persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing is identified.

Awake State: The waking background is continuous and appropriately modulated. No lateralized or generalized periodic discharges, rhythmic delta activity, or other potentially ictal patterns are present.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness is characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep is achieved, with symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform activation occurs during sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produces mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal abnormality or epileptiform discharges. The response resolves promptly after completion of HV.

Photic Stimulation: PS from 1-30 Hz produces a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response is seen.

Interictal Findings: No focal, multifocal, or generalized IEDs are identified. Specifically, there are no spikes or sharp waves over the temporal, frontal, central, parietal, or occipital regions. No TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs are present.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events are captured. No push-button events occur.

ECG: Single-channel ECG demonstrates a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures are identified.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. Clinical correlation is advised in this patient with a h/o focal aware seizures and drug-resistant focal epilepsy of unknown etiology.
