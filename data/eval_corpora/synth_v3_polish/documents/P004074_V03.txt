Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and focal cerebral dysfunction.

Clinical History:  Patient with h/o focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 25. At peak, events occurred approximately 5 times/week. Seizures have been drug responsive, with improvement s/p initiation of the current dual-ASM regimen of levetiracetam and oxcarbazepine. Previously trialed perampanel, discontinued due to sedation. Brain MRI demonstrates a vascular malformation and also reportedly shows a probable abnormality consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis. Family hx is notable for the patient’s mother having generalized epilepsy of childhood. Routine EEG requested to r/o ongoing focal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel EKG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded throughout the study.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the bilateral frontocentral regions. No persistent focal attenuation or hemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  During maximal wakefulness, the background was composed predominantly of alpha frequencies posteriorly with admixed low-amplitude beta and theta activity anteriorly. There was intermittent polymorphic 3-6 Hz slowing over the left temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, at times extending to P7.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was associated with attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No additional epileptiform abnormalities were activated by sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Mild symmetric buildup of theta activity without focal accentuation. No epileptiform activation.

Photic Stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings:  Occasional low- to moderate-amplitude sharp waves were present independently at F7/T7, with a field extending to the left frontotemporal region. These discharges were more apparent during drowsiness and light sleep. No independent right-sided IEDs, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or generalized spike-wave discharges were seen.

Ictal Events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The absence of a recorded habitual event does not r/o epilepsy.

EKG:  Regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional left anterior temporal sharp waves, indicating increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region.
2. Intermittent left temporal polymorphic slowing, consistent with focal cerebral dysfunction in that region.
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded.

In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support a diagnosis of focal epilepsy and may be concordant with the reported structural MRI abnormalities. Correlation with the vascular malformation and the probable finding consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis is recommended.
