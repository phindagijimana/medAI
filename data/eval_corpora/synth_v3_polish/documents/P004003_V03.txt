Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: Patient with h/o structural focal epilepsy, with seizure onset at age 75. Typical events are characterized by impaired awareness and occur approximately 4/week. Brain MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis. The epilepsy has been drug responsive on the current ASM regimen of carbamazepine and valproate. Previously trialed perampanel, discontinued due to rash. No history of epilepsy in the family.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The recording was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and stepwise PS. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: In maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9 Hz PDR that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. No persistent focal background asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: The awake background was continuous and reactive. No generalized slowing was present. No LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs were seen.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was identified by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral frontocentral sleep spindles. Sleep architecture was otherwise preserved.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform activation.

Photic Stimulation: PS from 1-20 Hz produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: Rare sharp waves were present over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7 with a field extending to T7. These discharges were more apparent during drowsiness and sleep. In addition, there were occasional brief runs of irregular 2-4 Hz delta/theta slowing over the left temporal chain, maximal at F7-T7. Definite TIRDA was not observed. No independent right temporal IEDs were identified.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-lead ECG showed a regular rhythm during sampled portions of the study.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to rare left anterior temporal sharp waves, indicating increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region.
2. Occasional left temporal polymorphic slowing, consistent with focal cerebral dysfunction in that region.
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded.

In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support focal epilepsy, likely of temporal origin, and may be concordant with the known structural lesion of hippocampal sclerosis. A normal seizure sample is not available on this routine study; absence of an ictal event does not exclude ongoing focal impaired-awareness seizures.

Clinical correlation with MRI laterality is recommended. If MRI shows progression, PET/SPECT should be obtained to assess concordance. Should seizures recur or worsen despite the current drug-responsive regimen, revisit presurgical workup, including LTM/VEEG for event characterization and electroclinical localization.
