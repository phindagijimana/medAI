Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy beginning at age 33. The documented seizure type is focal impaired awareness, with a current frequency of approximately seven seizures per week. The etiology is structural, supported by MRI evidence of hippocampal sclerosis. No lateralization was supplied.

The epilepsy is characterized in the referral documentation as drug-responsive. The current ASM regimen consists of brivaracetam and valproate, but exact doses and frequencies remain unavailable. Despite the drug-responsive designation, the reported seizure burden is substantial and warrants careful medication reconciliation and review of the original diagnostic studies. I would not reclassify the epilepsy as drug-resistant based solely on the information currently available, particularly because prior adequate ASM trials and treatment response details were not provided.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Briviact (brivaracetam) and Depakote (valproate) without an empiric change until the exact formulations, doses, and administration frequencies can be verified. The patient should not abruptly discontinue either ASM.

2. Complete medication reconciliation through the patient’s pharmacy, referring neurology records, or current prescription bottles. We need to clarify dose, BID/TID schedule, adherence, tolerability, and the degree of response associated with each medication.

3. Obtain the original brain MRI images and complete radiology report for direct review. The present summary identifies hippocampal sclerosis but does not provide laterality or sufficient detail for independent assessment.

4. Obtain any available prior EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, or EMU/LTM reports. These records would help determine whether electrographic findings are concordant with the structural MRI abnormality.

5. Maintain a seizure log documenting the number of focal impaired awareness seizures per week. This will allow comparison with the currently reported baseline of approximately seven per week and help assess ongoing response to brivaracetam and valproate.

6. Continue standard seizure precautions while focal impaired awareness seizures remain active. Medication changes should be coordinated through the treating neurology team rather than made independently.

7. If MRI shows progression, obtain PET/SPECT for concordance. Any such evaluation should be interpreted together with the seizure semiology, EEG data, and the MRI abnormality rather than in isolation.

8. Follow up after medication doses and the original MRI and EEG records have been obtained. At that time, we can determine whether the current regimen should be continued, optimized, or otherwise revised.
