Indication:  Hx of drug-resistant epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic (GTC) seizures beginning at age 15, currently occurring approximately 7/week despite lamotrigine, carbamazepine, and lacosamide. Immune etiology attributed to GAD65 antibody. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and to r/o focal features.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with the expected anterior-to-posterior gradient. A 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and was reactive to eye opening. Anteriorly, lower-voltage beta and alpha frequencies were seen. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state:  During wakefulness, there were occasional bursts of generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity. Discharges were maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with rapid bilateral synchrony. Individual bursts lasted approximately 0.5-2 seconds and were not associated with a definite clinical change on video.

Drowsy/sleep:  With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Generalized discharges became somewhat more frequent during drowsiness. No definite vertex waves, sleep spindles, or K complexes were recorded.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild symmetric background slowing. There was a brief increase in generalized spike-and-wave activity without clinical correlate.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings:  Occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, frontally predominant, as described above. At times, the discharges showed subtle shifting hemispheric emphasis; however, no consistently localized lead-in was demonstrated. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or electrographic status epilepticus was present.

Ictal events:  No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured. Specifically, no GTC seizure occurred during this recording.

Clinical History/Medications:  Clinical h/o generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures since age 15, with a reported current frequency of approximately seven seizures per week. Epilepsy is considered drug-resistant and associated with GAD65 antibody-mediated immune disease. Current ASMs are lamotrigine, carbamazepine, and lacosamide. Previously trialed brivaracetam was discontinued due to inadequate seizure control.

Impression:

1. Abnormal awake and drowsy EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness and HV. These findings support an increased generalized epileptic potential and are consistent with the reported generalized epilepsy phenotype.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. Given subtle shifting asymmetry within some generalized discharges and the clinical uncertainty, possibly focal onset seizures with rapid bilateral synchrony cannot be excluded. Correlation with seizure semiology and long-term video EEG monitoring may be helpful if further electroclinical characterization is required.
