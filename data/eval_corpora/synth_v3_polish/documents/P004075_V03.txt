Indication: H/o drug-responsive focal epilepsy of immune etiology associated with LGI1 antibody, with onset at age 77 and focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Currently seizure-free (0 seizures/month) on oxcarbazepine. EEG obtained to r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. The recording included wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation (HV) and intermittent photic stimulation (PS). Current ASM was oxcarbazepine; dose and BID schedule were not provided.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm (PDR) that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the bilateral frontocentral regions. No persistent focal asymmetry was identified.

Awake state: The waking background was continuous and composed predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with admixed low-amplitude theta activity. There was no focal delta slowing. No lateralized attenuation or breach rhythm was present.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse theta activity, and slow rolling eye movements. Stage N2 sleep was achieved, with symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep did not activate epileptiform abnormalities.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation.

Photic stimulation: PS at sequential flash frequencies produced symmetric posterior photic driving at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal, multifocal, or generalized interictal epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA. No persistent focal slowing was present.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No patient-initiated event markers were recorded. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm during sampled portions of the study.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were recorded.

2. The absence of epileptiform abnormalities on this study does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. Clinical correlation is advised, particularly given the established hx of immune-mediated, LGI1-associated focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures.

3. The reported epilepsy is drug responsive, with current seizure freedom at 0 seizures/month while taking oxcarbazepine. No medication change can be recommended on the basis of this EEG alone.
