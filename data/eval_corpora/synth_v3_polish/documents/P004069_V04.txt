**Referral Question:**  
Diagnostic neuropsychological evaluation to establish a cognitive baseline in the context of focal epilepsy and an MRI-identified tumor. Findings are intended to inform PRC discussion and potential future presurgical planning should seizures recur or become medically refractory.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a hx of focal epilepsy beginning at age 6. Seizures are classified as focal to bilateral tonic-clonic events, reportedly occurring approximately three times per month. The available record characterizes the epilepsy as drug responsive; this designation should be reconciled clinically with the reported seizure frequency.

Etiology is considered structural in the context of an MRI finding described as a tumor. More specific information regarding lesion location, dimensions, pathology, and interval stability was not available for review. EEG localization and lateralization data were also not provided; therefore, neuropsychological findings cannot be directly compared with electrographic localization.

Current antiseizure medications are zonisamide and perampanel. Doses were not available. Previously trialed medications include topiramate, discontinued due to mood side effects, and brivaracetam, discontinued due to cognitive side effects. Educational, occupational, developmental, psychiatric, and substance-use histories were not sufficiently documented in the available referral materials. These omissions limit estimation of premorbid ability and interpretation of possible change from baseline.

**Behavioral Observations:**

**Appearance and Cooperation:**  
Arrived appropriately groomed. In NAD. Alert and generally attentive. Cooperative throughout the interview and testing session.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent and grammatical, with normal rate and prosody. No overt paraphasic errors. Comprehension adequate for multistep test instructions.

**Mood and Affect:**  
Mood described as generally stable. Affect appropriate to content and normal in range. No behavioral evidence of marked anxiety or depression during the evaluation.

**Effort and Engagement:**  
Task persistence adequate. Frustration tolerance intact. Performance on the Test of Memory Malingering was valid (TOMM Trial 2 = 50/50). Results are considered a reliable estimate of current cognitive functioning.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; Trail Making Test Parts A and B; Boston Naming Test; Controlled Oral Word Association Test; Animal Fluency; CVLT-3; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test; Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; BDI-II; BAI; PHQ-9; TOMM.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
Overall intellectual functioning fell within the Average range (WAIS-IV FSIQ = 96, 39th percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 98, 45th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 102, 55th percentile). Working Memory was Average (WMI = 92, 30th percentile). Processing Speed was Low Average (PSI = 89, 23rd percentile), representing a relative weakness compared with perceptual reasoning.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Auditory attention and working memory were broadly intact (Digit Span scaled score = 9; Arithmetic scaled score = 8). Visual scanning and sequencing were Low Average to Average (Trail Making A, T = 42). Speeded symbol transcription was Low Average (Coding scaled score = 7), while visual discrimination speed was Average (Symbol Search scaled score = 9). The overall pattern suggests mild psychomotor and processing-speed inefficiency without a primary attention disorder.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Low Average (BNT = 52/60, T = 42), with benefit from phonemic cues. Phonemic verbal fluency was Low Average (FAS, T = 43), and semantic fluency was at the lower end of the Low Average range (Animals, T = 39). Vocabulary and verbal abstract reasoning were Average. These findings indicate mild inefficiency in lexical retrieval. Confrontation naming can be sensitive to dominant temporal dysfunction; however, the weakness was modest and, in the absence of lesion laterality, handedness, and language-mapping data, should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of dominant-hemisphere localization.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On list learning, acquisition was somewhat shallow but progressive across trials (CVLT-3 Trials 1-5 total scaled score = 8, Average). Short-delay and long-delay free recall were Low Average (scaled scores = 7 and 7, respectively). Recognition discriminability was also Low Average due to several false-positive responses (scaled score = 7). WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 8), while delayed recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7); recognition fell between the 17th and 25th percentiles. Overall, verbal memory showed mildly inefficient learning and retrieval rather than rapid forgetting or a dense amnestic syndrome.

**Visual Memory:**  
WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate recall was Average (scaled score = 9), and delayed recall was Average (scaled score = 8). ROCF delayed recall was Low Average (T = 43), with retention generally commensurate with initial organization. There was no clear material-specific visual memory impairment.

**Executive Function:**  
Mental flexibility was Low Average (Trail Making B, T = 40) without errors. On the WCST, the patient completed four categories; perseverative responding was Average (T = 45). Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T = 41). Results suggest mild executive inefficiency under speeded or high-interference conditions, with preserved problem solving and capacity to use feedback.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10). ROCF copy was intact (32/36), although the approach was mildly piecemeal. There was no evidence of a clinically significant visuoconstructional deficit.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average for the dominant hand (T = 39) and Average for the nondominant hand (T = 43). Given that handedness was not documented, motor lateralization cannot be interpreted confidently.

**Mood/Personality:**  
Self-report measures did not indicate clinically significant current emotional distress: BDI-II = 8, BAI = 6, and PHQ-9 = 4, all within minimal ranges. This does not establish the absence of prior psychiatric symptoms, particularly given the reported mood side effects on topiramate.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
The evaluation documents broadly Average intellectual functioning with relative strengths in verbal reasoning, perceptual reasoning, visuospatial construction, and untimed problem solving. Mild weaknesses were observed in processing speed, lexical retrieval, speeded executive control, and verbal learning/retrieval. Visual memory was comparatively preserved. There was no evidence of global cognitive impairment, a dense amnestic syndrome, or a strongly lateralizing neuropsychological pattern.

The mild verbal retrieval weakness could be consistent with disruption of dominant temporal networks, but this conclusion remains tentative because tumor location, EEG findings, handedness, and language dominance are unknown. The profile should therefore be integrated cautiously with MRI, video-EEG, and functional mapping findings. Current results provide a useful baseline for monitoring cognitive change associated with seizures, medication adjustment, tumor management, or future intervention.

**Recommendations:**

1. Review findings at PRC with direct correlation to MRI lesion location and available EEG data.  
2. Clarify current seizure burden and the apparent discrepancy between approximately three seizures per month and the designation of drug-responsive epilepsy.  
3. Should recurrence occur, revisit the presurgical workup, including prolonged video-EEG monitoring and updated structural imaging.  
4. If intervention involving dominant temporal or language networks is considered, obtain fMRI language mapping; consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant.  
5. If resection or ablation is not feasible and seizures become drug resistant, neuromodulation options, including the NeuroPace RNS System, may be discussed in the broader PRC context.  
6. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation following a major treatment change or approximately 6-12 months after any surgical intervention.
