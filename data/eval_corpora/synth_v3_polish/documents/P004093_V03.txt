Indication: Drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: Adult with h/o focal epilepsy beginning at age 55. Habitual seizures are classified as focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic. Epilepsy is considered genetic in etiology, associated with a causative STXBP1 c.902A>G variant. The patient is currently seizure-free, with 0 seizures/month, despite a historical classification of drug-resistant epilepsy. Current ASMs are carbamazepine and lacosamide. Previously trialed oxcarbazepine, discontinued due to rash; levetiracetam, discontinued due to GI intolerance; and zonisamide, discontinued due to GI intolerance. MRI demonstrated nonspecific white matter changes without a reported focal structural epileptogenic lesion. Denies syncope or loss of consciousness outside the recognized epileptic events. Patient was In NAD during electrode application.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and became drowsy; stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized, with a symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and voltage gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was noted anteriorly. No persistent interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: No focal attenuation or sustained focal slowing was present. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, K complexes, and bilateral sleep spindles. Sleep architecture was preserved.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges.

Photic Stimulation: PS elicited a symmetric posterior driving response at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal Findings: No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, or generalized SWD. No electrographic pattern was seen to suggest a specific seizure onset zone.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-lead ECG showed a regular rhythm during the sampled recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. In the appropriate clinical context, prolonged ambulatory EEG or LTM/VEEG may be considered if further electroclinical characterization or r/o of unrecognized events is needed.
3. Clinical correlation is recommended given the hx of drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, currently seizure-free on carbamazepine and lacosamide.
