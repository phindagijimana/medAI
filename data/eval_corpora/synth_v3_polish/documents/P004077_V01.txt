Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy manifested by focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 35. Events continue at a reported frequency of approximately one per month. The described etiology is genetic, related to a causative SCN1A c.5734C>T variant.

The current regimen is dual therapy with lamotrigine and brivaracetam. Levetiracetam was previously stopped because of mood side effects. Although the supplied classification characterizes the epilepsy as drug responsive, the persistence of monthly seizures on two current ASMs makes probable drug-resistant focal epilepsy a reasonable concern. I would not make a definitive DRE designation until the exact doses, adherence, duration of treatment, and adequacy of prior ASM trials are verified.

A possible autoimmune contribution has been suggested. At present, this remains possible rather than established because no supporting laboratory, CSF, imaging, or EEG data were available for review. The known genetic finding provides an identified etiology, but the available information does not by itself resolve whether an additional autoimmune process contributes to the clinical course.
