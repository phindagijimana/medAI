Assessment / Impression

This is a patient with seizure onset at age 10 and combined generalized and focal epilepsy due to a structural etiology. The identified structural abnormality is focal cortical dysplasia. The reported clinical seizure type is focal impaired awareness. The patient denies preceding aura and denies complete loss of consciousness.

Despite inadequate seizure control with prior lamotrigine, the patient is currently seizure-free on combination therapy with topiramate and valproate, with a reported frequency of 0 seizures per month. The current course is therefore most consistent with drug-responsive epilepsy rather than drug-resistant epilepsy.

The MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia is clinically relevant, but the patient’s current seizure freedom does not support a need for presurgical evaluation at this time. Direct review of the MRI images and any prior EEG data would be helpful for more complete electroclinical characterization, particularly given the description of combined generalized and focal features.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Topamax (topiramate) and Depakote (valproate) without change pending formal medication reconciliation. The current combination is associated with seizure freedom and appears effective.

2. Confirm the exact dose, formulation, and frequency of both current ASMs through the patient’s pharmacy, medication bottles, or prescribing clinician. These details were not available in the referral documentation.

3. Lamictal (lamotrigine) should remain documented as a prior ASM discontinued because of inadequate seizure control, not as an active medication.

5. Obtain any prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, or EMU/VEEG reports if available. These would help clarify the relationship between the reported generalized and focal features and the structural MRI lesion.

6. No presurgical epilepsy evaluation is recommended at present because the patient is seizure-free and the epilepsy is drug responsive.

7. Continue longitudinal epilepsy follow-up after medication reconciliation and review of the available MRI and EEG records.
