Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Drug-resistant epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures, onset at age 26, occurring approximately once yearly; r/o interictal epileptiform activity. Structural etiology is suspected given focal cortical dysplasia on MRI.

Clinical History: Patient with h/o generalized epilepsy manifested by GTC seizures. Current ASMs are perampanel, levetiracetam, and brivaracetam. Previously trialed lamotrigine, discontinued due to sedation; oxcarbazepine and lacosamide, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control; and topiramate and carbamazepine, discontinued due to mood side effects. Family hx notable for a maternal aunt with febrile seizures.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included synchronized video. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.

Description:

Background: In the awake state, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm of 30-60 µV, reactive to eye opening and closure. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. No persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing was identified.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was associated with attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of epileptiform abnormalities.

Photic Stimulation: PS at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz elicited symmetric photic driving, best seen over O1/O2. No photoparoxysmal response occurred.

Interictal Findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, with maximum negativity over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. These occurred as isolated complexes and brief bursts lasting up to 2 seconds, without clinical change on video. Discharges were accentuated during drowsiness and sleep. No consistent focal lead-in was identified. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or electrographic status epilepticus was present.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

ECG: Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are consistent with the patient’s hx of generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. No persistent focal slowing or focal epileptiform abnormality was identified on this study.

3. The known focal cortical dysplasia on MRI is not specifically localized electrographically by this recording. Clinical and imaging correlation is advised, particularly given the structural lesion and drug-resistant course. A normal focal background does not exclude a focal epileptogenic contribution.
