Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of structural focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, onset at age 33, occurring approximately 7 times/week. MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis. EEG obtained to r/o interictal epileptiform activity and assess seizure risk.

Current ASMs: Brivaracetam and valproate. Epilepsy is clinically classified as drug-responsive.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and stepwise PS. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording; stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity at 35-55 µV, reactive to eye opening and closure. An appropriate anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent focal asymmetry was identified.

Awake state: The patient remained alert and cooperative, in NAD. Background organization and reactivity were preserved. Occasional eye-movement and myogenic artifacts were present without limiting interpretation.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity, and slow roving eye movements. Brief vertex transients were seen, but sustained stage N2 sleep with well-formed sleep spindles and K complexes was not achieved.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric buildup of diffuse theta activity without focal slowing, epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizure.

Photic stimulation: PS at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced symmetric photic driving, maximal posteriorly. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings: Rare low-amplitude sharp transients were present independently over the temporal regions during drowsiness but lacked a consistent field or after-going slow wave and were not definitively epileptiform. No focal spikes, sharp waves, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or other definite IEDs were identified. There was no sustained focal or generalized slowing.

Ictal events: No clinical events, electrographic seizures, or subclinical seizures were captured. The single-lead ECG showed a regular rhythm at approximately 65-75 beats/min.

Impression:

1. Normal awake and drowsy routine EEG. No definite focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities were identified, and no seizures were recorded.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. The patient’s focal impaired-awareness seizures and structural etiology related to hippocampal sclerosis remain clinical and imaging diagnoses.
3. Continued management with current ASMs, brivaracetam and valproate, is recommended as clinically appropriate. If interval MRI shows progression, obtain PET/SPECT for concordance and further localization. Prolonged ambulatory EEG or LTM/VEEG may be considered if event characterization or seizure quantification is needed.
