Indication: Hx of structural focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Epilepsy is considered drug-responsive. Current ASMs are carbamazepine and lacosamide. Brain MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia, Palmini type IIa, consistent with a structural epilepsy etiology.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, supplemented by a single-lead ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. The recording was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-modulated 10 Hz alpha rhythm at 30-50 µV, reactive to eye opening and closure. An appropriate anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. No persistent asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: The background remained continuous and organized. There was no focal delta or theta slowing. No breach rhythm was present. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs were identified.

Drowsy/Sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric 4-7 Hz theta activity. Occasional vertex transients were present. Sustained stage N2 sleep was not recorded; therefore, sleep spindles and K complexes were not adequately assessed.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric buildup of generalized theta activity without focal accentuation. No epileptiform discharges or electrographic seizures were provoked.

Photic Stimulation: PS at sequential flash frequencies produced symmetric photic driving, maximal over O1/O2, at several frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings: No definite spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, or other IEDs were seen. No focal slowing was identified.

Ictal Events: None captured. No electrographic seizures occurred. No clinical events were marked by the patient or technologist. Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm during the sampled portions of the study.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake and drowsy states. No focal slowing, epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. The clinical hx remains consistent with focal impaired-awareness seizures in the setting of structural focal epilepsy due to MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia, Palmini type IIa. Clinical correlation is advised; prolonged ambulatory EEG or LTM may be considered if event characterization or electroclinical localization is required.
