Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures beginning at age 7. Reported historical seizure frequency approximately 5/week, currently considered drug responsive on carbamazepine. Routine EEG to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o focal electrographic features in the setting of a structural MRI lesion.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Relevant Clinical History: Generalized epilepsy characterized by GTC seizures, with onset at 7 years of age. Current ASM is carbamazepine. The epilepsy has been drug responsive. Brain MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia; this focal MRI lesion is probably, and likely, epileptogenic, although correlation with the electroclinical data is required. No family history of epilepsy. Patient denies photosensitivity.

Description:

Background: During wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha rhythm with amplitudes of 35-60 µV, reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges occurred in brief bursts lasting 0.5-2 seconds. These were maximal over Fp1/Fp2-F3/F4, with broad bilateral synchrony and no consistent focal lead-in. Discharge frequency within bursts was approximately 3.5-4.5 Hz. No clinical change accompanied these discharges.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was associated with attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges were mildly activated by sleep. No focal slowing, TIRDA, LPDs, or independent focal IEDs were seen.

Hyperventilation: HV produced symmetric generalized theta slowing. One brief generalized spike-and-wave burst occurred without a clinical correlate.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited, consistent with the reported absence of photosensitivity.

Interictal Findings: Occasional generalized frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, increased during drowsiness and sleep.

Ictal Events: No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No GTC event occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with mild activation during drowsiness and sleep. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized seizures and are consistent with the patient’s generalized epilepsy syndrome and h/o GTC seizures.

2. No electrographic seizures, focal slowing, or consistently focal epileptiform discharges were identified. The known focal cortical dysplasia remains a structural lesion that is likely epileptogenic; however, its clinical significance cannot be established from this routine recording. Clinical and MRI correlation is recommended.
