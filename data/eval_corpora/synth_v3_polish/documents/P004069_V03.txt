Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm of medium amplitude, reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen frontocentrally. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No focal delta slowing or attenuation was present. No LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns were seen.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse mixed theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was marked by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No sleep-activated IEDs were identified.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges.

Photic Stimulation: PS elicited a symmetric photic driving response at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was present.

Interictal Findings: No spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, or focal slowing were identified.

Ictal Events: No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No patient-reported events occurred during this study.

ECG: Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal awake, drowsy, and sleep routine EEG. No focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities were identified.  
2. No electrographic seizures were recorded.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy. The clinical history remains consistent with structural focal epilepsy manifesting as focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Continued clinical and imaging correlation is recommended. If seizure recurrence occurs after the present period of improved control, revisit presurgical workup, including prolonged video-EEG monitoring for event characterization and seizure-onset localization.
