Assessment / Impression

This patient has epilepsy beginning at age 7, with a reported generalized epilepsy phenotype and generalized tonic-clonic seizures occurring approximately 5 times per week. The documented etiology is structural, with MRI evidence of focal cortical dysplasia. The focal MRI lesion is probably epileptogenic.

There is some diagnostic discordance between the generalized epilepsy classification and the focal structural lesion. Generalized tonic-clonic semiology alone does not localize seizure onset, and focal seizures can evolve to bilateral tonic-clonic activity. Conversely, an MRI lesion may be present without establishing the electroclinical onset. Review of prior EEG data, or updated EEG evaluation when needed, will be important to confirm the current classification and r/o a focal electroclinical onset related to the dysplasia.

The referral characterizes the epilepsy as drug-responsive on carbamazepine. At the same time, the listed frequency of 5 seizures per week warrants clarification regarding whether this frequency reflects the current treated state, an earlier baseline, or incomplete response. Current information does not establish drug-resistant epilepsy, and there is no indication from the submitted records that the patient is undergoing a presurgical evaluation.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Tegretol (carbamazepine) for now. The exact formulation, dose, and frequency should be reconciled with the patient, pharmacy, and referring office before any medication adjustment is made.

2. Obtain the prior MRI images for direct review at xxx Medical Center. The report identifies focal cortical dysplasia, but image review is needed to define its location and assess whether it is likely to account for the seizure disorder.
