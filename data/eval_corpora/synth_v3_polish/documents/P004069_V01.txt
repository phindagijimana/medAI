Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy beginning at age 6, with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures reported at a frequency of approximately 3 per month. The epilepsy is classified as structural in etiology given the tumor identified on MRI.

The patient is currently taking zonisamide and perampanel. The supplied clinical characterization identifies the epilepsy as drug-responsive, and there is no current designation of drug-resistant epilepsy. Prior trials of topiramate and brivaracetam were limited by mood and cognitive side effects, respectively. Those adverse-effect-related discontinuations should be distinguished from medication failures due to persistent seizures.

The available data are not sufficient to localize the seizure-onset zone or assess concordance among semiology, EEG, and MRI. In particular, the tumor location and prior EEG findings were not included. At this time, the history supports focal epilepsy with evolution to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures and a structural MRI abnormality.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Zonegran (zonisamide) and Fycompa (perampanel) as the current ASM regimen. The patient and referring office should confirm the exact doses and frequencies, as those details were not included in the records available today.

2. Maintain the current classification of focal structural epilepsy with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. The reported seizure burden is approximately 3 events per month.

3. Avoid restarting Topamax (topiramate) without careful consideration of the prior mood side effects. Likewise, the prior cognitive side effects on Briviact (brivaracetam) should be considered if future medication changes are discussed.

4. The prior MRI demonstrating a tumor remains clinically relevant to the structural epilepsy diagnosis. Any future evaluation should incorporate direct review of the MRI images and available radiology reports, particularly if more definitive localization is needed.

5. Should seizure recurrence occur after control is established, we would revisit a presurgical workup. That evaluation could include updated epilepsy-protocol MRI review and EMU admission for prolonged VEEG/LTM to better define seizure onset and determine concordance with the structural lesion.

6. No current recommendation is made for epilepsy surgery or neuromodulation because the supplied phenotype is drug-responsive and the localization data are incomplete. If the clinical course changes, the need for a formal multidisciplinary presurgical review can be reconsidered.
