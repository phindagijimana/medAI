Indication: H/o focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o focal electrographic abnormality.

Clinical History: The patient has focal epilepsy of unknown etiology, with seizure onset at age 11. Typical events are focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures occurring approximately once per month. The patient denies a preceding aura. Epilepsy has been drug responsive on the current ASM regimen of lacosamide and perampanel. Valproate was previously trialed and discontinued due to inadequate seizure control. No personal h/o febrile seizures in childhood. The patient denies a family history of epilepsy; a brother reportedly had febrile seizures.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Brief stage N1 and N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded concurrently.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm of medium amplitude, reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the bilateral frontocentral regions. No persistent focal asymmetry or attenuation was identified.

Awake State: The waking background was continuous and symmetric. There was no focal delta slowing. No LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns were present.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform activation occurred during sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation.

Photic Stimulation: PS at sequential flash frequencies produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal Findings: No definite focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, or other IEDs were identified. No focal slowing was present.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No patient-initiated event markers occurred.

ECG: Single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and briefly asleep states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. This recording does not provide electrographic evidence of a specific epileptogenic focus. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy, including focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures.
3. If further electrographic characterization is clinically needed, particularly in the setting of continued approximately monthly breakthrough seizures, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may increase diagnostic yield.
