Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: H/o structural focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures beginning at age 36, currently occurring approximately 2/month despite multiple ASMs. MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia. EEG requested to evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system. Additional electrocardiographic monitoring was utilized. The study was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages. Activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Relevant Clinical History/Medications: The patient has drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures, onset at age 36 and current frequency of approximately two per month. Presumed etiology is structural, based on MRI evidence of focal cortical dysplasia. Current ASMs are perampanel, carbamazepine, and valproate. Previously trialed lacosamide, discontinued due to mood side effects; zonisamide, discontinued due to GI intolerance; and topiramate, discontinued due to mood side effects. No h/o psychiatric comorbidity or family history of epilepsy. The patient denies photosensitivity.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and organized, with appropriate anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradients. A symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and reactive to eye opening. Anteriorly, there was the expected pattern of lower-voltage beta activity with intermixed alpha and theta frequencies. No persistent focal asymmetry was identified.

Awake State: During maximal wakefulness, the background remained symmetric and well organized. No focal delta slowing, lateralized attenuation, or breach rhythm was present.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, slow roving eye movements, and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Brief vertex transients were seen. Definitive stage N2 sleep, including well-developed sleep spindles and K complexes, was not achieved.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric background slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges.

Photic Stimulation: PS at sequential flash frequencies produced symmetric photic driving, maximal over O1/O2. There was no photoparoxysmal response. No electroclinical event was elicited.

Interictal Findings: No focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs were identified. No definite focal slowing was present.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The patient’s habitual focal aware events did not occur during this recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake and drowsy states. No focal or generalized epileptiform discharges and no electrographic seizures were identified.
2. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude epilepsy. In the appropriate clinical context, the established diagnosis remains drug-resistant focal epilepsy of structural etiology associated with MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia.
3. If further electroclinical localization is required, particularly for presurgical evaluation, prolonged video EEG monitoring may provide greater diagnostic yield.
