Assessment / Impression

This patient has epilepsy beginning at age 6, currently classified as generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures. The documented seizure burden is approximately two per week. The patient denies tongue biting. Keppra is the current ASM, and the epilepsy is characterized in the referral information as drug responsive.

There is an important electroclinical and imaging question that should be clarified. The epilepsy is labeled generalized and structurally mediated, while the MRI is reported as normal. In addition, separate referral language notes a probable finding consistent with MTS. A definite diagnosis of MTS cannot be made from the information currently available. Direct image review is needed, since a mesial temporal lesion could suggest focal onset with rapid bilateral spread rather than a primary generalized epilepsy syndrome.

Despite the ongoing reported event frequency, the available phenotype is identified as drug responsive rather than drug-resistant epilepsy. There is no documented failure of adequate trials of multiple appropriate ASMs. Accordingly, a presurgical epilepsy evaluation is not indicated solely on the current information, although diagnostic clarification is warranted. Dr. xxx and I discussed the case and agree that the immediate priorities are medication reconciliation, confirmation of event classification, and review of the imaging discrepancy.
