Assessment / Impression

This is an XX-year-old patient with seizure onset at age 34 and recurrent convulsive events described as generalized tonic-clonic seizures, reportedly occurring approximately 3 times per month. The referral lists generalized epilepsy, current valproate therapy, and drug-responsive status. It also identifies a structural cause with hippocampal sclerosis on MRI.

The phenotype is not yet internally concordant. A generalized epilepsy classification may account for generalized tonic-clonic seizures and the use of valproate. Conversely, hippocampal sclerosis is a focal structural abnormality and may be consistent with MTS. Convulsive semiology alone does not reliably distinguish generalized onset from focal onset with bilateral tonic-clonic evolution.

Given the reported seizure frequency despite current treatment, the overall presentation is likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy, but this conclusion is only probable at present. The conflicting designation of drug responsiveness must be reconciled by confirming the current event frequency, adherence, prescribed dose, treatment response, and whether the reported episodes are all epileptic seizures. Direct review of MRI and EEG data will be important before changing the epilepsy classification or pursuing a surgical evaluation.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Depakote (valproate) at the currently prescribed regimen pending confirmation of the exact dose, formulation, and frequency. The patient should not independently stop or reduce valproate, as abrupt withdrawal may increase seizure risk.

2. Please forward the complete medication list and valproate dosing instructions. We will also clarify adherence and whether the reported frequency of 3 seizures per month reflects the current treated burden.

3. Obtain the original MRI brain images and formal report for direct review at xxx Medical Center. The reported hippocampal sclerosis is potentially consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis, but confirmation of laterality and imaging characteristics is needed.

4. Obtain all prior EEG data, including routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, and any VEEG or EMU/LTM reports and tracings. The immediate diagnostic goal is to distinguish generalized epilepsy from focal structural epilepsy presenting with bilateral convulsive seizures.

5. Check valproate monitoring studies if not recently completed, including a trough valproate level and routine safety laboratory testing. Results should be interpreted in conjunction with the confirmed dose and adherence.

6. Maintain a seizure diary documenting each convulsive event, including frequency and medication timing. This will help determine whether the epilepsy is genuinely drug responsive or whether the patient continues to have seizures despite an adequate valproate trial.
