Indication:  Focal impaired-awareness seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and focal cerebral dysfunction.

Clinical History:  Patient with h/o focal epilepsy beginning at age 6 years, characterized clinically by focal impaired-awareness seizures. Current frequency is approximately 1 seizure/year. Referral documentation attributes the epilepsy to a metabolic etiology. The patient is s/p multiple appropriate ASM trials, and the overall clinical phenotype is likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Prior neuroimaging is described as probably consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis, without additional imaging details available for this report. No history of epilepsy in the family.

Current ASMs:  Lacosamide and carbamazepine.

Prior ASMs:  Perampanel, discontinued due to mood side effects; topiramate, discontinued due to sedation; and zonisamide, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control. These are prior therapies and are not included among current medications.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and stage N2 sleep was achieved. The patient remained In NAD during the recording.

Description:

Background:  The awake background was continuous and organized, with a symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent generalized slowing was identified.

Awake State:  During wakefulness, there were occasional sharply contoured waveforms over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, with a field extending to FT9. A subset had a clear after-going slow wave and was morphologically distinct from the background, consistent with interictal epileptiform discharges.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and increased diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves and sleep spindles. Left anterior temporal sharp waves became more frequent during drowsiness and sleep. No independent right temporal IEDs were identified.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation.

Photic Stimulation:  PS elicited a symmetric photic driving response at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response occurred.

Interictal Findings:  Occasional left anterior temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/T7 and activated by sleep. In addition, rare brief runs of rhythmic 2–3 Hz left temporal delta activity were present, suggestive of TIRDA. No LPDs, GPDs, SIRPIDs, or electrographic status epilepticus were seen.

Ictal Events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The absence of a recorded seizure does not r/o epilepsy.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional sleep-activated left anterior temporal epileptiform discharges and rare brief left temporal rhythmic delta activity.
2. These findings indicate increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region and support a diagnosis of focal epilepsy in the appropriate clinical context.
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. EEG findings alone do not establish mesial temporal sclerosis; correlation with the prior MRI reportedly consistent with probable mesial temporal sclerosis is recommended.
