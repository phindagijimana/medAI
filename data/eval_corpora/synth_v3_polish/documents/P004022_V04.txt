**Referral Question:**  
Neuropsychological evaluation to characterize cognitive functioning in the setting of epilepsy and establish a baseline for multidisciplinary treatment planning. Specific considerations include cognitive lateralization, the functional significance of a focal MRI lesion, and potential cognitive risk should surgical or neuromodulation treatment be pursued.

**Relevant History:**  
The patient has a h/o generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 7. Current seizure frequency was reported as approximately five events per week. Etiology has been classified as structural. MRI demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia; this focal lesion is probably epileptogenic, although its relationship to the reported generalized seizure phenotype requires electroclinical correlation.

Current antiseizure medication is carbamazepine; dose and schedule were not provided. Available records characterize the epilepsy as drug-responsive. Given the reported frequency of five seizures per week, the apparent discrepancy between drug-responsive classification and ongoing event burden should be clarified at PRC, including confirmation that all reported events are epileptic seizures.

The patient denies photosensitivity. There is no family h/o epilepsy. Detailed EEG findings, lesion laterality, prior ASM trials, developmental history, psychiatric treatment history, and educational and occupational records were not available for review. Allergy status was not provided.

**Behavioral Observations:**

**Appearance and Cooperation:**  
Arrived appropriately dressed and groomed. In NAD. Alert and oriented. Cooperative throughout interview and testing. No observed seizure activity.

**Speech and Language:**  
Speech fluent and normally articulated. Comprehension adequate for conversational and task instructions. No obvious paraphasic errors.

**Mood and Affect:**  
Mood mildly concerned regarding health status. Affect appropriate and stable. No marked behavioral dysregulation.

**Effort and Engagement:**  
Sustained engagement across the evaluation. Performance on the Test of Memory Malingering was valid (TOMM Trial 2 = 49/50). Embedded indices, including Reliable Digit Span, were also within acceptable limits. Results are considered a valid estimate of current cognitive functioning.

**Tests Administered:**  
WAIS-IV; Trail Making Test, Parts A and B; Boston Naming Test; COWAT-FAS and Animal Fluency; CVLT-3; WMS-IV Logical Memory and Visual Reproduction; Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure; Wisconsin Card Sorting Test; Stroop Color-Word Test; Grooved Pegboard; BDI-II; BAI; and TOMM.

**Results:**

**Intellectual Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Full Scale IQ was in the Low Average range (FSIQ = 89, 23rd percentile). Verbal Comprehension was Average (VCI = 96, 39th percentile), as was Perceptual Reasoning (PRI = 94, 34th percentile). Working Memory was Low Average (WMI = 86, 18th percentile), and Processing Speed was Low Average (PSI = 81, 10th percentile). The profile suggests broadly preserved reasoning abilities with relative inefficiency in speeded output and mental manipulation.

**Attention/Processing Speed:**  
Basic auditory attention was Average (Digit Span Forward scaled score = 9), while working memory was Low Average (Digit Span Backward scaled score = 7). Trail Making Test Part A was Low Average (T = 40). WAIS-IV Coding was Borderline (scaled score = 5), whereas Symbol Search was Low Average (scaled score = 7). This pattern reflects slowed processing speed rather than a primary deficit in basic attention.

**Language:**  
Confrontation naming was Low Average on the Boston Naming Test (T = 40), with benefit from phonemic cueing. Letter fluency was Low Average (FAS T = 41), and semantic fluency was Average (Animals T = 46). Verbal abstraction was Average. Findings indicate mild word-retrieval inefficiency without clear evidence of aphasia. Because lesion laterality was not provided, the naming result cannot independently establish dominant temporal dysfunction.

**Verbal Memory:**  
On the CVLT-3, acquisition across five learning trials was Low Average (Trials 1-5 standard score = 84). Initial learning was reduced, though repetition produced a positive learning slope. Short-delay free recall was Borderline (scaled score = 5), and long-delay free recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7). Recognition discrimination was Average (scaled score = 9), with limited false-positive responding. WMS-IV Logical Memory immediate recall was Low Average (scaled score = 7), while delayed recall was Borderline (scaled score = 6). The overall pattern suggests inefficient encoding and retrieval, with comparatively better retention when recognition support is available.

**Visual Memory:**  
ROCF copy was intact and well organized. Delayed recall was Average (T = 48). WMS-IV Visual Reproduction immediate and delayed recall were Average (scaled scores = 9 and 10, respectively), with intact recognition. Visual memory represented a relative strength compared with verbal list and story recall.

**Executive Functioning:**  
Trail Making Test Part B was Low Average (T = 39), without set-loss errors. Stroop inhibition was Low Average (T = 41). On the WCST, the patient completed five categories, with performance generally Average; perseverative responses were mildly elevated (T = 42). Findings indicate mild inefficiency under conditions requiring rapid cognitive flexibility and inhibition, without a frank dysexecutive syndrome.

**Visuospatial Functioning:**  
WAIS-IV Block Design was Average (scaled score = 10). ROCF copy was intact, with no evidence of visuospatial neglect, constructional apraxia, or significant organizational disturbance.

**Motor Functioning:**  
Grooved Pegboard performance was Low Average bilaterally, without a clinically meaningful lateralized discrepancy. No focal motor pattern emerged.

**Mood/Personality:**  
BDI-II and BAI scores fell in the minimal range. Self-report did not indicate clinically significant depression or anxiety at the time of evaluation. Ongoing monitoring is appropriate given the psychosocial burden associated with frequent seizures.

**Summary and Impressions:**  
This was a valid neuropsychological evaluation. Overall intellectual functioning fell in the Low Average range, with preserved verbal and nonverbal reasoning. Relative strengths included visuospatial construction, visual learning and memory, recognition memory, and basic language comprehension. Weaknesses were observed in processing speed, working memory, rapid executive control, confrontation naming, and verbal learning/retrieval.

The pattern shows relatively weaker verbal than visual memory; however, it is not sufficiently specific to determine seizure onset or lesion laterality. The MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia is probably epileptogenic, but neuropsychological data alone cannot reconcile a focal structural lesion with the reported generalized tonic-clonic phenotype. Further review of prolonged video-EEG, MRI lesion location, and functional mapping is necessary. Current data do not provide a basis to attribute the profile to a specific temporal lobe.

The patient’s current cognitive baseline suggests some vulnerability to additional decline in verbal memory and naming if treatment involves dominant-hemisphere temporal or perisylvian cortex. Conversely, preserved visual memory does not exclude nondominant-hemisphere seizure involvement. Surgical risk estimates should therefore remain provisional until electroclinical localization and language dominance are established.

**Recommendations:**

1. Discuss findings at PRC, with attention to the discordance between generalized seizure classification, focal cortical dysplasia, reported frequency of five seizures/week, and drug-responsive designation.
2. Review prolonged video-EEG and high-resolution epilepsy-protocol MRI to determine whether the dysplasia is concordant with seizure onset; r/o nonepileptic events if electroclinical findings are inconsistent.
3. If resection or ablation is considered near language networks, obtain fMRI language mapping. Consider Wada testing if fMRI is inconclusive or discordant.
4. If a focal epileptogenic network is confirmed but resection is not appropriate, PRC may consider neuromodulation options such as the NeuroPace RNS System, with candidacy determined by the treating epilepsy team.
5. Use written schedules, repetition, external reminders, and reduced time pressure to compensate for slowed processing and verbal retrieval inefficiency.
6. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation approximately 6-12 months s/p any surgical or neuromodulation intervention, or earlier if there is a substantial cognitive change.
