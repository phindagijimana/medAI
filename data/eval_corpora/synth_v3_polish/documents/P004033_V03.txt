Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  H/o combined generalized and focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 10; r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History:  Patient with structural epilepsy and MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia. Typical seizures are classified as focal impaired-awareness events. Patient denies preceding aura and denies complete loss of consciousness. Seizures are currently drug responsive, with no recent seizures (0/month). Current ASMs are topiramate and valproate. Previously trialed lamotrigine, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control.

Technique:  A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded throughout the study.

Description:

Background:  The awake background was continuous and symmetric, with appropriate anterior-posterior organization. A well-formed 9-10 Hz PDR was present bilaterally and reactive to eye opening. Low-amplitude beta activity was maximal anteriorly. No persistent focal background asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  Rare, medium-amplitude sharp waves were present independently over the frontotemporal regions, maximal at F7/T7 on the left and F8/T8 on the right, with a mild left-sided predominance. In addition, occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave bursts occurred, lasting 0.5-2 seconds and without clinical correlate. Some generalized discharges demonstrated shifting frontal emphasis.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep showed symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized epileptiform discharges were mildly activated by sleep. Rare independent bilateral frontotemporal sharp waves persisted during sleep. No TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs were seen.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of additional epileptiform abnormalities.

Photic Stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings:  
1. Occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, frontally predominant.  
2. Rare independent bilateral frontotemporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/T7 and F8/T8, left greater than right.

Ictal Events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. Patient remained in NAD throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges and rare independent bilateral frontotemporal IEDs.  
2. These findings indicate both generalized and focal epileptogenic potential and are consistent with the patient’s hx of combined generalized and focal epilepsy.  
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal or seizure-free recording interval does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. Correlation with the known MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia and the broader clinical history is recommended.
