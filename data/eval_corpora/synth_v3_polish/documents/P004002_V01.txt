Assessment / Impression

This patient has generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 7. Current seizure frequency is reported as approximately five per week. The epilepsy is categorized as structural, with MRI evidence of focal cortical dysplasia. The focal MRI lesion is likely epileptogenic, but this remains a probable rather than definitive clinicoradiographic association in the absence of EEG and direct imaging review.

The patient is currently taking carbamazepine and has been characterized as drug responsive. Medication reconciliation is essential because the carbamazepine dose and frequency are not available. The reported frequency of five generalized tonic-clonic seizures per week also warrants clarification, particularly in the setting of a documented drug-responsive course.

There is potential discordance between the generalized epilepsy classification and the focal structural MRI abnormality. Review of prior EEG data, or updated EEG evaluation if those data cannot be obtained, will be important to confirm the electroclinical classification and r/o a focal onset pattern with rapid bilateral involvement. At present, the available information is insufficient to classify the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy or to recommend a presurgical evaluation.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Tegretol (carbamazepine) at the presently established regimen pending formal medication reconciliation. The patient or referring office should provide the exact dose, formulation, and frequency. I would avoid an empiric dose change without that information.

2. Obtain prior EEG reports and, when possible, the original EEG tracings for review. We need to assess whether the available electrophysiology supports generalized epilepsy or suggests a focal onset related to the MRI lesion.

3. If prior EEG data are unavailable or nondiagnostic, obtain an updated EEG. Given the reported event frequency, VEEG/LTM through the EMU may be useful for electroclinical characterization and to r/o a focal onset pattern with rapid bilateral spread.
