Neurology Progress Note

Chief Complaint: Follow-up focal epilepsy

History of Present Illness:  
This is a 3.5-year-old boy with h/o focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures beginning at age 3 years, etiology unknown. As documented in prior notes, he is currently treated with Topamax (topiramate) and Vimpat (lacosamide). Events have persisted despite trials of multiple appropriately selected ASMs, raising concern for probable drug-resistant focal epilepsy.

Since the last visit, his caregiver reports an average of approximately 4 focal aware seizures per week per seizure calendar. He remains aware during the events. There is no separate preceding aura. No new seizure type was described today. Current medication doses were reviewed with the caregiver. Previously trialed zonisamide was discontinued due to mood side effects. Carbamazepine was discontinued because of inadequate seizure control.

The primary concern remains the ongoing seizure burden despite dual therapy and prior ASM trials. The family continues to track events on a seizure calendar. Medication adherence and the importance of contacting the epilepsy clinic before making any ASM changes were reviewed.

Assessment and Plan:

1. Focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures, nonlateralized, probable drug-resistant  
   Epilepsy began at age 3, with current frequency of approximately 4 seizures/week despite topiramate and lacosamide. He has also failed carbamazepine because of inadequate seizure control and could not tolerate zonisamide because of mood side effects. Overall presentation is consistent with focal epilepsy of unknown etiology and is likely drug-resistant. No separate aura is reported.
   - Continue topiramate 25 mg PO BID.
   - Continue lacosamide 30 mg PO BID.
   - No ASM changes without discussion with the epilepsy clinic.
   - Continue detailed seizure calendar, including date, duration, retained awareness, and recovery.
   - Obtain CMP, including serum bicarbonate, for topiramate monitoring.

   - Age-appropriate precautions reviewed, including direct supervision around water, bathing, climbing, and other situations in which impaired awareness or motor control could result in injury.
