Medical Decision-Making (MDM):  
Presentation is most consistent with breakthrough generalized tonic-clonic seizure in a patient with known drug-resistant epilepsy. Differential considered included syncope, convulsive syncope, metabolic provocation, toxic exposure, TIA, intracranial hemorrhage, and psychogenic non-epileptic event. Witnessed convulsive semiology, post-ictal period, and established epilepsy make epileptic seizure most likely. No persistent altered mental status or recurrent seizure to suggest status epilepticus. Neurologic exam now nonfocal.

No clear reversible trigger identified on initial ED evaluation. He remains hemodynamically stable and s/p return to neurologic baseline, but seizure frequency of approximately 7/week despite lamotrigine, carbamazepine, and lacosamide indicates poorly controlled, drug-resistant disease. Given high recurrence and injury risk, admission favored over discharge. Neurology will guide ASM changes and need for LTM/EMU evaluation.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough generalized tonic-clonic seizure.  
2. Drug-resistant generalized epilepsy, possibly focal onset with bilateral spread.  
3. GAD65-associated autoimmune epilepsy.

Disposition:  
Admit to Neurology, stable condition. Continue seizure precautions and home ASMs pending Neurology recommendations. No driving, swimming alone, climbing heights, operating heavy machinery, or bathing unattended. Medication compliance emphasized. Return/emergent escalation for seizure lasting >5 minutes, recurrent seizures without recovery, injury, breathing difficulty, or persistent confusion.
