Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: H/o combined generalized and focal epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 10 years. Current seizure frequency approximately 2/month despite improvement on topiramate. MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis, supporting a structural etiology. EEG obtained to characterize interictal epileptiform activity and r/o ongoing subclinical seizures.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Data were reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. No ASM changes were made for this study.

Relevant Medications: Current ASM is topiramate. Previously trialed levetiracetam, discontinued due to mood side effects; now s/p that medication trial. The epilepsy has otherwise been clinically drug responsive.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized, with a symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent hemispheric asymmetry.

Awake State: During maximal wakefulness, there were occasional bursts of generalized, frontally predominant 3–4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity lasting 0.5–2 seconds without clinical correlate. In addition, rare sharp waves were present independently over the left anterior-to-mid temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, with a field extending to FT9 and P7.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and sleep spindles. The left temporal sharp waves became mildly more frequent during sleep. No electrographic seizures were identified.

Hyperventilation: HV produced symmetric generalized theta slowing. One brief generalized spike-and-wave burst occurred without behavioral change.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings:
1. Occasional generalized 3–4 Hz spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges with frontal predominance.
2. Rare independent left anterior temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/T7.
3. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or sustained focal slowing.

Ictal Events: None captured. No clinical events were reported by the technologist, and no electrographic seizures occurred.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges and rare independent left anterior temporal epileptiform discharges.
2. These findings indicate both generalized and focal epileptogenic potential and are consistent with the patient’s established combined generalized and focal epilepsy.
3. The left temporal discharges may correlate with the known hippocampal sclerosis and structural epilepsy; clinical and imaging correlation is recommended.
4. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal ictal sample is not expected during a short routine study and does not exclude the reported generalized tonic-clonic seizures occurring approximately twice per month.
