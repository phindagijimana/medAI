Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: H/o drug-resistant focal structural epilepsy with focal impaired awareness seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures.

Clinical History: Seizure onset at age 33 years. Typical events are focal impaired awareness seizures, currently occurring approximately 2 times/year despite treatment with topiramate and brivaracetam. Previously trialed lacosamide, discontinued because of inadequate seizure control, and zonisamide, discontinued because of cognitive side effects. MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia, Palmini type IIA. MRI also reportedly shows a probable finding consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis. The patient denies photosensitivity.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and stepwise PS. Video was recorded throughout. The patient was awake and drowsy, with brief N1 and N2 sleep achieved. No ASM changes were made for this study.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz PDR with amplitudes of 30-60 µV, reactive to eye opening and closure. Low-amplitude frontocentral beta activity was present. No persistent interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state: Intermittent polymorphic 3-6 Hz delta-theta slowing was present over the left temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, occasionally extending to P7. This was most apparent during quiet wakefulness and drowsiness.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. N2 sleep contained symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. The focal left temporal slowing persisted intermittently during sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild symmetric background slowing without activation of epileptiform abnormalities.

Photic stimulation: PS elicited symmetric photic driving at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was present.

Interictal Findings: Rare, medium-amplitude sharp waves were present independently over the left anterior-to-mid temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, with a field to Fp1 and P7. These discharges were more evident during drowsiness and sleep. No independent right temporal IEDs were identified. No TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or generalized spike-and-wave discharges were seen.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-lead ECG showed a regular rhythm during the sampled recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal awake and sleep EEG due to rare left anterior-to-mid temporal sharp waves, indicating increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region.
2. Intermittent left temporal delta-theta slowing, supportive of focal cerebral dysfunction in that region.
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. No photoparoxysmal response was seen.

These findings support a focal epilepsy syndrome in the appropriate clinical context. Correlation with the structural MRI abnormalities, including Palmini type IIA focal cortical dysplasia and the probable finding consistent with mesial temporal sclerosis, is recommended. A normal or seizure-free brief recording would not exclude ongoing focal seizures.
