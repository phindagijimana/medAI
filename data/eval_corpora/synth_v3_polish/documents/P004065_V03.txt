Indication: H/o immune-mediated focal epilepsy associated with CASPR2 antibody, with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 11 and currently occurring approximately 5 times/week; r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: Patient has a hx of focal impaired-awareness seizures attributed to an immune etiology with CASPR2 antibody. Events began at age 11. Current reported frequency is approximately five per week. Patient denies complete loss of consciousness with the habitual events. Current ASM is levetiracetam. The epilepsy has been documented as drug-responsive; however, given the ongoing event frequency and multiple prior ASM trials, the overall clinical course is also described as probable/likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Clinical correlation is required.

Previously trialed ASMs include carbamazepine and brivaracetam, both discontinued due to mood side effects; perampanel, discontinued due to rash; and lacosamide and zonisamide, both discontinued due to sedation. Family hx is notable for the patient’s mother having generalized epilepsy of childhood.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and stepwise PS. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. Patient remained in NAD throughout the recording.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous and symmetric, with appropriate anterior-posterior organization. A well-formed 10 Hz PDR was present bilaterally and was reactive to eye opening. Low-amplitude beta activity was maximal over the frontocentral regions. No persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing was identified.

Awake State: Normal admixture of alpha and beta frequencies. No generalized or focal rhythmic delta activity was seen.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No sleep-activated epileptiform abnormality was identified.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal predominance. No epileptiform activation occurred.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal Findings: No focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs were present. No definite focal cerebral dysfunction was identified.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No patient-triggered events occurred.

ECG: Regular rhythm throughout the sampled recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states.  
2. No focal or generalized epileptiform discharges and no electrographic seizures were recorded.  
3. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude epilepsy. The reported semiology remains consistent with focal impaired-awareness seizures in the appropriate clinical context. This routine study does not establish electrographic localization or clarify the apparent discrepancy between charted drug-responsive epilepsy and the clinical concern for probable/likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy. If events remain frequent, prolonged video EEG monitoring may provide improved electroclinical characterization.
