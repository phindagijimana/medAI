Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Hx of combined generalized and focal epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic (GTC) seizures beginning at age 10. Reported habitual seizure frequency approximately 2/month. Structural etiology, with hippocampal sclerosis on prior brain MRI. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o ongoing subclinical seizures.

Current Antiseizure Medication:  Topiramate. Epilepsy is clinically classified as drug-responsive. Previously trialed levetiracetam, discontinued due to mood side effects; now s/p transition to topiramate.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded throughout the study. The patient was awake and drowsy, and stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background demonstrated appropriate organization with anterior-to-posterior gradients. A symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was noted anteriorly. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  During wakefulness, there were occasional generalized bursts of sharply contoured 3.5-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity, lasting 0.5-1.5 seconds and occurring without an apparent clinical correlate. In addition, rare independent temporal sharp waves were seen, maximal at F7/T7 and F8/T8, with a slight left-sided predominance. Occasional brief runs of rhythmic temporal theta activity were present independently over either hemisphere, without clear evolution or clinical change.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and increased diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Temporal IEDs were modestly activated by sleep. No electrographic seizure occurred.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced symmetric generalized theta and delta slowing. A single brief generalized spike-and-wave burst was elicited, without behavioral change.

Photic Stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal Findings:  Occasional generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges and rare independent bilateral temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/T7 and F8/T8. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing was identified.

Ictal Events:  None captured. No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were recorded.

ECG:  Regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, indicating increased generalized epileptic potential.
2. Rare independent bilateral temporal sharp waves, slightly more prominent on the left, indicating additional focal epileptic potential in the temporal regions.
3. No seizures were recorded.

The coexistence of generalized and focal IEDs is consistent with the patient’s established diagnosis of combined generalized and focal epilepsy. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended, particularly given the known structural finding of hippocampal sclerosis. A normal ictal sample during this brief recording does not exclude ongoing epilepsy or account for the reported GTC seizure frequency.
