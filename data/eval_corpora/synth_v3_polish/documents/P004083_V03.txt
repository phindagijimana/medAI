Indication: Hx of structural focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures; r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: Patient with h/o focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 4 years. Current seizure frequency is approximately one per month. Epilepsy is considered structural in etiology, with a vascular malformation identified on prior brain MRI. Seizures have been drug responsive on lacosamide, which remains the current ASM. No history of developmental delay. Denies febrile seizures in childhood.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved. No sedation was administered.

Description:

- **Background:** The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized, with a symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the bilateral frontocentral regions.

- **Awake state:** No persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing was identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or other rhythmic/periodic patterns.

- **Drowsiness/sleep:** Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse mixed theta activity. Brief vertex transients were present. Definitive stage N2 sleep architecture was not observed.

- **Hyperventilation:** HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges.

- **Photic stimulation:** PS at sequential flash frequencies produced symmetric photic driving, maximal over O1/O2. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

- **Interictal findings:** No epileptiform spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, or focal electrographic abnormalities were identified.

- **Ictal events:** No electrographic seizures were recorded. No clinical events or patient-reported habitual spells were captured.

- **ECG:** Single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm during sampled portions of the study.

Impression:

1. Normal awake and drowsy routine EEG. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. No habitual focal impaired-awareness seizure was captured. A normal routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy; clinical correlation with the established hx of structural focal epilepsy and the MRI-demonstrated vascular malformation is advised.
3. If further electroclinical characterization is needed because of continued approximately monthly events, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may provide greater diagnostic yield.

Current ASM: Lacosamide. Continue management through the treating neurology team. Should he have a cluster, use nasal midazolam rescue PRN and call 911.
