Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  H/o generalized epilepsy with GTC seizures beginning at age 7. Reported seizure frequency approximately 5/week. Epilepsy is classified as structural and described clinically as drug-responsive on current carbamazepine therapy. MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia, a lesion that is probably epileptogenic. EEG requested to characterize interictal epileptiform activity and r/o subclinical seizures. Patient denies photosensitivity and has no family history of epilepsy.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. The study was reviewed using referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy, and stage N2 sleep was achieved. No antiseizure medication was withheld; current ASM is carbamazepine.

Description:

Background:  
The awake background was continuous and symmetric, consisting of appropriately organized alpha and beta frequencies with an 9-Hz posterior dominant rhythm. The PDR was symmetric, well sustained, and reactive to eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  
Occasional brief bursts of generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, typically lasting 0.5-1.5 seconds. These discharges were maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with a broad bilateral field and no consistent focal lead-in. There was no associated clinical change on video. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs were seen.

Drowsy/Sleep:  
Drowsiness was marked by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges increased modestly during drowsiness and sleep but did not evolve in frequency, morphology, or spatial distribution. No electrographic seizure occurred.

Hyperventilation:  
HV produced expected diffuse high-amplitude theta slowing. Two brief generalized spike-and-wave bursts occurred during recovery, without behavioral correlate.

Photic Stimulation:  
Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited, consistent with the reported absence of photosensitivity.

Interictal Findings:  
Occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave IEDs, frontally predominant and enhanced in drowsiness/sleep. No independent focal epileptiform discharge was identified over the region sampled by standard scalp electrodes.

Ictal Events:  
None captured. No clinical spells, electrographic seizures, or nonconvulsive status epilepticus.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, enhanced during drowsiness and sleep. These findings support a generalized epileptiform potential in a patient with h/o GTC seizures.

2. No focal slowing or focal IED was identified. Although MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia that is likely epileptogenic in the appropriate clinical context, this routine scalp recording does not electrographically localize epileptogenicity to that lesion.

3. No seizures were recorded. Clinical correlation is required; a normal ictal sample during this brief study does not exclude epilepsy. Continued management with the treating neurologist is recommended given the reported drug-responsive course on carbamazepine.
