Indication:  H/o combined generalized and focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 10. Events currently occur approximately 7/week but have improved on levetiracetam and lamotrigine, consistent with drug-responsive epilepsy. Structural etiology is suspected given MRI evidence of a vascular malformation. EEG obtained to characterize interictal abnormalities and r/o subclinical seizures.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded throughout. The patient was awake and drowsy, and stage N2 sleep was achieved. Current ASMs were levetiracetam and lamotrigine; neither medication was withdrawn for this study.

Description:

Background:  In the awake state, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An anterior-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent background asymmetry.

Awake state:  Occasional brief bursts of generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity were present, lasting less than 1 second and without clinical correlate. Independently, rare sharp waves were maximal over the left anterior temporal region at F7/FT9, with a field to T7.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and increased diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Left anterior temporal sharp waves were mildly activated by sleep. No electrographic seizure occurred.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing. A single brief generalized spike-and-wave burst was seen without behavioral change.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was identified.

Interictal findings:  
1. Occasional brief generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges with frontal predominance.  
2. Rare independent left anterior temporal IEDs, maximal at F7/FT9.  
3. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing.

Ictal events:  No clinical or electrographic seizures were captured. No habitual focal impaired-awareness event occurred during the recording.

Clinical History:  Seizures began at age 10 and are described as focal impaired-awareness events, currently occurring approximately seven times per week. Epilepsy has shown a clinically meaningful response to levetiracetam and lamotrigine and is categorized as drug responsive. Previously trialed topiramate was discontinued due to GI intolerance. MRI has demonstrated a vascular malformation, supporting a structural contribution. The patient denies head trauma. A possible autoimmune contribution has also been raised, although this EEG does not establish an autoimmune etiology.

Impression:

1. Abnormal awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges and rare independent left anterior temporal sharp waves.  
2. These findings support increased epileptogenic potential with both generalized and focal features, consistent with the reported combined generalized and focal epilepsy phenotype.  
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. Correlation with seizure semiology and the location of the known vascular malformation is recommended.
