Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  H/o combined generalized and focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 10; r/o interictal epileptiform activity. Structural etiology with focal cortical dysplasia on prior MRI.

Clinical History:  Seizures are characterized by impaired awareness without a preceding aura or complete loss of consciousness. The patient denies aura and denies episodes of frank loss of consciousness. Currently seizure-free, with 0 seizures/month, on topiramate and valproate. Epilepsy has been drug responsive. Previously trialed lamotrigine, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control.

Technique:  A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy, and stage N2 sleep was achieved. Current ASMs were not held for this study.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized, with a symmetric 9-Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. No persistent focal background asymmetry was identified.

Awake State:  During maximal wakefulness, there were occasional sharply contoured theta transients over the left frontotemporal region, maximal at F7/T7, with rare unequivocal sharp waves showing an appropriate field and after-going slow wave. In addition, rare brief bursts of generalized, bifrontally predominant 3.5-4 Hz spike-and-wave activity were present, lasting less than 1 second and without clinical correlate.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Left frontotemporal sharp waves were mildly activated during sleep. No electrographic seizure occurred.

Hyperventilation:  HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of additional epileptiform abnormalities.

Photic Stimulation:  PS elicited a symmetric photic driving response at multiple flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal Findings:  
1. Rare left frontotemporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/T7.  
2. Rare, very brief generalized bifrontally predominant spike-and-wave discharges at 3.5-4 Hz.  
3. No sustained focal slowing.

Ictal Events:  No clinical or electrographic seizures were captured. No typical focal impaired-awareness event was reported by the patient or observed on video.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to rare left frontotemporal epileptiform discharges and rare generalized spike-and-wave discharges. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support increased epileptogenic potential of both focal and generalized network types, consistent with the patient’s diagnosis of combined generalized and focal epilepsy.  
2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal seizure frequency during this study does not exclude epilepsy; clinically, the patient remains seizure-free at 0 seizures/month on topiramate and valproate.  
3. The focal discharges may be relevant to the known structural etiology, including MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia; correlation with lesion location and prior imaging is recommended.
