Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: H/o generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 27; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity and r/o focal features.

Clinical History: The patient has generalized tonic-clonic seizures with a reported frequency of approximately 5/week. Epilepsy etiology remains unknown. The epilepsy has been drug responsive on the current regimen of zonisamide and lamotrigine. Levetiracetam was previously trialed and discontinued due to GI intolerance. Denies head trauma; no seizures s/p traumatic injury. No habitual clinical event occurred during this recording.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included HV for 3 minutes and intermittent PS. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized. A 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present over the frontocentral regions. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Drowsy/Sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing. No clinical event was elicited.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present across several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal Findings: Occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, maximal bifrontally at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with a broad and symmetric field. Discharges occurred singly and in brief 3-4 Hz runs lasting up to 2 seconds without clinical correlate. No independent focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or focal rhythmic delta activity were seen.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical GTC events were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, at times occurring in brief 3-4 Hz runs. These findings are supportive of a generalized epilepsy syndrome and are concordant with the reported generalized tonic-clonic seizure type.
2. No focal epileptiform abnormality, persistent focal slowing, or electrographic seizure was identified. A normal or seizure-free sampling during this short recording would not exclude additional seizure types.
3. Clinical correlation and continued treatment with zonisamide and lamotrigine are advised; the epilepsy is currently classified as drug responsive. Levetiracetam remains a prior ASM, discontinued because of GI intolerance.
4. If MRI demonstrates progression, obtain PET/SPECT for concordance. Should he have a seizure cluster, use nasal midazolam rescue PRN and call 911.
