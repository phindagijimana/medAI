Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with seizure onset at age 8, generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and epilepsy currently classified as generalized and structurally mediated in the setting of MRI-reported hippocampal sclerosis. The patient is presently seizure-free, with a current seizure frequency of zero per month, on triple-ASM therapy with perampanel, levetiracetam, and oxcarbazepine.

The epilepsy is drug-resistant based on failure or intolerance of multiple prior ASMs. The MRI finding introduces important electroclinical uncertainty. Hippocampal sclerosis may indicate that the events are possibly focal onset seizures progressing to bilateral tonic-clonic activity rather than primary generalized seizures, but this cannot be concluded without direct review or repeat acquisition of EEG/VEEG data.

In addition, the available clinical record is suggestive of a possible PNES component. This is not a definitive diagnosis. If events recur, capturing representative episodes in the EMU would be important both to r/o nonepileptic events and to determine whether there is a focal or generalized electrographic onset.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Fycompa, Keppra, and Trileptal without change for now, given the current seizure-free status. The patient should continue the doses and frequencies prescribed by the treating neurologist; exact dosing should be reconciled in the EHR.

2. Do not abruptly discontinue any ASM. Medication refills and questions regarding the current regimen may be addressed with the prescribing clinician or our clinic PRN.

3. Obtain the original MRI images for review, with particular attention to the reported hippocampal sclerosis. The written report alone does not establish concordance with the seizure network.

4. Obtain prior routine EEG and any LTM/EMU reports or recordings, if available. These data are needed to clarify whether the epilepsy is generalized versus possibly focal onset with rapid bilateral propagation.

5. Should recurrence occur, we will revisit presurgical workup. This would include inpatient EMU VEEG to characterize typical events, r/o a PNES component, and determine electrographic onset. Updated epilepsy-protocol MRI and additional presurgical studies could then be considered based on the VEEG results.

6. If recurrent seizures remain drug-resistant and a focal network is confirmed, surgical and neuromodulation options can be reviewed in a multidisciplinary epilepsy conference. Depending on localization and candidacy, options could include resection or a device such as the NeuroPace RNS System. Other neuromodulation approaches, including LivaNova VNS SenTiva or Medtronic DBS Percept, would depend on the final electroclinical classification. No device intervention is recommended while the patient remains seizure-free and before the epilepsy type is clarified.
