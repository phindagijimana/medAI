Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Hx of focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, onset at age 56; r/o interictal epileptiform activity.

Clinical History: Patient with h/o focal impaired-awareness seizures of unknown etiology. Historical treatment course is consistent with likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy, although the patient is currently seizure-free with a reported frequency of 0 seizures/month. Current ASMs are levetiracetam, topiramate, and lacosamide. Previously trialed brivaracetam, discontinued due to GI intolerance, and perampanel, discontinued due to inadequate seizure control.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG channel. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable technical limits. The recording included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. No ASM changes were made for this routine study.

Description:

Background: In maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm measuring 25-50 µV, reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was preserved. Low-amplitude beta activity was present over the bilateral frontocentral regions. No persistent hemispheric asymmetry or focal attenuation was identified.

Awake state: Background activity remained continuous and symmetric. No focal delta slowing, lateralized rhythmic delta activity, LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs were present.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was associated with expected attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform abnormalities were activated by sleep.

Hyperventilation: HV produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation.

Photic stimulation: PS at sequential flash frequencies produced symmetric photic driving, maximal posteriorly. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings: No definite spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, or other IEDs were identified. No focal slowing was seen.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures were recorded. No clinical events or patient-reported typical spells occurred during the study.

ECG: Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal awake, drowsy, and sleep routine EEG. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. The absence of epileptiform abnormalities on a single routine EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. Clinical correlation is recommended in this patient with focal impaired-awareness seizures and a likely drug-resistant focal epilepsy phenotype, currently seizure-free on levetiracetam, topiramate, and lacosamide.
