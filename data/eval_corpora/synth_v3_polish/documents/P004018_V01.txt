Chief Complaint: Follow-up generalized epilepsy

History of Present Illness:  
The patient is a 33-year-old woman with hx of generalized epilepsy manifested by generalized tonic-clonic seizures, onset at age 29. Her epilepsy has been considered structural in etiology, with prior brain MRI demonstrating hippocampal sclerosis. She remains on Depakote (valproate) monotherapy and has been drug responsive.

She reports an average seizure frequency of approximately three generalized tonic-clonic seizures per year. She remains on valproate without a reported interval medication change. Exact dates of recent events were not available today; she was encouraged to maintain a seizure diary documenting date, duration, possible triggers, missed doses, and recovery. She was previously treated with Fycompa (perampanel), which was discontinued because of mood side effects. This is a prior ASM and is not part of her current regimen.

Her primary ongoing concern is the intermittent breakthrough convulsive seizure burden despite overall response to valproate. We reviewed the importance of adherence and avoiding abrupt interruption of ASM therapy. Given use of valproate in a woman of childbearing potential, its substantial pregnancy-related risks were reviewed, including major congenital malformations and adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes. She was advised to contact neurology before any planned pregnancy and not to stop valproate abruptly.
