Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy manifested by focal impaired-awareness seizures, with onset at age 75. The reported seizure burden at presentation was approximately four seizures per week. MRI demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis, supporting a structural etiology.

The epilepsy has been characterized as drug responsive to the current combination of carbamazepine and valproate. Based on the information available today, this is not established drug-resistant epilepsy. The prior perampanel trial was limited by rash rather than documented lack of efficacy.

Because the MRI finding is hippocampal sclerosis, interval imaging should be interpreted carefully for stability versus progression. If progression is demonstrated, additional functional imaging may be useful to assess concordance. Likewise, any future recurrence after the current drug-responsive course would warrant reassessment of seizure burden, medication therapy, and possible presurgical evaluation.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Tegretol (carbamazepine) and Depakote (valproate) on the established outpatient regimen. No dose changes can be recommended from the available documentation because the current doses and frequencies were not supplied.

2. Reconcile the medication list with the patient, pharmacy, and referring clinician, including exact carbamazepine and valproate formulations, doses, and schedules.

3. Avoid re-exposure to Fycompa (perampanel) given the prior rash. This remains a prior ASM and should not be listed as an active medication.

4. Review the prior brain MRI directly when images are available, with attention to the reported hippocampal sclerosis and its laterality. If MRI shows progression, obtain PET/SPECT for concordance.

5. Continue clinical surveillance for recurrence of focal impaired-awareness seizures. Should recurrence occur, revisit presurgical workup, including consideration of prolonged VEEG/LTM in the EMU as appropriate to define electroclinical onset.

6. At present, the available information supports a drug-responsive structural focal epilepsy rather than DRE. Any future presurgical discussion should be based on documented recurrence, updated imaging, and concordant localization data.
