Indication:  Drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures occurring approximately six times weekly. Seizure onset was at age 28. Current antiseizure medications are zonisamide and valproate. Prior MRI demonstrates encephalomalacia, supporting a structural etiology. EEG obtained to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. The recording was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present, with low-amplitude beta activity anteriorly. There was no persistent hemispheric asymmetry. The ECG channel demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Awake state:  Intermittent polymorphic 3-6 Hz delta-theta slowing was present over the left temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, occasionally extending to P7. This activity was more prominent during drowsiness but was also seen during wakefulness.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Normal sleep architecture was preserved.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild symmetric background slowing without activation of epileptiform discharges.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Occasional sharp waves were present over the left anterior-to-mid temporal region, maximal at F7 and T7, with a field extending to Fp1 and P7. These discharges were more frequent during drowsiness and sleep. No independent right hemispheric epileptiform discharges were identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured during this routine recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional left anterior-to-mid temporal sharp waves, indicating increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region.
2. Intermittent left temporal polymorphic delta-theta slowing, consistent with focal cerebral dysfunction in that region.
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded.

These findings support a focal epilepsy syndrome in the appropriate clinical context. The patient’s established clinical diagnosis is drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures and a structural etiology, with MRI-demonstrated encephalomalacia. Correlation with the location of the structural lesion is recommended. If further electroclinical localization is required, prolonged video EEG monitoring may be considered.
