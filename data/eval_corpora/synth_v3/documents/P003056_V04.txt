Emergency Department Provider Note

Chief Complaint: Breakthrough focal seizure.

HPI:  
51-year-old female with immune-mediated focal epilepsy, onset at age 23, presents by EMS after a witnessed breakthrough seizure. Per EMS report from xxx EMS, family observed her typical event consisting of right facial and upper-extremity twitching with preserved awareness and ability to follow commands. Episode lasted approximately 2 minutes and resolved without rescue medication. No generalized convulsions, loss of consciousness, fall, head strike, tongue biting, or incontinence. She had brief fatigue afterward but no prolonged confusion.

Patient reports focal aware seizures approximately once monthly. Medical Decision-Making (MDM):  
Presentation is most consistent with a breakthrough focal aware seizure in a patient with known immune-mediated focal epilepsy. Event was typical for her, brief, self-limited, and without impaired awareness or status epilepticus. No evidence of trauma, CNS infection, major metabolic trigger, intoxication, stroke, or intracranial hemorrhage. Mild hyponatremia may be related to oxcarbazepine but is not severe enough to independently explain the event or require acute correction.

Differential included epileptic seizure, syncope, TIA, and psychogenic nonepileptic event. Preserved awareness with stereotyped focal motor activity and known epilepsy favors focal seizure. No prodrome, hypotension, dysrhythmia, persistent deficit, or atypical features suggesting syncope/TIA. No further seizure activity during observation. No Ativan (lorazepam) or IV antiseizure load indicated. Patient tolerated PO, ambulated steadily, and remained at baseline.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough focal aware seizure.  
2. Immune-mediated focal epilepsy, drug responsive.  
3. Mild hyponatremia.

Disposition:  
Discharged home in stable condition after neurology consultation. Continue oxcarbazepine and zonisamide exactly as prescribed; do not miss or double doses. Contact epilepsy clinic for expedited follow-up and review of pending level and sodium. Seizure precautions reviewed, including no driving per applicable state law, no swimming/bathing alone, no heights, and no operation of dangerous machinery. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, new weakness, persistent confusion, severe headache, fever, injury, or other worsening symptoms.
