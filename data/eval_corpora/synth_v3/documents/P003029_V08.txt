MRI Brain — Epilepsy Protocol

Exam Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx

Indication: Generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures beginning at age 66, occurring approximately three times per month; drug responsive on oxcarbazepine. Evaluation for structural etiology.

Comparison: None.

Technique: Multiplanar, multisequence MRI of the brain was performed on a 3T scanner. Sequences included sagittal T1, axial and coronal T2, axial and coronal FLAIR with thin coronal imaging through the mesial temporal structures, double inversion recovery, axial DWI/ADC, axial SWI, and 3D T1 MPRAGE. Postcontrast T1-weighted sequences were obtained following intravenous administration of gadolinium-based contrast.

Findings:

Ventricles and Extra-axial Spaces: Ventricles and sulci are within normal limits for age. No hydrocephalus or extra-axial collection. No midline shift.

Brain Parenchyma:

Supratentorial: There is an approximately 2.2 x 1.8 x 1.7 cm expansile cortical and subcortical T2/FLAIR hyperintense lesion involving the left superior frontal gyrus. The lesion is T1 hypointense and demonstrates no associated restricted diffusion, susceptibility artifact, or appreciable enhancement. Mild localized sulcal effacement is present without significant mass effect. Findings are suspicious for a primary low-grade glial neoplasm and may represent a structural epileptogenic substrate. No acute infarction or intracranial hemorrhage.

Infratentorial: No focal parenchymal signal abnormality or enhancing lesion.

Mesial Temporal Structures: The hippocampi are symmetric in size and signal, with preserved internal architecture. No hippocampal volume loss or abnormal T2/FLAIR hyperintensity. No evidence of mesial temporal sclerosis.

Cortex: Aside from the left superior frontal lesion described above, cortical morphology and gyration are preserved. No additional focal cortical thickening, blurring of the gray-white junction, polymicrogyria, heterotopia, or other malformation of cortical development.

White Matter: Minimal scattered punctate supratentorial white matter FLAIR hyperintensities are nonspecific and likely reflect mild chronic microvascular change.

Basal Ganglia and Thalami: Normal signal and morphology.

Brainstem and Cerebellum: Normal. No cerebellar tonsillar ectopia.

Vasculature: Major intracranial vascular flow voids are preserved.

Skull Base and Calvarium: No focal marrow-replacing lesion.

Paranasal Sinuses and Mastoid Air Cells: Mild bilateral ethmoid sinus mucosal thickening. Mastoid air cells are clear.

Orbits: Unremarkable.

Impression:

1. Nonenhancing 2.2 cm expansile cortical/subcortical lesion in the left superior frontal gyrus, suspicious for a low-grade primary glial tumor. This may constitute a structural epileptogenic substrate. Neurosurgical consultation and correlation with EEG localization are recommended.
2. Normal appearance of the bilateral hippocampi. No mesial temporal sclerosis or additional malformation of cortical development.
3. No acute infarction, intracranial hemorrhage, hydrocephalus, or midline shift.
