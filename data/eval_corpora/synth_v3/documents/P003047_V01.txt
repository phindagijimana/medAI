Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy beginning at age 28, manifested by focal impaired-awareness seizures. The current seizure frequency is approximately six per week despite ongoing therapy with zonisamide and valproate. The epilepsy is classified as drug resistant and has a structural etiology, with encephalomalacia reported on prior MRI.

The high ongoing seizure burden warrants expedited diagnostic clarification. The principal next step is to establish the electroclinical features of the habitual events and determine whether seizure onset can be localized. The relationship between the reported encephalomalacia and the seizure onset zone remains unknown because neither the MRI images nor prior EEG data were available for independent review.

If the habitual seizures can be captured and localized, the patient may be appropriate for discussion in a multidisciplinary epilepsy surgery conference. Any determination regarding resection, ablation, or neuromodulation with VNS, RNS, or DBS would require a complete presurgical evaluation and cannot be made from the currently available information.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue zonisamide and valproate for now. I would not recommend an empiric dose change until the exact formulations, doses, dosing schedule, adherence, tolerability, and prior treatment response have been verified.

2. Complete medication reconciliation through the patient, pharmacy records, and referring neurology records. Please forward the full list of previously tried ASMs, maximum doses, duration of therapy, efficacy, adverse effects, and reasons for discontinuation.

3. Arrange inpatient EMU admission for prolonged VEEG/LTM to capture the habitual focal impaired-awareness events. The goals will be to confirm that the reported episodes are epileptic seizures, characterize the electroclinical semiology, quantify the seizure burden, and assess localization or lateralization.

4. Obtain the prior brain MRI images for direct review. If the existing study is not an epilepsy-protocol examination or is otherwise inadequate for presurgical assessment, an updated epilepsy-protocol brain MRI should be considered to better define the encephalomalacia and assess for additional structural abnormalities.

5. Obtain all prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, and EMU reports and, when possible, the original EEG data. Concordance among semiology, ictal EEG, interictal findings, and MRI will be important in determining candidacy for further surgical evaluation.

6. After the above information is available, we will reassess treatment options. If the data support a consistent focal onset, the case can be reviewed at the multidisciplinary epilepsy surgery conference. Additional testing would be selected based on the results of VEEG and imaging.

7. Until seizures are better controlled, the patient should follow standard seizure precautions, including no driving, avoiding unsupervised swimming or bathing, avoiding heights and open flames, and avoiding operation of dangerous machinery. Emergency evaluation is appropriate for a prolonged seizure, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, or a clinically meaningful change from the usual pattern.
