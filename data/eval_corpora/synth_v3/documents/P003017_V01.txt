Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy characterized by focal aware seizures, with onset at age 10. The seizures currently occur approximately twice per week. The epilepsy is drug resistant, and the underlying etiology remains unknown.

The patient is reportedly taking levetiracetam, carbamazepine, brivaracetam, and valproate. Because doses and frequencies were not supplied, medication changes cannot be made safely on the basis of the available information alone. A complete reconciliation is especially important given the reported concurrent treatment with levetiracetam and brivaracetam.

Given the persistent seizure frequency and drug-resistant status, further evaluation at a comprehensive epilepsy center is appropriate. The initial goals are to verify the event semiology, confirm the medication history, assess for a structural etiology, and obtain electrographic data. Candidacy for resective surgery, laser ablation, VNS, RNS, or DBS cannot yet be determined.

Recommendations / Plan

1. The patient should continue the currently prescribed antiseizure medications without abrupt discontinuation while the regimen is being verified. I did not recommend dose changes today because the exact doses and frequencies are not available.

2. We will complete medication reconciliation, including formulation, dose, schedule, adherence, adverse effects, pharmacy records, and the chronology of prior antiseizure medication trials. The need for both levetiracetam and brivaracetam should be specifically reviewed.

3. We recommend obtaining prior EEG reports and tracings, MRI reports and images, laboratory results, and neurology notes. If these studies have not been completed or cannot be obtained, updated testing should be arranged.

4. A brain MRI using an epilepsy protocol is recommended to evaluate for a structural cause of focal epilepsy.

5. EEG evaluation is recommended. Given events occurring approximately twice weekly, inpatient EMU admission for prolonged VEEG should be considered to characterize typical events and assess seizure localization. The timing will depend on review of any prior EEG data.

6. Baseline laboratory monitoring should be obtained as clinically appropriate for the confirmed regimen, including blood counts, hepatic and metabolic testing, and antiseizure medication levels when useful.

7. The patient was asked to maintain a seizure diary documenting event frequency, duration, associated symptoms, and any missed medication doses. A witness description or video may also be useful if it can be obtained safely.

8. General seizure precautions were reviewed, including avoidance of unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operating dangerous machinery. The patient should follow applicable driving restrictions and should not drive unless cleared under the relevant state requirements.

9. Once the MRI, EEG/VEEG, and complete medication history are available, we will determine whether to optimize medical therapy or proceed with a formal presurgical evaluation. Additional testing may be considered depending on the localization data.

10. Follow-up will be arranged after the requested records and diagnostic studies are available. The patient should contact the treating neurology team sooner for increased seizure frequency, medication intolerance, or other concerning changes.
