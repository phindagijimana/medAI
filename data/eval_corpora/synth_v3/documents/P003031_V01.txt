Assessment / Impression

The patient has childhood-onset focal epilepsy with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Seizures began at approximately 5 years of age and are currently reported at a frequency of three per week. The epilepsy is classified as drug resistant despite current treatment with valproate and topiramate. Etiology remains unknown.

Given the high reported seizure burden, further diagnostic characterization is indicated. Inpatient EMU admission for video-EEG monitoring would help confirm the electroclinical diagnosis, characterize the focal onset, identify any lateralizing or localizing features, and determine whether all habitual events represent the same seizure type. A dedicated epilepsy-protocol MRI is also appropriate if one has not already been completed or if the prior study is unavailable for direct review.

If focal epilepsy is confirmed and seizures remain uncontrolled, the patient may be appropriate for a comprehensive surgical evaluation. The eventual treatment options could include resective or ablative surgery if a suitable epileptogenic focus is identified. If resection is not feasible, neuromodulation with VNS, RNS, or DBS may be considered. No specific procedure can be recommended until the noninvasive evaluation is complete.
