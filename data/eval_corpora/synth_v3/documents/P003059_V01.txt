Assessment / Impression

The patient has focal epilepsy of unknown etiology, manifested by focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 24, and the current frequency is approximately three per month.

The epilepsy is classified in the supplied information as drug-responsive, and the patient remains on dual therapy with oxcarbazepine and zonisamide. Drug-responsive does not necessarily mean seizure-free; the ongoing event frequency requires confirmation and careful review of medication doses, adherence, tolerability, and the degree of benefit attributable to each ASM. Based on the limited information available, I would not characterize the epilepsy as drug-resistant.

The reported MRI lesion is considered incidental in the available record. Without the original study or report, its relationship to seizure onset cannot be assessed. The current information does not identify a definitive epilepsy etiology or establish a need for surgical evaluation.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Trileptal (oxcarbazepine) and Zonegran (zonisamide) at the current prescribed doses for now. I would not make a dose change until the exact dose, frequency, adherence, and tolerability of both medications have been verified.

2. Reconcile the medication list using the patient’s prescription bottles, pharmacy records, and referring neurology documentation. This is particularly important before considering any escalation or taper.

3. Maintain a seizure diary documenting event date, approximate duration, suspected focal onset features, progression to bilateral tonic-clonic activity, and recovery. This will help confirm whether the reported frequency remains approximately three per month and whether treatment changes affect seizure burden.

4. Please forward the original brain MRI images and full radiology report for direct review. If the study cannot be obtained or is not adequate for epilepsy evaluation, an updated epilepsy-protocol MRI may be considered after review of the available imaging.

5. Please forward any prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, LTM, or EMU reports. If no prior electrographic evaluation is available, an EEG may be obtained to further characterize the focal epilepsy.

6. The patient should observe standard seizure precautions. No driving unless permitted under applicable law and cleared by the treating clinician. The patient should avoid unsupervised swimming, bathing alone, working at unprotected heights, and operating dangerous machinery while seizures remain active.

7. We discussed that prolonged convulsive activity, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, or breathing difficulty requires emergency evaluation.

8. At present, I do not recommend epilepsy surgery evaluation solely on the basis of the information supplied. If seizures continue despite adequately dosed and tolerated ASM trials, the treatment history and drug-resistance status should be reassessed, with consideration of EMU evaluation as clinically appropriate.

9. Follow-up should be arranged after medication doses, prior EEG data, and MRI images have been obtained. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if seizure frequency increases or medication-related concerns arise.
