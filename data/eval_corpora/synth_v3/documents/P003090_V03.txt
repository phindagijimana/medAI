Indication: Focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, reportedly beginning at age 24 and currently occurring approximately five times per month. The epilepsy is considered genetic in etiology, with a reported causative SCN1A variant, c.5734C>T. The patient’s seizures have been drug responsive and are currently treated with levetiracetam and oxcarbazepine. Brain MRI demonstrated nonspecific white matter changes. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, supplemented by a single-channel ECG. The study was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was appropriately organized, with clearly defined anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradients. There was a symmetric, well-sustained 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. Anteriorly, the background consisted of expected lower-voltage mixed alpha and beta frequencies. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, the background remained continuous and symmetric. No generalized slowing, breach rhythm, or attenuation was identified.

Drowsiness / sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the posterior dominant rhythm and emergence of diffuse, symmetric theta activity. Positive occipital sharp transients of sleep were occasionally present. No definitive vertex waves, sleep spindles, or K complexes were recorded.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric buildup of theta activity without focal abnormality or epileptiform activation.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at sequential flash frequencies produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal spikes or sharp waves, generalized spike-and-wave discharges, periodic discharges, or TIRDA. No electrographic pattern suggesting increased epileptogenic potential was seen.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured during this recording.

ECG: The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness and drowsiness. There are no focal, lateralized, or epileptiform abnormalities.
2. No electrographic seizures were recorded.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. The absence of interictal epileptiform discharges on this brief recording does not preclude focal epilepsy, including focal impaired-awareness seizures. Clinical correlation is recommended.
