Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy of structural etiology, with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 70. MRI reportedly demonstrates encephalomalacia, which may provide the structural substrate for the focal epilepsy. The epilepsy has been classified as drug-resistant, although the detailed prior medication history needed to confirm the basis for that classification was not included with the referral.

The patient is presently seizure-free, with zero seizures in the current monthly interval, while taking dual therapy with levetiracetam and topiramate. The exact medication doses, duration of seizure freedom, and date of the most recent seizure remain unclear. These details are important before considering medication changes or determining whether additional diagnostic or surgical evaluation is appropriate.

Current seizure freedom does not by itself negate the prior drug-resistant designation. For now, I would avoid changing an apparently effective regimen until medication doses, tolerability, adherence, prior treatment failures, and the longitudinal seizure history can be confirmed. If seizures recur despite appropriate therapy, the combination of focal epilepsy, a reported structural lesion, and historical drug resistance would support further evaluation at a comprehensive epilepsy center.
