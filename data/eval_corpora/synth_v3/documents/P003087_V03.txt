Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset was at age 56, with a reported frequency of approximately four seizures per month. Etiology is unknown. The epilepsy is characterized clinically as drug-responsive. Current antiseizure medications are valproate and lacosamide.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including a single-lead electrocardiogram. The recording was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined anterior-to-posterior gradient. A 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and was reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of lower-voltage beta activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During wakefulness, there were occasional generalized bursts of high-amplitude 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. These were frontally predominant, synchronous, and symmetric, with shifting hemispheric emphasis. Bursts generally lasted 0.5-2 seconds and were not associated with a definite clinical correlate on video. No prolonged runs meeting electrographic seizure criteria were identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the posterior dominant rhythm attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized epileptiform discharges were modestly activated by drowsiness and sleep. No focal epileptiform discharge was seen.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild diffuse theta slowing. There was no sustained activation of epileptiform activity and no electrographic seizure.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced symmetric photic driving at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3.5-4.5 Hz, enhanced during drowsiness and sleep. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing were present.

Ictal events: No clinical or electrographic seizures were captured. No habitual generalized tonic-clonic event occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with activation during drowsiness and sleep.

2. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are consistent with the reported diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

3. No electrographic seizure or focal cerebral dysfunction was identified during this study. The absence of a recorded seizure does not exclude epilepsy. Clinical correlation with the reported late onset at age 56, seizure frequency of approximately four per month, unknown etiology, and response to valproate and lacosamide is recommended.
