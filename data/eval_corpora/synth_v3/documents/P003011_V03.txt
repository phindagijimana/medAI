Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of a patient with drug-responsive combined generalized and focal epilepsy attributed to a metabolic etiology. Seizure onset occurred at age 29 years. The reported seizure type includes generalized tonic-clonic seizures, occurring approximately five times per month. Current antiseizure medication is topiramate.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system, with additional electrocardiographic monitoring. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures consisted of 3 minutes of hyperventilation and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and organized, with appropriate anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradients. A symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of low-voltage anterior beta and diffuse theta activity was seen. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or generalized background slowing.

Awake state: During wakefulness, occasional brief bursts of generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present. These bursts lasted 0.5-2 seconds and did not have an identifiable clinical correlate. In addition, rare independent sharp waves were seen over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, with a field extending to FT9. These discharges were morphologically distinct from the generalized activity.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity increased. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. Both the generalized discharges and left anterior temporal sharp waves were activated by sleep. No TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or electrographic seizures were identified.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing. There was a modest increase in generalized spike-and-wave fragments, without sustained ictal evolution or clinical change.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1-20 Hz produced symmetric posterior photic driving at several frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was observed.

Interictal findings:
1. Occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3.5-4.5 Hz.
2. Rare independent left anterior temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/T7.
3. No persistent focal slowing.

Ictal events: No clinical or electrographic seizures were captured. Specifically, no generalized tonic-clonic seizure occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges and rare independent left anterior temporal epileptiform discharges.
2. The generalized discharges indicate increased susceptibility to generalized seizures, while the independent left anterior temporal sharp waves indicate an additional focal epileptogenic potential.
3. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support the established diagnosis of combined generalized and focal epilepsy. No electrographic seizure was recorded.
