Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of epilepsy of unknown type characterized by unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures. Seizures began at age 33 and are reported to occur approximately four times per month. The epilepsy is considered structurally mediated and drug responsive; the patient is currently treated with topiramate. Prior brain MRI reportedly demonstrated a vascular malformation.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including standard bipolar and referential montages. A single-channel electrocardiogram was recorded. Activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. The technical quality of the study was adequate for interpretation.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity over the bilateral posterior head regions. The PDR was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present, with low-amplitude beta activity over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No focal delta or theta slowing was identified. There were no lateralized rhythmic delta patterns, LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR, increased generalized theta activity, and slow roving eye movements. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform abnormalities were activated by drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal predominance. No epileptiform discharges or clinical events were elicited.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, polyspike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, or other lateralizing interictal abnormalities. No electrographic correlate referable to the reported vascular malformation was evident during this recording.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.

2. This recording does not provide electrographic evidence permitting classification of the epilepsy as focal or generalized; the epilepsy type and onset of the reported tonic-clonic seizures remain unknown. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. Clinical correlation with seizure semiology and the known structural MRI abnormality, described as a vascular malformation, is recommended.
