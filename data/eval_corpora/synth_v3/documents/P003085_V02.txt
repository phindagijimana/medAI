Emergency Department Provider Note

Chief Complaint: Breakthrough seizure.

HPI: 23-year-old male with drug-resistant focal epilepsy, onset at age 10, presents by ambulance after a witnessed breakthrough focal impaired-awareness seizure. Per EMS report from xxx EMS, family observed behavioral arrest, staring, and decreased responsiveness lasting approximately 2 minutes, followed by confusion for 10–15 minutes. No reported generalized tonic-clonic activity, fall, head strike, tongue biting, or incontinence. Glucose normal en route. No benzodiazepines administered.

Patient reports adherence to Trileptal (oxcarbazepine) and Fycompa (perampanel), including his most recent doses. He had otherwise been seizure-free, averaging 0 seizures/month prior to today. No recent medication changes. Epilepsy etiology remains unknown; prior MRI reportedly showed an incidental lesion not felt to explain his seizures. Denies fever, headache, neck stiffness, URI symptoms, vomiting, diarrhea, dysuria, sleep deprivation, EtOH use, or recreational drugs.

Medical Decision-Making (MDM): Presentation is most consistent with a breakthrough focal impaired-awareness seizure in a patient with known drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Brief postictal confusion has fully resolved. No evidence of status epilepticus. He has no fever, meningismus, persistent AMS, focal deficit, significant metabolic derangement, intoxication, infection, or traumatic injury. Although he reports adherence, subtherapeutic antiseizure medication exposure remains possible; send-out level is pending.

Differential considered included epileptic seizure, syncope, TIA, metabolic event, and psychogenic nonepileptic event. Witnessed stereotyped impaired awareness with postictal confusion favors seizure. No prodromal chest symptoms, persistent deficit, or features suggesting acute stroke. No indication for IV Ativan (lorazepam) or antiseizure medication loading because the event self-terminated and there was no recurrence during several hours of observation. Patient tolerated PO and ambulated independently.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough focal impaired-awareness seizure.  
2. Drug-resistant focal epilepsy of unknown etiology.  
3. Incidental lesion on prior brain MRI.

Disposition: Discharged home in stable condition after neurology evaluation and ED observation. Continue oxcarbazepine and perampanel exactly as prescribed; do not miss or double doses. Follow up with epilepsy neurology within 1–2 weeks. Seizure precautions reviewed, including no driving per applicable state law, no swimming alone, no baths, no heights, and no operation of dangerous machinery. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, recurrent seizures without return to baseline, injury, fever, severe headache, weakness, persistent confusion, or other worsening symptoms.
