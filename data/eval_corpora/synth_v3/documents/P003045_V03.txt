Indication:  Evaluation of interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with structural focal epilepsy, with seizure onset at age 70 and a history of focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. The epilepsy has been considered drug-resistant, although the patient currently reports seizure freedom with a frequency of 0 seizures per month while taking levetiracetam and topiramate. Prior MRI demonstrates encephalomalacia.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. The study was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages. Hyperventilation was performed for 3 minutes, and intermittent photic stimulation was delivered at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was appropriately organized, with preserved anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradients. A symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected pattern of lower-voltage, predominantly beta-frequency activity was present anteriorly. No persistent interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state:  During wakefulness, there were occasional brief runs of polymorphic 2–5 Hz delta-theta slowing over the left temporal region, maximal at F7 and T7, at times extending to P7. This activity was not rhythmic and did not evolve.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the posterior dominant rhythm and diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. The focal left temporal slowing remained intermittently apparent during sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of epileptiform discharges.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation elicited symmetric photic driving responses, most prominent posteriorly. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Rare medium-amplitude sharp waves were present independently over the left anterior-to-mid temporal region, maximal at F7/T7, with a field extending to P7. These discharges were more readily apparent during drowsiness and sleep. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or generalized spike-and-wave discharges were seen.

Ictal events:  No clinical events or electrographic seizures were captured. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to rare left anterior-to-mid temporal sharp waves, indicating increased epileptogenic potential in the left temporal region.
2. Occasional intermittent left temporal polymorphic delta-theta slowing is consistent with focal cerebral dysfunction. In the appropriate clinical context, this may relate to the known structural abnormality of encephalomalacia.
3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. The absence of seizures during this routine study does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy.

The findings support a focal epilepsy syndrome in a patient with a history of focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended.
