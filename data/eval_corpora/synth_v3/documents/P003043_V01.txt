Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 13. The epilepsy has a reported genetic etiology due to a causative KCNQ2 c.881C>T variant. Current seizure burden is approximately two seizures per year, and the epilepsy is characterized as drug responsive.

The current medication record lists both carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine. Because these are closely related sodium-channel antiseizure medications, the regimen should be carefully reconciled before any prescribing changes are made. I do not have the doses, frequency, treatment sequence, tolerability information, or laboratory monitoring required to recommend an immediate adjustment safely.

At present, the available history does not establish drug-resistant epilepsy. On the information provided, there is not a current indication to proceed directly to a presurgical EMU evaluation or evaluation for resection, ablation, VNS, RNS, or DBS. This should be reconsidered if seizures increase, become disabling, or persist despite adequate and tolerated trials of appropriate antiseizure medications.

Recommendations / Plan

1. We will reconcile the antiseizure medication regimen with the patient, pharmacy, and referring clinician. Specifically, we need to confirm the doses and frequencies of carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine and whether concurrent use of both medications is intentional.

2. Until medication reconciliation is complete, no abrupt discontinuation should occur. Sudden withdrawal of antiseizure medication may increase seizure risk.

3. Please forward the prior EEG reports and tracings, including any routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG/LTM, or EMU data. If no prior study is available, an EEG may be considered to further characterize the epilepsy.

4. Please forward the prior brain MRI report and images, if performed. Although a genetic etiology has been identified, structural imaging remains relevant in the evaluation of focal epilepsy when not previously completed or available for review.

5. Please provide the complete genetic laboratory report for KCNQ2 c.881C>T. Genetic counseling may be considered to review the result, inheritance, and implications for family members, depending on the original report and prior counseling.

6. The patient should maintain a seizure diary documenting event dates, impaired awareness, duration, recovery, possible triggers, and any missed medication doses. This will help determine whether the current frequency remains approximately two events per year.

7. Standard seizure precautions were reviewed, including avoiding unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. The patient should not drive unless permitted under applicable NY law and cleared based on the timing of the most recent event.

8. Follow-up should be arranged after the outside EEG, MRI, genetic report, and complete medication history have been obtained. Earlier reassessment is appropriate for increased seizure frequency, prolonged seizures, medication intolerance, or other clinical change.
