Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of a patient with genetic focal epilepsy characterized by focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, with onset at age 35 years and a reported frequency of approximately five seizures per week. Genetic testing identified a KCNQ2 c.881C>T variant considered causative. The epilepsy has been classified as drug-responsive. Current antiseizure medications are lacosamide and carbamazepine. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. The study was reviewed using referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized, with an anterior-to-posterior gradient of frequencies and amplitudes. A symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was observed anteriorly. There was no persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, the background consisted predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with preserved organization and reactivity. No focal slowing was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was preserved.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric diffuse theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation elicited a symmetric photic driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Rare sharp-wave discharges were present independently over the temporal regions, more prominent on the left, with maximal negativity at F7/T7 and a field extending to FT9. Less frequent right temporal sharp transients were maximal at F8/T8. The discharges were accentuated during drowsiness and sleep. No generalized spike-and-wave discharges, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs were identified.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to rare, left-predominant independent temporal sharp-wave discharges. These findings indicate increased epileptogenic potential involving the temporal regions, greater on the left, and support the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal or mildly abnormal interictal recording does not quantify seizure frequency and does not exclude focal seizures, including focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Clinical correlation is recommended.
