Assessment / Impression

This patient has generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures, beginning at age 75. The current reported seizure burden is approximately four absence seizures per month. Etiology remains unknown.

The epilepsy is described as drug-responsive, and the patient is currently taking brivaracetam. The dose and schedule will need to be confirmed before medication changes can be recommended safely. Although the referral diagnosis is generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures, the age at onset is unusually late for a typical generalized epilepsy presentation. For that reason, review of the prior EEG and imaging will be particularly important to confirm the classification and to r/o an alternative explanation for the reported episodes. At present, there are insufficient source data to further define the syndrome or etiology.

There is no indication from the available information that this is drug-resistant epilepsy. A presurgical evaluation, VNS, RNS, or DBS evaluation is therefore not indicated on the basis of the current record.

1. Continue Briviact (brivaracetam) at the currently prescribed regimen pending confirmation of the exact dose and frequency. The patient should not stop the medication abruptly.
