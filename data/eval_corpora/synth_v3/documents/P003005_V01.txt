Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with epilepsy beginning at age 7, with reported generalized tonic-clonic seizures. The supplied classification is generalized epilepsy with a structural etiology based on MRI evidence of focal cortical dysplasia. The epilepsy is drug responsive, and the current reported seizure frequency is zero per month on combination therapy with topiramate and zonisamide.

There is a point of potential electroclinical uncertainty that warrants record review. Focal cortical dysplasia is a focal structural abnormality, whereas the referral classifies the epilepsy as generalized. This does not by itself establish that either classification is incorrect, but review of the MRI and prior EEG data is important before making a more specific syndromic or localization diagnosis. I would not infer focal seizure onset solely from the reported MRI finding, nor would I disregard the structural lesion without reviewing the images.

Because the patient is currently seizure-free and the epilepsy is described as drug responsive, there is no present indication to pursue resective epilepsy surgery, VNS, RNS, or DBS on the information available. Surgical evaluation would become relevant only if disabling seizures recur despite appropriately selected and tolerated medication trials and if subsequent VEEG and imaging data support such an evaluation.
