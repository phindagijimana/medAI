Assessment / Impression

The patient has generalized epilepsy manifested by generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 57, and the current reported frequency is approximately one seizure per month. The etiology remains unknown.

The patient is currently taking carbamazepine, and the epilepsy has been categorized as drug responsive. However, monthly generalized tonic-clonic seizures represent ongoing clinically significant seizure activity. Before changing therapy, it will be important to confirm the exact carbamazepine dose and schedule, review prior ASM exposure, and obtain the original EEG and neuroimaging data. In particular, because the epilepsy is described as generalized while the current medication is carbamazepine, confirmation of the electroclinical classification will help guide longer-term medication selection.

At present, there is insufficient information to characterize the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy or to recommend a surgical evaluation. The available designation is drug-responsive generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Tegretol (carbamazepine) at the current prescribed regimen pending complete medication reconciliation. I did not recommend an empiric dose change without knowing the current dose, dosing frequency, prior response, or tolerability.

2. The patient should provide or bring the medication bottle so that the exact carbamazepine formulation, dose, and frequency can be confirmed. We will also request prior neurology documentation and a list of any previously tried ASMs.

3. We will request prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, or EMU/LTM reports and tracings, if available. If prior diagnostic EEG data cannot be obtained, an updated EEG should be considered to confirm the generalized epilepsy classification.

4. We will request any prior brain MRI report and images. If no adequate prior study exists, MRI brain with an epilepsy protocol may be considered, particularly given seizure onset at age 57 and the presently unknown etiology.

5. The patient was advised to maintain a seizure diary documenting each generalized tonic-clonic seizure and any possible precipitating circumstances. Accurate frequency documentation will be important when assessing response to carbamazepine and determining whether a medication adjustment is needed.

6. We reviewed general seizure safety. With ongoing convulsive seizures, the patient should avoid driving unless permitted under applicable state requirements and cleared by the treating clinician. The patient should also avoid unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Emergency evaluation is appropriate for prolonged or repeated convulsive seizures, significant injury, or failure to recover as expected.

7. Follow-up should occur after the outside EEG, MRI, and medication records have been obtained. At that visit, we can reassess the epilepsy classification, current seizure burden, and whether continuation or modification of carbamazepine is appropriate.
