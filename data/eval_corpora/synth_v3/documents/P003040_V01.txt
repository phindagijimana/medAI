Assessment / Impression

This patient has drug-resistant epilepsy beginning at age 54. The reported seizure type is unknown-onset tonic-clonic. At present, there is insufficient electroclinical information to determine whether the epilepsy is focal or generalized, and the epilepsy type therefore remains unknown.

The reported hippocampal sclerosis supports a structural etiology and raises the possibility of temporal lobe epilepsy with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. However, that localization cannot be assumed from MRI alone, particularly when the observed seizures have not had a documented onset. Correlation with the patient’s detailed semiology, EEG findings, and direct review of imaging would be needed.

The patient is currently seizure-free at a reported frequency of 0 seizures per month while taking brivaracetam and carbamazepine. This current seizure freedom is encouraging, although it does not negate the documented history of drug resistance. The immediate priority is to verify the medication doses, obtain the prior treatment history, and clarify the electroclinical diagnosis before making changes.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Briviact (brivaracetam) and Tegretol (carbamazepine) without change for now. Exact doses and frequencies must be confirmed. Abrupt discontinuation should be avoided.

2. Complete formal medication reconciliation, including current doses, adherence, tolerability, and all previously tried antiseizure medications. The prior medication history will be important in confirming the basis for drug resistance.

3. Obtain the original MRI images for direct review. The reported hippocampal sclerosis should be characterized with respect to laterality and associated structural findings. If prior imaging is not adequate for epilepsy evaluation, an epilepsy-protocol brain MRI may be considered.
