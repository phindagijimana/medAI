Assessment / Impression

This patient has generalized epilepsy manifested by generalized tonic-clonic seizures, with onset at 13 years of age and a reported frequency of approximately two seizures per year. The etiology is genetic, with a reported causative SCN1A c.5734C>T variant. The patient remains on topiramate, and the epilepsy is characterized in the available records as drug responsive.

The distinction between drug responsiveness and the ongoing frequency of approximately two generalized tonic-clonic seizures per year should be clarified through a more complete seizure and medication history. In particular, medication dose, adherence, tolerability, prior ASM exposure, and the timing and circumstances of recent seizures will be important before considering any treatment adjustment.

The MRI lesion is described as incidental. On the information currently available, it should not be assumed to represent an epileptogenic focus in a patient whose electroclinical diagnosis is generalized epilepsy. Independent review of the MRI images and report is nevertheless appropriate. At present, the available information does not support a focal resective epilepsy surgery evaluation.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Topamax (topiramate) at the current regimen for now. The patient should provide the exact dose and frequency so that medication reconciliation can be completed. No medication change was made on the basis of the limited information available.

2. Obtain the original genetic testing report documenting the SCN1A c.5734C>T variant. This will allow confirmation of the laboratory classification and review of any testing-specific recommendations.

3. Request prior EEG reports and, if available, the original EEG tracings. If adequate prior electrophysiologic data cannot be obtained, an EEG may be considered to further document the generalized epilepsy pattern.

4. Obtain the prior brain MRI images and complete radiology report for direct review. Any need for repeat imaging or surveillance of the incidental lesion should be determined after its location and imaging characteristics are clarified.

5. Maintain an updated seizure log, including the date and duration of each generalized tonic-clonic seizure, possible triggers, missed medication doses, injuries, and recovery time. This will help determine whether the current regimen is providing adequate control.

6. We reviewed the importance of consistent ASM use. General seizure precautions apply, including avoiding unsupervised swimming, bathing alone, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Driving should follow applicable NY regulations and physician guidance.

7. Follow-up in the epilepsy clinic after outside EEG, MRI, medication, and genetic testing records have been obtained. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if seizure frequency increases or medication concerns arise.
