Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Deidentified  
Indication:  Evaluation for interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with genetic focal epilepsy and focal impaired-awareness seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 13 years, with a current frequency of approximately two seizures per year. The epilepsy is attributed to a causative KCNQ2 variant, c.881C>T, and has been drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. The recording was obtained during wakefulness and drowsiness. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Sleep was not achieved. The study was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz alpha activity, which was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was distributed symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent hemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No focal attenuation, persistent focal slowing, or generalized slowing was identified.

Drowsy state:  With drowsiness, there was expected attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric theta activity. Brief positive occipital sharp transients of sleep were present as a normal variant. Stage N2 sleep was not recorded.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of hyperventilation.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz elicited a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No focal, multifocal, or generalized epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No focal slowing was present.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No push-button events were recorded.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness and drowsiness. There are no focal abnormalities or interictal epileptiform discharges.
2. No electrographic or clinical seizures were recorded.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. The patient’s established clinical syndrome remains focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, with onset at age 13 years and a reported frequency of approximately two per year. The epilepsy is genetically attributed to the causative KCNQ2 c.881C>T variant and is drug responsive on current therapy with carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine. Clinical correlation is recommended.
