Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  History of combined generalized and focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 10. Structural etiology based on MRI evidence of focal cortical dysplasia. The epilepsy is drug responsive, and the patient is currently seizure-free, with no seizures during the past month, while receiving topiramate and valproate. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. The study was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. No antiseizure medication changes were made for this study.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-developed 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was maximal over the occipital regions and reactive to eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry.

Awake state:  During maximal wakefulness, mixed alpha and beta frequencies predominated. Occasional brief runs of generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz rhythmic delta activity were present without evolution or clinical correlate. No definite generalized spike-and-wave or polyspike-and-wave discharges were identified.

Drowsy / sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse theta activity, and slow rolling eye movements. Stage N2 sleep contained symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was preserved.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild symmetric generalized theta-delta slowing that resolved promptly after cessation. No epileptiform discharges were activated.

Photic stimulation:  Photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced symmetric photic driving, best expressed posteriorly. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Rare low-amplitude sharp waves were present independently over the left frontotemporal region, maximal at F7/T7, and the right frontotemporal region, maximal at F8/T8. In addition, there were rare generalized frontally predominant spike-and-wave discharges lasting less than 1 second and without a clinical correlate. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or sustained focal slowing were seen.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to rare independent bilateral frontotemporal sharp waves and rare brief generalized spike-and-wave discharges. These findings indicate both focal and generalized epileptogenic potential and are compatible with the reported diagnosis of combined generalized and focal epilepsy.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. The patient’s focal impaired-awareness seizures are clinically drug responsive, with current seizure freedom on topiramate and valproate.

3. The focal interictal abnormalities should be correlated with the known structural lesion, described on MRI as focal cortical dysplasia. The absence of seizures during this brief recording does not exclude epilepsy.
