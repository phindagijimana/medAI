Indication:  Drug-resistant epilepsy of unknown etiology with combined generalized and focal features. The patient has had generalized tonic-clonic seizures since age 23, currently occurring approximately four times per week despite treatment with lamotrigine, brivaracetam, and lacosamide. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with an 8.5-9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was distributed anteriorly. There was no persistent focal attenuation or background asymmetry.

Awake state:  During wakefulness, occasional generalized bursts of frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity were present. These discharges lasted approximately 0.5-2 seconds and were not associated with a definite clinical change on video. In addition, there were occasional independent sharp waves maximal over the left anterior temporal region at F7/T7, with a field extending to FP1 and P7.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep contained symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges became more frequent during drowsiness and sleep. Left anterior temporal sharp waves were also activated by sleep. No electrographic seizure occurred.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild symmetric generalized theta slowing. There was a brief increase in generalized spike-and-wave activity without clinical accompaniment.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  
1. Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges.  
2. Occasional independent left anterior temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/T7 and potentiated during sleep.  
3. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing.

Ictal events:  No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured.

ECG:  Single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges, supporting an increased propensity for generalized seizures, including generalized tonic-clonic seizures.  
2. Independent left anterior temporal epileptiform discharges indicate an additional focal epileptogenic potential in that region.  
3. In the appropriate clinical context, the combination of generalized and focal interictal epileptiform abnormalities is compatible with the patient’s diagnosis of combined generalized and focal epilepsy. No seizure was recorded during this routine study; absence of an ictal event does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of drug-resistant epilepsy.
