Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy manifested by focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 25. Current seizure burden is approximately three seizures per year. The epilepsy has been classified as metabolic in etiology and drug responsive on Briviact and Lamictal.

The present information is sufficient to support the broad diagnosis of focal epilepsy but is not sufficient to establish localization, define the exact metabolic mechanism, or assess whether the current regimen has been fully optimized. In particular, medication doses, adherence, tolerability, prior ASM exposure, EEG findings, MRI findings, and the detailed semiology remain unknown.

Given the designation of drug-responsive epilepsy, there is no basis in the available record to label the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy or to initiate a presurgical epilepsy evaluation at this time. If seizure frequency increases or events persist despite appropriately selected and adequately dosed ASMs, that assessment may need to be revisited after confirmation of treatment history and diagnosis.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Briviact (brivaracetam) and Lamictal (lamotrigine) without change pending confirmation of the current doses, frequencies, adherence, and tolerability. The patient should not stop either medication abruptly.

2. Obtain an updated medication reconciliation from the patient, pharmacy, or referring office, including tablet strengths, dosing schedule, refill history, and prior ASM trials.

3. Request prior neurology notes, EEG or VEEG/LTM reports, brain MRI reports and images, and laboratory documentation supporting the reported metabolic etiology.

4. 7. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after the outside records and medication doses have been obtained. Earlier reassessment is appropriate for increased seizure frequency, a change in seizure type, medication intolerance, or prolonged/repetitive events requiring urgent evaluation.
