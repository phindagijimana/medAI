Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Evaluation of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 11 years. Seizures currently occur approximately 7 times per week despite treatment with oxcarbazepine and valproate, consistent with drug-resistant epilepsy. The reported etiology is structural; prior brain MRI demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Electrode impedances were maintained within acceptable limits. The recording was reviewed using referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded concurrently.

Description:

Background:  During wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and voltage gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was marked by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. No focal sleep asymmetry was identified.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing. In addition, there were brief bursts of generalized spike-and-wave activity as described below. No clinical change accompanied these discharges.

Photic Stimulation:  Photic stimulation produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings:  Occasional bursts of generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, maximal over the bilateral frontal regions at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. Bursts lasted approximately 0.5-2 seconds and were more apparent during drowsiness and following hyperventilation. No definite clinical correlate was observed on video. There were no independent focal epileptiform discharges, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal abnormalities.

Ictal Events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured during this recording.

ECG:  Single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. These findings indicate an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are supportive of the patient’s diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal or near-normal background does not exclude clinically active epilepsy, particularly given the reported frequency of approximately seven seizures per week despite current oxcarbazepine and valproate therapy.

3. No focal electrographic abnormality was identified to correspond with the reported MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia. The significance of that structural lesion should be determined through correlation with the MRI location, seizure semiology, and, if clinically indicated, prolonged video-EEG monitoring as part of the evaluation of drug-resistant epilepsy.
