Assessment / Impression

This patient has drug-resistant epilepsy with seizure onset at age 68. The documented epilepsy type is combined generalized and focal epilepsy, and the active seizure type is generalized tonic-clonic seizure occurring approximately once per month. Seizures remain active despite concurrent treatment with topiramate, zonisamide, and oxcarbazepine.

The reported etiology is genetic due to a KCNQ2 c.881C>T variant. The primary genetic report should be reviewed before making further conclusions regarding variant interpretation or genotype-phenotype correlation.

At present, the available information is insufficient to determine whether the generalized tonic-clonic seizures have generalized onset or represent focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic events. This distinction is clinically important because it may affect medication selection and whether a focal surgical evaluation, VNS, RNS, DBS, or another treatment pathway should be considered. Drug resistance supports proceeding with a more comprehensive epilepsy evaluation, but candidacy for resection, ablation, or neuromodulation has not yet been established.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current antiseizure medications—topiramate, zonisamide, and oxcarbazepine—without an empiric change until the exact doses, frequencies, adherence, prior response, and adverse effects can be confirmed. The patient should not discontinue any ASM abruptly.
