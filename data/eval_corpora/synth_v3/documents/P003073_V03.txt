Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. XXX  
Indication: Evaluation of generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 78 in the setting of an infectious etiology. The epilepsy has been classified as drug-resistant, although the patient is currently seizure-free, with 0 seizures per month, while receiving brivaracetam and perampanel.

Technique: A  thirty-minute digital EEG was recorded using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system, with additional electrocardiographic monitoring. The recording was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Hyperventilation was performed for 3 minutes, and intermittent photic stimulation was delivered at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and symmetrically organized, with an 8.5-9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing.

Awake State: During maximal wakefulness, the background was composed predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with intermixed theta activity. No LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs were present.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was associated with attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, predominantly central theta activity. Definite vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K-complexes were not observed.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild generalized theta slowing. Two brief bursts of generalized, frontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave discharges occurred during hyperventilation, lasting 1.5 and 2 seconds, respectively. There was no apparent behavioral arrest or other clinical change on video.

Photic Stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at intermediate flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: Rare generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, with bifrontally maximal amplitude at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4 and no consistent focal lead-in. Discharges occurred singly or in brief 1- to 2-second runs at approximately 3 Hz. No independent focal epileptiform discharges, TIRDA, or focal rhythmic slowing was identified.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. Specifically, no typical absence seizure with sustained generalized 3-Hz spike-and-wave activity and associated behavioral impairment occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to rare generalized, frontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. These findings indicate an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are compatible with the reported generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures.

2. No electrographic or clinical seizures were recorded. A normal clinical state during this limited recording does not exclude epilepsy. The patient is reported to be currently seizure-free at 0 seizures per month on brivaracetam and perampanel despite a history of drug-resistant epilepsy.
