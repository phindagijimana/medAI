Indication:  Focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures, reportedly beginning at age 23 and occurring approximately once monthly. Epilepsy is considered immune-mediated and drug-responsive. Current antiseizure medications are oxcarbazepine and zonisamide. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. The recording was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages. Activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with the expected anterior-to-posterior gradient of frequencies and amplitudes. A well-formed 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. Anteriorly, there was low-amplitude beta activity with intermixed alpha and theta frequencies. There was no persistent focal or generalized background slowing.

Awake state:  No asymmetry of the PDR was identified. There were no sustained focal abnormalities. Occasional low-amplitude temporal theta activity occurred independently over both hemispheres during transition to drowsiness and was consistent with a normal state-related variant.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was normally expressed and showed no consistent lateralization.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of hyperventilation.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation at sequential frequencies produced a symmetric photic driving response, maximal over the posterior regions. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or other epileptiform discharges were identified. No focal slowing was present.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events of concern were captured. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states. There are no focal, lateralized, or epileptiform abnormalities.
2. No electrographic seizures or typical focal aware clinical events were recorded.

A normal interictal EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. Clinical correlation is advised, particularly given the reported history of immune-mediated, drug-responsive focal epilepsy treated with oxcarbazepine and zonisamide.
