Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of epilepsy of unknown type, characterized by unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 14 and occurring historically up to approximately 6 times per week. The reported etiology is immune-mediated. Seizures have been drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are levetiracetam and lacosamide.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. The study was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages. Activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded throughout the study.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz alpha rhythm of 30-60 microvolts that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the bilateral frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing.

Awake state: The awake background was continuous and appropriately organized. No lateralized attenuation, breach rhythm, or pathologic delta activity was present.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was associated with attenuation of the PDR and emergence of symmetric generalized theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by bilaterally synchronous and symmetric sleep spindles, vertex waves, and K complexes. No epileptiform activation occurred during sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal abnormality or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of hyperventilation.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation from 1 to 30 Hz elicited a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal, multifocal, or generalized interictal epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, polyspike-and-wave complexes, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA. No electrographic seizures were recorded.

Ictal events: No clinical events or electrographic seizures were captured. The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm during the sampled portions of the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal awake and sleep routine EEG. There are no focal abnormalities, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures.

2. The absence of epileptiform activity on a single routine EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. This study does not establish whether the patient’s unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures are focal to bilateral tonic-clonic or generalized in onset. Clinical correlation and, if needed, prolonged video EEG monitoring may be useful for seizure classification.
