Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 36. Seizures previously occurred approximately seven times per week and have been drug responsive to zonisamide. Etiology remains unknown. Prior brain MRI demonstrated an incidental lesion not considered clearly explanatory for the generalized epilepsy.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included simultaneous video recording. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with the expected anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradients. A well-formed 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated normally with eye opening. Anteriorly, there was the expected pattern of lower-voltage mixed alpha and beta activity. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During wakefulness, occasional generalized bursts of sharply contoured 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity were present. These discharges were frontally predominant, synchronous, and symmetric, with maximal expression over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. Bursts typically lasted less than 2 seconds and were not associated with an evident clinical change on video. No focal or consistently lateralized lead-in was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike-and-wave discharges were mildly activated by drowsiness and sleep, at times occurring as isolated fragments. There were no focal sleep-activated epileptiform discharges.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced symmetric diffuse theta and delta slowing. A brief generalized spike-and-wave burst occurred without an observable clinical correlate. No electrographic seizure was induced.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation produced symmetric photic driving at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness and sleep. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing were present.

Ictal events: No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured. No habitual generalized tonic-clonic event occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, mildly activated by drowsiness and sleep. These findings support an increased generalized seizure tendency and are consistent with the patient’s known generalized epilepsy and history of generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

2. No focal epileptiform abnormality or focal cerebral dysfunction was identified. The previously reported incidental MRI lesion does not have a corresponding focal EEG abnormality in this study.

3. No seizures were recorded. A normal or seizure-free sampling interval does not otherwise determine current seizure control; clinical correlation with the reported drug-responsive course on zonisamide is recommended.
