Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 11. Seizure frequency is reported as approximately five per week, with clinical responsiveness to antiseizure medication. Current ASMs are levetiracetam and lamotrigine. The epilepsy is considered structural in etiology in the setting of a vascular malformation demonstrated on MRI. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The recording was reviewed using referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded throughout the study. The patient was awake and drowsy. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized. A 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and was reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, there were occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3–4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges. These were synchronous and symmetric, typically lasting less than 1 second. Rare bursts lasted up to 2 seconds without an associated clinical change on video. No consistent focal lead-in was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Generalized epileptiform discharges became mildly more frequent during drowsiness. Definitive sleep architecture, including vertex waves and sleep spindles, was not observed.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced expected diffuse theta and delta slowing. There was a modest increase in the frequency of generalized spike-and-wave bursts, without an electroclinical seizure.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric photic driving response at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3–4 Hz were present during wakefulness and drowsiness. There were no focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or other periodic/rhythmic patterns. No focal slowing was identified.

Ictal events: No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured. No patient-reported events occurred during the recording.

ECG: Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and drowsy EEG due to occasional generalized 3–4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, mildly activated by drowsiness and hyperventilation.

2. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are consistent with the reported generalized epilepsy and generalized tonic-clonic seizure phenotype.

3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. There was no persistent focal slowing or focal epileptiform abnormality on this study. The known MRI vascular malformation and structural epilepsy classification should be correlated with the lesion location and other clinical data, as a normal focal background does not exclude a structural epileptogenic substrate.
