Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy characterized by focal aware seizures beginning at approximately age 30. The reported current frequency is about three seizures per week. The etiology is structural, with MRI demonstrating a vascular malformation.

The patient is currently taking perampanel, and the epilepsy is categorized as drug responsive. This designation suggests a clinically meaningful response to ASM therapy, although the available information does not establish seizure freedom and does not quantify the treatment response. The ongoing frequency of approximately three events per week warrants confirmation through a seizure diary and careful reassessment after the exact perampanel regimen has been reconciled.

The vascular malformation may be relevant to seizure generation, but additional characterization is needed. Specifically, the original images should be reviewed to determine the lesion type and location and to assess whether the imaging abnormality is anatomically concordant with the clinical seizure phenotype. At present, there is insufficient information to recommend lesion-directed surgery, intracranial monitoring, or neuromodulation. Because the epilepsy is described as drug responsive, medication management remains the immediate focus while the diagnostic records are assembled.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Fycompa (perampanel) at the currently prescribed regimen pending confirmation of the exact dose and dosing frequency. The medication should not be stopped abruptly.

2. Complete medication reconciliation, including the current perampanel schedule and any prior ASM trials, if applicable. This will allow us to confirm the degree of drug responsiveness and determine whether further adjustment is appropriate.

3. Maintain a prospective seizure diary documenting the date and approximate number of focal aware seizures. Any evolution in awareness or new motor, sensory, autonomic, or behavioral manifestations should also be recorded.

4. Obtain the original brain MRI images and complete radiology report for direct review at xxx Medical Center. The vascular malformation should be characterized by type, location, and relationship to a possible seizure onset zone before any lesion-specific treatment is considered.

6. Continue medical management at this stage. A surgical or device-based evaluation is not recommended solely on the limited information currently available. This question can be revisited if seizures become drug resistant or if subsequent MRI and EEG data identify a compelling concordant target.

7. Follow up with xxx Medicine Epilepsy after medication reconciliation and review of the MRI and EEG records. The patient should contact the clinic sooner if seizure frequency increases, awareness becomes impaired, or there are concerns regarding medication tolerability.
