Neurology Progress Note

Chief Complaint: Follow-up focal epilepsy

History of Present Illness:

The patient is an 11-year-old boy with focal epilepsy of unknown etiology, with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 10. His epilepsy has been considered drug-resistant, although he is currently seizure-free on combination therapy with Depakote (valproate), Vimpat (lacosamide), and Keppra (levetiracetam). Lateralization has not been established.

Since the last visit, the patient and parent report no breakthrough seizures. The seizure calendar was reviewed and documents 0 seizures over the past month. There have been no episodes concerning for convulsion, impaired awareness, unexplained loss of time, or nocturnal seizure. His antiseizure medication regimen has remained unchanged. They report good adherence without missed doses. No interval ED visits, hospitalizations, or seizure-related injuries.

He is tolerating the current regimen without reported excessive sedation, dizziness, gait difficulty, tremor, abdominal symptoms, or behavioral change. He remains active and is participating in his usual school and home activities. The family’s primary concern today is maintaining seizure control while minimizing medication burden; we discussed that medication reduction would not be advisable at this time given the relatively recent onset, prior drug-resistant course, and need for three ASMs to achieve current control.
