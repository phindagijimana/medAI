Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures beginning at age 12 in the setting of CASPR2 antibody-associated autoimmune disease. Events occur approximately five times per week. Epilepsy has been clinically drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are zonisamide and lamotrigine. EEG obtained to evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures consisted of hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded concurrently.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz alpha rhythm of moderate amplitude that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately reactive. Muscle and eye movement artifact intermittently obscured the frontotemporal leads. No pathologic delta activity was identified.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, an increase in diffuse theta activity, and slow rolling eye movements. Stage N2 sleep was marked by symmetric vertex waves and synchronous sleep spindles. No epileptiform activation occurred during drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal asymmetry or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of hyperventilation.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced symmetric photic driving, best expressed posteriorly. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, periodic discharges, TIRDA, or other unequivocal interictal epileptiform abnormalities were present. No focal or generalized slowing was identified.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures were recorded. No habitual clinical events were reported or marked during this study.

ECG:  Single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states. There are no focal, lateralized, or epileptiform abnormalities.
2. No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

A normal interictal EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. The patient’s reported focal aware seizures, including the history of onset at age 12, frequency of approximately five per week, drug responsiveness, and CASPR2 antibody-associated immune etiology, should be correlated with the broader clinical history. If further electroclinical characterization is needed, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may provide a higher diagnostic yield.
