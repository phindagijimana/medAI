Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy characterized by focal impaired awareness seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 67. The current reported frequency is approximately two seizures per month.

The patient is taking valproate and is considered drug responsive. Because the exact valproate formulation, dose, adherence pattern, and dosing schedule were not available, I cannot recommend a specific dose adjustment today. Medication reconciliation and review of the prior treatment history are important before any change is made.

The epilepsy etiology remains unknown. Given the late age at onset, obtaining and personally reviewing prior MRI and EEG data will be important. If those studies were not completed or were not adequate for epilepsy evaluation, updated testing would be reasonable. There is not enough information at present to localize seizure onset beyond the diagnosis of focal epilepsy.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Depakote (valproate) at the currently prescribed regimen pending confirmation of the formulation, dose, and frequency. The patient should not abruptly discontinue valproate, as sudden withdrawal may increase seizure risk.

2. Complete medication reconciliation through the patient, pharmacy, and referring office. Please forward the current prescription information as well as a list of any previously attempted antiseizure medications, including highest doses, treatment duration, response, and reasons for discontinuation.

3. Obtain prior neurology notes and any available witness descriptions of the habitual events. Clarification of seizure onset, duration, level of responsiveness, motor or behavioral features, postictal state, and any history of progression to bilateral tonic-clonic activity would help refine the electroclinical diagnosis.

4. Please send prior EEG reports and, when possible, the original EEG data for review. If no prior study is available, a routine EEG should be considered. Prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient VEEG/EMU monitoring may be considered later if the diagnosis or event classification remains uncertain.

5. Obtain prior brain MRI reports and images. If an adequate epilepsy-protocol MRI has not been performed, MRI of the brain with an epilepsy protocol should be considered to evaluate for a structural cause of focal epilepsy.

6. Continue a seizure calendar documenting the date and approximate time of each event, suspected triggers, missed medication doses, level of awareness, duration, and recovery. The present baseline is approximately two focal impaired awareness seizures per month.

7. Routine safety counseling was reviewed. Because these events impair awareness, the patient should avoid driving unless permitted under applicable NY requirements and cleared through the appropriate process. The patient should also use caution around open water, heights, open flames, and operating hazardous machinery.

8. Valproate monitoring should be coordinated with the prescribing clinician. Once the current dose is verified, appropriate laboratory surveillance and a valproate level may be obtained as clinically indicated.

9. At this time, the available information supports continued medical management. Surgical or device-based therapies such as VNS, RNS, or DBS are not being pursued based on the current description of drug-responsive epilepsy and the limited diagnostic records available.

10. Follow-up will be arranged after the outside EEG, MRI, medication history, and current valproate regimen have been obtained. The patient should contact the treating neurologist sooner for an increase in seizure frequency, a change in seizure type, prolonged events, medication intolerance, or other urgent concerns.
