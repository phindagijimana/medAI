Summary and Impressions:

The reported genetic finding is an STXBP1 c.902A>G variant. Current therapy includes topiramate, levetiracetam, and zonisamide.

A neuropsychological profile cannot be derived because behavioral observations, performance validity findings, and standardized test scores were not included in the available materials. Consequently, cognitive strengths and weaknesses, language dominance, memory laterality, and change from estimated baseline cannot be determined. No neuropsychological evidence is presently available to support focal hemispheric or temporal-lobe localization. This limitation is particularly important in the context of generalized epilepsy, for which any consideration of focal resection would require compelling and concordant evidence from the broader presurgical workup.

Topiramate and zonisamide may be associated with slowed processing speed and word-retrieval inefficiency in some patients, while frequent generalized tonic-clonic seizures may also disrupt cognitive functioning. These are general clinical considerations and should not be interpreted as established causes of impairment in this patient without valid test data and longitudinal clinical correlation.

Recommendations:  
1. Discuss the patient’s history and complete diagnostic findings at PRC, with particular attention to the generalized epilepsy classification, ongoing seizure burden, genetic etiology, and drug resistance.

2. Obtain the complete neuropsychological testing protocol, including raw scores, demographically corrected standardized scores, behavioral observations, and performance validity findings. If testing has not been completed, a comprehensive baseline evaluation is recommended.

3. Correlate neuropsychological findings with prolonged video-EEG, structural imaging, and other available presurgical studies before drawing conclusions regarding localization or candidacy for a focal procedure.

4. If a focal resection is considered despite the generalized epilepsy history, language mapping with fMRI should be considered. Wada testing may be appropriate if language or memory lateralization remains clinically necessary and noninvasive findings are inconclusive or discordant.

5. Review the cognitive and behavioral effects of the current antiseizure medication regimen in conjunction with seizure control. Medication changes should be directed by the treating epileptologist.

6. Repeat neuropsychological evaluation following a major treatment intervention, typically at approximately 6 to 12 months, to assess cognitive outcome and guide rehabilitation.
