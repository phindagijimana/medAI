Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures. Events reportedly began at age 71 in the setting of a metabolic etiology and currently occur approximately four times per month. The epilepsy has been drug responsive. Current antiseizure medication is lacosamide.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single electrocardiographic channel. The study was reviewed using multiple referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded throughout the study. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous and symmetric, with normal anterior-to-posterior organization. A 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During wakefulness, occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges occurred in brief bursts lasting 1-2 seconds. These were frontally predominant, maximal over F3/F4 and Fz, with a frequency of approximately 3-3.5 Hz and no consistent lateralized lead-in. No definite behavioral change accompanied these brief bursts.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized epileptiform discharges became more frequent in sleep, at times occurring within K complexes. No focal sleep-activated epileptiform discharges were identified.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced symmetric diffuse theta and delta slowing. In addition, two generalized 3 Hz spike-and-wave bursts were elicited, lasting 3 and 4 seconds, respectively. The patient remained responsive to technologist questioning, and no definite clinical absence seizure was demonstrated.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced symmetric photic driving at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, enhanced by drowsiness, sleep, and hyperventilation. No independent focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or SIRPIDs were seen.

Ictal events: No electroclinical seizures or habitual clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with activation during hyperventilation and sleep. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are compatible with the reported history of typical absence seizures.

2. No electroclinical seizure was recorded. The background organization was otherwise normal, without focal slowing or focal epileptiform abnormality.

Clinical correlation is advised, particularly given the reported late onset at age 71 and metabolic etiology. A normal behavioral response during brief generalized discharges does not exclude clinically occurring absence seizures outside this recording.
