Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with drug-resistant generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology. Seizures began at approximately 30 years of age and are described as generalized tonic-clonic seizures occurring about once per year. Current antiseizure medications are levetiracetam, brivaracetam, and oxcarbazepine.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The record was reviewed using standard longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was observed over the bilateral frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, the background demonstrated mixed alpha and beta frequencies with preserved organization. No focal attenuation was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. Epileptiform discharges were more apparent during drowsiness and sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing. A brief generalized spike-and-wave burst was elicited without clinical change.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation produced symmetric photic driving at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, with maximal negativity over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. These occurred as isolated complexes and brief 3-4 Hz bursts lasting approximately 0.5-2 seconds. Discharges were enhanced during drowsiness and N2 sleep. No consistent focal lead-in was seen. There were no focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or electrographic status epilepticus.

Ictal events: No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured. No patient-reported events occurred during the recording.

ECG: Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, enhanced during drowsiness and sleep. These findings indicate an increased propensity for generalized seizures and are supportive of the reported generalized epilepsy syndrome in the appropriate clinical context.

2. The background organization is otherwise normal, without evidence of focal cerebral dysfunction.

3. No electrographic or clinical seizure was recorded. A normal or seizure-free recording interval would not exclude epilepsy; clinical correlation is recommended.
