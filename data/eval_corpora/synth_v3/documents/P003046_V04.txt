Medical Decision-Making (MDM): Presentation is most consistent with a breakthrough typical absence seizure in a patient with known drug-resistant generalized epilepsy. Autoimmune etiology is known; there are no current findings suggesting encephalitis or an acute inflammatory relapse.

Differential included epileptic seizure, syncope, TIA, and psychogenic nonepileptic event. Abrupt staring/unresponsiveness with brief post-event confusion and known matching seizure semiology favors seizure. No prodrome, hypotension, dysrhythmia, focal deficit, or persistent AMS. No benzodiazepine or IV antiseizure load indicated because the event self-terminated and did not recur. Observed in ED with serial exams remaining normal.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough typical absence seizure.  
2. Drug-resistant generalized epilepsy due to CASPR2 antibody–associated autoimmune disease.

Disposition: Discharged home in stable condition after neurology consultation. Continue topiramate, valproate, lacosamide, and brivaracetam exactly as prescribed; no missed or extra doses. Epilepsy clinic follow-up within 1 week. Reviewed seizure precautions, including no driving per applicable state law, no swimming/bathing alone, no heights, and no operation of heavy machinery. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, injury, fever, severe headache, focal weakness, or persistent confusion.
