Indication: Presurgical characterization of drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 11 and currently occurring approximately six times per week. The epilepsy is considered structural in etiology; MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis. Current antiseizure medications are brivaracetam, valproate, and lacosamide.

AED Changes During Admission: Brivaracetam and lacosamide were reduced after admission and subsequently held to facilitate event capture. Valproate was continued at a reduced dose. Home medications were resumed before discharge.

Technique: Continuous digital video EEG was recorded using the international 10-20 electrode placement system with additional anterior temporal electrodes. EEG and synchronized video were reviewed continuously. The study included wakefulness, drowsiness, and sleep.

Background: The awake background was organized, with a symmetric 9-Hz posterior dominant rhythm reactive to eye opening. An anterior-predominant low-amplitude beta rhythm was present. Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep showed symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. There was intermittent polymorphic 2-4 Hz delta slowing over the left temporal region, maximal at F7/T7.

Interictal Findings:
1. Frequent left anterior temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7 and T7, with a field extending to Fp1 and P7. These discharges increased in frequency during drowsiness and sleep.
2. Occasional runs of left temporal intermittent rhythmic delta activity (TIRDA), consisting of 1.5-2.5 Hz rhythmic delta maximal at T7.
3. No independent right hemispheric epileptiform discharges, LPDs, or generalized epileptiform abnormalities were identified.

Ictal Events:

Total events captured: 3 electroclinical seizures.

Event 1 (Day 2, 03:14): The patient aroused from sleep, stared, became unresponsive, and developed oral automatisms followed by repetitive right-hand picking movements. EEG onset consisted of rhythmic 5-6 Hz theta activity maximal at F7/T7, evolving in amplitude and slowing to 3-4 Hz before spreading across the left hemisphere and then contralaterally. The seizure lasted 78 seconds. Postictally, there was left temporal delta slowing and confusion for approximately 4 minutes.

Event 2 (Day 2, 16:47): Behavioral arrest and impaired responsiveness were followed by lip smacking and right-hand automatisms. Electrographically, low-amplitude rhythmic theta arose over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7, with subsequent evolution to sharply contoured delta activity and bilateral spread. Duration was 64 seconds. Postictal language testing showed delayed naming and recovery over several minutes.

Event 3 (Day 3, 07:26): The patient reported a poorly defined internal sensation, then demonstrated behavioral arrest, oral automatisms, and right upper extremity automatisms. EEG showed a left anterior temporal rhythmic theta onset at F7/T7 with clear evolution in frequency, morphology, and spatial distribution. The seizure lasted 71 seconds and was followed by focal left temporal slowing.

Impression:
1. Abnormal long-term video EEG due to frequent left anterior temporal IEDs, left TIRDA, intermittent left temporal slowing, and three electroclinical focal impaired-awareness seizures with left anterior temporal onset.
2. These findings support a focal epilepsy arising from the left temporal region. In the clinical context of drug resistance and MRI-demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis, the study supports further multidisciplinary epilepsy surgery evaluation and correlation with formal imaging review and neuropsychological testing.
