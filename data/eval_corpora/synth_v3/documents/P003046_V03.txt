Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  History of drug-resistant generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures beginning at age 35, attributed clinically to CASPR2 antibody-associated immune disease. The patient is currently seizure-free, with 0 seizures per month, while taking topiramate, valproate, lacosamide, and brivaracetam. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. The study was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-formed 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen predominantly over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  During maximal wakefulness, the background consisted of mixed alpha and beta frequencies with preserved organization. No focal attenuation was identified.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was otherwise preserved.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced expected diffuse theta and delta slowing. No clinical absence seizure was elicited.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation produced symmetric photic driving at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Rare, brief bursts of generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, maximal over the bilateral frontal regions at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. These occurred in runs lasting less than 2 seconds, without a definite clinical correlate. No independent focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or sustained rhythmic patterns were observed.

Ictal events:  No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No typical absence event was recorded during this study.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to rare, brief generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges with frontal predominance. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support a generalized epilepsy and are compatible with the patient’s history of typical absence seizures.

2. No seizures were recorded. The absence of an ictal event during this brief study does not exclude epilepsy or establish continued seizure freedom.

3. There is no focal slowing or focal epileptiform abnormality. The clinically reported CASPR2 antibody-associated immune etiology cannot be determined from the EEG alone and requires correlation with the broader neurologic and immunologic evaluation.
