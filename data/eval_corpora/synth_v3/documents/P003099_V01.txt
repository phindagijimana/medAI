Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with reported generalized epilepsy of metabolic etiology, manifesting as typical absence seizures that began at age 71. The current event burden is approximately four seizures per month. The epilepsy has been classified as drug responsive, and the patient is currently taking lacosamide.

The late onset of typical absence seizures merits careful electroclinical confirmation. At present, however, I do not have primary EEG data, imaging, laboratory results, or sufficient semiologic detail to revise the diagnosis provided in the referral. The relationship between the metabolic abnormality and the ongoing seizure frequency also requires clarification.

Although lacosamide is the current treatment, its exact dose and schedule are unknown. I would not make an empiric change until medication reconciliation and review of the prior diagnostic data are completed. The ongoing frequency of approximately four events per month should be tracked prospectively to determine whether additional treatment optimization is needed, while recognizing that the epilepsy is reported to be drug responsive.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Vimpat (lacosamide) at the currently prescribed regimen pending confirmation of the dose and frequency. The patient should not independently stop or alter the medication.

2. Complete medication reconciliation using the prescription bottle, pharmacy dispensing history, or records from the prescribing clinician.

3. Request prior neurology documentation, including the original event description and the basis for classifying the events as typical absence seizures and the epilepsy as generalized.

4. Obtain prior EEG reports and, when possible, the original tracings. If no adequate EEG has been completed or if the prior study does not clarify the diagnosis, an updated EEG with video correlation should be considered.

5. Obtain available brain imaging reports and images. No MRI findings were provided with this referral.

6. Review the laboratory evaluation supporting a metabolic etiology, including the abnormality identified at seizure onset and whether it remains active or has resolved.

7. Maintain a seizure diary documenting the date and approximate time of each event. This will help verify the reported frequency of approximately four per month and assess response to therapy.

8. Until the events are better characterized and controlled, the patient should follow applicable seizure safety precautions and local driving regulations. Avoid situations in which an episode of impaired awareness could cause injury.

9. Follow up after the outside EEG, imaging, laboratory, and medication records have been obtained. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if seizure frequency increases or medication-related concerns arise.
