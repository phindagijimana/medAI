Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation for interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with focal epilepsy characterized by focal impaired-awareness seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 25. Current frequency is approximately three seizures per year. The epilepsy is considered metabolic in etiology and drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are brivaracetam and lamotrigine.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single electrocardiographic channel. The recording was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages. Hyperventilation was performed for 3 minutes, and intermittent photic stimulation was delivered at sequential flash frequencies. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study, and stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: In the maximally alert state, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity at 30-60 microvolts. This rhythm was reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present, with low-amplitude mixed alpha and beta frequencies anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry. The background was continuous and demonstrated normal variability and reactivity.

Awake state: During wakefulness, there was an appropriate admixture of alpha and beta activity. No sustained focal delta or theta slowing was identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or other rhythmic or periodic patterns.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and was replaced by diffuse low-amplitude theta activity with slow rolling eye movements. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was appropriately organized and symmetric.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline following completion of the procedure.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave complexes, or generalized epileptiform discharges. No focal cerebral slowing was present.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG demonstrated an apparently regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. There are no focal, lateralized, or epileptiform abnormalities.
2. No electrographic seizures or habitual clinical events were recorded.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. The absence of interictal epileptiform discharges on this study does not preclude an increased seizure propensity, particularly in a patient with an established history of focal impaired-awareness seizures. Clinical correlation and continued management with the current antiseizure regimen of brivaracetam and lamotrigine are recommended.
