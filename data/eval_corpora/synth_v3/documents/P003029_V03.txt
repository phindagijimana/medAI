Indication: Generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures beginning at age 66 in the setting of a structural etiology/intracranial tumor. Events currently occur approximately three times per month and have been responsive to oxcarbazepine. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A  thirty-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness and drowsiness. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. No antiseizure medication was withheld for this study.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-formed 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen frontocentrally. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During wakefulness, there were occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3-3.5 Hz. These were bilaterally synchronous and symmetric, with maximal expression over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. Most occurred as isolated complexes or brief runs lasting 1-2 seconds without an apparent clinical correlate.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike-and-wave discharges were mildly activated by sleep, at times occurring in brief 2- to 3-second bursts. No electrographic seizure was identified.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced expected diffuse theta and delta slowing. One 4-second burst of generalized 3 Hz spike-and-wave activity occurred during hyperventilation. Video review showed subtle behavioral arrest and interruption of counting, consistent with a brief electroclinical absence seizure. There was immediate return to baseline without postictal slowing or confusion.

Photic stimulation: Photic stimulation at stepwise frequencies produced symmetric photic driving, most prominent posteriorly. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, with frontal predominance and no consistent focal lead-in. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or lateralized periodic activity were observed.

Ictal events: One brief electroclinical absence seizure was captured during hyperventilation, characterized by behavioral arrest associated with a 4-second burst of generalized 3 Hz spike-and-wave activity.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, supporting an increased propensity for generalized seizures.

2. One brief electroclinical seizure with behavioral arrest and generalized 3 Hz spike-and-wave activity was elicited by hyperventilation, consistent with a typical absence seizure.

3. No focal slowing or focal epileptiform abnormality was identified on this recording. The EEG findings support generalized epilepsy; clinical and imaging correlation remains necessary given the reported structural tumor etiology. The patient’s seizures are clinically described as drug responsive on current oxcarbazepine therapy, with residual events approximately three times per month.
