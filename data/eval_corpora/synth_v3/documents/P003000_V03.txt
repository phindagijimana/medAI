Indication:  Drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 33 and occurring approximately twice yearly despite current treatment with topiramate and brivaracetam. The epilepsy is considered structural in etiology, with MRI demonstrating focal cortical dysplasia, Palmini type IIa. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A  thirty-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, supplemented by a single-lead electrocardiogram. The recording was reviewed using multiple referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. No antiseizure medications were withheld for this study.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with an anterior-to-posterior gradient of frequencies and amplitudes. There was a well-formed, symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. No persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state:  During maximal wakefulness, there was normal reactivity to eye opening and closure. No focal attenuation, breach rhythm, or rhythmic or periodic pattern was seen.

Drowsy/sleep:  With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, K complexes, and sleep spindles. There was no activation of epileptiform discharges during drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric buildup of generalized theta activity without focal accentuation. No epileptiform abnormality was provoked.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No focal or generalized interictal epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No definite focal slowing was present.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No push-button events occurred.

Electrocardiogram:  The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states. There are no focal abnormalities or interictal epileptiform discharges during this recording.
2. No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured.
3. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. The patient’s clinical history remains consistent with drug-resistant focal epilepsy manifesting as focal impaired-awareness seizures, with a structural etiology based on MRI evidence of Palmini type IIa focal cortical dysplasia. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended; prolonged video-EEG monitoring may be considered if further electroclinical localization is required.
