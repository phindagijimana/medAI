Indication:  Evaluation of focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 24, with a reported frequency of approximately three seizures per month and clinical responsiveness to antiseizure medication. Current medications include oxcarbazepine and zonisamide. Etiology remains unknown. Prior MRI demonstrated an incidental lesion not established as the epileptogenic substrate.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined anterior-to-posterior gradient. A 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  During maximal wakefulness, normal voltage and frequency gradients were maintained. No LPDs, GPDs, SIRPIDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns were identified.

Drowsy / sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, slow roving eye movements, and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was preserved.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of focal or generalized epileptiform discharges.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Occasional medium-amplitude sharp waves were present over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7 with a field involving T7 and FT9. These discharges were more apparent during drowsiness and sleep. No independent right temporal or generalized spike-and-wave discharges were seen. There was no TIRDA.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal awake and sleep EEG due to occasional left anterior temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/FT9-T7. These are interictal epileptiform discharges indicating increased seizure propensity arising from the left temporal region.

2. No electrographic or clinical seizures were recorded. The remainder of the background was normal, without persistent focal slowing.

These findings support a focal epilepsy syndrome in the appropriate clinical context. The absence of a recorded seizure does not exclude epilepsy or independently establish the relationship of the incidental MRI lesion to the patient’s seizures. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended.
