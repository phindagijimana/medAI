Assessment / Impression

This patient has epilepsy with unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 14. The current epilepsy type remains unknown because the available semiology does not distinguish generalized-onset tonic-clonic seizures from focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures.

The reported seizure frequency is six per week. At the same time, the epilepsy is labeled drug responsive, and the current regimen consists of levetiracetam and lacosamide. These elements require clarification. In particular, we need to establish whether six seizures per week represents the present frequency or a historical pretreatment frequency and whether there was a meaningful response after initiation of the current dual therapy.

The etiology is categorized as immune. Supporting records should be obtained before determining whether further immune evaluation or treatment is indicated. At present, there is insufficient source information to define the specific immune disorder or its current activity.

Although the patient is on two antiseizure medications, the supplied phenotype characterizes the epilepsy as drug responsive rather than drug resistant. Therefore, I would not initiate a presurgical epilepsy evaluation solely on the basis of the current documentation. If the reported six weekly events are ongoing despite appropriately selected and adequately dosed medications, the treatment-response classification would need to be revisited after medication doses, adherence, event type, and prior trials are verified.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue levetiracetam and lacosamide pending confirmation of the exact doses, frequencies, adherence, tolerability, and prescribing history. I would avoid making an empiric dose change until medication reconciliation is complete.

2. Obtain a more detailed seizure history, including witness descriptions and any available videos. Particular attention should be paid to initial motor activity, asymmetry, head or eye deviation, vocalization, duration, postictal findings, and whether events occur from sleep.

3. Clarify the seizure-frequency timeline. We need to determine whether approximately six events per week is the current burden or a historical baseline and how that frequency changed after levetiracetam and lacosamide were started.

4. Request prior EEG, ambulatory EEG, and EMU/LTM reports and tracings, if performed. If prior diagnostic EEG data cannot be obtained, EEG evaluation should be considered to help distinguish focal from generalized epilepsy.

5. Request prior brain MRI images and reports. If no appropriate epilepsy-protocol MRI has been completed, brain MRI with an epilepsy protocol should be considered after review of the available records.

6. Obtain the records supporting the immune etiology, including relevant laboratory, CSF, imaging, and treatment documentation. Any additional immune-directed evaluation should be guided by those results rather than repeated without a defined clinical question.

7. The patient should observe standard seizure precautions while tonic-clonic events remain possible, including avoiding driving unless permitted under applicable NY requirements, unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Showers are preferred to unsupervised baths.

8. Follow up after the outside records and medication details are available. If frequent events are confirmed despite adequate therapy, VEEG characterization and reassessment of drug responsiveness would be appropriate before considering advanced treatment options.
