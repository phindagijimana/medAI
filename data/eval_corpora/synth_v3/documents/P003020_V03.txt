Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures beginning at age 13, currently occurring approximately 7 times per week. Epilepsy is considered drug-responsive. Current antiseizure medication is perampanel. Reported structural etiology with hippocampal sclerosis on MRI. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The study was recorded with synchronized video. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and stage N2 sleep was achieved. No antiseizure medication was administered during the recording.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was noted frontocentrally. There was no persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During wakefulness, there were occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3.5-5 Hz. These complexes were maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with a broad bilateral field and no consistent focal lead-in. Discharges occurred singly and in brief bursts lasting up to 1.5 seconds. No definite clinical correlate was evident on video.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the posterior dominant rhythm attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike-and-polyspike-wave discharges became more frequent during drowsiness and sleep. No electrographic seizure was identified.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild symmetric diffuse theta slowing. There was a modest increase in generalized epileptiform discharge frequency, without a sustained ictal pattern or clinical change.

Photic stimulation: Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. Generalized polyspike-wave discharges were elicited at 15 and 18 Hz, consistent with a photoparoxysmal response. No clinical myoclonus or generalized tonic-clonic seizure occurred.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-5 Hz spike-and-polyspike-wave discharges, enhanced by drowsiness, sleep, hyperventilation, and intermittent photic stimulation. There were no independent temporal sharp waves, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. In particular, no persistent focal abnormality was identified over the temporal regions despite the reported MRI finding of hippocampal sclerosis.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or habitual clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-polyspike-wave discharges, with activation during drowsiness, sleep, hyperventilation, and photic stimulation. These findings support an increased generalized seizure propensity and are compatible with the reported generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures.

2. No electrographic seizure was recorded. No focal slowing or independent temporal epileptiform activity was present during this study. The reported hippocampal sclerosis and structural epilepsy classification should be correlated with the clinical semiology and MRI findings, as the electrographic abnormalities in this recording are generalized rather than focal.
