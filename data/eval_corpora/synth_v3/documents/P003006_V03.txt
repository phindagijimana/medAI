Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Evaluation of a patient with focal epilepsy of infectious etiology, with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 10 years. The reported seizure frequency is approximately two per month. The epilepsy is considered drug-responsive. Current antiseizure medication is perampanel.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz alpha activity at 30-60 µV, maximal over the bilateral occipital regions and reactive to eye opening. An appropriate anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or diffuse background slowing.

Awake state: The waking background was continuous and symmetric. No focal delta or theta slowing was identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of symmetric diffuse theta activity with slow rolling eye movements. Brief stage N2 sleep was characterized by bilaterally synchronous and symmetric sleep spindles, vertex waves, and K complexes. No epileptiform discharges were activated by drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal predominance. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of the maneuver. No epileptiform activity was elicited.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation was performed over a range of flash frequencies and produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal, multifocal, or generalized interictal epileptiform discharges were present. Specifically, no spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, or focal slowing were identified.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures were recorded. No habitual clinical events or patient-reported symptoms occurred during this study.

ECG: The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and brief stage N2 sleep. There are no focal abnormalities, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures.

2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. The reported history remains clinically consistent with focal epilepsy manifesting as focal impaired-awareness seizures. Clinical correlation is recommended, including correlation with the reported infectious etiology, seizure onset at age 10 years, current frequency of approximately two seizures per month, and ongoing drug-responsive treatment with perampanel.
