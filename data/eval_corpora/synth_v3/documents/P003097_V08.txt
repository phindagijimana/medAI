Assessment / Impression

The patient has recurrent unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 14, with a reported frequency of approximately six per week. The epilepsy type remains unknown because the available history does not establish a focal or generalized onset.

An immune etiology has been proposed, but the supporting clinical, laboratory, EEG, and imaging information is not yet available for review. We will need to clarify whether this designation reflects a previously established immune-mediated neurologic diagnosis or a working hypothesis.

The patient is currently receiving dual therapy with levetiracetam and lacosamide and has been categorized as having drug-responsive epilepsy. This designation appears potentially discordant with the reported seizure frequency and therefore must be verified. Accurate medication reconciliation and a more complete seizure chronology will be essential before changing therapy.

At present, there is insufficient information to determine whether the ongoing events are epileptic seizures, whether all events represent a single seizure type, or whether they arise from a focal or generalized network. Further electroclinical characterization is warranted.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current levetiracetam and lacosamide regimen without an empiric dose change until the exact doses, frequencies, adherence, tolerability, and prior response are confirmed. The patient should not stop either medication abruptly.

2. Obtain a detailed seizure history, including any warning, initial motor or behavioral change, symmetry, duration, loss of awareness, tongue injury, incontinence, postictal symptoms, nocturnal occurrence, triggers, and recovery time. Witness descriptions and available videos may be useful.

3. Request outside neurology records, prior EEG/VEEG reports and tracings, brain MRI reports and images, prior ASM history, and documentation supporting the suspected immune etiology.

4. Obtain an EEG if a recent interpretable study is not available. If routine EEG and history do not clarify the epilepsy type, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient EMU/LTM admission should be considered to capture and characterize the habitual events.

5. Obtain or review a brain MRI performed with an epilepsy protocol if this has not already been completed. Imaging should be reviewed for a structural correlate, including findings that could support focal epilepsy or an inflammatory process.

6. Clarify the basis of the proposed immune etiology. Additional laboratory or CSF evaluation should be guided by the prior workup and the broader clinical presentation rather than ordered indiscriminately.

7. Maintain a written seizure diary documenting event frequency, duration, possible triggers, missed medication doses, and recovery. This will help reconcile the reported frequency with the designation of drug-responsive epilepsy.

8. We reviewed standard seizure safety principles. The patient should avoid unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Driving must follow applicable NY regulations and clinician guidance based on ongoing seizure control.

9. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after outside records and diagnostic studies are available. Earlier reassessment is appropriate for a substantial increase in event frequency, prolonged seizures, repeated seizures without recovery, injury, or other urgent concerns.
