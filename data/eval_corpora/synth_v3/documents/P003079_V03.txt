Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation for interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with structural, drug-resistant focal epilepsy characterized by focal impaired-awareness seizures, with onset at age 9. MRI reportedly demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia. The patient is currently seizure-free, with 0 seizures per month, while taking lacosamide, perampanel, and levetiracetam.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. No antiseizure medication changes were made for this recording.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, consisting predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies. A well-formed 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was maintained. There was no persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry.

Awake state: Low-amplitude beta activity was present over the frontocentral regions. No sustained focal delta or theta slowing was identified. Muscle and eye-movement artifact intermittently obscured the anterior temporal derivations without materially limiting interpretation.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse mixed theta activity with slow roving eye movements. Brief stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform activation occurred during sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz elicited a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, periodic discharges, TIRDA, or other definite interictal epileptiform abnormalities were identified. No focal slowing was present.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal awake, drowsy, and briefly asleep routine EEG. There are no focal, lateralizing, or epileptiform abnormalities.
2. No electrographic seizures or clinical events were recorded.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. The patient’s history remains consistent with focal impaired-awareness seizures in the setting of a structural etiology, specifically MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia. Clinical correlation is recommended, particularly given the history of drug-resistant epilepsy despite current seizure freedom on lacosamide, perampanel, and levetiracetam.
