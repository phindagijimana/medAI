Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures beginning at age 30. Events currently occur approximately twice per month and are considered drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are topiramate and brivaracetam. The epilepsy is classified as structural in the setting of an MRI-demonstrated tumor.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures consisted of hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with the expected anterior-to-posterior gradient. A well-defined 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. Anteriorly, there was the expected admixture of low-amplitude beta and theta activity. No persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry was identified.

Awake state: During wakefulness, there were occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3-4 Hz. These were synchronous and symmetric, with maximal expression over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, and occurred both as isolated complexes and in brief bursts lasting 1-3 seconds. No definite behavioral impairment accompanied these brief bursts.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike-and-wave discharges became modestly more frequent during sleep. There were no focal sleep-activated IEDs.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced expected diffuse theta slowing and activated several brief runs of generalized 3-Hz spike-and-wave activity lasting up to 4 seconds. No clear interruption of responsiveness or other definite clinical correlate was observed.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced symmetric photic driving at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, with activation during hyperventilation and sleep. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or focal epileptiform discharges were identified.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or typical clinical absence seizures were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by hyperventilation and sleep. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized seizures and are compatible with the patient’s diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures.

2. No electroclinical seizure was recorded. A normal and symmetric background was maintained without definite focal cerebral dysfunction on this study.

Clinical correlation is advised regarding the reported structural etiology and MRI-demonstrated tumor, as the electrographic abnormalities in this recording are generalized rather than focal. The patient remains on topiramate and brivaracetam, with drug-responsive absence events currently reported approximately twice monthly.
