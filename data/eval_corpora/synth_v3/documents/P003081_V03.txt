Indication: Focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures. Seizure onset was reported at age 7, with a current frequency of approximately three seizures per month. The epilepsy is considered immune-mediated in association with LGI1 antibody and has been clinically drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are zonisamide and perampanel. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The recording was reviewed using referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and demonstrated appropriate anterior-posterior organization. A symmetric 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and appropriately reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or generalized background slowing.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, the background was well organized and symmetric. No focal slowing was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric 4-7 Hz theta activity with slow roving eye movements. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was preserved.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric background slowing without focal or epileptiform activation.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at sequential flash frequencies produced a symmetric posterior driving response. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No persistent focal delta slowing was present.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures were recorded. No habitual clinical events or push-button events occurred during this study.

ECG: Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. There are no focal or generalized interictal epileptiform abnormalities and no electrographic seizures.

2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. The absence of epileptiform discharges on this brief recording does not establish seizure remission or otherwise characterize the reported focal impaired-awareness events. If further electroclinical characterization is needed, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring may be considered in the appropriate clinical context.
