Emergency Department Provider Note

Chief Complaint: Status epilepticus.

HPI: 30-year-old male with drug-resistant generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology, characterized by myoclonic seizures since age 12, brought by ambulance for ongoing seizure activity. Per EMS report from xxx EMS, family witnessed abrupt generalized myoclonic jerking progressing to sustained bilateral convulsions and unresponsiveness. Seizure activity had continued approximately 12 minutes before EMS arrival. Midazolam 10 mg IM was given at 18:42 with transient improvement, followed by recurrent convulsions en route. No reported fall, head trauma, fever, or preceding illness.

Baseline seizure frequency is approximately 5 per month despite zonisamide and carbamazepine. Family believes he took his morning medications but cannot confirm the most recent doses or full adherence. No known alcohol withdrawal or recreational drug exposure. On ED arrival, patient was unresponsive with forced gaze and recurrent generalized tonic-clonic activity. History otherwise limited by acuity.

Neurology consultation: On-call neurology evaluated in ED. Recommends neurocritical care admission, continuous EEG, continuation of propofol, completion of levetiracetam load, and reassessment of chronic antiseizure regimen once medication history and levels are clarified.

Medical Decision-Making (MDM): Presentation is consistent with generalized convulsive status epilepticus, with no meaningful recovery between events. Differential included epileptic seizure, toxic/metabolic derangement, CNS infection, intracranial hemorrhage, syncope, TIA, and psychogenic nonepileptic event. Prolonged witnessed convulsions, post-event unresponsiveness, elevated lactate, and known epilepsy strongly support status epilepticus. No fever, meningismus, major electrolyte abnormality, or acute CT finding.

Possible trigger is subtherapeutic medication exposure/nonadherence in the setting of drug-resistant epilepsy. Airway protection was required after failure of benzodiazepines and second-line IV antiseizure medication to promptly terminate clinical activity. No recurrent visible convulsions after propofol; continuous EEG required to r/o nonconvulsive status.

Assessment:  
1. Generalized convulsive status epilepticus.  
2. Drug-resistant generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures.  
3. Acute respiratory failure requiring intubation.  
4. Subtherapeutic carbamazepine level.

Disposition: Admit to neurocritical care under neurology. Continuous EEG and ventilator management. Seizure precautions and medication reconciliation with family/pharmacy. Critical care time: 48 minutes, excluding separately billable procedures.
