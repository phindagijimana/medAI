Assessment / Impression

This patient has drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures beginning at age 66. The current reported seizure burden is approximately two seizures per year while taking lamotrigine, levetiracetam, and oxcarbazepine. MRI reportedly demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis, making a structural etiology the most likely category based on the available information.

The combination of persistent seizures on three current ASMs and a potentially relevant structural MRI lesion warrants comprehensive review. At present, however, the available information is insufficient to determine seizure onset laterality, electroclinical concordance, or suitability for a specific surgical or device-based therapy. The side of the hippocampal sclerosis is not stated, and no EEG data were available. Likewise, the exact doses and prior medication history must be established before medication management can be meaningfully optimized.

I discussed that a low annual seizure frequency does not eliminate the clinical significance of ongoing seizures. It can, however, make event capture during a limited EMU admission more challenging. The next steps should therefore be individualized after review of the original records and discussion with the patient regarding the goals of evaluation.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Lamictal, Keppra, and Trileptal at the currently prescribed doses pending complete medication reconciliation. I would not recommend an empiric dose change until the exact doses, frequencies, tolerability, and prior treatment responses are confirmed.

2. Obtain a complete antiseizure medication history, including previously attempted agents, maximum tolerated doses, reasons for discontinuation, and whether each trial was adequate. This information is important to confirm and characterize drug resistance.

3. Request the original brain MRI images and the complete radiology report for review at xxx Medical Center. Particular attention should be paid to the side and extent of the hippocampal sclerosis. Repeat epilepsy-protocol MRI can be considered if the prior study is unavailable or technically inadequate.

4. Obtain prior EEG records, if any. If the patient wishes to pursue a comprehensive presurgical evaluation, inpatient EMU admission for prolonged VEEG/LTM would be appropriate to characterize the habitual events and assess seizure onset localization. Given the reported frequency of approximately two seizures per year, the limitations of event capture should be discussed in advance.

5. If the electroclinical data and MRI prove concordant, further standard evaluation may include neuropsychological testing and multidisciplinary epilepsy surgery conference review. Decisions regarding resection, ablation, VNS, RNS, DBS, or invasive monitoring should be deferred until the necessary localization data are available.

6. We reviewed general seizure precautions, including avoiding unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery when seizures could create a safety risk. Driving should follow applicable NY requirements and the treating clinician’s guidance.

7. Follow up after the outside MRI, EEG records, and complete medication list have been obtained. The patient should contact the treating neurology team sooner for increased seizure frequency, a change in event type, or medication-related concerns.
