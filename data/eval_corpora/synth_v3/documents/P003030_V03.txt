Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of generalized epilepsy characterized by generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 36, with a reported frequency of approximately five seizures per month. The epilepsy is attributed clinically to an infectious etiology and is considered drug-responsive. Current antiseizure medications are zonisamide and brivaracetam.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized, with the expected anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradients. A symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and reactive to eye opening. Anteriorly, there was the expected low-voltage beta activity with intermixed alpha and theta frequencies. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During wakefulness, there were occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3-4 Hz. These occurred in brief bursts lasting 1-2 seconds, with shifting bifrontal emphasis but no consistent focal lead-in. No clinical change was apparent on video during these discharges.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized epileptiform discharges were mildly activated during drowsiness and sleep, at times occurring in association with K complexes. No focal sleep-related epileptiform activity was identified.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced a symmetric buildup of diffuse theta and delta activity. A single brief burst of generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave activity occurred without clinical correlate.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation from 1-20 Hz elicited symmetric photic driving at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, bifrontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, with activation in drowsiness and sleep. There were no independent focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, or TIRDA.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, most prominent bifrontally and activated by drowsiness and sleep. These findings indicate an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are concordant with the clinical history of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. The absence of a captured seizure does not exclude epilepsy. Clinical correlation is recommended, including assessment of the reported seizure frequency of approximately five per month despite a clinically drug-responsive course on zonisamide and brivaracetam.
