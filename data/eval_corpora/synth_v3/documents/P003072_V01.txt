Assessment / Impression

This is an adult with generalized epilepsy, manifested by generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 29. The current reported seizure burden is approximately one seizure per month. Etiology has been classified as metabolic, and the current ASM is valproate.

The supplied phenotype labels the epilepsy as drug-responsive. Nevertheless, monthly generalized tonic-clonic seizures represent clinically important ongoing seizure activity. Before changing therapy, we need an accurate medication reconciliation and additional records to determine whether the patient has had a meaningful response to valproate, whether the current regimen has been adequately dosed and tolerated, and whether metabolic abnormalities remain active contributors.

There is not enough information at present to label the epilepsy drug-resistant. Likewise, there is not enough information to recommend a presurgical epilepsy evaluation, VNS, RNS, DBS, or resective surgery. The immediate priorities are confirmation of the generalized epilepsy diagnosis, clarification of the metabolic etiology, and optimization of the current drug-responsive treatment strategy.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue valproate at the currently prescribed regimen pending verification of the exact formulation, dose, and frequency. The patient should not abruptly discontinue valproate because abrupt withdrawal may increase seizure risk.

2. Obtain a complete medication reconciliation, including adherence, adverse effects, prior valproate dose changes, and any previously tried ASMs. We will also clarify whether there have been seizure-free intervals while taking valproate.

3. Request prior neurology notes, EEG reports or tracings, MRI brain reports and images, prior EMU/LTM records, emergency evaluations, and relevant laboratory testing from the referring office and outside facilities.

4. Clarify the stated metabolic etiology. I recommend review of the prior diagnostic basis and relevant laboratory data rather than assuming a specific metabolic disorder that has not yet been documented for us.

5. Consider updated EEG testing if prior EEG data cannot be obtained or if the generalized classification remains uncertain after record review. Additional imaging or VEEG can be considered based on the prior workup and subsequent clinical course.

6. Obtain appropriate safety monitoring for valproate, including a CBC, hepatic function testing, and a valproate level, if these have not recently been performed. Interpretation should account for dose, formulation, and timing of the sample.

7. Maintain a seizure calendar documenting all generalized tonic-clonic seizures, missed medication doses, and any suspected metabolic or other precipitants. This will help establish the true treatment response.

8. We reviewed general seizure precautions. The patient should avoid driving unless permitted under applicable state requirements and cleared by the treating clinician. Additional precautions include avoiding unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and bathing alone. Emergency evaluation is appropriate for a prolonged seizure, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, or breathing difficulty.

9. Follow up after outside records, medication details, and relevant testing are available. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if seizure frequency increases or medication intolerance develops.
