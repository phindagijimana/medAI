Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Evaluation of drug-resistant generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 26, with a current reported frequency of approximately two seizures per week. Etiology remains unknown. Current antiseizure medications are perampanel, levetiracetam, and carbamazepine.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. The recording was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity over the occipital regions, reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and voltage gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. Frequent generalized epileptiform discharges were present, consisting of frontally predominant, bilaterally synchronous 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave complexes. These typically occurred as isolated discharges or brief bursts lasting 1-3 seconds, without an evident clinical correlate. At times, the discharges showed minor shifting hemispheric emphasis, although their overall morphology and field were generalized.

Drowsy / sleep:  With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and sleep spindles. Generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges became more frequent during drowsiness and sleep. No consistent focal lead-in was identified.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced symmetric generalized theta and delta slowing. There was a modest increase in the frequency of generalized spike-and-wave bursts, without clinical change or evolution into an electrographic seizure.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. No definite photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings:  Frequent generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, enhanced during drowsiness and sleep. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or focal slowing were identified.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to frequent generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with activation during drowsiness and sleep. In the appropriate clinical context, these findings support an increased seizure propensity associated with generalized epilepsy.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal and symmetric background was otherwise maintained, without evidence of focal cerebral dysfunction.

Clinical correlation is advised, including correlation with the reported drug-resistant generalized tonic-clonic seizures occurring approximately twice weekly despite treatment with perampanel, levetiracetam, and carbamazepine.
