Indication: Generalized epilepsy characterized by generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 32, with a reported frequency of approximately four seizures per month. The epilepsy is considered structural in etiology in the setting of an MRI-demonstrated tumor and has been drug responsive on current levetiracetam therapy. EEG obtained to evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including a single-channel electrocardiogram. Data were reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and well organized, with an 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or focal attenuation.

Awake state: During maximal alertness, the background consisted of an appropriate admixture of alpha and beta frequencies with intermittent low-amplitude theta activity. No focal slowing was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR, slow roving eye movements, and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was appropriately developed and symmetric.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline following completion of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal, lateralized, or generalized epileptiform discharges were present. Specifically, no generalized spike-and-wave or polyspike-and-wave discharges were identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns. No persistent focal slowing was seen.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. There are no focal or generalized interictal epileptiform abnormalities and no electrographic seizures.

2. This recording does not provide electrographic evidence of an increased focal or generalized epileptogenic potential. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of epilepsy, including generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Clinical correlation with the reported seizure semiology, structural MRI finding of tumor, and response to levetiracetam is recommended.
