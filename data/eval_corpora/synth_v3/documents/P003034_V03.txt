Indication:  Evaluation of epilepsy of unknown type characterized by unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures. Events began at age 68 and occurred approximately five times per month prior to treatment. Etiology is unknown. Seizures are reported to be drug responsive on current zonisamide therapy.

Technique:  A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including a single-lead electrocardiogram. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the study, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined anterior-to-posterior gradient. A symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or diffuse background slowing.

Awake state:  During maximal wakefulness, the background remained symmetric and reactive. No focal delta or theta slowing was identified. There were no lateralized periodic discharges, generalized periodic discharges, or other rhythmic or periodic patterns.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the posterior dominant rhythm and emergence of diffuse theta activity with slow rolling eye movements. Brief stage N2 sleep was marked by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. No epileptiform discharges were activated by drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal abnormality or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric posterior driving response at selected frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal spikes or sharp waves, generalized spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures were recorded. No clinical events or patient-reported habitual episodes occurred during this study.

Electrocardiogram:  The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and brief stage N2 sleep. There were no focal, lateralizing, or epileptiform abnormalities.
2. No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. In particular, this study does not establish the onset or localization of the patient’s reported tonic-clonic seizures. Clinical correlation is recommended, with prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient video-EEG monitoring considered if further electroclinical characterization is required.
