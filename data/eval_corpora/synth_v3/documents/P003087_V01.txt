Assessment / Impression

The patient has a reported generalized epilepsy manifested by generalized tonic-clonic seizures, with onset at 56 years of age and an average seizure frequency of four per month. Etiology is currently unknown. The patient is taking valproate and lacosamide, and the referral information classifies the epilepsy as drug responsive.

There are important gaps in the available information. In particular, the exact medication doses, adherence, prior medication trials, treatment response timeline, EEG findings, and neuroimaging results were not provided. The combination of ongoing seizures and a “drug responsive” designation should be clarified with the referring records and a seizure diary. At present, the available information does not establish drug-resistant epilepsy.

Because seizure onset occurred at age 56 and the etiology is unknown, it is appropriate to confirm the electroclinical classification and determine whether a structural cause has been adequately excluded. No conclusion regarding epilepsy surgery or device therapy can be reached from the limited records currently available.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Depakote (valproate) and Vimpat (lacosamide) without abrupt interruption while the exact doses, formulations, and dosing schedules are confirmed. I would not make a dose change based on the incomplete medication list alone.

2. Obtain the complete outside neurology record, including prior antiseizure medication trials, response and adverse-effect history, EEG reports or tracings, MRI reports and images, emergency evaluations, and available laboratory results.

3. Maintain a seizure diary documenting generalized tonic-clonic events, possible warnings, duration, postictal recovery, missed medication doses, and potential provoking factors. This will help determine whether the current frequency remains approximately four per month and whether the present regimen is providing a meaningful response.

4. If prior EEG data cannot be obtained or are nondiagnostic, obtain an EEG to evaluate for generalized IEDs and to help confirm the reported epilepsy classification. Prolonged ambulatory EEG or EMU-based VEEG may be considered if routine testing and the clinical history remain inconclusive.

5. If adequate prior neuroimaging is unavailable, obtain an epilepsy-protocol MRI of the brain given seizure onset at age 56 and the currently unknown etiology.

6. Complete medication safety monitoring appropriate for valproate, including a CBC, comprehensive metabolic panel with hepatic studies, and a valproate level, if these have not recently been performed. Results should be interpreted with the confirmed dose and timing of the blood draw.

7. We reviewed the importance of taking antiseizure medications consistently and avoiding abrupt discontinuation. Standard seizure precautions apply, including avoidance of unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. The patient should not drive unless permitted under applicable state requirements and cleared by the treating clinician.

8. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after medication reconciliation and receipt of the prior EEG and MRI data. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if seizure frequency increases or medication intolerance develops.
