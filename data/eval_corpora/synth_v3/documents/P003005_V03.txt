Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 7. The patient is currently seizure-free, with 0 seizures per month, and is considered drug responsive on topiramate and zonisamide. Brain MRI demonstrates focal cortical dysplasia, consistent with a structural etiology. EEG requested to assess the interictal background and epileptiform burden.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, supplemented by a single-channel electrocardiogram. The study was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-defined 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was distributed symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  During wakefulness, there were occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges at 3.5-4.5 Hz. These were maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, were bilaterally synchronous, and typically occurred as isolated complexes or brief bursts lasting less than 2 seconds. No clinical change accompanied these discharges.

Drowsy / sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges were mildly activated by sleep. No independent focal epileptiform discharges were identified.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced expected symmetric generalized theta and delta slowing. A brief generalized spike-and-wave burst occurred without clinical correlate. No electrographic seizure was induced.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with mild activation during drowsiness and sleep. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or focal slowing were present.

Ictal events:  No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, mildly activated by sleep. These findings support an increased risk for generalized-onset seizures and are concordant with the clinical diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. The waking and sleep backgrounds were otherwise normal.

3. No focal electrographic abnormality corresponding to the reported MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia was identified during this recording. A normal focal background does not exclude a structural epileptogenic lesion; correlation with the MRI and clinical history is recommended.
