Emergency Department Provider Note

Chief Complaint: Breakthrough seizure.

HPI: 36-year-old female with focal epilepsy secondary to CASPR2 antibody-associated autoimmune disease, onset at age 12, presents by private vehicle after a breakthrough focal aware seizure. Event was witnessed by family and consisted of her typical brief behavioral pause with right hand tingling and repetitive hand movement lasting approximately 60 seconds. She remained aware and recalls the entire event. No generalized shaking, loss of consciousness, fall, head strike, tongue biting, or incontinence. Medical Decision-Making: Patient with known focal epilepsy of immune etiology related to CASPR2 antibody disease presents after one typical focal aware seizure. She has a longstanding seizure history beginning at age 12 and remains on dual therapy with zonisamide and lamotrigine. She is afebrile, hemodynamically stable, neurologically intact, and at baseline. No evidence of status epilepticus, CNS infection, stroke/TIA, intracranial hemorrhage, significant metabolic trigger, intoxication, or traumatic injury.

Differential included epileptic seizure, syncope, TIA, and psychogenic nonepileptic event. Preserved awareness and stereotyped semiology matching prior focal seizures favor breakthrough focal aware seizure. Missed medication doses are the most likely trigger. No Ativan (lorazepam) or IV antiseizure load was indicated because the event resolved spontaneously and did not recur during ED observation. Patient took her scheduled home medications and tolerated PO.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough focal aware seizure.  
2. Focal epilepsy due to CASPR2 antibody-associated autoimmune disease.  
3. Missed antiseizure medication dose.

Disposition: Discharged home in stable condition after observation and neurology consultation. Continue zonisamide and lamotrigine exactly as prescribed; no medication changes from the ED. Follow up with epilepsy neurology within 1 week for review of seizure frequency and pending drug levels. Discussed strict medication adherence, seizure precautions, avoiding heights, open water, baths alone, and operating heavy machinery. No driving unless cleared under applicable state law. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, new weakness, persistent confusion, fever, severe headache, trauma, or other worsening symptoms. Patient verbalized understanding.
