Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with seizure onset at 29 years of age and epilepsy classified in the referral materials as combined generalized and focal epilepsy. The documented clinical seizure type is generalized tonic-clonic, with a reported frequency of approximately five per month. The etiology is categorized as metabolic. The patient is currently treated with topiramate and has been designated drug responsive.

The available information is insufficient to determine the basis for the combined generalized and focal classification or to specify the focal seizure phenotype. It is likewise unclear which metabolic condition is believed to underlie the epilepsy. The reported event frequency and the designation of drug-responsive epilepsy require clarification through a medication history and longitudinal seizure timeline. On the present record, I would not classify the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy, as adequate prior ASM trials and outcomes have not been documented.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Topamax (topiramate) at the patient’s currently prescribed home dose pending formal medication reconciliation. I would not make a dose change without confirmation of the exact dose, frequency, adherence, tolerability, and prior response.

2. Obtain a complete seizure history, including warnings, onset pattern, symmetry or lateralizing signs, duration, postictal symptoms, potential clustering, and witness observations. A seizure calendar should be maintained to verify the reported frequency of approximately five events per month.

3. Request prior EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG/LTM, or EMU reports and tracings, if they exist. These data will be important to assess the documented combined generalized and focal classification.

4. Request prior brain MRI reports and images, if previously obtained. If adequate epilepsy-protocol imaging has not been completed, this may be considered after review of the full history and prior testing.

5. Clarify the metabolic etiology, including the specific suspected or confirmed disorder and the laboratory data supporting that classification. Relevant prior metabolic testing should be forwarded to our office for review.

6. Obtain a complete prior ASM history, including medication names, highest tolerated doses, duration of therapy, reasons for discontinuation, and seizure response. This information is necessary to confirm the documented drug-responsive status and to determine whether additional medical therapy should be considered.

7. We reviewed standard seizure safety principles. Until seizure control and driving eligibility are clarified, the patient should not drive. The patient should avoid unsupervised swimming or bathing, working at heights, open flames, and operating dangerous machinery. Emergency evaluation is appropriate for a prolonged generalized tonic-clonic seizure, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, or respiratory concern.

8. Follow-up should be arranged after receipt of the prior diagnostic studies and completion of medication reconciliation. Consideration of additional EEG monitoring or treatment adjustment can then be based on verified seizure type, frequency, and response to topiramate.
