Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with genetic focal epilepsy beginning at age 35. The reported seizure type is focal to bilateral tonic-clonic, with a current frequency of approximately five events per week. The reported causative genetic finding is KCNQ2 c.881C>T.

The patient remains on dual ASM therapy with lacosamide and carbamazepine. Both are sodium-channel–acting agents commonly used in focal epilepsy; however, the exact regimen cannot be evaluated until the prescribed doses and frequencies are confirmed. The supplied classification is drug-responsive epilepsy. On the information currently available, I would not independently relabel the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy, and there is not enough supporting information to make a determination regarding epilepsy surgery or device-based therapies such as VNS, RNS, or DBS.

The ongoing reported frequency of focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures nevertheless warrants prompt medication reconciliation and review of the prior diagnostic data. Confirmation of the genetic report is also important, particularly because only the gene and nucleotide-level variant were supplied.
