Exam Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx

Indication:  Drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 28, currently occurring approximately 6 times per week despite zonisamide and valproate; suspected structural etiology.

Comparison:  None.

Technique:  Multiplanar, multisequence MRI of the brain was performed on a 3T scanner without intravenous contrast. Sequences included sagittal T1, axial and coronal T2, axial and coronal FLAIR with thin coronal imaging through the mesial temporal structures, double inversion recovery, axial DWI/ADC, axial SWI, and 3D T1 MPRAGE.

Findings:

Ventricles and Extra-axial Spaces: Ventricles are normal in size and configuration. No hydrocephalus or extra-axial fluid collection. No midline shift.

Brain Parenchyma:

Supratentorial: Focal encephalomalacia and surrounding gliosis are present in the left lateral temporal lobe, with mild associated ex vacuo prominence of the adjacent temporal horn and sulci. No restricted diffusion, acute intracranial hemorrhage, mass effect, or susceptibility abnormality. No intracranial mass is identified.

Infratentorial: No focal signal abnormality.

Mesial Temporal Structures: The hippocampi are symmetric in size and signal, with preserved internal architecture. No hippocampal volume loss or abnormal T2/FLAIR hyperintensity. No evidence of mesial temporal sclerosis.

Cortex: Aside from the left lateral temporal encephalomalacic change, cortical morphology and gyral pattern are preserved. No focal cortical thickening, blurring of the gray-white junction, transmantle sign, heterotopia, or other malformation of cortical development is identified.

White Matter: No additional significant white matter signal abnormality.

Basal Ganglia and Thalami: Normal in signal and morphology.

Brainstem and Cerebellum: Normal. No cerebellar volume loss or focal lesion.

Vasculature (as seen on conventional sequences): Major intracranial arterial flow voids are preserved.

Skull Base and Calvarium: No suspicious marrow-replacing lesion.

Paranasal Sinuses and Mastoid Air Cells: Mild mucosal thickening in the ethmoid air cells. Mastoid air cells are clear.

Orbits: Unremarkable.

Impression:

1. Focal left lateral temporal lobe encephalomalacia and gliosis, representing a potential structural epileptogenic substrate.
2. Normal appearance of the mesial temporal structures without evidence of mesial temporal sclerosis. No malformation of cortical development identified.
3. No acute intracranial abnormality, mass effect, or hydrocephalus.
