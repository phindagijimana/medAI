Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 14 and currently occurring approximately seven times per week. The reported epilepsy etiology is genetic. Current antiseizure medications are topiramate and oxcarbazepine. EEG obtained to evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single electrocardiographic channel. The study was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a well-developed anterior-to-posterior gradient. A 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected admixture of lower-amplitude anterior beta and theta activity was present. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, the background remained symmetric and reactive. No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, slow roving eye movements, and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform abnormalities were activated by sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of the procedure.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation was performed across a range of flash frequencies and produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal, multifocal, or generalized interictal epileptiform discharges were seen. Specifically, there were no definite spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, or persistent focal delta abnormalities.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures were recorded. No clinical events or habitual focal impaired-awareness episodes were captured during this study.

Impression:

1. Normal awake, drowsy, and sleep routine EEG. There are no focal abnormalities, epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures during this recording.
2. The absence of interictal epileptiform activity on a single routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy. Clinical correlation is advised, particularly given the reported history of drug-resistant focal impaired-awareness seizures occurring approximately seven times per week despite current therapy with topiramate and oxcarbazepine. Prolonged video EEG monitoring may be considered if electroclinical characterization of the habitual events is needed.
