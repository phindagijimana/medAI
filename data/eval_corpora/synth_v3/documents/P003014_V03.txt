Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Evaluation of a patient with generalized epilepsy characterized by myoclonic seizures, with onset at age 28 and a reported frequency of approximately two seizures per month. The epilepsy is clinically drug responsive on oxcarbazepine. The reported etiology is structural, with hippocampal sclerosis on MRI. Assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. Bipolar and referential montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief sleep was achieved. Video was recorded throughout the study.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity, which was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing.

Awake state:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No generalized spike-wave or polyspike-wave discharges were identified. No focal sharp waves, spikes, LPDs, GPDs, or rhythmic delta activity were present.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was associated with attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Brief stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. There were no epileptiform discharges activated by drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without epileptiform activation. No absence seizure pattern was elicited.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation was performed across standard flash frequencies and produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No focal, multifocal, or generalized interictal epileptiform discharges were observed. In particular, there were no generalized spike-wave or polyspike-wave complexes to provide electrographic support for a generalized epilepsy syndrome during this recording. No temporal slowing or temporal epileptiform activity was identified despite the reported MRI finding of hippocampal sclerosis.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures were recorded. No clinical myoclonic jerks or other habitual events were captured.

ECG:  The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and briefly asleep states.  
2. No focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities and no electrographic seizures were identified.  
3. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude epilepsy. The patient’s clinical diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures should be correlated with seizure semiology, medication response, and neuroimaging findings, including the reported hippocampal sclerosis.
