Assessment / Impression

This patient has genetic generalized epilepsy associated with a causative STXBP1 c.902A>G variant. The primary reported seizure type is generalized tonic-clonic, with onset at 8 years of age. Seizures remain active at an estimated frequency of three per month despite current treatment with topiramate, levetiracetam, and zonisamide. The epilepsy is therefore characterized as drug resistant.

The immediate priority is to reconcile the exact doses and schedules of all three current ASMs and to obtain the prior treatment history. Without this information, I would not recommend an empiric medication change today. Direct review of prior EEG and MRI data is also important before determining whether additional medication trials, dietary therapy, or a neuromodulation evaluation would be appropriate.

Although the epilepsy is reported to be generalized, prolonged VEEG may be useful if prior electroclinical data cannot be obtained or if the current events have not been captured. This would allow confirmation of the active seizure type and current electrographic epilepsy pattern. Any procedural or device-based recommendation should follow adequate review of the diagnostic data and prior treatment course.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue topiramate, levetiracetam, and zonisamide without change pending medication reconciliation. The patient should provide medication bottles or an updated pharmacy list so that dose, formulation, and frequency can be confirmed.

2. Obtain a complete prior ASM history, including maximum tolerated doses, clinical response, adverse effects, and reasons each medication was stopped. This information is necessary to guide the next treatment decision in the setting of drug-resistant epilepsy.

3. Request the original EEG recordings and reports, including any prior ambulatory EEG, VEEG, LTM, or EMU evaluations. If adequate prior studies cannot be obtained, I recommend inpatient VEEG monitoring to document the current events and confirm the generalized electroclinical classification.

4. Request prior brain MRI images and reports. If no adequate epilepsy-protocol MRI has been completed, an MRI brain with an epilepsy protocol should be considered as part of the comprehensive drug-resistant epilepsy evaluation.

5. Obtain and review the original genetic testing report documenting the causative STXBP1 c.902A>G variant. Genetic counseling may be helpful for review of the result and its implications.

6. Once the medication history, EEG data, imaging, and genetic report have been assembled, we will review treatment options. These may include optimization of the current regimen, consideration of another appropriate ASM strategy, and discussion of nonpharmacologic therapies. No determination regarding a specific neuromodulation therapy can be made from the currently available information.

7. Standard seizure precautions were reviewed. The patient should avoid driving unless permitted under applicable NY law and cleared by the treating clinician. Additional precautions include avoiding unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Showers are preferred to baths.

8. The patient or family should seek emergency care for a convulsive seizure lasting 5 minutes or longer, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, or breathing difficulty.

9. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after outside records and medication details have been obtained. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if seizure frequency increases or medication-related concerns arise.
