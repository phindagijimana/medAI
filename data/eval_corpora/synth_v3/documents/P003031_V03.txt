Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. XXX  
Indication: Drug-resistant focal epilepsy of unknown etiology with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 5 years and currently occurring approximately three times per week. Current antiseizure medications are valproate and topiramate. EEG requested to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness and drowsiness. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9 Hz alpha activity over the bilateral posterior head regions. This rhythm was reactive to eye opening and eye closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present, with low-amplitude beta activity anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry. The background was continuous.

Awake State: Muscle and eye movement artifacts were intermittently present but did not significantly limit interpretation. No sustained focal slowing was identified.

Drowsiness/Sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric 4-7 Hz theta activity. Brief vertex transients were seen. Definitive sleep spindles and K complexes were not present; therefore, stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation. No epileptiform activity was activated.

Photic Stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: Rare, sharply contoured waveforms were noted independently over the temporal regions during drowsiness but lacked a consistent field or after-going slow wave and were not definitively epileptiform. No focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, periodic discharges, TIRDA, or other definite IEDs were identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, or SIRPIDs.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured during this recording.

Electrocardiogram: The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. This is a normal routine EEG during wakefulness and drowsiness. No definite focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities were identified.
2. No electrographic seizures were recorded.
3. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. Given the reported drug-resistant focal epilepsy with frequent focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, prolonged video EEG monitoring may be considered if further electroclinical characterization or localization is needed.
