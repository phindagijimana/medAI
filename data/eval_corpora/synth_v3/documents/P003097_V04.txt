Emergency Department Provider Note

Chief Complaint: Breakthrough seizure.

HPI:  
32-year-old man with epilepsy of unknown type, immune etiology, onset at age 14, presents via ambulance after a breakthrough convulsive seizure. Per EMS report from xxx EMS, family found the patient with bilateral tonic-clonic activity; onset was not witnessed, and focal onset could not be established. Convulsive activity lasted approximately 2 minutes and stopped spontaneously without rescue medication. He was confused and somnolent afterward, gradually improving during transport. No recurrent seizure or persistent status epilepticus reported.

Patient reports a recent frequency of approximately 6 seizures per week. Current ASMs are Keppra (levetiracetam) and Vimpat (lacosamide). He states he took his most recent doses today and denies intentionally missed doses, although exact dosing and adherence could not be independently confirmed. His epilepsy has previously been considered drug-responsive. He denies recent medication changes, fever, headache, neck stiffness, focal weakness, chest pain, dyspnea, vomiting, diarrhea, or substance use. No significant traumatic injury reported. On ED reassessment, he feels tired but otherwise back to neurologic baseline.

No indication for emergent EEG or admission given return to baseline, reassuring evaluation, and absence of recurrent seizure. Recommended IV antiseizure medication load, continuation of current levetiracetam and lacosamide, and expedited outpatient epilepsy follow-up.

Medical Decision-Making (MDM):  
Presentation favors breakthrough epileptic seizure with post-ictal state. Differential included convulsive syncope, TIA, metabolic disturbance, infection/encephalitis, medication nonadherence, intoxication/withdrawal, and psychogenic nonepileptic event. TIA is unlikely given tonic-clonic activity and no persistent focal deficit. No fever, meningismus, sustained AMS, or systemic toxicity to suggest acute CNS infection or recurrent immune encephalitis. Glucose and electrolytes are reassuring. CT is negative for acute structural pathology.

Patient was given Keppra (levetiracetam) 1,500 mg IV and observed without recurrence. No Ativan (lorazepam) was required because seizure activity had terminated prior to arrival. He returned fully to baseline, tolerated PO, ambulated steadily, and had stable repeat vitals. After discussion with neurology and the patient, outpatient management is appropriate.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizure.  
2. Epilepsy of unknown type, immune etiology, historically drug-responsive.  
3. Resolved post-ictal confusion.

Disposition:  
Discharge home in stable condition. Continue levetiracetam and lacosamide exactly as prescribed; no unsupervised dose changes. Expedited neurology/epilepsy clinic follow-up advised. Seizure precautions reviewed: no driving per applicable state law and until cleared, no swimming or bathing alone, heights, ladders, open flames, or operating heavy machinery. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, persistent confusion, fever, severe headache, new weakness, significant injury, or inability to take medications.
