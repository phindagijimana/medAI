Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with reported generalized epilepsy and typical absence seizures beginning at age 66. The current seizure frequency is approximately 3 per month. The epilepsy is considered drug responsive on oxcarbazepine, although the patient is not presently seizure-free based on the supplied frequency.

The proposed etiology is structural because MRI demonstrated a tumor. At present, there is insufficient information to determine whether the tumor is causally related to the seizures, incidental, or relevant to possible surgical management. The lack of lesion details and the absence of the actual images limit further assessment.

Typical absence seizure onset at age 66 is atypical and merits objective confirmation. The reported generalized classification should be compared with a detailed description of the spells and EEG findings. I would avoid making a definitive surgical recommendation until the event type, EEG pattern, medication regimen, and MRI anatomy have been clarified. The designation of drug responsive is reassuring, but ongoing events at a rate of 3 per month still warrant further evaluation and treatment optimization.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Trileptal (oxcarbazepine) for now without an empiric change based solely on the limited information available today. The patient and referring office should confirm the exact dose, formulation, and frequency.

2. Obtain the original MRI images and formal radiology report for review at xxx Medical Center. The lesion’s location, size, imaging characteristics, and relationship to potential seizure networks should be clarified.

3. Obtain a routine EEG to assess for generalized epileptiform discharges and to evaluate whether the electrophysiologic findings support the diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures.

4. If routine EEG and clinical history do not establish the diagnosis, consider prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient VEEG/LTM in the EMU to characterize the recurrent events.

5. Request prior neurology records, including any previous EEG studies, antiseizure medication trials, oxcarbazepine dosing history, and available laboratory monitoring.

6. Maintain a seizure calendar documenting the approximately 3 monthly events and any change in frequency after the medication regimen is verified. If possible, a witness description should be obtained for future review.

7. Standard seizure precautions were reviewed, including avoiding unsupervised activities in which impaired awareness could result in injury. Driving should follow applicable state requirements and the guidance of the treating neurologist.

8. Once the MRI has been independently reviewed, referral to neurosurgery and/or neuro-oncology may be appropriate depending on the tumor characteristics. Surgical candidacy cannot be determined from the current information alone.

9. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after the MRI materials, medication details, and EEG results are available. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if seizure frequency increases or if prolonged events occur.
