Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy manifested by focal aware seizures. Seizure onset was at age 64, and the current reported frequency is approximately two events per year. The etiology is unknown. Brain MRI has shown nonspecific white matter changes without a documented specific epileptogenic lesion.

The patient remains on dual ASM therapy with brivaracetam and oxcarbazepine. The supplied clinical characterization is drug-responsive epilepsy; there is no indication in the available information that criteria for drug-resistant epilepsy have been met. Accordingly, an epilepsy surgery evaluation is not presently indicated on the limited information available.

Further clarification of the event semiology, exact medication doses, prior medication response, and prior EEG findings would improve diagnostic and treatment planning. In particular, obtaining the original EEG reports and MRI images would help determine whether there is objective evidence supporting localization.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Briviact (brivaracetam) and Trileptal (oxcarbazepine) at the currently prescribed doses pending complete medication reconciliation. The patient should not stop or change either medication abruptly without medical guidance.

2. Confirm the exact dose, frequency, adherence, and tolerability of both ASMs. This is important before considering any medication adjustment.

3. Obtain and review prior neurology notes, EEG reports or tracings, and the original brain MRI images if available. If no prior EEG has been completed or if the prior study cannot be obtained, a routine EEG may be considered to further characterize the focal epilepsy.

This will help determine whether the reported frequency remains approximately two seizures per year and whether all events have the same focal aware semiology.

5. Continue standard seizure safety precautions. The patient should avoid situations in which an unexpected event could cause injury and should follow applicable NY driving requirements and the guidance of the treating neurologist.
