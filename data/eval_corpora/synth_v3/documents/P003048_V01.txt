Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, with onset at 7 years of age. The epilepsy is considered structural based on the reported MRI finding of focal cortical dysplasia and is classified as drug-resistant. The patient is currently taking four ASMs—carbamazepine, valproate, lamotrigine, and zonisamide.

Reassuringly, the patient reports no seizures during the current monthly interval. However, the available information does not establish how long this seizure-free period has lasted. The current seizure freedom also does not, by itself, resolve the established history of drug-resistant epilepsy. Before altering a four-drug regimen, we will need to verify the exact formulations, doses, adherence, tolerability, prior ASM trials, and duration of seizure control.

Given the reported focal cortical dysplasia, it is important to review the original epilepsy-protocol MRI and available EEG data. If seizures recur or if the current regimen is poorly tolerated, a formal presurgical evaluation may be appropriate. Such an evaluation would generally require capture and characterization of habitual events with VEEG/LTM and correlation of the electroclinical findings with the structural lesion. At present, there are insufficient source data to determine candidacy for resection, ablation, VNS, RNS, DBS, or another advanced therapy.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current antiseizure medication regimen—carbamazepine, valproate, lamotrigine, and zonisamide—without changes pending complete medication reconciliation. Exact doses and frequencies should be confirmed.

2. Request the original brain MRI images and report for review at xxx Medical Center. If the prior study was not performed using an epilepsy protocol or is not adequate for lesion characterization, an updated epilepsy-protocol MRI may be considered.

3. Obtain prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, and any EMU/LTM reports and tracings. These records will be needed to assess whether the electrographic data are concordant with the reported focal cortical dysplasia.

4. Clarify the longitudinal seizure history, including the duration of current seizure freedom, historical seizure frequency, and prior ASM trials. This information is necessary to place the documented drug-resistant status in clinical context.

6. No medication taper is recommended at this initial consultation. The patient is presently seizure-free by the available monthly report, and the risks of changing a four-drug regimen cannot be assessed without exact dosing and prior treatment information.

7. Follow up with xxx Medicine Neurology after the requested imaging, EEG records, and medication details have been obtained. Earlier reassessment would be appropriate if seizures recur.
