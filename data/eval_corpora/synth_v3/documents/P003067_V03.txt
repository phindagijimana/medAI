Indication: Focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures; evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity. Seizures began at age 27 and currently occur approximately twice per year. Etiology is unknown. The epilepsy has been clinically drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are oxcarbazepine and valproate.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was recorded using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system. Additional electrocardiographic monitoring was performed. The study included referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures consisted of hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness and drowsiness. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Technical quality was adequate for interpretation.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm of medium amplitude that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal or generalized background slowing.

Awake state: The waking background was continuous and symmetric. No asymmetry of voltage or frequency was identified. No breach rhythm was present.

Drowsiness and sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse theta activity, and slow rolling eye movements. Stage N2 sleep was identified by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was appropriately organized.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline following completion of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at sequential flash frequencies elicited a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs were seen. There was no focal slowing.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured during this recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.  
2. No focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities were identified, and no seizures were recorded.

A normal routine EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. The patient’s history remains consistent with drug-responsive focal epilepsy manifesting as focal aware seizures, with onset at age 27 and a current reported frequency of approximately two seizures per year. Clinical correlation is recommended.
