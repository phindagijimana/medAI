Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with reported generalized epilepsy and typical absence seizures, with onset at age 35. The proposed etiology is immune-mediated and specifically associated with CASPR2 antibody. The epilepsy has been characterized as drug-resistant, but the patient is currently seizure-free, reporting 0 seizures per month, on substantial polytherapy with topiramate, valproate, lacosamide, and brivaracetam.

The current seizure freedom is encouraging. At the same time, the adult onset, immune attribution, generalized classification, and historical drug-resistant designation warrant careful confirmation through review of the original EEG, imaging, antibody testing, and treatment records. Drug-resistant epilepsy may remain the appropriate historical designation even during a period of seizure control on multiple ASMs, but the basis for that designation cannot be independently assessed from the records presently available.

I would not recommend empiric withdrawal of any ASM until the exact doses, adherence, adverse effects, prior medication failures, duration of seizure freedom, and electrodiagnostic data have been clarified. There is not enough information today to determine whether medication simplification would be safe or whether additional epilepsy monitoring is needed.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Topamax (topiramate), Depakote (valproate), Vimpat (lacosamide), and Briviact (brivaracetam) at the currently prescribed doses for now. The patient should not stop or reduce any ASM abruptly.

2. Complete medication reconciliation, including formulation, dose, frequency, adherence, tolerability, and prescribing pharmacy for each current medication.

3. Request prior neurology and hospital records, including all routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG/LTM or EMU reports and, when available, representative EEG tracings. These records will be important to confirm the generalized epilepsy and typical absence classification.

4. Obtain prior brain MRI reports and images for review. If prior imaging cannot be obtained, the need for updated imaging can be determined after the diagnostic history is clarified.

5. Request the original CASPR2 antibody results, including whether testing was performed in serum, CSF, or both. Any prior immune evaluation or treatment records should also be obtained. Neuroimmunology input may be considered after review of those materials.

6. Request a complete prior ASM history documenting adequate medication trials and the evidence supporting the diagnosis of drug-resistant epilepsy.

7. Maintain a seizure diary and report any recurrence of behavioral arrest, impaired awareness, unexplained lapse in time, or other suspected seizure activity. If events recur or the classification remains uncertain, inpatient EMU evaluation with VEEG should be considered.

8. Driving and other safety-sensitive activities should be guided by the documented seizure-free interval and applicable NY requirements. The patient should not drive if seizures recur and should promptly notify the treating neurology team.

9. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after the requested records and complete medication information have been received. Medication reduction or other changes can then be considered based on the confirmed diagnosis and risk of recurrence.
