Assessment / Impression

This is an XX-year-old patient with focal epilepsy beginning at age 14, manifested by focal impaired-awareness seizures. The current seizure burden is approximately seven events per week despite ongoing treatment with topiramate and oxcarbazepine. The condition has been classified as drug-resistant epilepsy and attributed to a genetic etiology.

The first priority is to confirm the nature and localization of the habitual events. Inpatient VEEG/LTM in the EMU would be appropriate if prior diagnostic monitoring has not already captured representative seizures. Because the patient has frequent events, the likelihood of capturing the habitual spell during monitoring may be favorable, although medication adjustment and monitoring strategy will be determined by the EMU team.

The patient also warrants a complete review of prior ASM trials and dose adequacy. I would not make an empiric medication change without confirming the current topiramate and oxcarbazepine doses, tolerability, adherence, and prior treatment history. If drug resistance is confirmed after adequate medication trials, a presurgical epilepsy evaluation should be considered. This does not imply that resective surgery is necessarily appropriate; candidacy depends on concordance among semiology, VEEG, epilepsy-protocol MRI, neuropsychological testing, and other localization studies. Neuromodulation with VNS, RNS, or DBS may also be considered if appropriate after completion of the evaluation.
