Assessment / Impression

This patient has focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 38. The reported seizure burden is approximately four per month. The etiology remains unknown based on the information currently available.

The patient is taking two ASMs, lamotrigine and valproate, and the condition has been categorized as drug-responsive. However, the ongoing event frequency warrants clarification. We need to confirm that the reported episodes are epileptic seizures, establish the current medication doses and adherence, review treatment response over time, and determine whether the frequency reflects a stable baseline or a change in control.

At present, the documentation is insufficient to classify the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy or to recommend a presurgical evaluation. Such a determination requires confirmation of the diagnosis and review of prior adequate ASM trials. Likewise, no localization hypothesis can be offered without seizure descriptions, EEG data, and epilepsy-protocol imaging.

Recommendations / Plan

1. The patient will continue Lamictal (lamotrigine) and Depakote (valproate) at the currently prescribed doses pending confirmation of the medication list. I did not recommend an empiric dose change without verified dosing, adherence, laboratory data, and prior treatment history.

2. We will obtain a complete medication reconciliation, including tablet strengths, dosing frequency, duration of treatment, adherence, perceived benefit, and adverse effects. The patient should also provide a list of any previously used ASMs and the reason each was discontinued.

3. We will request prior neurology notes, EEG or VEEG/LTM reports, brain MRI reports and images, and relevant laboratory results from the referring office and any prior treating institutions.

4. The patient should maintain a seizure diary documenting event frequency. If possible, future descriptions should include warning symptoms, responsiveness, observed behaviors, duration, recovery, and any associated progression.

5. After records are obtained, we will determine whether additional diagnostic testing is needed. Depending on the available prior workup, this may include routine EEG, prolonged ambulatory EEG, inpatient EMU admission for VEEG, or an epilepsy-protocol brain MRI.

6. Medication monitoring should be individualized after the exact lamotrigine and valproate regimens are verified. Relevant laboratory testing and ASM levels may be considered if clinically indicated based on dosing and prior results.

7. Standard seizure safety counseling should be reinforced, including avoidance of unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Driving should follow applicable NY law and clinician guidance based on ongoing seizure status.

8. Follow-up will be arranged after the outside records and medication details have been obtained. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if the events increase in frequency or severity.
