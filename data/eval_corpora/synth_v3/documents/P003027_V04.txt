Reason for Admission:  
This is a 68-year-old man with drug-resistant focal epilepsy and MRI evidence of hippocampal sclerosis who presents for elective long-term video EEG monitoring. The goal is to capture habitual seizures, characterize electroclinical onset, and obtain localization data as part of a presurgical epilepsy evaluation.

History of Present Illness:  
The patient developed seizures at age 66. Available history supports focal aware seizures. Detailed symptom chronology is limited in the referral documentation, and the precise aura, duration, lateralizing features, and degree of retained responsiveness will be clarified during this admission. His current reported seizure burden is approximately two events per year. The available record does not establish habitual progression to focal impaired awareness seizures, automatisms with post-ictal dysphasia, or focal to bilateral tonic-clonic activity. These features will be specifically assessed with the patient and observers and during any captured events.

Despite the relatively low reported frequency, seizures have persisted while taking three antiseizure medications, meeting clinical criteria for drug-resistant epilepsy. His current regimen consists of Lamictal (lamotrigine), Keppra (levetiracetam), and Trileptal (oxcarbazepine). Exact outpatient doses and administration times are being confirmed through medication reconciliation on arrival. Documentation supplied for this admission does not provide a complete list of prior ASM trials, maximum tolerated doses, reasons for discontinuation, or medication adverse effects. There is no documented prior epilepsy surgery or implanted neuromodulation device in the available record.

Brain MRI demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis, supporting a structural etiology and raising concern for mesial temporal lobe epilepsy. The side of the MRI abnormality is not specified in the material available at admission. A prior EEG report was reviewed in the academic epilepsy setting on 11/22/2025, but the actual EEG findings, including the presence or laterality of IEDs, TIRDA, or electrographic seizures, are not included in the current referral packet. No PET, ictal SPECT, MEG, neuropsychological testing, or prior LTM results are available for review today.

He is admitted for a planned five-day EMU evaluation. Given the infrequency of habitual events, antiseizure medications will be tapered in a stepwise fashion while maintaining continuous VEEG and standard seizure precautions. The principal presurgical questions are whether seizures are epileptic, whether there is a consistent unilateral temporal electroclinical onset concordant with the structural lesion, and whether additional noninvasive or invasive evaluation would be required before consideration of resection, laser ablation, RNS, or other therapy. Results will be considered for multidisciplinary patient review conference discussion after completion of the presurgical data set.
