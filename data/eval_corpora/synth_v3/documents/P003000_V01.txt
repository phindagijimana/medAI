Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy beginning at age 33. The habitual events are classified as focal impaired-awareness seizures and currently occur approximately twice per year. The epilepsy is considered drug-resistant, with ongoing treatment using topiramate and brivaracetam.

The reported focal cortical dysplasia, Palmini type IIa, provides a structural etiology and raises the possibility of a surgically remediable focal epilepsy. However, an MRI lesion alone does not establish that the dysplasia is the seizure onset zone. Presurgical evaluation should determine whether the clinical semiology, scalp EEG findings, and imaging are concordant and whether the suspected epileptogenic region can be treated without unacceptable functional risk.

At present, the available information is insufficient to recommend a specific procedure. Depending on the results of additional testing, options could include lesion-directed resection or ablation. If localization remains uncertain, phase II intracranial EEG evaluation may be considered. If resection or ablation is not appropriate, neuromodulation with VNS, RNS, or DBS may be discussed.
