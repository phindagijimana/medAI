Assessment and Plan:

1. Focal epilepsy, lateralization not established, drug-resistant  
   The patient has focal epilepsy of unknown etiology with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 26. He continues to have approximately five seizures per month while taking lacosamide and carbamazepine, consistent with drug-resistant epilepsy.

   - Continue lacosamide and carbamazepine at the currently prescribed doses pending confirmation of the complete medication reconciliation. He was advised not to stop or reduce either medication abruptly.
   - Obtain and review the exact formulations, doses, administration schedule, adherence, and prior antiseizure medication trials before making a medication adjustment.
   - Continue a seizure diary documenting date, time, duration, associated symptoms, potential triggers, missed doses, and postictal recovery.
   - Obtain medication-monitoring studies appropriate for carbamazepine therapy, including CBC and comprehensive metabolic panel with serum sodium. A trough carbamazepine level may be obtained to assess exposure and guide subsequent treatment decisions.
   - Given continued seizures on two current antiseizure medications, further evaluation for nonpharmacologic treatment options is appropriate. Prior EEG, brain MRI, and antiseizure medication records should be reviewed. If not already completed, consider updated epilepsy-protocol MRI and inpatient video-EEG monitoring for seizure classification and localization as part of a comprehensive epilepsy surgery evaluation.
   - Discussed that drug-resistant epilepsy may be amenable to resective surgery, ablation, or neuromodulation depending on localization and results of presurgical testing.
   - Reviewed seizure precautions, including avoiding unprotected heights, open flames, operating dangerous machinery, bathing alone, and swimming without direct supervision. Showering is preferred to bathing.
   - Driving restrictions were reviewed. He should not drive unless he meets applicable state seizure-free requirements and has been medically cleared.
   - He should seek urgent evaluation for a prolonged seizure, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, respiratory compromise, or a seizure pattern that is clearly different from baseline.
