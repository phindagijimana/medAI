Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 57, with a reported frequency of approximately one seizure per month. Etiology is unknown. The epilepsy is considered drug-responsive, and the current antiseizure medication is carbamazepine.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness and drowsiness. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded throughout the study.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing.

Awake state: The waking background was continuous and composed predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with admixed theta activity. Reactivity and variability were preserved.

Drowsiness/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the posterior dominant rhythm, slow roving eye movements, and an increase in diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was marked by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Sleep architecture was appropriately organized and symmetric.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline following completion of the procedure.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation from 1 through 30 Hz produced symmetric photic driving at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal, lateralized, or generalized epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no generalized spike-and-wave or polyspike-and-wave complexes. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns were present.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No push-button events occurred.

Electrocardiogram: The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm without an obvious sustained arrhythmia.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.  
3. A normal routine EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. In this patient with a history of generalized tonic-clonic seizures and clinically diagnosed generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology, correlation with the seizure semiology, medication response, and prior EEG studies is recommended.
