Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology, characterized by generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 26. The patient currently has approximately two seizures per week despite ongoing therapy with perampanel, levetiracetam, and carbamazepine. The epilepsy is drug resistant.

The immediate priorities are to confirm the current medication doses and adherence, obtain the prior diagnostic studies, and verify the electroclinical classification. This is particularly important before making substantive medication changes. Available records do not provide the prior EEG evidence, imaging findings, or a complete history of antiseizure medication trials.

Given the frequency of generalized tonic-clonic seizures, improved seizure control remains an important treatment goal. Further diagnostic characterization in an epilepsy program is appropriate because the seizures remain frequent on the present three-drug regimen. At this time, the supplied information is not sufficient to determine whether any procedural or device-based therapy should be considered. The next step is completion of the epilepsy evaluation and review of the underlying data.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current antiseizure medications—Fycompa, Keppra, and Tegretol—without an empiric change until the exact doses, frequencies, tolerability, and prior treatment history have been verified.

2. Complete formal medication reconciliation using the patient’s medication bottles, pharmacy dispensing history, and records from the referring neurology practice.

3. Obtain prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG/LTM, or EMU reports and, if available, the original EEG data for review. Confirmation of the generalized epilepsy classification will guide subsequent medication decisions.

4. Obtain prior brain MRI reports and images. If no adequate prior study exists, brain MRI using an epilepsy protocol should be considered as part of the evaluation of epilepsy of unknown etiology.

5. Consider inpatient EMU admission for VEEG/LTM if prior studies do not adequately confirm the seizure type or if additional event characterization is required. The goal would be electroclinical confirmation rather than presuming features that are not contained in the current record.

6. The patient should maintain a seizure diary documenting the date and number of generalized tonic-clonic seizures. This will help establish a reliable baseline and assess response to future treatment changes.

7. Standard seizure precautions were advised, including avoidance of driving unless permitted under applicable NY law, unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. 8. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after outside EEG, imaging, medication, and prior treatment records have been obtained. Medication optimization and the need for additional drug-resistant epilepsy evaluation can then be addressed with a more complete dataset.
