Indication:  Evaluation of a patient with genetic generalized epilepsy characterized by typical absence seizures, with seizure onset at age 33. The epilepsy has historically been drug-resistant and is attributed to an STXBP1 c.902A>G variant. The patient is currently seizure-free, with 0 seizures per month, while taking valproate and zonisamide.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, supplemented by a single-lead electrocardiogram. The record was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded throughout the study. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was observed anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  During wakefulness, occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present. These consisted of frontally predominant, bilaterally synchronous 3-4 Hz complexes, maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, occurring singly and in brief bursts lasting up to 2 seconds. No definite clinical change was apparent on video during these discharges.

Drowsy / sleep:  With drowsiness, the posterior dominant rhythm attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized epileptiform discharges were mildly activated by drowsiness and sleep, remaining frontally predominant and without a consistent focal lead-in. No focal or lateralized epileptiform discharges were identified.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced expected diffuse theta and delta slowing. A brief increase in generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave activity was observed, with bursts lasting up to 3 seconds. There was no clear behavioral arrest or other definite clinical correlate.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, enhanced during drowsiness, sleep, and hyperventilation. The morphology and distribution were consistent with a generalized epileptogenic potential.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or definite clinical absence seizures were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with frontal predominance and activation during drowsiness, sleep, and hyperventilation.

2. These findings support the diagnosis of generalized epilepsy and are compatible with the reported history of typical absence seizures. No focal epileptiform abnormality was identified.

3. No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were recorded during this study. The absence of a captured seizure does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. Clinical correlation is recommended, including consideration of the patient’s genetically established STXBP1 c.902A>G etiology, historical drug resistance, and current seizure freedom on valproate and zonisamide.
