Indication:  Evaluation of generalized epilepsy characterized by generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset was at age 29, with a historical frequency of approximately one seizure per month. The epilepsy is considered metabolic in etiology and is currently drug responsive on valproate.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved. Video was recorded throughout the study.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with an anterior-to-posterior gradient of voltage and frequency. A well-sustained 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected pattern of lower-voltage, predominantly beta-frequency activity was present over the anterior head regions. There was no persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  During wakefulness, occasional generalized bursts of high-amplitude 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity were present. These discharges were frontally predominant, maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, with a symmetric and synchronous field. Individual bursts lasted approximately 0.5-2 seconds and were not accompanied by an apparent clinical change on video. No consistent focal lead-in or lateralized emphasis was identified.

Drowsy/sleep:  With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. Generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges became more frequent during drowsiness and sleep, at times occurring in brief fragments. Sleep architecture was otherwise preserved.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced expected diffuse theta slowing. There was a modest increase in the frequency of generalized epileptiform discharges, without a sustained electrographic seizure or clinical correlate.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings:  Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness and sleep. No focal epileptiform discharges, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing were observed.

Ictal events:  No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No habitual generalized tonic-clonic event occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with activation during drowsiness and sleep. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized seizures and are consistent with the patient’s clinically established generalized epilepsy.

2. No focal epileptiform abnormality or electrographic seizure was recorded. A normal and symmetric background is preserved.

Clinical correlation is advised in the context of generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 29, historically occurring approximately monthly, with suspected metabolic etiology and current drug-responsive control on valproate.
