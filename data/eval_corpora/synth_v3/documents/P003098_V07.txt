Assessment / Impression

The patient has a reported generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures beginning at age 63. Current seizure frequency is approximately two per year. The epilepsy has been characterized as drug responsive on dual therapy with carbamazepine and levetiracetam, although the exact doses and frequencies remain to be confirmed. The etiology is unknown.

The reported age at onset is atypical for a classic genetic generalized epilepsy presenting with typical absence seizures. For that reason, I would like to confirm the event description and review the prior EEG data before making a more specific syndrome diagnosis. If the prior studies are unavailable or nondiagnostic, updated EEG evaluation would be reasonable.

Carbamazepine may aggravate absence seizures in some patients with generalized epilepsy. However, the patient is currently described as drug responsive, and I would not make an abrupt medication change without confirming the electroclinical diagnosis, exact medication doses, tolerability, and prior treatment history. Abrupt ASM withdrawal should be avoided.

There is no current indication for epilepsy surgery evaluation based on the information provided. The epilepsy is described as drug responsive, and the reported seizure type is generalized rather than focal. Further diagnostic clarification should precede any substantial alteration in therapy.

Recommendations / Plan

2. Obtain the prior neurology records, including original EEG reports and tracings when available. Any prior ambulatory EEG or EMU/LTM data would be particularly helpful in confirming generalized epileptiform activity.
