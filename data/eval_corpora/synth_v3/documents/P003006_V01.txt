Assessment / Impression

The patient has focal epilepsy manifested by focal impaired-awareness seizures, with onset at approximately 10 years of age. The epilepsy is attributed to an infectious etiology. Current seizure frequency is approximately two per month.

The epilepsy has been characterized as drug responsive, and the patient remains on perampanel. The available information is not sufficient to determine the precise localization of seizure onset, the nature of the underlying infectious process, or the extent of prior diagnostic testing. Likewise, the current perampanel dose, adherence pattern, tolerability, and prior ASM history have not yet been confirmed.

Although the reported seizure frequency indicates ongoing events, there is insufficient information to classify the epilepsy as drug resistant. In particular, the record does not establish failure of two appropriately selected, adequately dosed, and tolerated ASM regimens. At present, the priority is to confirm the medication regimen, obtain prior EEG and imaging data, and better characterize the events and treatment response.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Fycompa (perampanel) at the currently prescribed regimen pending confirmation of the exact dose and frequency. I did not recommend an empiric dose change without a complete medication reconciliation and tolerability history.

2. The patient should provide an updated medication list or medication bottles so that the perampanel dose, schedule, adherence, and prescribing pharmacy information can be verified.

3. Maintain a seizure diary documenting the date and approximate time of each event, impaired awareness, duration, recovery, possible triggers, and any missed medication doses. This will help determine whether the reported frequency of two seizures per month remains stable or changes over time.

4. We will request prior neurology records, including previous ASM trials, EEG reports or tracings, brain MRI reports and images, and documentation regarding the infectious etiology.

5. If adequate prior EEG data cannot be obtained, an updated EEG may be considered to help characterize and localize the focal epilepsy. The need for prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient VEEG/LTM can be reassessed after the event history and prior studies are reviewed.

6. If no adequate epilepsy-protocol MRI has previously been completed, updated brain imaging may be considered, particularly to clarify whether there is a structural sequela related to the reported infectious etiology.

7. We reviewed general seizure precautions. The patient should avoid unsupervised swimming, bathing in a tub, climbing at heights, and operating dangerous machinery when impaired awareness could result in injury. Showers are preferred to baths. Driving should follow applicable NY law and individualized guidance based on ongoing seizures with impaired awareness.

8. The patient should seek emergency care for a prolonged seizure, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, breathing difficulty, or a seizure pattern that is substantially different from baseline.

9. Follow-up will be arranged after medication reconciliation and receipt of the outside diagnostic records. Earlier reassessment is appropriate if seizure frequency increases or medication adverse effects emerge.
