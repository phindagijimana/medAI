Assessment / Impression

The patient has a reported diagnosis of generalized epilepsy manifested by typical absence seizures. Seizure onset was at age 63, and the current reported frequency is approximately two seizures per year. The underlying etiology is unknown.

The epilepsy is characterized as drug responsive, and the patient remains on dual therapy with carbamazepine and levetiracetam. There is not enough information to determine the degree of response attributable to each medication or whether the current events have been confirmed electrographically. The precise medication doses also need to be reconciled before any treatment adjustment is considered.

The reported onset of typical absence seizures at age 63 warrants careful confirmation of the electroclinical diagnosis. Carbamazepine may be poorly suited to some generalized epilepsy syndromes; however, it should not be discontinued abruptly. Any medication change should follow review of the prior EEG data, confirmation of the event type, and reconciliation of the current dosing regimen.

Based on the supplied characterization as drug-responsive generalized epilepsy, there is no current indication for resective epilepsy surgery or an invasive device evaluation. Additional diagnostic clarification would be appropriate before considering any substantial change in management.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current carbamazepine and levetiracetam regimen without abrupt changes until the exact doses, frequencies, adherence, tolerability, and prior response are confirmed.

2. Obtain and review prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, EMU/LTM, and MRI records, if these studies have previously been completed. Actual EEG tracings would be preferable when available.

3. If the generalized epilepsy and typical absence classification has not been electrographically confirmed, obtain an EEG to assess for generalized IEDs and to evaluate for a possible focal mimic.

4. Reconcile the current ASM regimen. Once the diagnosis and dosing history are verified, the appropriateness of continued carbamazepine can be reassessed in the context of generalized epilepsy.

5. Maintain a seizure diary documenting the date, approximate duration, witness description, potential triggers, and recovery from each event. This will help clarify whether the frequency remains approximately two per year and whether all events have a consistent absence semiology.

6. Follow standard seizure precautions. The patient should avoid driving when having ongoing events that impair awareness and should follow applicable NY driving requirements. Additional precautions include avoiding unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery.

7. No epilepsy surgery evaluation is recommended at this time because the epilepsy is reported to be drug responsive and generalized.

8. Follow up with xxx Medicine Neurology after outside records and the complete medication list have been obtained. Earlier reassessment would be appropriate for increased seizure frequency, a new seizure type, prolonged events, or medication intolerance.
