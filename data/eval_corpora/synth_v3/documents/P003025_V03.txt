Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Evaluation of interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with generalized epilepsy characterized by myoclonic seizures beginning at age 28, reported at approximately seven seizures per week. The epilepsy is considered structural and drug-responsive. Current antiseizure therapy is valproate. Prior MRI reportedly demonstrates hippocampal sclerosis.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system, with additional electrocardiographic monitoring. The record was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity at 30-50 microvolts, reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present, with low-amplitude beta activity over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent hemispheric asymmetry or focal attenuation.

Awake state:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No focal delta or theta slowing was identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns.

Drowsy/sleep:  With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and was replaced by diffuse mixed-frequency theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was achieved, with symmetric vertex sharp transients, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform abnormalities were activated by sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation was performed across standard flash frequencies and elicited a symmetric posterior driving response. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No generalized spike-and-wave, polyspike-and-wave, focal sharp waves, spikes, TIRDA, or other definite interictal epileptiform discharges were present. In particular, no temporal epileptiform abnormality or focal temporal slowing was identified during this recording.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No myoclonic jerks were observed during the study.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.

2. There is no electrographic evidence of a generalized epileptiform trait in this recording. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy, including generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures.

3. The reported hippocampal sclerosis is not accompanied by focal temporal slowing or temporal IEDs on this study. Clinical and imaging correlation is recommended, particularly given the history of generalized myoclonic seizures and a structural MRI abnormality.
