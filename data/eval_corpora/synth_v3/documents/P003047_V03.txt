Indication:  Evaluation of drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 28, with a current reported frequency of approximately six seizures per week despite treatment with zonisamide and valproate. The epilepsy is considered structural in etiology in the setting of encephalomalacia on brain MRI. Assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, supplemented by a single-lead electrocardiogram. Standard bipolar and referential montages were reviewed. Hyperventilation was performed for 3 minutes, and intermittent photic stimulation was delivered at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity, which was reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent hemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No sustained focal slowing was identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns.

Drowsiness / sleep:  With drowsiness, there was expected attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric 4-7 Hz theta activity with slow roving eye movements. Brief vertex transients were seen, but definite sleep spindles and K complexes were not present. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of hyperventilation.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No definite focal or generalized interictal epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, no spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, or focal rhythmic delta activity were seen.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures were recorded. No clinical events or habitual focal impaired-awareness episodes were captured during this study.

Electrocardiogram:  Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake and drowsy states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. No habitual clinical events were captured. A normal routine EEG does not exclude focal epilepsy, particularly in a patient with clinically established drug-resistant focal impaired-awareness seizures and a structural MRI abnormality. If further electroclinical characterization or localization is required, prolonged video EEG monitoring may provide greater diagnostic yield.
