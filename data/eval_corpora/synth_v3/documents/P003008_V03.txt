Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx

Indication:  Focal epilepsy with focal aware seizures beginning at age 30, currently occurring approximately 3 times per week. The epilepsy is considered structural in etiology in the setting of an MRI-demonstrated vascular malformation. Seizures have been responsive to antiseizure medication; current therapy is perampanel. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 42-minute digital EEG was performed using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system, with additional electrocardiographic monitoring. The study was reviewed using referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness, drowsiness, and sleep.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz alpha rhythm of medium amplitude that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal asymmetry.

Awake state:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately reactive. No sustained focal delta or theta slowing was identified. No LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns were present.

Drowsy / sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR, increased diffuse theta activity, and slow rolling eye movements. Stage N2 sleep was achieved, with symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. There was no activation of epileptiform discharges during sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline following completion of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation was performed across a range of flash frequencies and produced symmetric photic driving, maximal over the posterior head regions. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, or other definite interictal epileptiform abnormalities were seen. No focal slowing was identified.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. There were no patient- or technologist-initiated event markers.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and stage N2 sleep.  
2. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified during this recording.  
3. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. Clinical correlation with the reported focal aware seizures and the structural lesion demonstrated on MRI is recommended.
