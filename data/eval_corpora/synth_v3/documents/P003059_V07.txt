Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with focal epilepsy manifested by focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, with onset at age 24. The current reported seizure frequency is approximately three per month. Etiology remains unknown.

The patient is taking oxcarbazepine and zonisamide, and the epilepsy has been characterized as drug responsive. Based on the limited information provided, I would not label the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy at this time. A complete medication history—including current doses, adherence, tolerability, prior dose adjustments, and any previously attempted ASMs—is necessary before determining whether the current regimen is optimized.

The reported MRI lesion is considered incidental based on the available records. Without the original study or radiology report, its significance cannot be assessed and it should not be presumed to represent the cause of the epilepsy. Correlation with seizure semiology, EEG data, and direct review of the imaging will be important.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Trileptal (oxcarbazepine) and Zonegran (zonisamide) for now. The patient should not change or abruptly discontinue either medication without medical supervision.

2. Confirm the exact dose, formulation, and frequency of both current ASMs through formal medication reconciliation. We will also request a complete history of prior medication trials, if any, including maximum doses, response, and adverse effects.

3. Obtain the prior MRI brain report and images for direct review. The incidental lesion should be characterized by location and imaging appearance before any conclusion is made regarding its relationship to the epilepsy.

4. Obtain available prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, or EMU records. If no adequate EEG study has been completed, an updated EEG evaluation may be considered to help classify and localize the epilepsy.

5. Maintain a seizure diary documenting the date and approximate time of each event. This will help verify the reported frequency of approximately three seizures per month and assess response to the current regimen.

6. We reviewed general seizure safety principles. Until seizures are reliably controlled, the patient should avoid driving and follow applicable NY driving requirements. The patient should also avoid unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery.

7. Any seizure lasting five minutes or longer, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, or breathing difficulty warrants emergency evaluation.

8. Follow-up should occur after the medication doses and prior MRI and EEG records have been obtained. If seizures remain active despite appropriately selected and tolerated medication trials, further diagnostic evaluation, including possible prolonged VEEG or EMU admission, can be discussed. The currently supplied information does not establish drug-resistant epilepsy or an indication for epilepsy surgery evaluation.
