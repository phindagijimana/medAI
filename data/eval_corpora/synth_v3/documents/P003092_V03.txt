Indication: Generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures, onset at age 29, attributed clinically to an infectious etiology. Historical seizure frequency was approximately three per month. Epilepsy is considered drug responsive; current antiseizure medication is perampanel. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included synchronized video recording. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: In the awake state, the background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen predominantly over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry.

Drowsy/Sleep: Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep demonstrated symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without activation of additional epileptiform abnormalities.

Photic Stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced symmetric posterior photic driving. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: Occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present. These consisted of high-amplitude, bifrontally predominant 3.5-5 Hz spike/polyspike-and-wave bursts lasting approximately 0.5-2 seconds, with shifting hemispheric emphasis but no consistent focal lead-in. Discharges were more frequent during drowsiness and sleep. No clinical correlate was apparent on video. There were no focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or sustained rhythmic electrographic patterns.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

ECG: Single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, bifrontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, activated by drowsiness and sleep.

2. These findings indicate an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are supportive of the patient’s established diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

3. No electrographic or clinical seizures were recorded. The background organization was normal, without focal slowing.

Clinical correlation is advised. A normal seizure-free recording interval does not exclude epilepsy; continued management should be guided by the clinical history and treatment response to perampanel.
