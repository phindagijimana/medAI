Assessment / Impression

This is a patient with focal epilepsy beginning at age 79. The documented seizure type is focal impaired awareness, with a reported frequency of approximately five seizures per week. Etiology is currently unknown. The patient is receiving zonisamide, and the epilepsy has been characterized as drug responsive.

There are important limitations in the available history. Most notably, it is not clear whether five seizures per week reflects the current treated burden or the frequency before a response to zonisamide. Medication dose and frequency have not been confirmed, and no EEG or MRI data were available for review. Accordingly, I would not label this drug-resistant epilepsy on the basis of the current record. There is also insufficient information to recommend a presurgical evaluation at this time.

Given the late age at onset and unknown etiology, review of prior diagnostic testing is important. If adequate testing has not already been completed, EEG and brain MRI would be appropriate to support classification and assess for a potentially identifiable focal cause.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue zonisamide for now, without an empiric dose change based solely on the limited referral information. The patient should confirm the tablet strength, total dose, dosing frequency, adherence, and any adverse effects with our office or the referring clinician.

2. Clarify the seizure timeline, including whether the reported frequency of five per week occurred before or after initiation of zonisamide. A written seizure diary should be maintained, documenting date, approximate duration, impaired awareness, recovery, and any clustering.

3. Please forward any prior EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG/LTM, or EMU reports and, when possible, the original tracings. If no prior EEG has been performed, a routine EEG should be considered.

4. Please forward any prior brain MRI report and images for direct review. If no adequate study has been obtained, I would consider an epilepsy-protocol brain MRI to evaluate the currently unknown etiology.

5. Standard seizure safety precautions were reviewed in principle. The patient should avoid driving unless permitted under applicable NY requirements and cleared by the treating clinician. Additional precautions include avoiding unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operating dangerous machinery while seizures with impaired awareness remain possible.

6. At present, there is no indication to proceed directly to VNS, RNS, DBS, or resective epilepsy surgery evaluation because the available record characterizes the epilepsy as drug responsive. This can be reconsidered if seizures remain uncontrolled despite adequate trials of appropriately selected and tolerated ASMs.

7. Follow-up should occur after medication reconciliation and receipt of the prior EEG and MRI records, or sooner for increasing seizure frequency, prolonged events, injury, or a significant medication adverse effect.
