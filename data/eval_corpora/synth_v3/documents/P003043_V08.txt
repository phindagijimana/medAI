Assessment / Impression

The patient has focal epilepsy manifested by focal impaired-awareness seizures, with onset at age 13 and a reported current frequency of approximately two seizures per year. The epilepsy is described as drug responsive. A genetic etiology has been identified, specifically a reported causative KCNQ2 c.881C>T variant.

The current medication list includes both carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine. Because doses, frequencies, tolerability, and the rationale for concurrent therapy were not supplied, I would not recommend making a medication change until the regimen has been fully reconciled. The available data do not establish drug-resistant epilepsy, and a presurgical epilepsy evaluation is therefore not indicated on the basis of the information presently available.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current antiseizure medication regimen without change pending confirmation of the exact doses and frequencies:
   • Carbamazepine.
   • Oxcarbazepine.

2. Complete medication reconciliation, including formulation, dose, schedule, adherence, adverse effects, and prescribing clinician. We should also confirm whether both sodium-channel agents are intentionally being taken concurrently.

3. Obtain and review the original genetic testing report documenting the KCNQ2 c.881C>T variant, including the laboratory classification and any available genetics interpretation.

4. Request prior neurology records, including routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG/EMU or LTM reports, brain MRI reports and images, and prior antiseizure medication history, if these studies or records exist.

5. Maintain a seizure diary documenting the date and features of each event. Witness descriptions or video, when safely obtainable, may help clarify the habitual semiology.

6. Review standard seizure precautions, including avoidance of unsupervised swimming or bathing, heights, open flames, and operation of hazardous machinery. Driving should follow applicable NY regulations and individualized medical guidance.

7. No epilepsy surgery evaluation is recommended at this time because the epilepsy is reported to be drug responsive. This can be reconsidered if seizure control worsens or if future records demonstrate failure of appropriately selected and tolerated antiseizure medication regimens.

8. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after the medication list, prior EEG/MRI data, and genetic report have been obtained. The patient should contact the clinic sooner for an increase in seizure frequency or concerns related to the current medications.
