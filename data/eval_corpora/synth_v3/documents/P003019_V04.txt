Emergency Department Provider Note

Chief Complaint: Breakthrough seizure

Medical Decision-Making:  
Known focal epilepsy with a witnessed event matching his usual focal impaired-awareness semiology and a brief post-ictal period. He is now at neurologic baseline with stable serial vitals and no recurrent events during ED observation. No evidence of status epilepticus. Workup is reassuring against hypoglycemia, significant electrolyte abnormality, infection, intoxication, or acute organ dysfunction. No features suggesting meningitis, encephalitis, acute stroke, or intracranial hemorrhage.

Differential included epileptic seizure, syncope, TIA, and psychogenic non-epileptic event. Witnessed automatisms, impaired awareness, post-event confusion, and established epilepsy favor breakthrough seizure. Syncope is less likely without prodromal symptoms, pallor, loss of postural tone, or cardiac findings. TIA is unlikely without focal deficit. Patient received levetiracetam 1,000 mg IV and remained seizure-free. No lorazepam required.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough focal impaired-awareness seizure  
2. Drug-responsive focal epilepsy associated with GAD65 autoimmune disease

Disposition:  
Discharged home in stable condition after ED observation and neurology consultation. Continue levetiracetam exactly as prescribed; no medication change pending epilepsy clinic review and drug level. Follow up with neurology/epilepsy clinic within 1–2 weeks. Reviewed seizure precautions: no driving per applicable state law and until cleared by neurology, no swimming or bathing alone, climbing, open flames, or operating heavy machinery. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, persistent confusion, new weakness, severe headache, fever, injury, or other worsening symptoms.
