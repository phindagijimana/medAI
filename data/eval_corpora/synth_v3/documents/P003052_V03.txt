Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, reportedly beginning at age 9 and occurring approximately once monthly. Epilepsy is attributed to an infectious etiology. Current antiseizure medications are valproate, zonisamide, levetiracetam, and lacosamide. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. The recording was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and demonstrated appropriate anterior-to-posterior organization. There was a symmetric, well-formed 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An expected pattern of lower-voltage, predominantly beta-frequency activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry.

Awake State: During maximal wakefulness, the background consisted predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with admixed low-amplitude theta activity. No focal slowing was identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns.

Drowsiness/Sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric theta activity with slow lateral eye movements. Brief vertex transients were present. Definite stage N2 sleep architecture was not recorded.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focality or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of hyperventilation.

Photic Stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz elicited a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal Findings: No epileptiform discharges were seen. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, or generalized SWD. No focal delta slowing was present.

Ictal Events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the study.

Impression:

1. Normal awake and drowsy routine EEG. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.
2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. Given the history of drug-resistant focal impaired-awareness seizures occurring approximately monthly despite treatment with valproate, zonisamide, levetiracetam, and lacosamide, prolonged video EEG monitoring may be considered if further electroclinical characterization or presurgical evaluation is clinically indicated.
