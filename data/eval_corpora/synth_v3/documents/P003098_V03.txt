Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: [DEIDENTIFIED]  
Ordering Provider: [DEIDENTIFIED]  
Indication: Generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology with typical absence seizures beginning at age 63. Seizures are drug responsive and currently occur approximately twice per year. Current antiseizure medications are carbamazepine and levetiracetam.

Technique: A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. The study was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included 3 minutes of hyperventilation and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: The waking background demonstrated appropriate organization with a well-defined anterior-to-posterior gradient. A symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present over the occipital regions and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected admixture of lower-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, the background was continuous and symmetric. Occasional generalized spike-and-wave discharges were present, consisting of bifrontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-slow-wave complexes with maximal expression over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. These occurred as isolated complexes and brief bursts lasting 1-2 seconds. No definite clinical change was apparent on video during these discharges.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the posterior dominant rhythm and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Brief stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. Generalized spike-and-wave discharges were slightly more apparent during drowsiness but did not evolve into an electrographic seizure.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced the expected diffuse buildup of rhythmic delta and theta activity. A brief burst of generalized 3 Hz spike-and-wave activity occurred during hyperventilation without a clear behavioral correlate. No sustained electroclinical absence seizure was recorded.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation produced symmetric photic driving at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized, bifrontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave discharges were observed. No focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or focal slowing were identified.

Ictal events: No electrographic or electroclinical seizures were captured. No patient-reported events occurred during the recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, bifrontally predominant 3-3.5 Hz spike-and-wave discharges, including brief activation with hyperventilation. These findings indicate an increased potential for generalized-onset seizures and are compatible with the reported generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures.
2. No focal epileptiform abnormality or focal cerebral dysfunction was identified.
3. No electrographic or electroclinical seizure was recorded.

Clinical correlation is advised. The reported epilepsy is of unknown etiology, began at age 63, and remains drug responsive on carbamazepine and levetiracetam, with a current seizure frequency of approximately two per year.
