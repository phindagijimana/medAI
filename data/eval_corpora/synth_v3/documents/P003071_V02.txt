Emergency Department Provider Note

Chief Complaint: Breakthrough seizure.

HPI:  
11-year-old male with focal epilepsy with focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, onset at age 10, brought by ambulance per EMS report from xxx EMS after a witnessed breakthrough seizure at home. Parent reports initial behavioral arrest and rightward head/eye deviation followed by generalized tonic-clonic activity lasting approximately 2 minutes. No rescue medication was given. Event resolved spontaneously before EMS arrival. He was somnolent and confused for approximately 20 minutes afterward, then progressively returned to baseline. No recurrent seizure en route or in the ED. No reported fall, head strike, tongue injury, cyanosis, or prolonged apnea.

History is notable for drug-resistant epilepsy of unknown etiology. He had recently been seizure-free, averaging 0 seizures/month until today. Current ASMs are Depakote (valproate), Vimpat (lacosamide), and Keppra (levetiracetam). Parent reports adherence, including usual morning doses today, without recent missed or vomited doses. No recent medication changes. Denies fever, URI symptoms, headache, neck stiffness, vomiting, diarrhea, dysuria, poor intake, sleep deprivation, or known toxic ingestion. On ED evaluation, patient states he feels tired but otherwise well and is back to neurologic baseline per parent.

Medical Decision-Making (MDM):  
Presentation is most consistent with a breakthrough focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizure in a patient with known drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Seizure was brief, self-limited, and followed by a typical post-ictal period with complete return to baseline. No evidence of status epilepticus. Evaluation does not identify hypoglycemia, major electrolyte derangement, infection, intoxication, or traumatic intracranial injury as a clear provoking factor.

Differential considered included epileptic seizure, syncope, psychogenic nonepileptic event, TIA, CNS infection, and intracranial hemorrhage/mass. Witnessed semiology and post-ictal confusion favor epileptic seizure. Syncope and TIA are less likely given generalized convulsions with preceding focal features and no persistent deficit. No indication for lorazepam or IV levetiracetam in the ED because the seizure terminated prior to arrival and did not recur. After observation, patient remained hemodynamically stable, tolerated PO, ambulated normally, and remained at baseline.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizure.  
2. Drug-resistant focal epilepsy of unknown etiology.

Disposition:  
Discharge home with parent in stable condition after neurology consultation. Continue Depakote (valproate), Vimpat (lacosamide), and Keppra (levetiracetam) exactly as prescribed; no missed doses or abrupt discontinuation. Family instructed to contact epilepsy clinic for expedited follow-up within 1–2 weeks. Reviewed seizure precautions: no unsupervised bathing or swimming, heights, open flames, or operating vehicles/machinery; follow applicable driving restrictions. Return immediately for seizure lasting >5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, persistent confusion, new weakness, severe headache, fever/neck stiffness, breathing difficulty, injury, or other worsening symptoms.
