Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with seizure onset at age 30 and a reported diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures. The current seizure burden is approximately two events per month. The epilepsy is described as drug responsive on a two-medication regimen of topiramate and brivaracetam and therefore is not presently characterized as drug-resistant epilepsy.

A prior MRI reportedly demonstrated a tumor, leading to classification of the epilepsy etiology as structural. There is an important point requiring clarification: typical absence seizures are generally classified within generalized epilepsy, whereas a tumor is a focal structural abnormality unless imaging demonstrates otherwise. These findings are not necessarily mutually exclusive, but the MRI lesion should not be assumed to be seizure-generating without review of the imaging and correlation with EEG and clinical semiology.

Further review is needed to confirm whether the reported episodes are electroclinical typical absence seizures, whether there are additional unrecognized seizure types, and whether the MRI finding is causally related or incidental. The age at onset of 30 years also makes confirmation with prior EEG data or updated EEG evaluation particularly important.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Topamax (topiramate) and Briviact (brivaracetam) without an empiric change today. Exact doses, dosing schedules, adherence, tolerability, and prescribing history should be reconciled before medication adjustments are made.

2. Obtain the formal brain MRI report and the original MRI images for direct review at xxx Medical Center. Attention should be paid to the tumor’s location, imaging characteristics, and potential relationship to seizure networks.

3. Obtain prior EEG records, including original tracings if available. If prior studies cannot be obtained or did not capture the habitual episodes, updated EEG evaluation should be considered to confirm generalized IEDs and the classification of the reported absence seizures.

4. Maintain a seizure diary documenting the frequency of the episodes. The current reported baseline is approximately two seizures per month.

5. Because the epilepsy is described as drug responsive, there is insufficient information at this time to label the patient as having DRE or to recommend epilepsy surgery. Any future surgical consideration would require confirmation that the tumor is relevant to seizure onset, detailed MRI review, and concordant electroclinical data.

6. Follow up after medication reconciliation and receipt of the MRI and EEG records. Further recommendations can then be made regarding antiseizure medication optimization and whether additional VEEG/LTM or lesion-directed evaluation is appropriate.
