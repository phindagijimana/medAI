Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 9. Seizures are reported to occur approximately three times per week. The epilepsy is considered structurally associated in the setting of MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia and has been clinically drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are valproate and levetiracetam.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including an electrocardiographic channel. The recording was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and stage N2 sleep was achieved. No antiseizure medication changes were made for this study.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or sustained focal slowing.

Awake state: During wakefulness, occasional generalized bursts of sharply contoured 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity were present. These discharges were bilaterally synchronous and symmetric, with bifrontal amplitude predominance at Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4. Individual bursts lasted approximately 0.5-2 seconds and were not associated with a definite clinical change on video.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the PDR attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. Generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were mildly activated during drowsiness and sleep. No independent focal epileptiform discharge was identified over the region of the known focal cortical dysplasia.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced expected symmetric diffuse theta and delta slowing. A brief generalized spike-and-wave burst occurred without clinical correlate.

Photic stimulation: Photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 through 30 Hz produced symmetric photic driving responses at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: Occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, maximal bifrontally, were seen in wakefulness and were modestly activated by drowsiness and sleep. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or other periodic/rhythmic pattern was present.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. Specifically, no generalized tonic-clonic seizure occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with bifrontal predominance and activation in drowsiness and sleep. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are compatible with the patient’s clinical diagnosis of generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures.

2. No electrographic seizure was recorded. There was no persistent focal slowing or independent focal epileptiform activity to provide electrophysiologic localization to the MRI-demonstrated focal cortical dysplasia. The structural MRI finding should therefore be correlated with the complete electroclinical history, particularly given the generalized pattern on this study.
