Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Epilepsy with combined generalized and focal features of unknown etiology. Seizure onset at age 10 years. Current events are described as focal impaired-awareness seizures occurring approximately three times per month. Epilepsy is reported to be drug responsive. Current antiseizure medication: brivaracetam.

Technique: A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy, and stage N2 sleep was achieved. No antiseizure medication adjustment was made for this recording.

Description:

Background: The waking background was continuous and organized, with an anterior-to-posterior gradient. A symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and appropriately reactive to eye opening. An expected admixture of low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, occasional brief bursts of frontally predominant, bilaterally synchronous 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave activity were present, typically lasting less than 1 second and without clinical correlate. In addition, rare sharp waves were identified independently over the left anterior temporal region, maximal at F7 with a field involving FT9 and T7.

Drowsy/sleep: Drowsiness was accompanied by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and sleep spindles. Generalized epileptiform discharges were mildly activated by sleep. Rare left anterior temporal sharp waves persisted during sleep. No electrographic seizure was identified.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced a mild, symmetric buildup of theta activity. There was no sustained focal abnormality and no electroclinical seizure.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation from 1-20 Hz produced symmetric photic driving at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:
1. Occasional generalized, frontally predominant 3-4 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges.
2. Rare independent left anterior temporal sharp waves, maximal at F7/FT9 with extension to T7.
3. No LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing.

Ictal events: No clinical or electrographic seizures were captured. No patient-reported habitual focal impaired-awareness event occurred during the recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine awake and sleep EEG due to occasional generalized spike-and-wave/polyspike-and-wave discharges and rare independent left anterior temporal epileptiform discharges.
2. These findings indicate both generalized and focal epileptogenic potential and are compatible with the reported epilepsy syndrome with combined generalized and focal features. The focal discharges show a left anterior temporal predominance.
3. No electrographic seizure was recorded. A normal or seizure-free interictal recording would not exclude epilepsy; clinical correlation remains necessary regarding the reported focal impaired-awareness seizures occurring approximately three times monthly.
