Neurology Progress Note

**Chief Complaint:** Follow-up epilepsy.

**History of Present Illness:**  
The patient is a 32-year-old man with epilepsy of unknown type, characterized by tonic-clonic seizures of unknown onset, beginning at age 14. His epilepsy has been considered likely immune-mediated. He remains on dual antiseizure therapy with Keppra (levetiracetam) and Vimpat (lacosamide). This history is carried forward from prior neurology documentation.

Since the last visit, he reports an average of approximately six seizures per week. Events remain convulsive/tonic-clonic by description, although onset has not been established. He tracks events on a seizure calendar and reports that the overall frequency has remained near this baseline. No medication changes have been made since the prior encounter. He reports taking levetiracetam and lacosamide consistently. There have been no interval emergency department visits or hospitalizations documented since the last encounter.

He continues to have a meaningful seizure burden despite substantial response to his current regimen. His epilepsy has historically been categorized as drug-responsive because seizure frequency improved with antiseizure medications, although he is not presently seizure-free. His primary concern today is the persistence and unpredictability of the events. We reviewed the importance of maintaining an accurate seizure diary, including time of day, possible triggers, duration, and recovery period.
