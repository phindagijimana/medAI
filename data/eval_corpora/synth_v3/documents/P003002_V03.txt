Indication: Focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 9 in the setting of structural pathology, with MRI evidence of hippocampal sclerosis. The epilepsy has been drug responsive, and the patient is currently seizure free, with 0 seizures per month, while taking perampanel. EEG obtained to assess for interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel electrocardiogram. Referential and bipolar montages were reviewed. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. Video was recorded concurrently. The patient was awake and drowsy, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. Perampanel was continued.

Description:

Background: During wakefulness, the background was continuous, symmetric, and well organized. A 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally over the occipital regions and attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or generalized background slowing.

Awake state: The awake background contained predominantly alpha and beta frequencies with intermixed low-amplitude theta activity. No focal attenuation or sustained focal slowing was identified.

Drowsiness and sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric theta activity with slow roving eye movements. Brief stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and bilateral frontocentral sleep spindles. No epileptiform discharges were activated by drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform activity. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of hyperventilation.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No definite focal spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, LPDs, GPDs, or other epileptiform abnormalities were identified. There was no persistent focal delta slowing.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. No push-button events occurred.

Electrocardiogram: The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and briefly asleep states.  
2. No focal or generalized epileptiform discharges and no electrographic seizures were recorded.

A normal interictal EEG does not exclude epilepsy. In this patient with drug-responsive focal epilepsy, focal impaired-awareness seizures, and structural MRI evidence of hippocampal sclerosis, the findings should be interpreted in conjunction with the clinical history and neuroimaging. The patient is currently seizure free on perampanel, with a reported seizure frequency of 0 per month.
