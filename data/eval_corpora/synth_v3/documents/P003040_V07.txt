Indication:  Evaluation of epilepsy with unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 54. The epilepsy is considered drug-resistant and of structural etiology, with hippocampal sclerosis reported on MRI. The patient is currently seizure-free, with no seizures during the past month, while taking brivaracetam and carbamazepine.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The study included synchronized video recording. Standard activation procedures consisted of hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness and drowsiness. Brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity that was reactive to eye opening and closure. Low-amplitude beta activity was present over the frontocentral regions. The anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradients were preserved. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or focal slowing.

Awake state: The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No focal attenuation, breach rhythm, or excessive generalized slowing was present.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, symmetric 4-7 Hz theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform activation occurred during drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform abnormalities. The background returned promptly to baseline following cessation of hyperventilation.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced a symmetric posterior photic driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, periodic discharges, or TIRDA. No electrographic pattern preferentially involving either temporal region was observed.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and brief stage N2 sleep. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.

2. This recording does not provide electrographic evidence permitting classification of the epilepsy as focal or generalized; the epilepsy type and onset of the reported tonic-clonic seizures remain unknown. The absence of epileptiform abnormalities on a single routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. Clinical correlation with the reported structural MRI finding of hippocampal sclerosis and the patient’s longitudinal seizure history is recommended.
