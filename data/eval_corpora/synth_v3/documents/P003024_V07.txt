Assessment / Impression

This patient has recurrent unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 33, currently reported at a frequency of approximately four per month. On the information available today, the epilepsy type remains unknown because neither the clinical history nor EEG data establish focal versus generalized onset.

The reported vascular malformation supports a structural etiology. Depending on its location and characteristics, it could be relevant to seizure onset; however, this cannot be determined without review of the MRI images, formal radiology report, and electroclinical data.

The supplied phenotype characterizes the epilepsy as drug responsive, and the patient remains on topiramate. The reported ongoing frequency nevertheless warrants reconciliation with that designation. Specifically, we need to confirm the current topiramate regimen, treatment adherence, seizure dates, and the magnitude of improvement compared with the pretreatment baseline. There is not enough information to classify the patient as having drug-resistant epilepsy or to determine candidacy for epilepsy surgery.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Topamax (topiramate) at the currently prescribed regimen for now. The patient should not change or abruptly discontinue topiramate without guidance from the prescribing clinician. We will obtain the exact dose, frequency, duration of therapy, tolerability, and refill history.

2. Obtain a more detailed event history, ideally including a witness account. Particular attention should be paid to the earliest observable manifestation, duration, recovery, and whether all events are stereotyped. This will help determine whether the tonic-clonic seizures are focal to bilateral or generalized in onset.

3. Request the complete prior MRI report and MRI images for direct review at xxx Medical Center. The type, location, and extent of the vascular malformation should be clarified.

4. Obtain a routine EEG if one has not already been completed. If routine testing is nondiagnostic and events continue at the reported frequency, prolonged ambulatory EEG or inpatient VEEG/EMU evaluation may be considered to clarify seizure onset and classification.
