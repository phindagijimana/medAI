Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures beginning at age 9, reported at approximately three per week and responsive to antiseizure medication. Current medications are carbamazepine and topiramate. The epilepsy has been categorized as structural in the setting of MRI-demonstrated hippocampal sclerosis. EEG requested to assess interictal epileptiform activity.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, supplemented by a single-lead ECG. The recording was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm was a symmetric, well-organized 9 Hz alpha rhythm that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present anteriorly. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  Frequent generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges were present, typically lasting 0.5-2 seconds. These discharges were synchronous and symmetric, with maximal expression over Fp1/Fp2, F3/F4, and F7/F8. No definite clinical correlate was apparent on video.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep showed symmetric vertex waves and bilateral sleep spindles. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges became more frequent during drowsiness and sleep, at times occurring in brief runs lasting up to 3 seconds. No electrographic evolution or associated clinical myoclonus was identified. No independent temporal sharp waves were seen.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced expected generalized theta and delta slowing. There was a modest increase in generalized epileptiform discharge frequency, without a sustained electrographic seizure or observable clinical change.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at multiple flash frequencies. Generalized spike/polyspike-and-wave discharges were intermittently elicited at higher frequencies, consistent with a photoparoxysmal response. No clinical event occurred.

Interictal findings:  Frequent generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, enhanced by drowsiness, sleep, hyperventilation, and photic stimulation. There were no LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or focal epileptiform discharges. Specifically, no definite left or right temporal epileptiform abnormality was identified despite the reported MRI finding of hippocampal sclerosis.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical myoclonic events were captured.

Impression:

1. Abnormal awake and sleep EEG due to frequent generalized, frontally predominant 3.5-4.5 Hz spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, with activation in drowsiness and sleep and a photoparoxysmal response. These findings support an increased seizure propensity within a generalized epilepsy network and are compatible with the reported history of myoclonic seizures.

2. No focal slowing or independent temporal epileptiform discharges were present. The reported hippocampal sclerosis is not associated with a focal electrographic abnormality on this recording; clinical and imaging correlation is recommended.

3. No electrographic seizures were recorded. A normal ictal sample cannot be assessed because none of the patient’s typical events occurred.
