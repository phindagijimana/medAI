Assessment / Impression

This patient has generalized epilepsy of unknown etiology, manifesting as generalized tonic-clonic seizures with onset at age 36. The reported seizure frequency is approximately seven per week. Zonisamide is the current antiseizure medication, and the referral information describes the epilepsy as drug responsive.

There is an apparent need to reconcile the “drug responsive” designation with the reported frequency of seven generalized tonic-clonic seizures per week. If seven per week is the current frequency despite an adequate and tolerated zonisamide regimen, then the degree of response and overall treatment status require reassessment. Conversely, if this frequency reflects the pretreatment baseline, the current seizure burden should be documented separately.

The MRI finding is described only as an incidental lesion. Based on the information presently available, it should not be assumed to explain the generalized epilepsy. Review of the actual images and formal report will be necessary before assigning any clinical importance to that finding.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Zonegran (zonisamide) at the currently prescribed regimen pending verification of the exact dose, frequency, adherence, tolerability, and response. I would not make an empiric dose change without those details.

2. Clarify the seizure timeline, including whether seven generalized tonic-clonic seizures per week represents the current frequency or a historical baseline. A seizure diary is recommended to document dates, seizure type, and treatment response.

3. Obtain and review prior neurology records, including any routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, LTM/EMU evaluations, and prior antiseizure medication history.

4. Obtain the prior brain MRI images and formal radiology report for direct review. The lesion should remain classified as incidental unless imaging and electroclinical data support a relationship to the epilepsy.

5. If prior EEG data are unavailable or insufficient, obtain an EEG to help confirm the generalized epilepsy classification. More prolonged monitoring may be considered if the clinical events or epilepsy classification remain uncertain.

6. Review zonisamide dosing and monitoring once the medication regimen is verified. Future treatment decisions should be based on the confirmed current seizure frequency, medication exposure, tolerability, and objective diagnostic data.

7. We discussed that recurrent generalized tonic-clonic seizures carry meaningful safety risk. The patient should avoid driving unless permitted under applicable state law and cleared by the treating clinician. Additional precautions include avoiding unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Showers are preferred to baths.

8. The patient should seek emergency medical attention for a prolonged convulsive seizure, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury, or other acute concern.

9. Follow up in the epilepsy clinic after outside EEG and MRI records have been obtained, or sooner if seizure frequency worsens.
