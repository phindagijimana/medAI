Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation for interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with focal epilepsy characterized by focal impaired-awareness seizures. Seizures began at age 79 and initially occurred approximately five times per week. Etiology remains unknown. The epilepsy has been drug-responsive, and the current antiseizure medication is zonisamide.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead electrocardiogram. The recording was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages. Activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and became drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background: The awake background was continuous and organized, with an anterior-to-posterior gradient of mixed alpha and beta frequencies. A symmetric 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present and appropriately reactive to eye opening and closure. Low-amplitude beta activity was observed anteriorly. There was no persistent focal asymmetry.

Awake state: During maximal wakefulness, the background remained symmetric and well organized. No focal slowing, lateralized periodic discharges, generalized periodic discharges, or rhythmic delta activity was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, there was expected attenuation of the posterior dominant rhythm and emergence of symmetric diffuse theta activity. Vertex waves and sleep spindles were not present; therefore, definite stage N2 sleep was not recorded.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation was performed with fair effort and produced mild, symmetric background slowing without focal or epileptiform activation.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, or generalized epileptiform discharges.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. This is a normal routine EEG in the awake and drowsy states. No focal slowing, epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.

2. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude a clinical diagnosis of epilepsy. The reported history remains consistent with focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures, of unknown etiology, with onset at age 79 and an initial frequency of approximately five seizures per week. The epilepsy is reported to be drug-responsive on current zonisamide therapy. Clinical correlation is recommended.
