Assessment / Impression

This is an XX-year-old patient with seizures beginning at age 9. The reported events are myoclonic seizures occurring approximately three times per week, and the epilepsy has been classified as generalized and drug-responsive. The patient is currently taking carbamazepine and topiramate.

A structural etiology has been assigned on the basis of reported hippocampal sclerosis on MRI. However, the available documentation does not include the imaging itself, the formal MRI description, or EEG data. Hippocampal sclerosis is commonly considered in the evaluation of focal epilepsy, whereas the referral information describes a generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures. This does not establish that the reported diagnoses are incorrect, but it does make direct review of the prior studies and a more detailed electroclinical correlation particularly important.

The current frequency of approximately three myoclonic seizures per week should also be reconciled with the description of the epilepsy as drug-responsive. At present, there is insufficient information regarding medication doses, prior treatment response, or seizure burden over time to determine whether the current regimen is optimized. Carbamazepine can be relevant to the expression of myoclonic seizures in some generalized epilepsies, so I would not recommend an empiric medication change until the seizure classification, actual dosing, and prior response history are verified.

Recommendations / Plan

1. We will request the complete prior neurology records, including detailed seizure descriptions, prior antiseizure medication trials, and the current carbamazepine and topiramate doses and schedules.

2. We will obtain the formal MRI report and, if possible, the original brain MRI images for direct review, with particular attention to the reported hippocampal sclerosis.

3. We will request prior routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, VEEG, or EMU reports and tracings, if these studies were performed. If adequate EEG data are not available, updated EEG characterization should be considered to confirm the generalized classification and evaluate for IEDs associated with the reported myoclonic events.

4. The patient should continue the currently prescribed carbamazepine and topiramate regimen without an unverified dose change until the medication list and prescribing instructions are confirmed. A complete medication reconciliation is needed.

5. After the outside records and diagnostic studies are reviewed, we will reassess whether the current electroclinical picture is best classified as generalized epilepsy with myoclonic seizures and whether the reported hippocampal sclerosis is clinically concordant.

6. Although the epilepsy is currently characterized as drug-responsive, the reported frequency of three seizures per week warrants clarification at follow-up. Further treatment recommendations will depend on confirmation of event type, medication doses, prior response, and available EEG and MRI findings.
