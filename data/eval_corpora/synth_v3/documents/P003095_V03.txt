Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date: xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider: Dr. xxx  
Indication: Evaluation of focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures. Seizures began at age 21 and currently occur approximately once per month. The epilepsy is clinically categorized as structural and drug responsive. Current antiseizure medications are zonisamide and perampanel. The available brain MRI is reported as normal.

Technique: A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using 21 scalp electrodes placed according to the international 10-20 system. A single-lead electrocardiogram was recorded. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness and drowsiness. Brief stage N2 sleep was achieved. The study was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages.

Description:

Background: During maximal wakefulness, there was a symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm of medium amplitude that attenuated appropriately with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior voltage and frequency gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was distributed symmetrically over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal asymmetry or focal attenuation.

Awake state: The waking background was continuous and composed predominantly of alpha and beta frequencies with normal organization and reactivity. No focal slowing was identified.

Drowsy/sleep: With drowsiness, the posterior dominant rhythm attenuated and diffuse theta activity emerged. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform discharges were activated by drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation: Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal abnormality or epileptiform activation. The background returned promptly to baseline after completion of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation: Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz elicited a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings: No focal, multifocal, or generalized epileptiform discharges were present. Specifically, there were no spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, LPDs, or GPDs. No persistent focal or generalized slowing was seen.

Ictal events: No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-lead ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm during the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG during wakefulness, drowsiness, and brief stage N2 sleep. There are no focal or generalized epileptiform abnormalities and no electrographic seizures.
2. A normal interictal EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy. The reported focal impaired-awareness seizures, including their approximately monthly frequency despite current zonisamide and perampanel therapy, should be correlated with the clinical history. Although the epilepsy is clinically categorized as structural, the available MRI is reported as normal; correlation with epilepsy-protocol neuroimaging may be considered if clinically indicated.
