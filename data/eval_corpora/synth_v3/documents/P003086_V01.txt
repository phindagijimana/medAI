Assessment / Impression

This is a 74-year-old patient with seizure onset at age 74 and a referral diagnosis of drug-resistant generalized epilepsy with typical absence seizures. The present reported seizure frequency is 0 per month while taking valproate, lacosamide, perampanel, and lamotrigine.

There is an important electroclinical classification question. Typical absence seizures are generally associated with generalized epilepsy, whereas hippocampal sclerosis/MTS is a focal structural finding more commonly considered in the evaluation of temporal lobe epilepsy. The supplied information identifies both generalized epilepsy and structural etiology, but it does not include the semiologic details, EEG findings, or MRI images necessary to determine how these data fit together. Late onset at age 74 further supports confirming the event type and epilepsy classification before making major treatment decisions.

The referral labels the condition drug resistant despite a current frequency of 0 seizures per month. These statements are not necessarily mutually exclusive, as the drug-resistant designation may reflect the longitudinal history while the current four-drug regimen is providing seizure control. However, the treatment history and basis for the drug-resistant designation were not included in the records available today.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current antiseizure medication regimen for now. I would not recommend changing valproate, lacosamide, perampanel, or lamotrigine until the exact doses, frequencies, prior response, and treatment history have been reconciled.

2. Obtain a complete medication list, including formulation, dose, frequency, adherence, and any prior ASMs. Particular attention should be paid to the concurrent use of valproate and lamotrigine when the regimen is verified.

3. Request the original brain MRI images and complete radiology report for review at xxx Medical Center, with attention to the reported hippocampal sclerosis.

4. Obtain prior EEG records, including any routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, LTM, EMU, or VEEG data. The key question is whether the electrographic pattern supports generalized epilepsy, focal temporal epilepsy, or another classification.

5. If prior studies are unavailable or nondiagnostic, consider updated EEG evaluation. Inpatient EMU monitoring may be appropriate if events recur or if electroclinical clarification is needed before medication reduction or surgical evaluation.

6. Because the present seizure frequency is 0 per month, no immediate escalation is recommended based solely on frequency. Any future medication simplification should be cautious and guided by verified seizure history, EEG findings, MRI review, and confirmed doses.

7. The reported hippocampal sclerosis should not by itself establish surgical candidacy. If the epilepsy is ultimately shown to be focal, recurrent, and drug resistant, a formal presurgical evaluation could then be considered. If generalized epilepsy is confirmed, management would remain medically focused.

8. The patient should continue standard seizure precautions and follow applicable NY driving requirements. The patient should contact the treating neurologist promptly for recurrent events or medication concerns.
