Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Generalized epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 13. Seizures occur approximately twice yearly and are considered drug responsive on topiramate. Genetic testing identified a causative SCN1A c.5734C>T variant. Prior brain MRI demonstrated an incidental lesion. Evaluate for interictal epileptiform abnormalities.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-lead ECG. The record was reviewed using referential and bipolar montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was recorded during wakefulness and drowsiness, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The waking background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized, with a 9-10 Hz posterior dominant rhythm that attenuated normally with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude frontocentral beta activity was noted. There was no persistent focal slowing or significant interhemispheric asymmetry.

Awake state:  During wakefulness, occasional generalized spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges occurred in brief bursts lasting 0.5-2 seconds. These were frontally predominant, maximal over Fp1/Fp2 and F3/F4, broadly synchronous, and typically had a frequency of approximately 3.5-5 Hz. No definite clinical change accompanied these discharges.

Drowsy/sleep:  Drowsiness was characterized by attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was identified by symmetric vertex waves and sleep spindles. Generalized spike-wave discharges were modestly activated during drowsiness and sleep. No focal sleep activation was present.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced expected symmetric diffuse theta slowing. There was a mild increase in generalized epileptiform discharge frequency, without an electrographic seizure or clinical correlate.

Photic stimulation:  Symmetric photic driving was present at several flash frequencies. No photoparoxysmal response was elicited.

Interictal findings:  Occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave complexes were present, with activation during drowsiness, sleep, and hyperventilation. There were no independent focal IEDs, LPDs, GPDs, TIRDA, or persistent focal slowing.

Ictal events:  No electrographic or clinical seizures were captured. No habitual generalized tonic-clonic event occurred during this recording.

Impression:

1. Abnormal routine EEG due to occasional generalized, frontally predominant spike-and-wave and polyspike-and-wave discharges, mildly activated by drowsiness, sleep, and hyperventilation. These findings support an increased propensity for generalized-onset seizures and are concordant with the clinical diagnosis of genetic generalized epilepsy.

2. No electrographic seizures were recorded. There was no focal slowing or focal epileptiform activity to provide electrographic evidence that the previously described incidental MRI lesion is epileptogenic.

Clinical correlation is recommended. A normal or seizure-free sampling during this brief study does not exclude additional generalized tonic-clonic seizures outside the recording period.
