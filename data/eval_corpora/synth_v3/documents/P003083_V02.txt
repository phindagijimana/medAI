Medical Decision-Making (MDM):  
Presentation is most consistent with breakthrough focal seizure in a patient with known drug-resistant focal epilepsy. She had a brief event with typical semiology and short post-ictal period, followed by return to baseline. No recurrent seizure, status epilepticus, focal deficit, fever, meningismus, significant metabolic trigger, intoxication, or trauma. No benzodiazepine was required because the event resolved prior to arrival. No Keppra load was recommended by Neurology.

Differential included seizure, syncope, TIA, dysrhythmia, metabolic disturbance, infection, and psychogenic nonepileptic event. Syncope is less likely given witnessed staring with post-event confusion and absence of prodromal cardiopulmonary symptoms. TIA is unlikely without focal deficits. Serial exams remained normal, and patient tolerated PO and ambulated steadily.

Assessment:  
1. Breakthrough focal impaired-awareness seizure  
2. Drug-resistant focal epilepsy of unknown etiology

Disposition:  
Discharged home in stable condition after ED observation and Neurology consultation. Continue levetiracetam, lacosamide, and carbamazepine exactly as prescribed; no ED medication changes. Follow up promptly with established epilepsy specialist. Reviewed seizure precautions, including no driving per applicable state law, no swimming or bathing alone, no climbing heights, and no operating heavy machinery. Return immediately for seizure lasting over 5 minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, injury, fever, persistent confusion, severe headache, weakness, or other new symptoms.
