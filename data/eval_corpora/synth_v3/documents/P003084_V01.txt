Assessment / Impression

The patient has focal epilepsy with focal impaired-awareness seizures beginning at age 11. Seizures currently occur approximately 6 times per week despite treatment with brivaracetam, valproate, and lacosamide. The epilepsy is therefore drug resistant. The reported hippocampal sclerosis supports a structural etiology and raises the possibility of a potentially localizable epileptogenic focus; however, the currently available information is insufficient to establish seizure onset laterality or electroclinical concordance.

Given the frequent ongoing seizures and structural MRI finding, a comprehensive presurgical epilepsy evaluation is reasonable. This evaluation does not presume that the patient is a surgical candidate. Rather, its purpose would be to characterize the habitual events, determine whether seizures arise consistently from a focal region, review the MRI abnormality in detail, and assess whether the electroclinical and imaging data are concordant. Depending on those findings, treatment considerations could include resective or ablative surgery, neuromodulation with VNS, RNS, or DBS, or continued medication management.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue the current antiseizure medication regimen of brivaracetam, valproate, and lacosamide without change for now. Exact doses, formulations, and dosing frequencies should be confirmed before any medication adjustment.

2. Obtain a complete prior antiseizure medication history, including maximum tolerated doses, duration of treatment, efficacy, and reasons for discontinuation. This will help document the extent of medication resistance and identify any remaining reasonable pharmacologic options.

3. Request the prior MRI images and complete radiology report for direct review at xxx Medical Center. Particular attention should be paid to the laterality and extent of hippocampal sclerosis. If the prior study is unavailable or not technically adequate, an epilepsy-protocol MRI may be needed.

4. Arrange inpatient EMU admission for VEEG/LTM to capture the patient’s habitual focal impaired-awareness seizures and define electrographic seizure onset. With a reported frequency of approximately 6 seizures per week, event capture may be feasible, although no guarantee can be made.
