Neurology Progress Note

Chief Complaint: Follow-up drug-resistant focal epilepsy

History of Present Illness:  
The patient is a 15-year-old female with focal epilepsy of presumed genetic etiology, characterized by focal impaired-awareness seizures. Seizure onset was at age 14. Her epilepsy has remained drug-resistant despite treatment with multiple antiseizure medications, and she is currently taking Topamax (topiramate) and Trileptal (oxcarbazepine). Seizure lateralization has not been established. She was last seen on 09/25/2023 for her initial community neurology consultation.

Since the last visit, she continues to have approximately 7 focal impaired-awareness seizures per week. The family’s seizure calendar was reviewed and remains consistent with roughly daily events; individual event dates were not recorded for today’s visit. There have been no interval changes to topiramate or oxcarbazepine. No recent emergency department visits or hospitalizations for seizures were reported. She has not had a prolonged convulsive event or required emergency seizure treatment since the last appointment.

The primary concern today remains the continued seizure burden despite dual therapy. The family would like to improve seizure control while limiting medication adverse effects. No new focal neurologic symptoms are reported. We discussed that ongoing seizures at this frequency despite two appropriate antiseizure medications meet criteria for drug-resistant epilepsy and warrant further evaluation at a comprehensive epilepsy center.

Assessment and Plan:

1. Focal epilepsy, nonlateralized, drug-resistant  
   The patient has focal impaired-awareness seizures with onset at age 14 and a current frequency of approximately 7 per week despite treatment with topiramate and oxcarbazepine. Etiology is considered genetic. No clear lateralization has been established. Given persistent seizures on two antiseizure medications, her course is consistent with drug-resistant epilepsy.

   - Continue Topamax (topiramate) 100 mg twice daily.
   - Continue Trileptal (oxcarbazepine) 600 mg twice daily.
   - No medication changes today pending updated testing and review of prior records.
   - Check CBC and CMP, including sodium and serum bicarbonate, for medication safety monitoring.
   - Continue maintaining a seizure calendar with dates, duration, possible triggers, and recovery time.
   - Recommend referral to a comprehensive epilepsy center for prolonged video-EEG monitoring and further evaluation of seizure onset and treatment options.
   - Obtain and review prior EEG, neuroimaging, and genetic testing records if available. Additional epilepsy-protocol MRI or genetic evaluation can be considered based on prior workup.
   - Medication adherence was reinforced. Family was advised not to stop either medication abruptly.

2. Seizure Safety  
   Reviewed seizure precautions, including supervision around water, showers rather than unsupervised baths, avoidance of heights and open flames, and use of appropriate protective equipment. She should not drive. Family was instructed to call 911 for a seizure lasting longer than 5 minutes, repeated seizures without return to baseline, significant injury, breathing difficulty, or an event that is substantially different from her typical seizures.

3. Developmental History  
   Continue school-based and developmental supports. Further neuropsychological assessment may be useful as part of a comprehensive epilepsy evaluation, particularly if advanced treatment options are considered.

Follow-up: 3 months, sooner for increased seizure frequency, prolonged seizures, medication intolerance, or other new neurologic concerns.
