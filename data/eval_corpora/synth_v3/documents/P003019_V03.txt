Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Neurology  
Indication:  Evaluation for interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with focal epilepsy characterized by focal impaired-awareness seizures. Seizure onset was at age 39 in the setting of immune-mediated, GAD65 antibody-associated epilepsy. Current seizure frequency is approximately three per year. The epilepsy has been drug responsive, and the current antiseizure medication is levetiracetam.

Technique:  A 31-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The recording was reviewed using standard bipolar and referential montages. Activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording, and brief stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  In the maximally alert state, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 10 Hz alpha activity that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An appropriate anterior-to-posterior frequency and amplitude gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen anteriorly. There was no persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry.

Awake state:  The awake background was continuous and composed of an admixture of alpha and beta frequencies with occasional low-amplitude theta activity. No focal slowing was identified. There were no lateralized periodic discharges, generalized periodic discharges, or other rhythmic or periodic patterns.

Drowsy/sleep:  With drowsiness, there was expected attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse theta activity with slow roving eye movements. Brief stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves and synchronous sleep spindles. No epileptiform discharges were activated by drowsiness or sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal accentuation or epileptiform activity. The background returned promptly to baseline after cessation of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 30 Hz produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No definite spikes, sharp waves, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, or other interictal epileptiform discharges were present. Specifically, no convincing temporal epileptiform abnormality was identified at F7/T7 or F8/T8. No focal or generalized slowing was seen.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm throughout the recording.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and briefly asleep states. There are no focal, lateralizing, or epileptiform abnormalities.
2. No seizures or habitual clinical events were recorded. A normal routine EEG does not exclude the clinical diagnosis of focal epilepsy, including focal impaired-awareness seizures. Clinical correlation is recommended.
