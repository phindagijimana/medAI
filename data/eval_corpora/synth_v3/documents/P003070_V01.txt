Reason for Admission:  
This is a 38-year-old woman with drug-resistant generalized epilepsy and recurrent typical absence seizures despite treatment with three ASMs. She is admitted electively for up to 5 days of continuous video EEG monitoring to characterize her habitual events, confirm electroclinical seizure classification, quantify seizure burden, and guide medication management.

History of Present Illness:  
The patient developed seizures at age 38. Her habitual events have been classified clinically as typical absence seizures in the setting of generalized epilepsy. The available history describes recurrent episodes of impaired awareness/behavioral arrest occurring approximately four times per month. Further details regarding duration, associated eyelid flutter, automatisms, responsiveness, immediate recovery, and patient awareness of the events are not available in the referral documentation and will be clarified with the patient and observers during this admission. There is no supplied description of focal impaired awareness seizures, focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, generalized tonic-clonic seizures, lateral tongue biting, incontinence, prolonged post-ictal confusion, or prior status epilepticus.

She continues to have events despite concurrent treatment with Tegretol (carbamazepine), Keppra (levetiracetam), and Depakote (valproate), meeting clinical criteria for drug-resistant epilepsy. Exact outpatient doses, formulations, dosing schedules, adherence, serum levels, prior dose-limiting adverse effects, and the sequence of medication trials are pending formal medication reconciliation. The available record does not identify additional prior ASM trials. In particular, prior exposure to ethosuximide, lamotrigine, zonisamide, topiramate, clobazam, or other broad-spectrum ASMs is not documented. Because carbamazepine may aggravate absence seizures or generalized SWD in some generalized epilepsy syndromes, the electroclinical response to medication reduction will be assessed carefully during monitoring.

Her current event burden is approximately four seizures per month. Specific precipitants such as sleep deprivation, missed medication, illness, stress, alcohol use, or menstrual association have not been established from the available information. The epilepsy etiology remains unknown. The supplied records do not include prior MRI, routine EEG, ambulatory EEG, PET/SPECT, MEG, or prior LTM results. There is likewise insufficient documentation regarding prior epilepsy surgery or implanted neuromodulation; surgical history will be confirmed directly with the patient.

The goal of this admission is diagnostic rather than presurgical. We will attempt to record the habitual spells with simultaneous EEG, assess for generalized spike-and-wave or polyspike-and-wave discharges, determine whether reported events have an ictal EEG correlate, and evaluate for any unexpected focal features. Hyperventilation, photic stimulation, and controlled sleep deprivation may be used to increase diagnostic yield. Medication reduction will be staged to minimize the risk of generalized tonic-clonic seizures, injury, or SE.
