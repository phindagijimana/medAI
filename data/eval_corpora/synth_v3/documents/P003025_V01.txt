Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with epilepsy beginning at 28 years of age. The documented seizure type is myoclonic, with a reported frequency of approximately seven episodes per week. The epilepsy is classified as generalized and structural. The patient is currently taking valproate and is described as drug-responsive.

The reported hippocampal sclerosis introduces a possible discordance between imaging and the generalized myoclonic phenotype. Hippocampal sclerosis may be clinically relevant, incidental, or incompletely characterized in the absence of direct image review and EEG correlation. At present, I would not reclassify the epilepsy or infer a focal temporal onset based on the MRI report alone.

Although the patient continues to report frequent myoclonic events, the available history does not establish drug-resistant epilepsy. Only current valproate treatment is documented, and the case is specifically characterized as drug-responsive. There is therefore no current basis for a resective epilepsy surgery evaluation. Additional diagnostic clarification is appropriate before considering any change in classification or treatment.

Recommendations / Plan

1. Continue Depakote (valproate) without abrupt interruption pending medication reconciliation. The patient or referring office should confirm the exact formulation, dose, and dosing frequency before any treatment adjustment is made.

2. Obtain the outside brain MRI images and complete radiology report for direct review at xxx Medical Center. We will specifically assess the reported hippocampal sclerosis, including laterality and whether the study adequately evaluates the temporal structures.

3. Obtain prior EEG reports and tracings, if available. If no adequate EEG has been completed, I recommend an EEG to evaluate for generalized versus focal IEDs and to assess whether the reported myoclonic events have an electrographic correlate.

4. If the events remain insufficiently characterized after routine testing, VEEG or EMU monitoring may be considered. Capturing the habitual episodes would help determine whether they represent epileptic myoclonus and whether the electroclinical pattern is generalized or focal.

5. The patient should maintain a seizure diary documenting the number of myoclonic events, clustering, timing in relation to valproate dosing, and any missed medication doses. This will help quantify treatment response.

6. Routine monitoring appropriate for valproate therapy, including medication level and safety laboratory studies, should be reviewed or obtained through the treating clinician as clinically appropriate.

7. Standard seizure precautions were advised, including avoiding unsupervised swimming, heights, open flames, and operation of dangerous machinery. Driving should follow applicable NY requirements and the guidance of the treating clinician.

8. Follow-up with xxx Medicine Neurology is recommended after medication reconciliation and review of the MRI and EEG data.
