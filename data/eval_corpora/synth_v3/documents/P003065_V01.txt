Assessment / Impression

This is an adult patient with drug-resistant combined generalized and focal epilepsy of unknown etiology. The currently reported habitual events are generalized tonic-clonic seizures occurring approximately four times per week. Seizures began at age 23 and remain uncontrolled on lamotrigine, brivaracetam, and lacosamide.

The principal next step is diagnostic clarification. Generalized tonic-clonic semiology alone does not reliably distinguish generalized-onset seizures from focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. This distinction is particularly relevant because it will affect medication selection and whether a focal epilepsy surgical evaluation is appropriate. The reported combination of generalized and focal features should be confirmed through review of prior testing and, if needed, inpatient EMU monitoring.

The ongoing frequency despite a three-drug regimen is concerning and supports the diagnosis of drug-resistant epilepsy. Before changing therapy, we need to verify the exact doses, dosing schedule, adherence, prior ASM exposure, tolerability, and available EEG and imaging results. If focal onset is established, further presurgical evaluation may be appropriate. If the epilepsy proves generalized or multifocal without a resectable focus, neuromodulation options such as VNS or DBS may eventually be considered in the appropriate clinical context.
