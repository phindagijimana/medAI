Routine Electroencephalogram Report

Study Date:  xx/xx/20xx  
Ordering Provider:  Dr. xxx  
Indication:  Evaluation for interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with structural, drug-resistant focal epilepsy characterized by focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures. Seizure onset occurred at age 69. Brain MRI demonstrates encephalomalacia. The patient is currently seizure-free, with zero seizures per month, while taking topiramate, carbamazepine, and perampanel.

Technique:  A 32-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes and a single-channel ECG. The recording was reviewed using bipolar and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was achieved.

Description:

Background:  The awake background was continuous, symmetric, and appropriately organized. A well-formed 9 Hz posterior dominant rhythm was present bilaterally and attenuated with eye opening. An expected anterior-to-posterior gradient was maintained. Low-amplitude beta activity was present over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry.

Awake state:  No definite focal slowing was identified. There were no lateralized periodic discharges, generalized periodic discharges, or rhythmic delta patterns.

Drowsy/Sleep:  Drowsiness was associated with attenuation of the posterior dominant rhythm and the emergence of diffuse theta activity. Stage N2 sleep was characterized by symmetric vertex waves, sleep spindles, and K complexes. No epileptiform activation occurred during sleep.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal or epileptiform abnormalities.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation at frequencies from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric posterior driving response at several frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No definite epileptiform discharges were seen. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave complexes, TIRDA, or generalized spike-and-wave discharges.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured. The single-channel ECG demonstrated a regular rhythm.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake, drowsy, and sleep states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.

2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. The patient’s established clinical diagnosis remains structural, drug-resistant focal epilepsy with focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures, with MRI-demonstrated encephalomalacia. Clinical correlation is recommended.
