Indication:  Evaluation of interictal epileptiform activity in a patient with drug-resistant epilepsy of unknown type. The patient has a history of unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures beginning at age 54 and a structural etiology based on MRI evidence of hippocampal sclerosis. The patient is currently treated with brivaracetam and carbamazepine and reports no seizures during the past month.

Technique:  A 30-minute digital EEG was performed using the international 10-20 system with 21 scalp electrodes, including an electrode for cardiac monitoring. The EEG was reviewed using longitudinal bipolar, transverse bipolar, and referential montages. Standard activation procedures included hyperventilation for 3 minutes and intermittent photic stimulation. The patient was awake and drowsy during the recording. Stage N2 sleep was not achieved.

Description:

Background:  During maximal wakefulness, the posterior dominant rhythm consisted of symmetric, well-organized 9-10 Hz alpha activity that was reactive to eye opening and closure. An anterior-to-posterior gradient was present. Low-amplitude beta activity was seen over the frontocentral regions. There was no persistent focal attenuation or asymmetry.

Awake state:  The waking background was continuous and appropriately organized. No sustained focal delta slowing was identified. There were no LPDs, GPDs, or other periodic or rhythmic patterns.

Drowsy / sleep:  With drowsiness, there was expected attenuation of the PDR and emergence of diffuse, predominantly central theta activity with slow roving eye movements. Brief vertex transients were present. Definite sleep spindles and K complexes were not observed; therefore, stage N2 sleep was not attained.

Hyperventilation:  Hyperventilation produced mild, symmetric generalized theta slowing without focal activation or epileptiform discharges. The response resolved promptly after completion of the maneuver.

Photic stimulation:  Intermittent photic stimulation from 1 to 20 Hz produced a symmetric photic driving response at several flash frequencies. There was no photoparoxysmal response.

Interictal findings:  No definite epileptiform discharges were identified. Specifically, there were no focal sharp waves, spikes, spike-and-wave discharges, TIRDA, or generalized spike-and-wave complexes. No focal slowing was seen.

Ictal events:  No electrographic seizures or clinical events were captured.

Impression:

1. Normal routine EEG in the awake and drowsy states. No focal slowing, interictal epileptiform discharges, or electrographic seizures were identified.

2. A normal routine EEG does not exclude epilepsy. The patient’s clinical epilepsy type and seizure onset remain unknown based on this recording. The reported unknown-onset tonic-clonic seizures, structural MRI finding of hippocampal sclerosis, and history of drug resistance should be correlated with the broader clinical history and prior diagnostic studies. Current seizure frequency is zero over the past month while receiving brivaracetam and carbamazepine.
